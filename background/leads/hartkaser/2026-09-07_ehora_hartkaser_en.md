*Base value of the reference wind speed (and the corresponding base velocity pressure). Yellow dots on the overview map mark the Austrian reference-wind measurement stations.*

![Austria wind reference-station overview map](images/austria_wind.jpg)

**Values:**

- vb,0: 35.57 m/s
- qb,0: 0.79 kN/m²

**Data source:** Administrative data: BEV · Water bodies, DTM, DHM, DOP: BMLUK · Reference wind speed: ÖNORM B 1991-1-4 and ÖNORM EN 1991-1-4

**Explanation:**

The base value of the reference wind speed (vb,0) is defined at 10 m above ground for open, unobstructed terrain of terrain category II and corresponds to the provisions of ÖNORM B 1991-1-4 and ÖNORM EN 1991-1-4 in the current version, as well as the locality index in Annex A of ÖNORM B 1991-1-4:2023-04-01. If the site elevation is more than 250 m above that of the nearest locality listed in Table A.1 of ÖNORM B 1991-1-4:2023-04-01, values from Table A.2 of the same standard must be used, unless a specific wind assessment from one of the institutes named in Section 6.2.2 of ÖNORM B 1991-1-4:2023-04-01 is available.

The corresponding base value of the reference velocity pressure (qb,0) matches the locality index in Annex A of ÖNORM B 1991-1-4:2023-04-01, or was calculated according to ÖNORM B 1991-1-4, Formula (1). Reduction factors fs for reference velocity pressures per ÖNORM B 1991-1-4, Table 3 have not been applied. Sites with relative elevations up to 250 m above the nearest locality and below 800 m absolute elevation may also lie in wind-exposed positions. This must be taken into account in wind-load calculations where relevant.

The online version of the map underlying this query has been agreed with Austrian Standards International, but the above values are provided without warranty. The elevation model underlying the calculation, covering the Austrian federal states, has a resolution of 1 m (horizontal) and 0.1 m (vertical). Values for vb,0 and qb,0 have been rounded to one and two decimal places respectively.

![Seismic zone labels](images/legend_seismic_zones.png)

**Data source:** Administrative data: BEV · Water bodies, DTM, DHM, DOP: BMLUK · Seismic zones: ÖNORM EN 1998-1

**Explanation:**

The seismic-hazard map for Austria shows the "horizontal reference ground acceleration" as per the currently valid ÖNORM B 1998-1. The seismic action indicated has a 90% probability of not being exceeded within a 50-year period (average building use).

Map accuracy is 2 km, since that is the localisation accuracy of earthquake events. Consequently, in transition areas some localities appear to lie in the neighbouring zone. In such cases, the agR value stated for the specific locality applies. Zone assignment is defined in the standard: the zone to be used is the one into which the site-specific agR value falls. If the value lies exactly on a zone boundary, the higher zone must be chosen, with correspondingly stricter requirements.

## 4 — Hail hazard — maximum hailstone size

![Austria hail-hazard overview map](images/austria_hail.jpg)

**Values (return period → maximum hailstone size):**

- 30 years: > 4 cm — ≤ 5 cm
- 20 years: > 4 cm — ≤ 5 cm
- 10 years: > 3 cm — ≤ 4 cm

**Data source:** Administrative data: BEV · Water bodies, DTM, DHM, DOP: BMLUK · Hail hazard: ÖNORM B 3663:2026-07-15

**Explanation:**

The Austrian hail-hazard map is based on a meta-statistical model for calculating return periods of hailstone sizes. The complex dependencies of hail events are captured through location, time and selected atmospheric input variables. Hail events are derived from hail indicators in three-dimensional, high-resolution radar measurements over 2009–2022, calibrated against observed hailstone sizes from a hail-report archive. Hailstone sizes are available in five size classes (interpretable as "hail hazard") for return periods of 10, 20 and 30 years.

The hail-hazard map shows local structures at high resolution. Only in a few regions with no or too few hail events does the result carry larger uncertainty and may not be shown. The confidence measure represents these local uncertainties on a 5-star scale (5 stars: highest confidence).

The horizontal spatial resolution is 1 km. The elevation model underlying the presentation has a resolution of 50 m (horizontal) and 0.1 m (vertical). All regions up to 1500 m elevation are covered, since below that lies the highest concentration of agricultural and material assets and above that there is insufficient basis for validation. Class values are given as integers.
