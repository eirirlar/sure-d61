# 1. Excellence #@REL-EVA-RE@

The proposed project is built on the main result of the Horizon Europe project **SUREWAVE (Grant Agreement No. 101083342)**: an integrated floating photovoltaic (FPV) concept protected by a dedicated floating breakwater designed to reduce wave-induced loads, improve structural reliability, and enable FPV deployment in environments that are currently too exposed for conventional floating solar systems.

The SUREWAVE project developed and validated following technologies, at laboratory and model scale: (1) a floating breakwater specifically designed for FPV protection; (2) coupled aero-hydrodynamic and structural simulation tools; (3) mooring and anchoring concepts ; (4) structural health monitoring methodologies; (5) redesigned connection and hinge concepts ; and (6) experimental validation of the protected FPV concept through wave-basin campaign, published in Energies 17(9), 2059 (2024), DOI: 10.3390/en17092059 **Yes, this proposal is the main result of the previous project.**

**Relation between the previous project result and the innovation in this proposal:** The SUREWAVE result is the core technological foundation of this proposal and represents its main innovation component. SUREWAVE validated the protected FPV concept and its critical components in controlled laboratory and model conditions (TRL 4), but did not validate the fully integrated system during long-term, grid-connected operation at a real site. SUREWAVE-DEMO therefore focuses on the next step: integrating the validated building blocks, manufacturing a pilot-scale demonstrator, deploying it in a relevant operational environment, and generating the technical, commercial and conformity evidence required for market deployment and TRL 6.

Evidence supporting the TRL4 starting point and prior technical results. The SUREWAVE Interim Progress Report for the M19-M24 reporting period (1 April-31 October 2024) records: (i) completion of a 1:10 MARIN wave-basin campaign on the protected FPV concept; (ii) manufacture of a 3.4 m x 1.7 m x 1.05 m reduced-scale floating-breakwater prototype using the developed circular concrete solutions; (iii) laboratory three-point-bending, cyclic, pull-out, impact, water-penetration and chloride-resistance testing; (iv) a coupled aero-hydrodynamic and structural modelling chain covering 2 MW and 10 MW configurations, 50-year-wave load cases and mooring optimisation; and (v) connection testing that measured 402 kN ultimate strength for the breakwater-component wire system and 41 kN for the breakwater-to-FPV connection. The report also records a 34-59% reduction in mooring mass for Mediterranean and North Sea design cases and the identification and redesign of the PV-to-PV hinge as the remaining critical fatigue item. These results support TRL 4 because they demonstrate feasibility and validation in controlled laboratory and model conditions. They do not establish long-term and site-specific operational performance, **which is the purpose of SUREWAVE-DEMO**.

The innovation in this proposal is therefore the transition from laboratory-scale validation (TRL 4) to a fully integrated operational demonstrator and commercially validated system (TRL 6).

## 1.1 Technological breakthrough #@PRJ-OBJ-PO@

FPV technology is one of the fastest-growing segments of the renewable energy sector, yet almost all commercial deployments remain limited to sheltered lakes and reservoirs. The principal barrier to wider deployment is wave loading. In exposed reservoirs and inland water bodies, wind acting over long fetch distances generates short, steep waves that induce significant motions, fatigue loads, and impact forces on floating solar systems. These loads affect connectors, mooring systems, and floating structures, increasing maintenance requirements, reducing reliability, and limiting project economics.

Current FPV technologies address this challenge primarily by increasing the structural robustness of the system through thicker floaters, stronger connectors, heavier mooring systems, and additional reinforcement. While these solutions improve survivability, they also increase capital costs, installation complexity, and maintenance requirements. More ![](media/image1.jpeg){width="3.7916666666666665in" height="2.527083333333333in"}importantly, they do not address the root cause of the problem: the environmental loads acting on the FPV array. As wave exposure increases, the economic attractiveness of conventional FPV systems rapidly decreases, preventing deployment in many of the large reservoirs and inland water bodies that offer the greatest floating solar potential.

The breakthrough nature of SUREWAVE is that it changes the design philosophy from **load resistance** to **load management**. Instead of continuously strengthening the solar platform to withstand higher environmental loads, SUREWAVE creates a protected operating environment through a dedicated floating breakwater designed specifically for floating solar applications. The breakwater attenuates wave energy before it reaches the FPV array, significantly reducing motions, fatigue loading, and structural demands on the solar installation.

This approach provides several competitive advantages compared with state-of-the-art FPV systems. It enables deployment in more exposed reservoirs and inland waters, reduces structural loading and fatigue, lowers maintenance requirements, improves operational reliability, and creates the potential for lower lifecycle costs. The breakwater itself can also provide additional functionality by hosting electrical equipment, access infrastructure, marina facilities, or other waterside applications, improving the overall value proposition.

The project combines several innovations developed and validated within the Horizon Europe SUREWAVE project, including a floating breakwater optimized for FPV protection, advanced coupling and mooring concepts, high-fidelity aero-hydrodynamic and structural modelling tools, and structural health monitoring methodologies. A key strength of the concept is that critical technical risks have already been identified and addressed. Laboratory and connection testing measured an ultimate strength of 402 kN for the breakwater-component wire system and 41 kN for the breakwater-to-FPV connection. The PV-to-PV hinge was identified as the residual critical component under fatigue and snapping loads, leading to a redesigned hinge and a clearly defined follow-up validation activity. This gives the proposed project measured component evidence and a bounded residual risk, rather than an untested concept, while leaving the integrated system and long-term operational performance to be demonstrated in SUREWAVE-DEMO.

In summary, SUREWAVE is not simply a stronger floating solar platform. It is a technology that actively reduces the environmental loads acting on the solar array, enabling reliable and economically attractive deployment in water bodies that are currently beyond the practical limits of conventional FPV technologies. This represents a fundamentally new approach to floating solar deployment and creates a clear pathway towards unlocking large new markets for floating renewable energy systems.

**The commercial potential of SUREWAVE** lies in its ability to enable floating photovoltaic (FPV) deployment at water bodies that are currently uneconomic or technically unsuitable for conventional floating solar systems due to wave loading. The addressable market consists of two main segments. The first segment is reservoir-based floating solar. Existing reservoirs already benefit from grid connections, road access, and established permitting frameworks. Previous studies estimate that covering only **1% of global reservoir surface could support approximately 404 GW**[^1] **of solar capacity**, while 25% coverage could support around **4,400 GW**, highlighting the enormous, untapped potential of inland-water FPV deployment. The main barrier to accessing this market is wave exposure at large reservoirs, which increases structural loads, fatigue, and operating costs.

The second segment is harbours, ports, and sheltered coastal waters. These sites often suffer from limited land availability and high real-estate values, making floating solar an attractive alternative. However, wave-induced loading frequently limits the viability of conventional FPV systems. SUREWAVE addresses this challenge through a dedicated floating breakwater that creates a protected operating environment for the solar array. The global floating solar market is estimated at approximately **USD 7.9 billion in 2026** and is expected to grow at around **12% annually**[^2], while the nearshore and coastal floating solar segment is forecast to grow at approximately **35% CAGR to 2030**. SUREWAVE targets precisely the market gap between conventional sheltered-water FPV systems and more complex offshore floating solar concepts.

The key commercial advantage of SUREWAVE is its ability to make exposed sites economically viable through **environmental load management rather than structural reinforcement alone**. Instead of continuously increasing the strength, mass, and cost of the FPV array, SUREWAVE reduces the loads reaching the solar system through wave attenuation. This has the potential to reduce structural requirements, maintenance needs, and lifecycle costs while increasing the number of deployable sites.

The commercial attractiveness of the concept is further supported by the consortium\'s existing market access. **EDP** operates floating solar assets such as the **Alqueva's 5MW floating solar plant** and the **Floating PV Lab at Alto Rabagão**, while **Sunlit Sea** has an active Northern European project pipeline including **Skien Havn**, **Gunneklevfjorden**, and **Haugaland Næringspark**. These projects provide a direct pathway for future deployment and customer validation.

The commercial potential of SUREWAVE extends beyond energy generation. In harbour and waterfront applications, the floating breakwater can serve multiple functions in addition to wave protection. A well-known example is the **352 m floating breakwater at Port Hercule (Monaco)**[^3], which integrates approximately **25,000 m² of technical space** and a **360-car parking facility** in addition to harbour protection. Similarly, the SUREWAVE breakwater can host electrical equipment, charging infrastructure, walkways, marina facilities, or other waterfront services.

The project\'s commercial ambition is to enable a first **1--5 MWp commercial installation immediately after project completion**, followed by an illustrative cumulative deployment of **50--100 MWp within five years**. At an indicative system value of **€0.80/Wp**, this corresponds to approximately **€40--80 million** of potential contract value across the integrated SUREWAVE offering. The project will validate these assumptions through techno-economic analysis, customer engagement, and operational demonstration, establishing whether the protected-FPV architecture can become a commercially attractive solution for wave-exposed water bodies.

## 1.2 Objectives

The overall objective of SUREWAVE-DEMO is to advance the SUREWAVE protected floating photovoltaic (FPV) technology from TRL 4 to TRL 6 through the design, manufacturing, deployment, and operation of an integrated pilot-scale demonstrator in a relevant operational environment. The project will use Hainer See (Germany) as the baseline demonstration site, supported by the signed site-owner Letter of Intent attached to the proposal, while EDP\'s Portuguese test facilities (Alto Rabagão's Floating PV Lab) will be assessed during the first six months as alternative deployment option. The project will validate the ability of the floating breakwater to reduce wave-induced loads, verify structural, hydrodynamic, and energy-yield models using operational data, and quantify system reliability, durability, and performance through at least 12 months of operation. The project will also achieve an independent conformity assessment against relevant industry standards, generating the technical and commercial evidence required for future large-scale deployment and commercialization of the SUREWAVE concept.

In addition, the following objectives address the key uncertainties that currently limit commercial deployment, including the lack of field-scale evidence on system performance, reliability, installation procedures, operational costs, and long-term operational behaviour.

O5 addresses another critical barrier to commercialization, namely confidence in the engineering tools used to design future commercial-scale projects. The value of those models therefore depends directly on their demonstrated predictive accuracy.

O7 focuses on achieving TRL 6 and obtaining an independent conformity assessment. This objective is particularly important because DNV-RP-0584 already provides a recommended practice for floating photovoltaic systems, while DNV-ST-C108 (Structural Design of Floating Solar Structures) and DNV-ST-E309 (Mooring Systems for Floating Solar Installations), released in 2026, now provide a clear certification pathway for floating solar technologies. For the first time, there is a framework through which floating solar installations can be independently assessed, certified, insured, and financed using established industry practices. Achieving conformity with these standards therefore goes beyond technical validation and directly supports commercial deployment.

The business objectives run in parallel from M1 to ensure that customer requirements, economic constraints, certification needs, and market expectations influence the system design before the M12 design freeze. This reduces the risk of developing a technically successful solution that does not meet the requirements of future customers, investors, insurers, and project developers.

## 1.3 Methodology #@CON-MET-CM@# #@COM-PLE-CP@

The project builds directly on the results of the **Horizon Europe SUREWAVE project**, which successfully developed and validated the individual technological building blocks of the concept. These include the floating breakwater architecture, coupled aero-hydrodynamic and structural models, mooring concepts, monitoring methodologies, and critical structural components such as the redesigned hinge system. The proposed methodology is therefore not focused on developing new component technologies, but on integrating, demonstrating, validating, and commercialising these existing results. The methodology consists of four interconnected phases.

**Phase 1 -- Engineering and Design (M1-M12).** The project will establish the system requirements, assess candidate demonstration sites, and develop the detailed engineering design of the integrated FPV-breakwater system. Hainer See in Germany will serve as the baseline demonstration site because of its suitable environmental conditions, existing site agreement, and implementation readiness. In parallel, EDP\'s Portuguese candidate site (Alto Rabagão's Floating PV Lab) will be assessed during the first six months as alternative deployment options. Numerical models developed during SUREWAVE will be used to verify the design and optimise the FPV layout, breakwater configuration, and mooring system before manufacturing begins.

![](media/image2.jpeg){width="5.4944444444444445in" height="3.6638888888888888in"}**Phase 2 -- Manufacturing and Integration (M7-M20).** The demonstrator will be manufactured using industrial processes representative of future commercial deployment. Sunlit Sea will manufacture the FPV platform, while CLEMENT will manufacture the floating breakwater and mooring system. Instrumentation, telemetry, and monitoring systems will be installed and tested before deployment. Factory acceptance testing will verify compliance with design specifications and ensure readiness for deployment.

**Phase 3 -- Demonstration and Validation (M15-M34).** The integrated SUREWAVE system will be deployed and operated for at least 12 months under real environmental conditions. Continuous monitoring will provide structural, environmental, reliability, and energy-performance data to validate wave attenuation, load reduction, energy production, durability, and predictive engineering models. Operational data will reduce the key technical uncertainties preventing commercial deployment.

At the end of the campaign, the demonstrator will be recovered and inspected. The floating breakwater and mooring system will be reused by CLEMENT for future applications, while PV modules, inverters, and electrical equipment will be redeployed or transferred to secondary markets where feasible. This will provide additional evidence on durability, reliability, lifecycle performance, and component reuse.

**Phase 4 -- Commercialisation and Scale-Up (M1-M36).** Market validation, customer engagement, techno-economic assessment, investor outreach, and business-model development will run throughout the project. Commercial assumptions will progressively be replaced by real performance, cost, and operational data generated by the demonstrator, resulting in an investment-ready business plan and commercialization roadmap.

The methodology is well suited to address the remaining uncertainties associated with the technology. Laboratory and basin testing have already demonstrated technical feasibility; the major unknowns now relate to system integration, long-term operation, reliability, maintenance requirements, and commercial performance. These can only be addressed through deployment in a relevant operational environment. The modular nature of the FPV system and floating breakwater also provides flexibility to adapt the design if operational results indicate the need for optimization or risk mitigation.

**[Technology Readiness Level:]{.underline}** The key result generated by the SUREWAVE project is currently at TRL 4, corresponding to technology validated in laboratory conditions. The interim report provides the supporting evidence: a completed 1:10 wave-basin campaign; a manufactured 3.4 m x 1.7 m x 1.05 m reduced-scale breakwater prototype; mechanical and durability tests on the circular concrete solutions; numerical analysis of 2 MW and 10 MW configurations under 50-year-wave conditions; optimised mooring and connection designs; and laboratory connection-strength measurements. The report also documents the remaining integration gap: long-term, grid-connected operation and site-specific performance were not yet demonstrated. Major milestones already achieved include: (1) development of the protected floating-solar concept; (2) validation of the floating-breakwater concept through wave-basin testing; (3) development of coupled aero-hydrodynamic and structural models; (4) development of mooring and anchoring concepts; (5) identification and mitigation of critical structural failure modes; and (6) development of structural-monitoring methodologies.

The intended end-user application is **grid-connected floating solar generation on exposed reservoirs, industrial water bodies, and costal areas where wave loading limits conventional FPV deployment**.

The objective of this project is to advance the integrated system to **TRL 6** by demonstrating and operating it in a relevant environment. Achieving TRL 6 requires: Completion of site-specific design and engineering. Manufacturing and deployment of the integrated demonstrator. Successful operation for at least 12 months. Validation of energy production, structural performance, and reliability.

**[Unique Selling Point:]{.underline}** The unique selling point of SUREWAVE is straightforward. **SUREWAVE does not attempt to make floating solar systems stronger; it makes the water calmer.** Current solutions address increasing environmental loads by reinforcing the FPV array itself. SUREWAVE instead uses a dedicated floating breakwater to reduce wave energy before it reaches the solar platform. This shifts the design philosophy from **load resistance** to **load management**, enabling deployment in water bodies that are currently unsuitable for conventional FPV systems. This project will further develop that advantage by: Demonstrating the integrated concept at pilot scale. Validating performance under real operating conditions. Establishing a certified and bankable engineering basis. Quantifying installation, operation, and lifecycle costs. Developing the route to commercial deployment.

**[Gender Dimension:]{.underline}** The research and innovation activities focus on hydrodynamics, structural behaviour, floating renewable-energy systems, and energy production. These topics concern physical systems and do not involve human subjects or user groups for which sex- or gender-based analyses would affect the scientific outcomes. Consequently, a gender dimension is not considered relevant to the technical research content of the project.

**[Open Science:]{.underline}** The project will implement open-science practices in a manner that supports both scientific dissemination and commercial exploitation. **A Data Management Plan (DMP) will be prepared by M6 and updated throughout the project following FAIR principles**. Scientific publications will follow Horizon Europe open-science requirements and, where appropriate, be published through **Open Research Europe**. All public disclosures will undergo an internal review to balance openness with future commercialization objectives. Commercially sensitive information, including manufacturing processes, detailed engineering drawings, proprietary formulations, business models, and exploitation-related information, will remain protected where disclosure would compromise future commercialization. This balanced approach ensures that the project contributes to scientific knowledge while preserving the intellectual-property assets required for market uptake and long-term commercial impact.

## 2.1 Credibility of the impacts

SUREWAVE-DEMO will advance the laboratory-validated protected floating photovoltaic (FPV) concept to TRL 6 in exposed inland water and establish its route to commercial deployment. The commercial opportunity is to make suitable wave-exposed sites investable by reducing the loads and operating risks imposed on the solar array. The project will determine where these benefits justify the additional cost of a floating breakwater, using measured performance, customer purchasing requirements and a costed first-project opportunity.

The initial market is European reservoir and artificial-lake owners, energy developers and industrial water users whose sites face material wave constraints. Priority sites have a dominant wave direction that permits selective protection, limiting breakwater length per installed MW. Sheltered harbours and coastal sites are an adjacent market requiring additional marine qualification. This focus connects the inland demonstrator to a defined first market while preserving a route to wider deployment.

## 2.2 Economic and/or societal benefits

The main economic benefit is access to additional suitable water surfaces for renewable generation and a repeatable European equipment-and-service offering. For reservoir and industrial-water owners, value depends on the generating capacity made viable, lifecycle costs and compatibility with existing operations. Hainer See will establish the inland validation basis; coastal and multifunctional breakwater uses provide subsequent expansion options.

Scale-up relies on repeatable FPV units, modular breakwaters and validated engineering methods adapted to each site. The 1, 10 and 50 MW assessments will establish how protection length, water depth and logistics affect costs and where larger arrays improve economics. D6.5 will link the deployment ambition to production capacity, staffing and repeatable procurement, installation, quality control and service practices.

European value creation. Sunlit Sea and CLEMENT provide routes to retain floating-structure manufacture, integration, installation and services in Norway, Germany and other European markets, supported by SINTEF and EDP engineering capabilities. The opportunity concerns the non-cell portion of the solar value chain, including skills in marine structures, materials, monitoring and system integration. Initial exploitation is intended in EU Member States and Associated Countries. Later international supply or licensing would retain know-how and commercial returns with the European partners. Employment estimates will distinguish permanent production and engineering roles from temporary installation activity; local procurement expenditure will be distinguished from imported content.

Resource efficiency and climate impact. FPV can reduce the need for an equivalent ground-mounted installation and may share existing grid and access assets where capacity and approvals permit. These benefits will be assessed against a defined alternative for each replication site. The lifecycle assessment will include the additional breakwater materials, manufacture, transport, maintenance and end-of-life treatment, alongside delivered electricity. It will report results per unit of electricity with consistent lifetime and displacement assumptions. Mix selection will balance embodied emissions, durability and cost. Continued operation after the trial remains subject to agreements and permissions and will be assessed separately from immediate recovery. Water quality, shading and competing uses will inform site suitability; any evaporation benefit will require site-specific evidence.

## 3.2 KPIs, milestones and risks

Progress will be monitored through a combination of **technology KPIs (O1-O7)** and **business validation KPIs (O8-O12)**, which together track both technology maturation and market readiness. This dual approach is a key characteristic of EIC Transition projects, ensuring that technical development and commercial validation progress in parallel.

The technology KPIs focus on demonstrating the core value proposition of the SUREWAVE concept. In particular, **O2 (wave attenuation)** and **O3 (load reduction)** directly test whether the floating breakwater reduces environmental loads sufficiently to improve the technical and economic viability of floating solar deployment at exposed sites. Measurements from the monitoring system will provide quantitative evidence by M30, while O5 will establish the predictive accuracy of the engineering models used to design future commercial-scale projects. O7 will verify the advancement of the technology to **TRL 6** through demonstration in a relevant operational environment and independent conformity assessment.

The business validation KPIs focus on customer acceptance, market attractiveness, and investment readiness. These include engagement with at least **15 relevant organisations**, the completion of techno-economic assessments at commercial scales, confirmation of freedom to operate, acquisition of at least **three Letters of Intent (LOIs)**, and engagement with at least **ten investors or financial stakeholders**. Together, these KPIs validate customer demand, commercial viability, and the route to market.

Six project milestones mark the major decision points of the project: design basis frozen (M6), design released for manufacturing (M12), components manufactured and accepted (M20), demonstrator commissioned (M22), performance targets verified (M30), and TRL 6 plus commercial readiness achieved (M36). These milestones are aligned with the completion of key work packages and provide clear go/no-go decision points for both technical development and business validation.

**Critical Risks and Mitigation Measures**

The project faces both technical and market-related risks, which will be continuously monitored and managed throughout implementation.

**Applicability beyond inland-water environments.** The primary demonstration is conducted at Hainer See, representing the target market of exposed inland reservoirs. While the demonstrator does not reproduce all coastal conditions such as salinity or long-period swell, marine applicability will be supported through accelerated ageing, salt-spray testing, operational experience from Sunlit Sea installations, and numerical assessment of marine load cases. This approach ensures that coastal deployment remains a credible future market opportunity while TRL 6 is achieved for the primary inland-water application.

**Environmental conditions during the demonstration campaign.** There is a risk that the operational period does not experience sufficiently challenging environmental conditions. To mitigate this, the monitoring programme is designed to extract statistically meaningful fatigue and performance information from normal operating conditions. Model-based extrapolation and the possibility of extending the operating period provide additional confidence in the validation process.

**Manufacturing and deployment costs.** A significant share of the project budget is concentrated in demonstrator manufacturing and deployment activities. Cost overruns or delays could therefore affect project implementation. Mitigation measures include fixed-price procurement where feasible, factory acceptance testing before deployment, strict change-control processes after the M12 design freeze, and early involvement of suppliers and deployment partners.

**Market acceptance and commercial uptake.** Customer adoption may be slower than expected if the perceived benefits of protection do not outweigh the additional costs. The project addresses this risk through early customer engagement, techno-economic analysis, demonstrator visits, and continuous validation of the business model against real customer requirements. Success will be measured through stakeholder engagement activities, LOIs, and investor feedback.

A detailed summary of all KPIs and milestones is provided in **Table 3.3d**, while the complete risk register and mitigation measures are presented in **Table 3.3e**.

## 3.3 Work plan and resources #@WRK-PLA-WP@

The work plan is organised into six technical and business work packages plus project management over 36 months, with two reporting periods (**RP1: M1-M12; RP2: M13-M36**) in accordance with the EIC Transition reporting structure. WP6 runs throughout the project, ensuring that customer requirements, techno-economic assessment, investor engagement, exploitation planning, and dissemination activities mature in parallel with the technology.

The critical path is **WP2 → WP4 → WP5**, with key decision points at the M6 site-selection and design-basis freeze, the M12 design release for manufacturing, and the M22 commissioning milestone. Business validation activities start from M1 to ensure that customer requirements, commercial constraints, and market feedback are incorporated before the demonstrator design is frozen.
