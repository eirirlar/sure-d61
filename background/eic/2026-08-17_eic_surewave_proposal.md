## Technological breakthrough

-   Describe the degree of novelty and performance compared to other technologies available or in development, and the competitive advantages. In other words, how is it done today and what is the limit of the current practice?[^3]

**Degree of Novelty, Performance and Competitive Advantages**

Floating photovoltaic (FPV) technology has experienced rapid global growth during the last decade. However, nearly all commercial installations are located in reservoirs, lakes, quarry lakes and highly sheltered coastal waters. Deployment in exposed nearshore and offshore environments remains extremely limited due to the significant structural challenges associated with waves, wind, currents, corrosion and marine growth. Existing FPV technologies typically address these challenges by increasing the structural robustness of the PV floaters, connections and mooring systems. This approach results in increasing capital costs, higher maintenance requirements and growing system complexity while still limiting deployment to relatively benign environmental conditions. The fundamental limitation of current practice is that the floating solar platform remains directly exposed to environmental loads. SUREWAVE introduces a fundamentally different approach. Instead of strengthening the PV platform itself, the project has developed a protected floating solar concept where wave energy is attenuated before reaching the PV installation through an external modular floating breakwater system. This creates a protected operating environment that significantly reduces loads acting on the floating solar array and associated infrastructure. The innovation therefore shifts the design philosophy from "resisting environmental loads" to "controlling environmental loads", representing a major departure from current FPV development pathways.

The novelty of the solution is not limited to the floating breakwater itself. It combines several technological innovations into an integrated renewable energy platform:

-   A dedicated floating breakwater specifically designed for floating solar protection.

-   Innovative coupling systems between the breakwater and floating PV array.

-   New hinge and connection concepts designed for harsh marine environments.

-   Circular lightweight concrete and cellular concrete materials with reduced environmental footprint.

-   Optimized mooring and anchoring systems for large floating solar structures.

-   Advanced coupled aero-hydrodynamic simulation methodologies.

-   Structural integrity, fatigue and health-monitoring capabilities for long-term operation.

A major technological strength of SUREWAVE is that these innovations have already undergone extensive validation. During the project, two separate wave basin testing campaigns were carried out at MARIN. The first campaign evaluated the floating breakwater/FPV concept under both mild and harsh environmental conditions and compared system behaviour with and without breakwater protection. Measurements were collected on floater motions, accelerations, wave overtopping and relative movements between PV modules. Results demonstrated the protective effect of the breakwater and generated validation data for numerical models. These findings were published in the peer-reviewed journal *Energies*.

The first testing campaign also identified critical loading conditions for mechanical connections between PV modules. Based on these findings, SUREWAVE carried out detailed ultimate-strength testing of critical connections and developed a new hinge design to address the weakest identified component in the system. By M36, the optimized connection system between the external breakwater and the floating PV array was designed, tested and demonstrated the ability to withstand critical load conditions, significantly reducing one of the major technical risks associated with offshore floating solar deployment.

Beyond physical testing, the project completed extensive numerical validation activities. High-fidelity aerodynamic and hydrodynamic simulations demonstrated that the breakwater substantially reduces environmental loads acting on the floating PV system. Structural integrity models, finite-element analyses, fatigue assessments, mooring optimization studies and hydrodynamic investigations for both 10 MW and modular 2 MW configurations have been completed. These activities provide a robust engineering foundation and significantly reduce uncertainty for future commercial deployment. A major technological strength of SUREWAVE is that these innovations have already undergone extensive validation. During the project, two separate wave basin testing campaigns were carried out at MARIN. The first campaign evaluated the floating breakwater-FPV concept under both mild and harsh environmental conditions and compared system behaviour with and without breakwater protection. Measurements were collected on floater motions, accelerations, wave overtopping and relative movements between PV modules. Results confirmed the protective effect of the breakwater and generated validation data for numerical models. In parallel, critical mechanical connections were tested under ultimate loading conditions, leading to the development of an improved hinge design capable of withstanding the most critical load cases identified during the project. Furthermore, a floating breakwater prototype based on the newly developed circular concrete solutions was successfully manufactured and validated at laboratory scale, significantly reducing the technical risks associated with future deployment. These achievements provide a robust engineering foundation and substantially increase technology readiness for integrated system demonstration.

Compared with state-of-the-art floating solar systems, the SUREWAVE concept offers several clear competitive advantages:

-   **Access to new deployment areas** by enabling floating solar installations in nearshore and semi-exposed coastal waters that are currently inaccessible to conventional FPV solutions.

-   **Reduced structural loading and fatigue** through wave attenuation before environmental loads reach the photovoltaic array.

-   **Improved reliability and operational availability**, leading to higher energy production and longer service life.

-   **Reduced maintenance requirements and lifecycle costs** through optimized structural design and protection mechanisms.

-   **Improved environmental performance** through the use of circular and low-carbon construction materials.

-   **Improved bankability and reduced investment risk** through validated engineering methodologies, advanced monitoring approaches and lower structural uncertainty.

The project has therefore moved beyond proof-of-concept and subsystem feasibility. The remaining challenge is the demonstration of the complete integrated system under operational conditions in a relevant nearshore environment. As a result, SUREWAVE has progressed significantly beyond conceptual design. The scientific principles, engineering methodologies, critical materials, breakwater technology, mooring solutions and connection systems have already been validated through modelling, laboratory testing and wave basin experiments. The remaining challenge is therefore not whether the concept works, but rather demonstrating the fully integrated system in a representative nearshore environment and validating its operational, economic and commercial performance. This is precisely the gap that the proposed EIC Transition project will address.

-   Describe the breakthrough nature of the innovation in this proposal. What is new in your approach and why do you think it will be successful?

In SUREWAVE project, the concept was driven by an idea where instead of designing the solar array to resist increasingly severe environmental loads, we create a **protected operating environment** through an integrated floating breakwater specifically designed for floating solar applications. The breakwater attenuates wave energy before it reaches the photovoltaic array, significantly reducing structural motions, fatigue accumulation, connector loading, and operational disruptions. This transforms the challenge from \"surviving harsh marine conditions\" into \"controlling the marine environment around the solar installation.\"

The innovation combines several technological breakthroughs into a single integrated energy system:

-   A modular floating breakwater specifically optimized for floating solar protection.

-   Novel circular concrete materials with high durability, low environmental footprint, and improved buoyancy characteristics.

-   Advanced coupling systems and transition elements connecting the breakwater and the FPV array.

-   New hinge concepts designed to withstand dynamic marine loading.

-   Optimized mooring and anchoring solutions for both harsh and moderate sea states.

-   Coupled aerodynamic, hydrodynamic, structural integrity and fatigue modelling tools.

-   Structural health monitoring capabilities enabling predictive maintenance and reduced lifecycle costs.

A key differentiator of this proposal is that the innovation has already been extensively de-risked through the Horizon Europe SUREWAVE project. Unlike many early-stage technologies, the concept is supported by a comprehensive validation programme. The consortium has completed advanced numerical modelling, finite element structural analysis, full system hydrodynamic studies, material characterization, durability testing, prototype manufacturing and physical validation activities.

Particularly important was the execution of two successive wave basin testing campaigns at MARIN. The first campaign validated the fundamental breakwater-protected FPV concept under both mild and harsh environmental conditions and demonstrated the effectiveness of the breakwater in reducing motions and loads acting on the PV array. The tests also identified critical weaknesses in existing PV connection systems. This led directly to the development of an improved hinge design and optimized coupling concepts. Subsequent testing and characterization demonstrated that the new connection systems are capable of withstanding critical load conditions while maintaining flexibility and structural integrity.

We believe the innovation will be successful because the main technological risks have already been addressed at component and subsystem level. The breakwater concept, structural materials, mooring systems, connectors, numerical models and monitoring approaches have all been validated through a combination of simulations, laboratory testing, prototype development and wave basin experiments. The remaining challenge is no longer proving that the concept works but demonstrating the complete integrated system in a representative nearshore environment. This is precisely the objective of the proposed EIC Transition project.

The successful demonstration of a 200--300 kWp protected floating solar installation in Trondheim will provide the final step necessary to transition the technology from a validated engineering concept to a commercially deployable solution. By enabling floating solar deployment in nearshore and semi-exposed coastal waters that are currently inaccessible to conventional FPV technologies, the innovation has the potential to unlock a completely new market segment for renewable energy generation and establish Europe as a leader in next-generation marine floating solar systems.

-   Describe the commercial potential of the technology.

The commercial potential of the SUREWAVE technology is substantial because it addresses one of the largest untapped opportunities in the solar energy sector: the deployment of floating photovoltaics (FPV) in nearshore and semi-exposed coastal waters. While conventional FPV systems are primarily restricted to reservoirs, lakes, and highly sheltered waters, SUREWAVE enables deployment in marine environments where the available area is significantly larger and where competition for land is limited.

The global floating solar market is already experiencing rapid growth, with forecasts indicating annual growth rates above 20% and market values reaching several billion euros by 2030. Importantly, market studies increasingly identify offshore and nearshore floating solar as the next major growth segment, driven by land scarcity, increasing electricity demand, and the need for large-scale renewable energy deployment close to coastal load centres.

Today, only a very limited number of companies worldwide are developing marine FPV solutions specifically designed for exposed coastal conditions. Existing commercial deployments remain largely confined to sheltered inland waters, while nearshore and offshore solutions are still at an early stage of development. This creates a significant first-mover opportunity for SUREWAVE. Competitors such as Oceans of Energy, Ocean Sun, Sungrow FPV and a small number of emerging marine FPV developers are currently demonstrating the feasibility of offshore solar, confirming growing market interest in this segment.

The addressable market is exceptionally large. Europe alone has tens of thousands of kilometres of coastline, while the global coastline exceeds several hundred thousand kilometres. Even if only a small fraction of protected nearshore waters is suitable for floating solar deployment due to environmental, navigational, and regulatory constraints, the available area remains enormous. For example, assuming that only 1% of the most suitable nearshore coastal zone along European coastlines were utilized for FPV deployment, the potential technical capacity would be measured in hundreds of gigawatts, far exceeding the current installed FPV capacity worldwide

SUREWAVE is particularly well positioned because it targets a market gap between conventional inland FPV systems and highly exposed offshore renewable energy installations. The technology can be deployed in ports, industrial coastal zones, sheltered fjords, aquaculture areas, coastal infrastructure developments, islands, and nearshore energy hubs where conventional FPV solutions face technical limitations. This flexibility significantly expands the potential customer base, including utilities, port authorities, industrial energy users, aquaculture operators, island communities, and renewable energy developers.

The commercial attractiveness is further strengthened by the extensive validation already completed within SUREWAVE. The technology has undergone advanced numerical modelling, structural testing, material validation, and wave basin testing, reducing key technical uncertainties and increasing investor confidence. The proposed EIC Transition project will provide the critical demonstration step required to move from technology validation to commercial deployment, positioning SUREWAVE to capture a share of the rapidly emerging nearshore floating solar market.

Overall, SUREWAVE has the potential to unlock a new market segment for renewable energy generation by making large-scale floating solar viable in coastal and nearshore environments that are currently inaccessible to conventional FPV technologies. This creates a strong pathway toward commercialization and significant growth opportunities in Europe and globally.

### WP3 — Design and Performance Assessment (SINTEF)

**Objectives**

- **Task 3.1 Numerical Modelling (SINTEF/WavEC)**
This task will develop and apply advanced numerical models to support the design, optimization, and validation of the SUREWAVE FPV system and its protective floating breakwater concept. Building on the outcomes of the SUREWAVE project, SINTEF has developed and validated a suite of high-fidelity numerical tools capable of accurately representing the complex interaction between waves, floating structures, mooring systems, and structural components under realistic offshore conditions.

For the hydrodynamic analysis, SINTEF will utilize Computational Fluid Dynamics (CFD) and Smoothed Particle Hydrodynamics (SPH) methodologies that have been validated through laboratory testing and previous SUREWAVE activities. These numerical frameworks integrate both **OpenFOAM** and **SPH solvers** with the dynamic mooring analysis tool **MoorDyn**, enabling fully coupled simulations of the floating breakwater, FPV platform, and mooring system. The models will be used to optimize the layout and configuration of the FPV array, assess wave attenuation performance, evaluate mooring loads, and investigate the response of the system under operational and extreme environmental conditions.

On the structural side, SINTEF has developed and validated advanced numerical models for concrete structural components, including material behaviour under static, cyclic, and extreme loading conditions. The modelling framework incorporates non-linear material behaviour, hinge response, joint performance, and coupled finite element (FEM) analysis to accurately predict structural integrity and failure mechanisms. These models will be applied to assess the structural response of the floating breakwater and FPV supporting structures under combined wave, wind, and mooring loads.

- **Task 3.2 Energy Yield Modelling and Assessment (EDP/SiS)**
-   Define energy-yield assessment methodology adapted to nearshore FPV systems.

-   Evaluate the impact of platform motions on PV energy production.

-   Assess losses induced by varying module orientations and wave-driven movement.

-   Quantify effects of (marine) environmental conditions on PV performance (salt deposition, humidity, increased wind cooling, shading, reflection from water surface?).

-   Develop simulation models combining metocean data, irradiation data, and PV performance models.

-   Assess uncertainty ranges for long-term energy production forecasts (P50/P90?).

-   Identify key design parameters affecting energy yield and bankability.

- **Task 3.3 Model Development (SINTEF/EDP)**
This task will develop a real-time monitoring framework that integrates sensor measurements, numerical models, and advanced data analytics to assess the performance and integrity of the SUREWAVE FPV system during operation. Data collected from load, motion, environmental, water ingress, and energy monitoring sensors will be combined with the hydrodynamic, mooring, structural, and energy performance models developed in Tasks 3.1 and 3.2. These models will be validated using experimental and demonstration data prior to their integration into the monitoring platform. Building on existing EDP and SINTEF digital solutions, machine learning and data-driven approaches will be employed to support performance assessment, load prediction, anomaly detection, and predictive maintenance. The resulting framework will provide real-time insights into system behaviour, structural health, and energy performance, supporting both the pilot demonstration and future commercial offshore FPV deployments.**
