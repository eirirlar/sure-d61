Casting Progress and Design Focus
Eirik described the polyurethane casting work, strong adhesion results with primer, and the time-consuming mold and curing cycle. He suggested focusing on the unresolved cavity infill design as a separate line of work.

2:29
Infill Concept and Material Options
Eirik outlined the moisture-ingress concern beneath the solar panel and proposed closed-cell foam, potentially combined with aluminum honeycomb. The group considered whether foam alone could provide adequate support and discussed the rationale for adding honeycomb.

10:14
Panel Specifications and Honeycomb Fit
The participants discussed glass construction, panel dimensions, and the need to accommodate commercially available modules. They clarified that the proposed honeycomb would be used without its skin layers.

12:16
Model, Infill Installation, and Bottom Plate
Eirik presented a model showing the junction boxes, cable routing, panel frame, and bottom plate. He explained why prefabricated infill would be difficult to install and favored filling the cavity before fitting the bottom plate, which also provides structural support and splash protection.

19:26
Heat Transfer and Silicone Alternative
The group discussed the expected heat-transfer performance of a foam-filled design and considered material alternatives. Matas proposed silicone spray, while Eirik weighed its potential cooling and simplicity benefits against water-protection and walkability concerns; whether to investigate it remained open in the available discussion.

24:28
PU Foam Manufacturability and Safety
Eirik raised questions about the manufacturability and application of PU foam. The group discussed primer toxicity, workplace safety, and the need to research and test production methods.

28:13
Prototype and Performance Testing
The participants considered sourcing panels and building a test rig despite the team’s lack of framed panels. Proposed tests included water ingress, leakage, bending, and static loading.

31:52
