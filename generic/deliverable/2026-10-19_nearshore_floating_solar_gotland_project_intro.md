## The project in brief

- The installation is a civilian photovoltaic demonstrator of approximately 1 MWp in Kappelshamnsviken bay off northern Gotland, comparable in size to a few hundred rooftop solar systems. Floating solar panels packed in a matrix are moored at a fixed location, with an external concrete breakwater intercepting incoming waves.
- It is part of a Horizon Europe application under the EUPI-PV call, to be submitted in December 2026, for two in-water demonstrators: approximately 4 MW in Germany and approximately 1 MW off northern Gotland. If funded, project start is preliminarily second half of 2027.
- The system combines two technologies: Sunlit Sea's second-generation CONNECT floater, and a concrete breakwater developed in the predecessor Horizon Europe SUREWAVE research project (Grant Agreement 101083342, 2022–2026, coordinated by SINTEF with seven partners from Norway, Spain, Germany and the Netherlands).
- Design life approximately 25 years, with regular maintenance by small boats from land.

\begin{itemize}
  \item The installation lies off northern Gotland in Kappelshamnsviken bay, close to Storugns on the north-western coast — a long-established industrial area around Nordkalk's limestone extraction and port operations.
  \item The water is shallow and relatively sheltered; wind and wave exposure are within the mild range the breakwater and floater are designed and verified for.

## Why floating solar

- Europe is diversifying its energy supply across generation types, geographies and suppliers. Floating solar adds a new location category to that mix, alongside land-based solar, wind and hydro.
- Floating solar uses surfaces that are not otherwise in productive use — reservoirs, lakes, sheltered coastal water. It does not compete with agriculture, forestry or housing for land.
- Most floating solar deployed to date sits on inland water. The Gotland installation is a step toward verifying the technology in nearshore marine conditions, where salt and temperature cycling are more demanding than any inland site.
- The sector is in early commercial deployment globally. European capability to design, build, operate and maintain these systems is a strategic asset for the energy transition.

## Sunlit Sea

- Norwegian technology company developing floating solar for marine conditions. The purpose is zero-emission electricity, economically competitive with land-based solar parks, delivered on water surfaces that are otherwise unused.
- The first-generation product has been in commercial operation at two sites: 60 kWp at the Mannheim Cablepark in Germany since 2023, and 105 kWp at Skiftestjørna in Haugaland Næringspark since autumn 2024 under Sunlit Sea's own power-purchase agreement. Both have run as expected.
- The second-generation product, Sunlit Sea CONNECT, is close to commercial release. The Gotland installation would be the first pilot at MW scale — the step the design needs to take from 100-kWp-class references to the size at which the technology competes in the wider market.
- The four-year Horizon Europe demonstration project funds the installation and its first years of operation. If the pilot performs as designed, the floater stays in commercial operation for its 25-year design life, with power sold to local industrial users.

## Local site coordination

- The project needs a civilian site coordinator on Gotland, responsible for practical coordination between Sunlit Sea and local actors on the island.
- Tasks: dialogue with Länsstyrelsen Gotland and Region Gotland on permits; contact with landowners and industrial neighbours; coordination of seabed surveys and environmental investigations; logistics around port and transport; information meetings with nearby residents; ongoing reporting back to Sunlit Sea.
- Sunlit Sea has asked Olle Roman to take this role, for his knowledge of Gotland and of the local actors a site coordinator needs to work with.

## Scope and timeline

- The coordinator role runs 10–30 % time on average, higher around permit submissions, seabed surveys and installation preparation.
- Approximately 24 months in total, counted from project start — preliminarily second half of 2027 if the Horizon Europe application is funded.
- Carried out entirely on Gotland — no travel required.
