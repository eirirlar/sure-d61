Hi Morten,

Thanks for the extension and the three clarifications — revised proposal attached (rev. 2).

**1. Delivery schedule.** We can't fix calendar dates before contract signature. The proposal now shows the schedule in weeks from signing. If we sign around 1 December 2026, handover is early June 2027 and first kWh around 10 June — matching your permit target. If signing is delayed, everything moves with it. Details in §3.1.

**2. On-site scope.** Confirmed: Sunlit Sea delivers the floating platform to Risø and does not do installation, mooring, anchoring or shore-side electrical. Your local contractors handle that side. Scope split in §4.1.

Your question about mooring made us wonder if there's a project constraint behind it we're missing — could you tell us what prompted it? To be clear on what the array actually needs: it floats in open water, so wind and current will push it around. It has to be held in place at perimeter points around its full outline — some can attach to the Risø peninsula (NE side), but the three open-water sides need either bottom anchors or existing marine infrastructure to attach to. There is no configuration where a 35 kWp array in open water does not need mooring at multiple perimeter points.

We don't design or install mooring. The array is delivered with a continuous perimeter rope running 360° around its full outline; the mooring contractor attaches their mooring lines to that rope wherever the mooring design requires. Sunlit Sea supplies the weight of the installation, how it sits in the water, and the perimeter-rope specification. A mooring contractor on your side designs and installs whatever fits the site, the permit and any infrastructure already built. To finalise §2.5 we need to know:

- Is a mooring contractor already lined up on your side, or still to be procured?
- What does the Risø permit currently cover — attaching to the floating bridge, bottom anchors in the fjord, or both?
- Are there existing dolphins, bollards or seabed fixtures at the pier that can be re-used?

We will update §2.5 once we know.

**3. Inverter in scope.** Priced at €10,000 for a Tier-1 European 30 kWac string inverter (Fronius Tauro, Huawei SUN2000-33KTL-A or SMA Tripower X-25 class), delivered to Risø. Installation by your electrical contractor.

Honest note: inverter supply is not our business. The €10,000 covers the hardware plus a conservative handling margin — it is not a competitive price. If DTU can buy the inverter directly, the base scope drops to €61,000 (Table 4 line 16) and you likely get better unit economics. Worth checking against a couple of Danish wholesaler quotes before deciding.

Totals: with inverter €71,000; without inverter €61,000.

**4. Payment terms.** The inverter is now in our scope and we pay our supplier for it early, so the upfront share moved from 20 % to 40 %. Full split: 40 / 40 / 10 / 10 at signature / factory release / delivery / handover. If 40 % upfront is a problem for DTU procurement, we are open to alternatives (letter of credit against factory release, back-weighted progress payments) at contract.

Happy to walk through any of this before 13 November.
