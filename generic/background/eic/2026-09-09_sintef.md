## Technological breakthrough \#@PRJ-OBJ-PO@#

- Describe the degree of novelty and performance compared to other technologies available or in development, and the competitive advantages. In other words, how is it done today and what is the limit of the current practice?[^3]

**Degree of Novelty, Performance and Competitive Advantages**

Floating photovoltaic (FPV) systems are primarily deployed in sheltered lakes and reservoirs, where wave loading is limited. Current approaches to operate in more energetic environments rely on strengthening floaters, connectors, and mooring systems, resulting in higher costs and increased complexity.

The SUREWAVE concept introduces a different approach: instead of reinforcing the FPV platform itself, an external floating breakwater attenuates wave energy before it reaches the solar array. This reduces motions, structural loads, and fatigue, creating a protected operating environment for the FPV system.

The concept combines: 1) A dedicated floating breakwater for FPV protection. 2)Optimized coupling and mooring systems. 3) Advanced aero-hydrodynamic and structural simulation tools. 4)Monitoring and predictive maintenance methodologies.

These technologies have already been validated through numerical modelling, laboratory testing, and wave-basin experiments in the Horizon Europe SUREWAVE project, demonstrating the effectiveness of the protected FPV concept. Compared with existing FPV technologies, SUREWAVE offers: 1) Deployment in more exposed lakes and reservoirs. 2)Reduced structural loads and fatigue.3) Improved reliability and operational availability. 4) Lower maintenance requirements and lifecycle costs. 5) Reduced investment risk through validated engineering tools and monitoring approaches.

The remaining challenge is demonstrating the complete integrated system under real operating conditions. The proposed EIC Transition project addresses this gap through pilot-scale deployment and operational validation, providing the technical and commercial evidence required for future scale-up.

> Describe the breakthrough nature of the innovation in this proposal. What is new in your approach and why do you think it will be successful?

In SUREWAVE project, the concept was driven by an idea where instead of designing the solar array to resist increasingly severe environmental loads, we create a **protected operating environment** through an integrated floating breakwater specifically designed for floating solar applications. The breakwater attenuates wave energy before it reaches the photovoltaic array, significantly reducing structural motions, fatigue accumulation, connector loading, and operational disruptions. This transforms the challenge from "surviving harsh marine conditions" into "controlling the marine environment around the solar installation."

The innovation combines several technological breakthroughs into a single integrated energy system:

- A modular floating breakwater specifically optimized for floating solar protection.

- Novel circular concrete materials with high durability, low environmental footprint, and improved buoyancy characteristics.

- Advanced coupling systems and transition elements connecting the breakwater and the FPV array.

- New hinge concepts designed to withstand dynamic marine loading.

- Optimized mooring and anchoring solutions for both harsh and moderate sea states.

- Coupled aerodynamic, hydrodynamic, structural integrity and fatigue modelling tools.

- Structural health monitoring capabilities enabling predictive maintenance and reduced lifecycle costs.

A key differentiator of this proposal is that the innovation has already been extensively de-risked through the Horizon Europe SUREWAVE project. Unlike many early-stage technologies, the concept is supported by a comprehensive validation programme. The consortium has completed advanced numerical modelling, finite element structural analysis, full system hydrodynamic studies, material characterization, durability testing, prototype manufacturing and physical validation activities.

Particularly important was the execution of two successive wave basin testing campaigns at MARIN. The first campaign validated the fundamental breakwater-protected FPV concept under both mild and harsh environmental conditions and demonstrated the effectiveness of the breakwater in reducing motions and loads acting on the PV array. The tests also identified critical weaknesses in existing PV connection systems. This led directly to the development of an improved hinge design and optimized coupling concepts. Subsequent testing and characterization demonstrated that the new connection systems are capable of withstanding critical load conditions while maintaining flexibility and structural integrity.

We believe the innovation will be successful because the main technological risks have already been addressed at component and subsystem level. The breakwater concept, structural materials, mooring systems, connectors, numerical models and monitoring approaches have all been validated through a combination of simulations, laboratory testing, prototype development and wave basin experiments. The remaining challenge is no longer proving that the concept works but demonstrating the complete integrated system in a representative nearshore environment. This is precisely the objective of the proposed EIC Transition project.

The successful demonstration of a 200–300 kWp protected floating solar installation in Trondheim will provide the final step necessary to transition the technology from a validated engineering concept to a commercially deployable solution. By enabling floating solar deployment in nearshore and semi-exposed coastal waters that are currently inaccessible to conventional FPV technologies, the innovation has the potential to unlock a completely new market segment for renewable energy generation and establish Europe as a leader in next-generation marine floating solar systems.

> Describe the commercial potential of the technology.

The commercial potential of the SUREWAVE technology is substantial because it addresses one of the largest untapped opportunities in the solar energy sector: the deployment of floating photovoltaics (FPV) in nearshore and semi-exposed coastal waters. While conventional FPV systems are primarily restricted to reservoirs, lakes, and highly sheltered waters, SUREWAVE enables deployment in marine environments where the available area is significantly larger and where competition for land is limited.

The global floating solar market is already experiencing rapid growth, with forecasts indicating annual growth rates above 20% and market values reaching several billion euros by 2030. Importantly, market studies increasingly identify offshore and nearshore floating solar as the next major growth segment, driven by land scarcity, increasing electricity demand, and the need for large-scale renewable energy deployment close to coastal load centres.

Today, only a very limited number of companies worldwide are developing marine FPV solutions specifically designed for exposed coastal conditions. Existing commercial deployments remain largely confined to sheltered inland waters, while nearshore and offshore solutions are still at an early stage of development. This creates a significant first-mover opportunity for SUREWAVE. Competitors such as Oceans of Energy, Ocean Sun, Sungrow FPV and a small number of emerging marine FPV developers are currently demonstrating the feasibility of offshore solar, confirming growing market interest in this segment.

The addressable market is exceptionally large. Europe alone has tens of thousands of kilometres of coastline, while the global coastline exceeds several hundred thousand kilometres. Even if only a small fraction of protected nearshore waters is suitable for floating solar deployment due to environmental, navigational, and regulatory constraints, the available area remains enormous. For example, assuming that only 1% of the most suitable nearshore coastal zone along European coastlines were utilized for FPV deployment, the potential technical capacity would be measured in hundreds of gigawatts, far exceeding the current installed FPV capacity worldwide

SUREWAVE is particularly well positioned because it targets a market gap between conventional inland FPV systems and highly exposed offshore renewable energy installations. The technology can be deployed in ports, industrial coastal zones, sheltered fjords, aquaculture areas, coastal infrastructure developments, islands, and nearshore energy hubs where conventional FPV solutions face technical limitations. This flexibility significantly expands the potential customer base, including utilities, port authorities, industrial energy users, aquaculture operators, island communities, and renewable energy developers.

The commercial attractiveness is further strengthened by the extensive validation already completed within SUREWAVE. The technology has undergone advanced numerical modelling, structural testing, material validation, and wave basin testing, reducing key technical uncertainties and increasing investor confidence. The proposed EIC Transition project will provide the critical demonstration step required to move from technology validation to commercial deployment, positioning SUREWAVE to capture a share of the rapidly emerging nearshore floating solar market.

Overall, SUREWAVE has the potential to unlock a new market segment for renewable energy generation by making large-scale floating solar viable in coastal and nearshore environments that are currently inaccessible to conventional FPV technologies. This creates a strong pathway toward commercialization and significant growth opportunities in Europe and globally.

**Approach and Methodology**

The project follows a structured and risk-driven methodology that builds directly on the results achieved in the SUREWAVE project. SUREWAVE has already validated the key technological building blocks through extensive numerical modelling, material development, structural testing, hinge characterization, hydrodynamic simulations, and two dedicated wave basin testing campaigns. These activities significantly reduced the main technical uncertainties and identified the areas requiring validation under real operating conditions. \[mordorinte...igence.com\]

The proposed work therefore focuses on demonstrating the complete integrated system in a representative nearshore environment and generating the technical and commercial evidence required for market adoption. The methodology is divided into four interlinked phases.

**Phase-1: Design and Planning**

The first phase of the project focuses on transforming the validated SUREWAVE concept into a site-specific nearshore demonstrator design. A 200–300 kWp floating photovoltaic system will be engineered based on Sunlit Sea’s proven floating solar platform and integrated with the SUREWAVE floating breakwater concept. Here long-term data on wave heights, wind speeds, temperatures, geophysical surveys of depths (bathymetric), underwater topography, water level variations, permissible locations, power connection options and existing infrastructure will be carried out. The demonstrator configuration will be optimized for the selected site by evaluating alternative layouts, including circular and square arrangements, to identify the best balance between hydrodynamic performance, installation feasibility, operational accessibility and overall cost-effectiveness.

Building on the validated models, simulation tools and engineering knowledge developed during SUREWAVE, the project will perform the detailed design of all major system components. This includes final sizing of the floating PV array, selection and optimization of the floating breakwater configuration, design of the mooring and anchoring systems according to local environmental conditions, assessment of the electrical architecture and grid connection strategy, selection of sensors and monitoring equipment, and specification of inverters and other electrical components. The selected demonstration site will be fully characterized, and detailed hydrodynamic simulations of the complete integrated system will be conducted to verify performance and support final design decisions.

The phase will also refine installation, operation and maintenance procedures based on the selected configuration and site conditions. By relying on the extensive testing, simulations and subsystem validations already completed within SUREWAVE, this engineering process significantly reduces development risk and ensures that all design decisions are supported by quantitative evidence. The outcome of this phase will be a fully engineered, installation-ready demonstrator that provides the foundation for the subsequent deployment and operational validation activities.

**Phase 2 – Construction and Installation**

The second phase focuses on the physical realization and deployment of the demonstrator. Based on the engineering completed in Phase 1, all major system components will be procured, manufactured and prepared for installation, including the floating PV modules, floating breakwater elements, mooring and anchoring systems, electrical equipment and monitoring infrastructure. Close collaboration with suppliers, subcontractors and installation partners will ensure that all components meet the technical specifications established during the design phase.

The project team will manage the complete installation process, including logistics planning, transportation of components to the selected site, assembly and pre-installation testing, deployment of the mooring and anchoring systems, installation of the floating breakwaters and PV arrays, electrical integration and grid connection, and deployment of monitoring and data acquisition systems. Once installation is completed, the entire system will undergo commissioning and performance verification to confirm that all components operate as designed and that the integrated demonstrator is ready for long-term operation.

This phase represents a critical step in reducing the remaining uncertainties associated with nearshore floating solar deployment. Beyond validating the constructability of the SUREWAVE concept, it will provide valuable practical knowledge regarding installation methodologies, supply chain requirements, logistics planning, deployment costs and interaction between the different subsystems during real-world implementation. The experience gained during this phase will form an essential foundation for future commercial-scale deployments and contribute directly to the business case and commercialization strategy developed later in the project.

**Phase 3 – Operational Validation and Performance Demonstration**

The third phase focuses on demonstrating the performance of the integrated SUREWAVE system under real nearshore operating conditions. Following commissioning, the demonstrator will operate continuously while a comprehensive monitoring programme collects technical, operational and environmental data. The monitoring system will assess energy production, structural behaviour, platform motions, environmental loading, mooring and anchoring performance, reliability of electrical systems, maintenance requirements, operational availability and the durability of critical components. The collected data will provide direct evidence of the system’s behaviour in a real marine environment and will be used to validate and further calibrate the numerical models and design tools developed during SUREWAVE.

This phase is critical because it addresses the main remaining uncertainty identified after SUREWAVE: the long-term performance of the complete integrated system under operational conditions. The results will provide the technical evidence required by future customers, investors and regulators, while also supporting future design optimisation and scale-up activities.

**Phase 4 – Business Validation and Exploitation**

The final phase focuses on translating the technical achievements of the project into commercial readiness. The demonstrator site will be used as a living showcase where potential customers, investors, policymakers, port authorities, renewable energy developers and other stakeholders can observe the technology operating under real conditions. Throughout the project, a structured programme of stakeholder engagement activities, site visits, demonstration events and workshops will be carried out to validate market interest and gather feedback from potential adopters.

Operational and financial data collected from the demonstrator will be used to validate maintenance costs, update the techno-economic model, assess project revenues under real electricity market conditions and quantify the value proposition of the technology. These activities will support the development of commercialisation strategies, market entry plans and future deployment scenarios. The project will also engage directly with investors and industrial partners to assess financing opportunities and build a pathway towards commercial deployment. By the end of the project, the consortium will have developed an investment-ready business plan supported by operational evidence from the demonstrator, substantially reducing investment risk and improving the credibility of future commercial projects.

**Why the Methodology is Well-Suited:** The proposed methodology is particularly appropriate because it directly addresses the remaining unknowns identified during SUREWAVE.

The project is no longer investigating whether the individual technological components work. The breakwater concept, hinge designs, mooring systems, circular materials and simulation tools have already undergone substantial validation. The main remaining challenge is validation of the complete integrated system at operational scale and in a representative marine environment. The phased approach is therefore designed specifically to bridge this gap between technology validation and commercial deployment.

Furthermore, each phase generates information required by the subsequent phase, ensuring progressive risk reduction throughout the project. Technical data collected during operation feeds directly into the business validation activities, creating a strong link between technical performance and commercial viability.

**Flexibility and Alternative Pathways**

A key strength of the methodology is its flexibility and ability to respond to unforeseen challenges.

Several alternative pathways are built into the project:

- Circular and square system configurations will be evaluated before final design selection.

- Different breakwater layouts can be implemented depending on site-specific wave conditions.

- Mooring and anchoring configurations can be adjusted based on simulation results and installation feedback.

- Monitoring systems will provide early detection of potential operational issues, enabling corrective actions during the demonstration period.

- The modular nature of the SUREWAVE concept allows individual components to be modified or upgraded without redesigning the complete system.

If certain technical risks materialize, the project can draw upon the extensive modelling tools, testing experience and validated design methodologies already developed during SUREWAVE to rapidly evaluate mitigation measures and implement improvements. This adaptive approach ensures that the project remains robust despite the uncertainties associated with demonstrating an innovative technology in a real marine environment.

Overall, the proposed methodology provides a credible, systematic and low-risk pathway from a validated concept to a demonstrated and commercially credible nearshore floating solar solution.

> What is you Unique Selling Point and how do you intend to further develop it?

The unique selling point of SUREWAVE is its protected floating solar concept, which combines a floating photovoltaic system with an integrated floating breakwater. Unlike conventional floating solar solutions that are directly exposed to wave action and therefore limited to sheltered waters, SUREWAVE reduces wave loads before they reach the solar array, enabling deployment in nearshore and semi-exposed marine environments. This approach improves system reliability, reduces structural loading and maintenance requirements, and opens access to a significantly larger deployment area than is currently available for conventional floating PV technologies. The concept has already been validated through advanced simulations, structural testing, connection testing and two wave basin campaigns conducted during the SUREWAVE project.

The proposed project will further strengthen this competitive advantage by demonstrating the complete integrated system in a real nearshore environment, generating operational and economic performance data, validating installation and maintenance procedures, and refining the business case for future commercial deployments. This will provide the evidence required to support large-scale market adoption and accelerate the commercialization of protected nearshore floating solar systems.

This task will establish the site-specific metocean design basis required for the engineering, installation and operation of the demonstrator. ### Task 3.1 Numerical Modelling (SINTEF) (M1-M12)

**Task 3.1.1: Aero-hydrodynamic assessment:** This task will apply advanced aero-hydrodynamic numerical models to support the design, optimization, and validation of the SUREWAVE FPV system and its protective floating breakwater concept. Building on the outcomes of the SUREWAVE project, SINTEF has developed and validated a suite of high-fidelity numerical tools capable of modelling the coupled interaction between wind, waves, floating structures, and mooring systems. These tools will be applied to assess the performance of the integrated FPV-breakwater system under the environmental conditions expected at the demonstration site. Validated Computational Fluid Dynamics (CFD), Smoothed Particle Hydrodynamics (SPH), and MoorDyn-based mooring models will be used to quantify wave attenuation performance, aerodynamic and hydrodynamic loads, platform motions, and mooring loads. The results will support the optimization of the floating breakwater configuration, FPV array layout, and mooring system while providing the engineering basis for demonstrator deployment and future scale-up of the SUREWAVE concept.

**Task 3.1.2: Structural assessment** On the structural side, SINTEF has developed and validated advanced numerical models for concrete structural components, including material behaviour under static, cyclic, and extreme loading conditions. The modelling framework incorporates non-linear material behaviour, hinge response, joint performance, and coupled finite element (FEM) analysis to accurately predict structural integrity and failure mechanisms. These models will be applied to assess the structural response of the floating breakwater and FPV supporting structures under combined wave, wind, and mooring loads.

### Task 3.2 Energy Yield Modelling and Assessment (EDP) (M1-M36)

EDP will benchmark the demonstrator performance against utility-scale floating solar requirements and establish energy-production KPIs relevant for future commercial projects. This task will develop an energy-yield assessment methodology for FPV systems operating in exposed inland waters and other floating solar environments. The analysis will combine site-specific environmental data, solar resources, and electrical, optical, and thermal PV performance models. Particular attention will be given to the effects of platform motions, module orientation, wave-induced movements, cooling effects from the water surface, humidity, soiling, reflection from the water surface, and other environmental factors affecting energy production.

The methodology will establish the expected energy-production baseline for the demonstrator and identify the technical and environmental parameters with the greatest influence on energy yield, forecast uncertainty, and project bankability. The resulting models will provide the reference against which operational performance data collected during demonstration will be evaluated.

### Task 3.3: Model Validation (L:SINTEF, P:ALL) (M24-M36)

This task will validate the numerical models developed in Tasks 3.1 and 3.2 using data collected from the demonstrator during operation. Measurements of environmental conditions, structural loads, motions, mooring tensions, reliability indicators, and energy production will be compared against model predictions to assess their accuracy under representative operating conditions.

The validated models will be calibrated and refined to improve predictive capabilities and reduce uncertainties. Prediction errors, model sensitivities, and confidence intervals will be quantified to support future design optimization, performance forecasting, and investment assessments. The validated modelling framework will provide a robust basis for future commercial floating photovoltaic deployments in exposed inland and nearshore environments.

### Task 3.4 Predictive monitoring framework (EDP) (M18-M36)

This task will develop a predictive monitoring framework integrating sensor measurements, numerical models, and advanced data analytics to assess the performance and integrity of the FPV system during operation. Data from structural, environmental, reliability, and energy-monitoring systems will be combined with validated models developed in Tasks 3.1 and 3.2.

Building on existing SINTEF and EDP digital solutions, machine-learning and data-driven approaches will be applied to support performance assessment, anomaly detection, load prediction, reliability analysis, and predictive maintenance. The framework will provide real-time insights into structural behaviour, system health, and energy production, supporting both demonstrator operation and future commercial deployments.

### Task 4.1 Monitoring Requirements (Lead: SiS, Participants: SINTEF, Clement, EDP)

Sunlit Sea leads Task 4.1: defining the instrumentation specification for the demonstrator so that instru ments are installed at manufacturing time (WP5) and the data collected during operation (WP7) directly validates the design assumptions from WP2/WP3 and supports the reliability assessment in T7.2. The instrumentation specification covers: • Structural monitoring. Strain gauges at critical points of the PU frame and aluminium bottom, load cells at the CONNECT hinge attachments (FPV-to-FPV and FPV-to-breakwater), IMUs on selected units to capture 6-DoF motion, mooring-line load cells (coordinated with WP2 T2.4). • Environmental monitoring. Wave, wind, current and irradiance sensors coordinated with the site campaign of EDP. Panel-surface and water-side temperature sensors on selected units to close the thermal loop against the D6.1 CFD chain. • Reliability monitoring. Water-ingress sensors embedded in the dummy modules and at defined risk points in the operational modules; corrosion coupons at representative locations; junction-box humidity monitoring. Rationale: our Gen 2 water-ingress test on Prototype 3 identified specific interface regions to instrument. • Energy performance monitoring. DC-side per-string current/voltage; module-level monitoring on a defined subset for spatial performance mapping; AC-side power quality at the inverter output (coordinated with EDP). Sensor selection prioritises rugged, salt-water-tolerant instruments with proven track records in offshore or marine service, and standard interfaces (Modbus RTU, IEC 61850, 4-20 mA) compatible with the WavEC data-acquisition platform in T4.3. Sampling rates and data-retention policies are specified so that the data volume is manageable while preserving the frequency content needed for fatigue analysis (T7.1) and reliability assessment (T7.2). The instrumentation specification is frozen at M9 to feed procurement in WP5. Deliverable: D4.1 Instrumentation specification report (M9)
