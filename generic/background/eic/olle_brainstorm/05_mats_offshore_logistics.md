## 1. Marine logistics problems and risks

**1.1 Port-of-staging selection — Storugns**
Storugns is the obvious candidate: it is the closest hard-surfaced quay to the site at 57.84°N 18.80°E, operated by Nordkalk with a bulk terminal designed for heavy cargo. The berth handles vessels up to roughly 10 000 DWT with roll-on/roll-off capability for heavy equipment. The problem is industrial priority: Nordkalk ships limestone to Swedish and Finnish steel mills on a year-round schedule, and a demonstration FPV project will be a low-priority tenant. Lead time to negotiate a temporary lay-down agreement will be 6–12 months and may require Nordkalk corporate approval beyond the local site manager. Any slot at Storugns is also subject to displacement by scheduled bulk shipments, so a staging plan that depends on Storugns must include a contingency.

**1.2 Port-of-staging selection — Fårösund**
Fårösund is the next closest option to the north, a small civilian ferry and fishing harbour with limited hard-standing area. Quay depth is reported around 3–4 m alongside at best, which rules out any vessel with draft over 3.5 m and is marginal for the anchor handler or crane barge that will be needed for breakwater placement. The advantage is civilian access, no competing industrial traffic, and proximity to site. It would work as a crew-boat and support base but not as the primary heavy-lift staging port. Depth survey of the Fårösund harbour approach needs to be verified before any commitment.

**1.3 Port-of-staging selection — Visby and mainland alternatives**
Visby is the main Gotland port with proper depth (8–10 m in the commercial harbour) and established marine services, but it sits roughly 80 km south of the site. A barge tow from Visby to the north Gotland site in open Baltic conditions adds a weather-window dependency and typically a half-day tow per load, driving up vessel day-rates. Mainland alternatives — Oskarshamn (130 km west-southwest across open water), Nynäshamn (200 km), Oxelösund (200 km) — are competitive for concrete casting and fabrication but add a 6–10 hour open-sea tow for each breakwater unit, with weather downtime typically running 20–30 % of tow days on Baltic routes in autumn. The integrated decision is: pre-cast on mainland, tow to Storugns for final assembly/staging, install from Storugns or directly from barge at site.

**1.4 Ice window at northern Gotland**
The Baltic around northern Gotland is ice-affected in severe winters. Long-term SMHI statistics show that the sea off Fårö and Storugns can be ice-covered from roughly mid-January to mid-March in a cold winter, with the worst years (as in 2010 and 2023) producing ice thickness of 20–40 cm and occasional pressure-ridge fields drifting down from the northern Baltic. The 2029 installation target means the ice design load on the concrete breakwater units must be specified now — typically 200–350 kN/m lateral pressure for a 20 m face unit in this ice regime, per ISO 19906 and Swedish Sjöfartsverket guidance. Anchor handling and FPV installation work is effectively shut down November–March in a median winter and November–April in a bad winter. The installation window is therefore May–September, which exactly coincides with Baltic offshore wind high season.

**1.5 Concrete breakwater pre-casting logistics — island versus mainland**
Each breakwater unit is 20 m x 5 m x 2.5 m, 123.4 t at full scale. On-Gotland casting would require a temporary yard with a large flat concrete apron and a crane capacity of at least 150 t — nothing of that scale is currently available outside Nordkalk's own heavy-equipment area. Renting a portable concrete batch plant to Gotland via Visby and building a temporary casting pad is possible but adds 4–6 weeks of site preparation and significant mobilisation cost, likely 500–800 kSEK for the yard setup alone. Mainland yards at Oxelösund (SSAB port with heavy slipways) or at Lövsta/Freeport in Nynäshamn have existing heavy aprons and floating crane access. Casting on mainland and wet-towing or barge-transporting finished units to Gotland is likely cheaper overall and avoids the Gotland logistics constraint, but adds open-water tow risk for each unit.

**1.6 Barge and crane vessel availability in Baltic high season**
Baltic offshore wind projects — Gotland Offshore (Vattenfall/OX2), Skåne and Södra Gotland EEZ projects, Havel Wind off Rügen — pre-book heavy-lift crane barges and anchor handlers for the May–September weather window typically 18 months in advance. For a 2029 installation season, vessel options need to be identified by mid-2027 and letters of intent placed before end of 2027. The primary Baltic crane barge operators with self-propelled units suitable for 130–150 t lifts and North Gotland transit include Boskalis' Svanen (primarily for monopile work, but can handle breakwater lifts), Jan De Nul's crane pontoons, and the regional player Normann & Thomsson out of Gothenburg. Smaller jack-up units from Navigea (formerly Geosea Baltic) or Pon Power's barge fleet may be available on shorter lead times but lack the lift capacity.

**1.7 Anchor installation — driven piles versus drag anchors**
The mooring system uses catenary stud-link chain (54 mm nominal), which can terminate at drag embedment anchors, driven piles, or suction caissons. At 10–30 m water depth on Baltic limestone/glacial till seabed, suction caissons are not preferred (limited penetration in rock). Drag anchors (Vryhof Stevpris Mk6 or Boskalis-supplied HA anchors) are the most cost-effective but require an anchor handler with sufficient bollard pull — minimum 60 t BP for a 1 MWp array in the Gotland swell regime. The only Baltic-based anchor handler with that capacity readily available is Boskalis Smit Merger, Siem Offshore or the Havila fleet; all are North Sea-mobilised and need 4–6 weeks transit to site. If the seabed proves to be bare limestone (probable given Nordkalk's quarry operations nearby), driven steel piles with hydraulic hammer will be required, adding a second specialist vessel and 30–50 % to the anchor installation budget.

**1.8 FPV module shipping from Norway to Gotland**
Sunlit Sea manufactures aluminium pressed PV float modules in Norway (likely Vestfold/Telemark region or Rogaland). A full 1 MWp array in the SUREWAVE configuration is estimated at roughly 500–800 flat-pack module assemblies. The shipping route is Bergen or Stavanger → Gothenburg → Norrköping/Nynäshamn → Visby or Storugns by RoRo or container ferry. Gothenburg–Visby RORO service runs twice weekly. Transit time Bergen to Gotland is typically 4–6 days door-to-quay. Module dimensions and pallet weights need to be confirmed to check compatibility with standard RoRo trailers — a key logistics detail that needs Sunlit Sea's manufacturing drawings early in the planning cycle.

**1.9 Pilotage requirements for Storugns**
Storugns is classified as a special berth under Sjöfartsverket's compulsory pilotage zones. Any vessel over 70 m LOA entering Storugns requires a Sjöfartsverket-licensed pilot. Baltic Pilot, who cover Gotland, operates from Visby with a 2–4 hour response time to northern Gotland positions. For a crane barge or anchor handler of 80–100 m length on multiple port calls, pilotage cost runs 12–18 kSEK per transit, plus the delay for pilot boarding. This cost is budgetable but must be included; it is often forgotten in first-pass cost estimates for demonstration projects.

**1.10 Local tug capacity on Gotland**
Gotland has no resident ocean-going tug. Sjöfartsverket's rescue tug Triton is based at Visby and is available for emergency towage, but not for commercial hire on installation work. Renta-tug style services from Göteborg or Malmö involve 1–2 day mobilisation. For breakwater assembly work requiring precise positioning, a dedicated 30–50 t BP tug will need to be mobilised from the mainland for the duration of the installation campaign, adding approximately 45–80 kSEK/day including crew and fuel. This is a fixed overhead that should be sized against the total installation duration — for a 1 MWp FPV + breakwater assembly that is likely 3–5 weeks on-site, which means 1–2 million SEK just for the tug component.

**1.11 Assembly sequencing and critical path**
The installation sequence is fixed by physics: (1) concrete breakwater perimeter units towed to site and ballasted to operational draft, (2) breakwater chain and inter-unit connectors set and tensioned, (3) main mooring anchors installed and chain led, (4) FPV module assembly inside the breakwater ring, (5) electrical trunk cable pulled, (6) onshore cable termination and inverter commissioning. Each step is a weather-window gate: the breakwater tow needs calm sea state (Hs < 0.8 m for a 123 t tow without a crane barge standing by), mooring installation needs sea state < 1.5 m, FPV assembly can tolerate Hs up to 0.5–0.8 m. The consequence is that the entire installation must be planned as one continuous campaign in the May–September window to avoid winter lay-up with the array half-built, which would be an unacceptable risk for a first-of-kind system.

**1.12 Weather window planning — northern Gotland specifics**
SMHI hindcast data for 57.84°N 18.80°E shows that May–June is the most favourable installation window: typical Hs < 1 m on 75–80 % of days, wind < Beaufort 5 on 65 % of days. July–August has the most benign conditions but is also when Baltic pleasure-craft traffic is heaviest, creating potential navigation hazards in the coastal zone. September weather degrades rapidly: probability of > 1.5 m Hs rises to 30–35 %. A May–June start requires all pre-installation work (casting, vessel bookings, permit clearances) to be completed by March–April of the installation year, giving approximately 10 months of project ramp-up from a December 2028 decision to proceed.

**1.13 Marine insurance and HSE pricing for first-of-kind**
Marine insurers (Marsh Marine, WTW Marine) will price this as a novel floating structure with no loss history — expect significant loading on the Construction All Risk (CAR) premium. For a 1 MWp FPV array with a replacement value of roughly 25–35 million SEK, typical CAR rates for conventional floating solar are 0.8–1.2 % of insured value, but the breakwater + mooring system and the novel hinge connections will attract additional surveyor scrutiny and likely require a pre-placement inspection by DNV or Bureau Veritas. Budget 300–450 kSEK/year for marine insurance during the deployment phase. The HSE plan for marine operations must include a man-overboard drill protocol, rescue boat on standby during all water-based work, and an emergency tow plan filed with Sjöfartsverket before any offshore installation phase.

**1.14 Decommissioning cost reserve**
EU funding (EIC Transition) will require a decommissioning bond or cost reserve. For a floating concrete and aluminium structure of this scale, decommissioning is non-trivial: the breakwater units must be lifted and either towed to a demolition yard or sunk at a licensed disposal site (not preferred for concrete in Swedish territorial waters). Provisional decommissioning cost estimate: 3–5 million SEK for a 1 MWp installation at 130 m diameter. This should appear as a line item in the project financial plan from the outset so it does not surface as a shock at the end-of-project review.

**1.15 Cable and grid connection logistics**
A 1 MWp FPV installation will require a subsea HV cable to the Nordkalk substation or to the local Gotland Energi LV grid. The subsea cable route from the Storugns-area site to shore is short (< 1 km) but requires a cable pull vessel or jet-sled trenching unit. The bigger logistical question is whether the local distribution grid at northern Gotland can absorb 1 MWp without reinforcement — Gotland's grid is effectively islanded and has historically been a constraint. An early grid connection application to Gotland Energi and to Svenska Kraftnät is recommended before any construction commitment.

## 3. Suggested sequencing

### Pre-grant (now to Q2 2027): soundings only, no commitments

The Baltic vessel market does not require a committed booking before grant confirmation, but operators do fill their 2029 calendars from Q3 2027 onwards. The window between EIC grant announcement (expected Q2–Q3 2027) and Baltic high season 2029 is approximately 18–22 months — barely enough if vessel bookings are placed immediately after grant signature. Pre-grant activities that cost nothing are: initial port dialogues (Storugns, Fårösund, Visby), SMHI metocean data purchase, casting yard shortlisting, and the decommissioning concept for the EIC proposal. These should start now, parallel to proposal finalisation.

The critical-path driver is vessel availability, not site readiness. If the crane barge and anchor handler are not secured by end of 2027, the installation will slip to 2030 because Baltic high season 2029 will be fully committed to offshore wind. That single risk dominates all scheduling decisions.

### Post-grant (Q3 2027–Q1 2028): commit the critical-path items immediately

Within 60 days of grant signature: place letters of intent with the selected crane barge operator and anchor handler. Kick off the geotechnical site survey (L12) and the towing permit application (L11). Issue the PQQ for marine contractors (L10). Confirm the casting yard (L04/L05) and place a manufacturing slot reservation.

### Installation preparation (Q2–Q4 2028): design-dependent tasks

Anchor design (L12→L13), marine operations plan (L18), classification review (L20), and the winter lay-up plan (L17) all depend on the detailed engineering output from SINTEF and Sunlit Sea. These are on the critical path between grant signing and installation start and must not be sequenced loosely.

### Installation campaign (May–September 2029)

Target start: 1 May 2029. Total campaign: 6 weeks including 10 weather contingency days. Week 1–2: breakwater units towed from staging area and deployed. Week 2–3: mooring chains set. Week 3–4: FPV module assembly inside breakwater ring. Week 5: cable pull. Week 6: commissioning and handover. A June start is more conservative from a weather standpoint; a May start allows for weather delays without compromising commissioning before winter.

## 5. Open questions and things to verify

**5.1 Actual ice frequency at 57.84°N 18.80°E**
SMHI's ice charts show northern Gotland coastal waters ice-free in mild winters and carrying 10–40 cm fast-ice in severe winters, but the open-sea position at the candidate site may be less affected than the near-shore areas. An SMHI custom ice statistics query for the specific coordinates over 1990–2025 is needed before finalising ice-load design input. The relevant SMHI product is HIROMB ice hindcast or the FMI/SMHI historical sea-ice atlas.

**5.2 Local tug capacity on Gotland**
No commercial tug is known to be permanently based on Gotland as of 2026. Sjöfartsverket's Triton is emergency-only. Confirming whether any tug is available on call from Visby (e.g. a harbour tug contracted by Region Gotland for port operations) would reduce mobilisation cost and lead time. Olle should make a direct inquiry to Visby hamn.

**5.3 Storugns port physical capability for an anchor handler**
Nordkalk's Storugns terminal is designed for bulk carriers up to approximately 80–90 m LOA. Anchor handlers of the required bollard pull (60+ t BP) typically run 65–80 m LOA, so they should fit alongside, but the berth's design load for a moored vessel making anchor-handling manoeuvres (side forces, dynamic loads) needs to be confirmed with Nordkalk's port engineer before including Storugns in the marine operations plan.

**5.4 Fårösund depth and harbour approach**
Sjöfartsverket's harbour database lists Fårösund with limited information. The ferry crossing is by small vehicle ferry (Fårösundsfärjan), suggesting very shallow approach depth on the vehicle ferry berth side. The possibility of a separate deeper quay on the military/civilian side needs to be confirmed. Olle should obtain the current Sjöfartsverket harbour chart (sjökort) for Fårösund.

**5.5 Sunlit Sea manufacturing location and shipping route**
The brainstorm assumes a Norwegian manufacturing base (Vestfold/Rogaland region), but the actual factory location for the demonstration volume has not been confirmed. If modules are manufactured at a subcontractor's premises in Sweden or continental Europe, the shipping plan (L14) changes substantially. This needs to be confirmed directly with Sunlit Sea's production manager before logistics planning proceeds.

**5.6 Local marine contractor presence on Gotland**
There may be small marine construction contractors based on Gotland serving the harbour and coastal infrastructure sector (breakwater repairs, quay construction). A search through Byggföretagen's member registry and Region Gotland's contractor lists would reveal whether any local firm has relevant equipment (small crane barges, dive vessels), which could reduce mobilisation cost for secondary tasks.

**5.7 Grid capacity at northern Gotland**
Gotland has been a grid-constrained island with limited import/export capacity to the mainland. Svenska Kraftnät is building the Gotland grid reinforcement (SydVäst Gotland cable), but northern Gotland distribution capacity for a new 1 MWp injection point needs to be verified with Gotland Energi before any design finalization. If grid connection requires a new transformer or line extension, the cost and permitting timeline could rival the marine logistics on the critical path.

**5.8 Environmental permit (Miljötillstånd) timeline**
Installing a floating structure with concrete anchors in Swedish territorial waters at this scale will require a permit under the Swedish Environmental Code (Miljöbalken, 11 kap.). Applications to Länsstyrelsen Gotland typically take 12–18 months from submission to decision for novel marine structures. If the permit application is not filed by Q1 2028, it may become the critical path driver ahead of vessel availability. Olle should identify the permitting authority contact at Länsstyrelsen Gotland early.
