## 1. Community-engagement problems and risks

**Post-Klinthagen fatigue and hypervigilance.** The Klinthagen permit battle ran from roughly 2015 to June 2026, when Mark- och miljööverdomstolen confirmed Nordkalk's 30-year permit. The community spent a decade mobilised — letters, court interventions, media campaigns, Länsstyrelsen submissions. The ruling landed hard on the losing side (Naturskyddsföreningen, environmental organisations, some permanent residents) and delivered relief but no catharsis to the pro-industry side. What remains across both camps is exhaustion combined with sharpened reflexes: anything that arrives as a new "development" proposal in northern Gotland will be scanned immediately for who benefits, who pays the cost, and whether the promoter is truthful. A project that arrives without prior relationship will be treated as another outside interest testing the island's resistance.

**The Norwegian-company framing problem.** Sunlit Sea is a Norwegian company; SINTEF is headquartered in Trondheim. Gotland has a well-developed suspicion of what locals call fastlandsagenter — mainland Swedes and, by extension, any Scandinavian outsider — who arrive with capital, permits, and PowerPoints and depart after extraction. "A Norwegian energy company with a Trondheim research institute wants to put a 130-metre installation off our coast" is the headline that writes itself in Gotlands Allehanda if the project lands wrong. The framing must be reversed early: the local community's knowledge and conditions shape the project, not the other way around.

**Nordkalk adjacency — caught in the middle.** The proposed site is off Storugns, which is the port for Nordkalk's Klinthagen operation. Any meeting in Lärbro socken will contain both people who fought Nordkalk for a decade and people who depend on Nordkalk economically. Being perceived as aligned with Nordkalk (sharing industrial framing, even sharing the harbour approach vector) will alienate the environmental-community bloc. Being perceived as anti-industrial will alienate the pro-employment bloc. The project must be positioned orthogonally to the Nordkalk conflict — not an industrial extraction activity, not a protest against one either.

**Socken identity and formal entry points.** The proposed footprint at 57.844°N, 18.797°E sits in Lärbro socken, not Hangvar. Storugns itself is in Lärbro socken. Hangvar socken is to the northwest. This distinction matters operationally: the relevant socken forum, any local hembygdsförening affiliate, and the community voices who will feel most directly affected are in Lärbro, not Hangvar — though Hangvar residents will have views on sightlines and coastal access. Arriving at a Hangvar forum as if it were the primary affected community would be noticed and resented in Lärbro.

**The summer-resident asymmetry.** Northern Gotland has a large proportion of fritidshus — summer homes whose owners are present roughly May through August and largely absent otherwise. Summer residents often have strong opinions about visual landscape and coastal access, and they are vocal because they tend to be educated, networked, and accustomed to advocacy. Permanent residents, however, are the ones who will live with the project year-round, who carry the community's institutional memory, and who do not appreciate having decisions about their home shaped by people who arrive in June and leave in September. Engagement that is timed or pitched primarily at summer residents will generate resentment among permanent residents — and vice versa. Both groups need dedicated pathways.

**The fishing community's cultural weight.** Commercial fishing off northern Gotland is small in vessel count — a handful of licensed fishers working coastal traps, flounder nets, and eel gear in the Lärbro and Slite-area waters. But the fishing community carries cultural capital entirely disproportionate to its size. On Gotland, kustfiskaren is a heritage identity as much as an economic one. If a fisher at a community meeting says "det här blockerar mina fiskebåtar," the room moves with them regardless of the technical specifics. Preliminary dialogue with the local fiskelag or any individual licensed fishers in the area must happen before any public meeting — not at the first public meeting.

**Tourism and sightline concerns.** Northern Gotland's tourism economy is anchored in landscape: the alvar, the raukar, the coastal light, the sense of unmediated nature. Visit Gotland's marketing for the northern tip and the Fårö area leans explicitly on pristine seascapes. A 130-metre diameter structure one to two kilometres offshore will be visible from coastal roads and walking paths. Tourism operators — kayak guides, small guesthouses, the ferry visitors to Fårö — will want to know what it looks like, whether it creates navigation hazards for small boats, and whether it changes the character of the coastline. Photomontages from specific viewpoints (not just generic renderings) are necessary, and they need to be ready before any public session.

**Summer-boat traffic.** The stretch of coast off Storugns-Lärbro is used by recreational sailors transiting between Fårösund, Slite, and the east coast. The installation's navigation marking requirements and any exclusion zone need to be addressed explicitly. Sailors who discover an unmarked or poorly explained structure in their usual track will generate adverse media quickly; the boating community talks through its own channels (Gotlands Segelsällskap, online forums) and can mobilise opinion faster than formal consultation processes allow.

**Local media dynamics — merged but influential.** Gotlands Allehanda and Gotlands Tidningar merged their editorial operations in late 2024 into a shared newsroom (published through Helagotland.se alongside the legacy mastheads). The result is a smaller editorial team covering more ground with fewer reporters. Energy and environment stories tend to land on generalist reporters who will cover the conflict angle before the technical angle. Radio Gotland P4 (Sveriges Radio) has a dedicated local desk and regularly covers permit disputes. A project that first appears in the media through a leaked planning document or an objection lodged with Länsstyrelsen will be framed by whoever frames it first. Olle needs to have informal relationships with at least one GA/GT reporter and the P4 Gotland desk before the first formal filing.

**The risk of misreading polite silence as consent.** Gotlanders — and northern Gotlanders particularly — communicate displeasure obliquely in formal settings. A community meeting where nobody raises a direct objection does not mean support; it may mean that people are reserving judgment, that they do not trust the forum, or that they are waiting to see what their neighbours think. The signal for real acceptance is when a trusted local voice — a socken elder, a longtime fiskeläge family, a hembygdsföreningens ordförande — says something positive unprompted. Absence of objection at a samråd meeting is not this signal.

**Natura 2000 proximity and the bird-organisation factor.** Several Natura 2000 sites sit in and around northern Gotland, and Gotlands Ornitologiska Förening (GOF) has an active member base with organisational roots in the Hangvar-Lärbro area (a GOF representative is registered in Hangvar Bäcks, Lärbro). GOF participated in the Klinthagen proceedings. They will want early, specific answers about breeding bird disturbance, migratory routes, and any light-pollution effects from navigation marking. Engaging GOF too late — after they have already formed an opinion from public documents — is a standard mistake.

**Fårö and the Bergman cultural weight.** Fårö is geographically close and culturally outsized. The Bergman heritage, the nature reserve, the strong local identity, and the organised Fårö community make it a secondary but non-trivial actor. Any installation visible from Fårö's south-facing shores, or that affects boat access between Fårö and the mainland, will draw Fårö voices into the process. Fårö residents tend to be articulate, well-connected nationally, and unimpressed by technical framing.

**Language and format failures.** Formal presentations in conference-room style with projected slides and corporate spokespeople consistently backfire at socken-level engagement on Gotland. The effective format is a table, coffee, a map, a local person asking the questions — and enough time for people to disagree without feeling they are objecting to authority. Any engagement format that resembles a public hearing or a corporate information event will produce a defensive audience.

**Institutional credibility gap — TRL 4-5 technology.** The technology is still at TRL 4-5 as of late 2026. A community that just watched Nordkalk promise careful environmental management over thirty years of permits will be attentive to the gap between project claims and operational track record. Saying "this is a demonstration project that will help us learn" is more credible than overselling readiness. Olle should carry this framing consistently.

## 3. Suggested sequencing

The overriding principle for Gotland is: relationship before announcement, announcement before application, application with allies already briefed.

**Now — pre-grant, months 1-4 (autumn 2026 through early 2027).** Olle's primary work during this phase is entirely below the public radar. No press releases, no public meetings, no formal filings. The tasks are: T-OG-01 (socken mapping), T-OG-02 (fisher inventory), T-OG-03 (tourism inventory), T-OG-04 (courtesy visit to hembygdsförening ordförande), T-OG-05 (coffee with fishers), T-OG-06 (GOF data request), T-OG-07 (Region Gotland informal contact), T-OG-12 (reporter mapping). These relationships take time to build and cannot be rushed. If Olle is local to Gotland, much of this happens through existing social networks — it is worth mapping which contacts he already has before designing the engagement programme.

**Post-grant confirmation, months 5-9 (mid-to-late 2027, assuming funded).** Once the project is confirmed and the installation design is taking shape, a second layer of engagement opens: T-OG-08 (Länsstyrelsen informal), T-OG-13 (reporter background conversation), T-OG-14 (P4 Gotland), T-OG-20 (Region Gotland councillors), T-OG-21 (Hembygdsförbund). Photomontages (T-OG-24) and the Swedish summary (T-OG-23) should be ready in this window. The summer session T-OG-15 should be timed to the first summer (2027 or 2028) when design is clear enough to show.

**Formal samråd phase, months 9-18.** The formal miljöbalken samråd process requires a samrådsunderlag to be sent to all named counterparties and samrådsmöten to be held. By this point, everyone who will attend a samrådsmöte should already have met Olle informally. The formal session is a confirmation of positions already understood, not a first introduction. T-OG-09 through T-OG-11, T-OG-17, and T-OG-18 land here.

**What must not be rushed.** Do not schedule any public information meeting before the fishing community and the hembygdsförening have been spoken with privately. Do not file the samrådsunderlag before the GOF data has been incorporated and the bird questions have been answered or at least acknowledged. Do not approach the media before Olle has a working relationship with at least one local reporter.

**Pacing caution.** Gotland's community calendar has a hard seasonal rhythm: late autumn and winter meetings are the ones permanent residents attend; summer events are when summer residents appear. Formal samrådsmöten should be held at a time when permanent residents can attend — not in July. If the legal timeline forces a summer samrådsmöte, hold a separate summer session as a supplementary information event.

## 4. Key Gotland local actors and institutions

**Region Gotland** is the single-tier municipality. The relevant functions are: stadsarkitektkontoret (planning, mark- och vatten), tekniska förvaltningen (coastal infrastructure), kulturmiljöenheten (built and natural heritage), and the politiska ledningen — particularly the nämnd with responsibility for miljö och bygg. Region Gotland will have a formal role in the samråd and will likely have a position on marine renewable energy; this position should be understood early.

**Länsstyrelsen Gotland** holds the primary regulatory functions for nature protection, Natura 2000 consultation, artskyddsdispenser, and the samrådsprocess under miljöbalken. The naturvårdsenheten and havsplaneringsenheten are the relevant internal units. Länsstyrelsen Gotland participated actively in the Klinthagen proceedings and will have institutional awareness of the northern Gotland coastal zone.

**Lärbro socken and hembygdsförening.** Lärbro hembygdsförening is the first-contact socken body. Storugns sits in Lärbro socken administratively. The ordförande is the relevant contact. The hembygdsföreningen's primary currency is local history and cultural heritage, but they are the natural convener of community opinion in the socken and will know which other voices matter.

**Hangvar socken and hembygdsförening.** Hangvar is adjacent to the northwest. Its community will have sightline and coastal-access interests even if the installation footprint is in Lärbro waters. Contact after Lärbro, not before.

**Gotlands Ornitologiska Förening (GOF).** Active membership, participates in environmental proceedings, has local representation in the Hangvar-Lärbro area. GOF is an expert counterparty, not merely an objector. Treating them as a data source first, a consultation counterparty second, will produce a better outcome than treating them as a potential obstacle.

**Naturskyddsföreningen Gotland.** Based in Visby (Lummelundsväg 24, 621 41 Visby). Participated in Klinthagen proceedings on the opposing side to Nordkalk. Their instinct on a new industrial-scale installation off northern Gotland will be cautious until they understand the project. They need early, substantive briefing.

**Gotlands Fiskarförbund.** The regional fishers' association. Formal counterparty in the samråd. Individual licensed fishers in the Lärbro-Storugns coastal area are the operational priority; Fiskarförbundet is the formal organisational route.

**Visit Gotland / Tillväxt Gotland.** Visit Gotland manages tourism promotion; Tillväxt Gotland covers broader business development. Both have an interest in whether the installation is framed as an asset or a burden. Early engagement can shape this framing constructively.

**Gotlands Hembygdsförbund.** Umbrella for all socken hembygdsföreningar. A courtesy contact here ensures that the project's relationship with Lärbro and Hangvar hembygdsföreningar is understood at the regional level.

**Gotlands Allehanda / Gotlands Tidningar / Helagotland.se.** Since late 2024, these operate under a merged editorial structure. Local coverage, particularly for environment and planning stories, is done by a small team of generalist reporters. Radio Gotland P4 (Sveriges Radio local) is a parallel channel reaching a different demographic. Both will cover the samråd when it is filed; the question is whether that coverage is based on a pre-existing understanding of the project or on the objections lodged.

**Local community voices — Lärbro sockens informella opinionsledare.** These are harder to name from outside the community, but Olle — if genuinely local to Gotland — will know them: typically a farming family with long land-tenure, a retired teacher or priest with community trust, the person who writes the letters-to-the-editor that get read seriously. These voices are not on any official list. They are the ones whose reaction at a community meeting shapes everyone else's.

## 5. Open questions and things to verify

**Which socken does the installation footprint belong to?** Based on coordinates (57.844°N, 18.797°E) and the confirmed location of Storugns in Lärbro socken, the footprint is almost certainly in Lärbro socken or in the adjacent coastal waters subject to Lärbro's administrative area. Hangvar socken begins further northwest. This should be verified against Lantmäteriets socken map before any communication that names the socken.

**What is the current demographic and housing mix in Lärbro and Hangvar?** Understanding the permanent-to-seasonal-resident ratio, the age profile, and whether the area has seen any recent in-migration (often from Stockholm) will shape engagement format and timing. Region Gotland's statistics service should have parish-level population data.

**Who are the current local political voices on energy and environment in northern Gotland?** The Region Gotland council has seats from multiple parties; the relevant voices for this project are whoever sits on the miljö och bygg nämnd or equivalent and represents northern Gotland constituencies. This changes with election cycles (most recently 2022, next 2026). The 2026 regional election results should be checked for changes in council composition.

**What is the community mood post-Klinthagen?** The June 2026 ruling confirmed Nordkalk's 30-year permit, with conditions on species protection and water supply. The environmental-community bloc lost the core argument; the question is whether this produced resignation, re-organisation, or continued mobilisation (e.g. an appeal to Högsta domstolen). Olle should find out whether environmental organisations in the area are currently in an active or a depleted state — this affects how quickly they will mobilise in response to a new proposal.

**Are there any ongoing community energy or cooperative renewable projects in northern Gotland?** Gotland has had several community wind and solar cooperative projects. If there is an ongoing energigemenskaps project or a local solförenings initiative in the Lärbro-Hangvar area, early coordination could provide local anchoring — a "this is their project too" framing that reduces the fastlandsagent perception. Tillväxt Gotland and Region Gotland's energienheten would know.

**Are there any ongoing cultural or heritage projects that could cross-pollinate?** Gotland has active archaeology, landscape management, and cultural heritage projects, some EU-funded. A project that can be associated — even loosely — with Gotland's own EU-funded activities may land better than one that arrives as a standalone external proposal.

**What is the exact navigation status of the proposed site?** Sjöfartsverket chart coverage, any existing seabed cables, the shipping fairway status for the Storugns harbour approach, and any existing protected submarine heritage sites should be verified before the samrådsunderlag is drafted. A cultural heritage find at the installation site would introduce a new set of actors (Riksantikvarieämbetet, Gotlands Museum) and community dynamics.

**Does Olle have existing relationships with any of the key local actors?** Given that Olle lives on Gotland, the most efficient engagement programme builds on what he already has. The first practical task is therefore a mapping conversation with Olle about his existing network: who he knows in Lärbro, whether he has any connection to the fishing community or the hembygdsförening, and whether he has any prior exposure to the Klinthagen debate.
