## 1. Environmental problems and risks

**1. Natura 2000 screening — multiple overlapping designations likely**
Northern Gotland is one of the most designation-dense coastal stretches in Sweden. The coastal zone between Slite and Fårösund contains several SCIs (Special Conservation Interest sites) and SPAs (Special Protection Areas) under the Habitats and Birds Directives. The site at 57.844°N, 18.797°E, approximately 2 km northwest of Storugns, must be screened against all sites within at least 10 km — Natura 2000 sites SE0340066 (Norska träsk), SE0340148 (Gotlands nordkust) and adjacent coastal sites are candidates, but the exact list must be confirmed via the ECOS/NV register before MKB framing begins. The key risk is that even a partial overlap with a SCI boundary triggers a habitats-regulation Appropriate Assessment (Artskyddsförordningen; Miljöbalken 7 kap.), which can halt the project unless cumulative impact on protected habitats and species is ruled out.

**2. Benthic habitat loss — charophyte meadows and the Fucus vesiculosus littoral belt**
Northern Gotland's shallow coastal zone (0–10 m) supports the largest charophyte (Chara tomentosa, Tolypella nidifica) meadows in the Baltic — these are Natura 2000 habitat type 3140 (Oligo-mesotrophic calcium-rich lakes) in lagoonal enclosures and analogous coastal equivalents. Below 5 m, Fucus vesiculosus (bladderwrack) forms the dominant structural habitat; it has been in documented decline due to eutrophication but persists in clearer water off northern Gotland. The FPV structure's mooring anchors, anchor chains, and catenary lines will generate a benthic footprint; the circular concrete breakwater will shadow a disc of seabed that, at 130 m diameter, covers approximately 1.3 ha. A detailed pre-installation benthic habitat map at ≥1:1000 scale is the non-negotiable baseline item.

**3. Benthic macrofauna — amphipod and mysid indicator species**
The Baltic benthic soft-bottom community is characterised by low species richness but high ecological sensitivity. The primary indicator species are Monoporeia affinis (isopod) and Mysis mysis (mysid) — both are sensitive to oxygen deficiency, sediment disturbance, and shading. Anchor chain scour can eliminate a 5–10 m corridor around each anchor point where these organisms recolonise slowly (3–7 year trajectories documented in offshore wind surveys off Blekinge). Their density and biomass prior to installation must be documented at the anchor positions and at a matched reference station at comparable depth and sediment type.

**4. Shading of benthic primary production**
The FPV array intercepts 60–80 % of incident light over its footprint. Under the array, benthic macroalgae and aquatic vegetation requiring light will suffer from reduced primary production. This is particularly relevant if the site overlies a charophyte or Fucus bed at 3–12 m. The relevant effect zone extends beyond the direct shadow: elevated turbidity from wave dampening within the breakwater perimeter reduces light even outside the direct footprint. A benthic primary production model calibrated to local Secchi depth and extinction coefficient must be provided in the MKB to bound the effect zone.

**5. Grey seal (Halichoerus grypus) — haul-out and foraging**
Grey seal numbers in the Baltic have recovered to approximately 45,000 individuals, and northern Gotland has documented haul-out sites, most notably on Fårö and on the outer skerries north of Storugns. Construction noise — impact and vibratory pile-driving for any temporary works, anchor installation, concrete breakwater tow and positioning, vessel engine noise — affects seal orientation, foraging, and pup behaviour. In Sweden, the grey seal is covered by Artskyddsförordningen (SFS 2007:845, Appendix 1) and EU Habitats Directive Annex 2. A dedicated marine mammal survey protocol must confirm presence, seasonality, and minimum separation distances from the site.

**6. Harbour porpoise (Phocoena phocoena) — Baltic Proper subpopulation**
The Baltic Proper harbour porpoise is critically endangered (IUCN, HELCOM Red List). Sightings off northern Gotland are infrequent but occur; acoustic monitoring with C-PODs has detected the species in the Gotland Basin approaches. Under the Habitats Directive (Annex 2 and 4) and Artskyddsförordningen, any impact on the species, even from an installation that is not in its primary habitat, requires documentation. Underwater noise from installation vessels and the concrete breakwater mooring must be assessed against the 160 dB re 1 µPa (rms) behavioural disturbance threshold and the 183 dB (pk-pk) TTS threshold used in HELCOM and Swedish Transport Agency (Trafikverket) noise guidelines.

**7. Bird migration — Baltic flyway bottleneck**
Gotland lies directly on the trans-Baltic migratory flyway. Spring migration peaks in April–May, with wading birds (Calidris canutus, Limosa lapponica, Pluvialis apricaria), raptors (Buteo buteo, Pernis apivorus, Circus aeruginosus), and passerines crossing the Baltic at a rate of millions of individuals per week. The autumn migration September–November is dominated by seabirds (Larus spp., Sterna hirundo, Alca torda, Uria aalge) moving south-southwest. An FPV array on open water creates a barrier effect and a collision risk surface, especially in low-visibility conditions and at night. Gotlands ornitologiska förening (GOF) maintains the Nabben ringing station at Fårö, and the time-series from that station is the most relevant local baseline for quantifying passage volume.

**8. Seabird breeding colonies in proximity**
Gotska Sandön (SPA SE0340118), approximately 38 km north of the site, is the most important seabird colony in the Swedish Baltic: Alca torda (razorbill), Uria aalge (common guillemot), and Phalacrocorax carbo (cormorant) breed there in nationally significant numbers. Common eiders (Somateria mollissima) and black guillemots (Cepphus grylle) breed on northern Gotland's outer skerries. Foraging routes from these colonies pass through the project zone. The FPV array must be assessed for its effect on foraging connectivity between colonies and open-water feeding grounds using GIS analysis of known colony positions and reported foraging radii.

**9. Winter ice and ice-scour on benthic habitats**
The northern Baltic freezes in most years; Gotland's north coast experiences fast ice in 6 of 10 years on average and drifting ice almost annually. Ice scour on the seabed at 1–5 m depth occurs where ice grounding lines intersect the littoral zone. The FPV installation's concrete breakwater and mooring system must be designed for ice loading, but from an environmental perspective, the installation may itself alter ice formation patterns in its lee, changing the natural scour regime on adjacent habitats. The baseline must document current ice-scour frequency and seabed disturbance marks at the site using side-scan sonar.

**10. Eutrophication context and sediment mobilisation**
The Baltic Sea is a semi-enclosed basin with 30–50 year water residence time. Northern Gotland's water is comparatively clean relative to the central and southern Baltic, but elevated nutrient levels persist (SMHI monitoring programme). Anchor installation and seabed works will resuspend fine sediments. At depths where soft sediments contain anoxic layers (common in the Baltic below the summer thermocline), resuspension mobilises phosphorus, hydrogen sulphide, and heavy metals — triggering localised algal blooms and toxicity events. The project's construction method statements must quantify resuspension volumes and model dispersal; benthic water chemistry must be characterised before works begin.

**11. Fouling community establishment and non-native species**
Floating structures in the Baltic attract rapid fouling colonisation. In Swedish Baltic waters, the primary concern is Dreissena polymorpha (zebra mussel) and, further south, the increasingly range-expanding Mytilopsis leucophaeata (dark false mussel). The concrete breakwater and aluminium floats will provide hard substrate that does not exist naturally at the site. The ecological effect may be positive (reef function) or negative (non-native species vectors, altered nutrient cycling). The MKB must assess the risk of introduction of alien species via biofouling on vessels arriving from other Baltic ports (Biosäkerhetsförordningen, SFS 2021:30).

**12. Limestone quarry dust and suspended particulate deposition**
Nordkalk's Klinthagen quarry and associated limestone handling at Storugns produces particulate dust that is deposited in the surrounding marine area by prevailing westerly and southwesterly winds. Nordkalk's existing environmental monitoring (required under their environmental permit from Länsstyrelsen) likely includes turbidity and suspended solids measurements in the receiving waters. This existing data is a potentially valuable baseline for the FPV project — and the combined particulate burden from both the quarry and construction activities must be assessed jointly in the MKB under Miljöbalken chapter 6.

**13. Underwater noise from installation vessels**
Barge-mounted cranes, anchor-handling vessels, and tug operations generate continuous broadband underwater noise in the 10–1000 Hz range at source levels of 150–175 dB re 1 µPa at 1 m. In Baltic shallow-water conditions, propagation loss is lower than in open ocean, meaning noise footprints extend further. The construction calendar must be optimised to avoid the grey seal pupping season (February–March) and peak spring bird migration (April–May). The construction noise assessment must follow HELCOM noise guidelines and Naturvårdsverket's guidance on underwater noise (NV rapport 6800 series).

**14. Electromagnetic field (EMF) from submarine cable**
The 1 MWp array will require a submarine export cable to the nearest grid connection. Submarine AC cables generate electromagnetic fields that affect elasmobranchs (electroreception); the species concern in Baltic waters is primarily the thornback ray (Raja clavata) which has been recorded off Gotland, though rarely. A brief EMF assessment noting the cable route, operating voltage, and published sensitivity thresholds for the local species assemblage must be included.

**15. Cumulative effects — interaction with wind energy planning**
The Swedish energy authority (Energimyndigheten) and Länsstyrelsen have identified northern Gotland coastal waters as areas under consideration for offshore wind development. The MKB must explicitly consider cumulative effects of the FPV installation with reasonably foreseeable future wind installations, even if they have not yet received permits. This is required under Miljöbalken 6 kap. 36 § and the updated EIA provisions from 2022.

## 2. Field survey programme — tasks for Olle

### Baseline bathymetry and seabed mapping

**T-B1. High-resolution multibeam bathymetric survey**
Deploy a hull-mounted or ROV-mounted multibeam echo sounder (MBES; e.g. Kongsberg EM2040C or equivalent) to map the entire site and a 500 m buffer at ≤0.5 m grid resolution. Deliverable: georeferenced bathymetric model and slope map. Time: 2–3 days vessel time. Dependencies: vessel access, weather window. Contractor: DHI Sverige or WSP Marine.

**T-B2. Side-scan sonar survey for seabed substrate and ice-scour marks**
Run concurrent side-scan sonar (towfish at 100–400 kHz) during the MBES survey to map sediment type (hard rock, gravel, sand, mud) and document any ice-scour tracks. Deliverable: georeferenced SSS mosaic. Time: combined with T-B1. Lab: interpretation by AquaBiota Water Research or DHI.

**T-B3. Sediment core sampling**
Collect 6–12 gravity/push cores at representative sediment types identified in T-B2. Cores to be split, described, and submitted for grain size, LOI (loss on ignition), total carbon/nitrogen, heavy metals (Cd, Pb, Hg, Cu, Zn per SS-EN ISO standards), PAH, and redox potential. Deliverable: sediment geochemistry report. Time: 1 field day. Lab: IVL Svenska Miljöinstitutet (Göteborg) or ALS Scandinavia.

**T-B4. Sub-bottom profiler for superficial sediment thickness**
Run a parametric sub-bottom profiler (e.g. Innomar SES-2000) to characterise soft sediment thickness over hard rock. Required for anchor holding capacity and cable burial feasibility. Deliverable: sub-bottom profiles along planned cable route and anchor positions. Time: 1 day vessel.

### Benthic habitat and species

**T-H1. Benthic habitat mapping — video and drop-camera transects**
Run 6–10 video transects (towed drop-camera or diver-operated video at <15 m, ROV video at 15–30 m) across depth zones 0–5, 5–15, 15–30 m. Classify habitats to EUNIS level 4, quantify cover of Fucus vesiculosus, Chara spp., Zostera marina if present, macroalgae, and hard-bottom invertebrate communities. Deliverable: habitat map at 1:2500, species list. Time: 3–4 field days, June–August (growing season, maximum visibility). Contractor/lab: AquaBiota Water Research (they hold the national benthic mapping contract for Länsstyrelsen Gotland).

**T-H2. Benthic macrofauna grab sampling**
At each of 8–12 stations (stratified by depth and sediment type), collect 3 replicate Van Veen grabs (0.1 m²). Sieve at 1 mm; preserve in 70 % ethanol; identify and count all macrofauna. Target indicators: Monoporeia affinis, Mysis mysis, Marenzelleria spp., Macoma balthica, Mytilus edulis, Saduria entomon. Deliverable: species abundance and biomass table, ecological quality ratio (EQR) per BSII index (SLU Aqua method). Time: 1 field day per occasion; minimum 2 occasions (June and September). Lab: SLU Institutionen för akvatiska resurser (SLU Aqua), Öregrund — they hold the Baltic benthic quality index methodology.

**T-H3. Diver transects at anchor positions**
At each of the 6–8 proposed anchor/mooring positions, have a scientific diver (SSBA cert., Olle can coordinate) conduct a 25 m × 2 m belt transect photographing and recording all benthic organisms. Deliverable: photographic record, species list per anchor point. Time: 2–3 dive days, June–August.

**T-H4. Littoral intertidal survey**
Survey the supralittoral and eulittoral zone along the 500 m coastal segment nearest the cable landfall and mooring areas. Record Fucus spiralis belt, barnacle zone, and any protected coastal habitats (8210 Calcareous rocky slopes, 1630* Boreal Baltic coastal meadows). Deliverable: 1:500 habitat sketch map. Time: 1 field day, May or August.

### Bird surveys (migration seasons and breeding)

**T-Bi1. Spring migration monitoring — seawatch counts**
From a fixed observation point at or near Storugns (to be selected in consultation with GOF), conduct standardised seawatch counts 2 hours before to 2 hours after sunrise, daily during peak spring migration, minimum 20 count days spanning April 10 – May 20. Record all species, numbers, flight direction, and height band. Deliverable: species abundance by date and direction, comparison with Nabben reference data. Time: ~20 field days over 6 weeks. Olle coordinates; GOF ringing station data requested in parallel.

**T-Bi2. Autumn migration monitoring — radar or visual**
Same fixed point, daily counts September 1 – November 10, focusing on seabird and wader movement over the proposed marine site. If budget allows, deploy a small ornithological radar (Robin Radar or equivalent) for 4–6 weeks to quantify nocturnal passage that visual counts miss. Deliverable: passage volume estimate, seasonal phenology curve. Time: ~40 field days over 10 weeks (can overlap with other autumn surveys). Contractor for radar: Calluna AB or Vindval-certified ornithologist.

**T-Bi3. Breeding bird inventory — local colony check**
In May–June, conduct 3 boat transects along the coast from Storugns north to Helgumannen to document breeding eider, black guillemot, and any colonial waterbird species on skerries within 3 km of the site. Deliverable: colony location map with species and pair count. Time: 2 boat days.

**T-Bi4. GOF historical data request and Nabben time series extraction**
Formal data request to Gotlands ornitologiska förening for all Nabben ringing and seawatch data 2000–present for the northern Gotland sector. Coordinate via Länsstyrelsen Gotland naturvårdsenhet. Deliverable: collated dataset in Excel or GIS format. Time: 1–2 weeks desk work, Olle.

### Marine mammals (grey seal, porpoise, harbour seal)

**T-M1. Grey seal haul-out counts — aerial or boat survey**
Contract one aerial survey flight over the coastal zone from Fårö to Slite in March (peak seal haul-out on ice/skerries) and one in August (post-moulting haul-out). Standard counting protocol per HELCOM MSTATE method. Deliverable: haul-out count, position map, pup count if March. Contractor: Naturhistoriska riksmuseet (NRM) Stockholm — they run the Swedish national seal monitoring programme.

**T-M2. Passive acoustic monitoring for harbour porpoise — C-POD deployment**
Deploy 2 C-PODs (Chelonia Ltd.) at the site and at a 2 km reference station for minimum 12 months, recovering and redeploying every 6 months. The C-POD records porpoise echolocation click trains (CPOD.exe software). Deliverable: porpoise presence index (KDET/day) per month. Time: 12 months continuous. Lab: NRM's cetacean group for data validation, or AquaBiota. Equipment hire/service: Chelonia distributor (currently DHI Sverige).

**T-M3. Opportunistic marine mammal log during all vessel surveys**
During every vessel survey (T-B1, T-B2, T-H1, T-H2, T-M1 vessel phase), maintain a dedicated mammal observer watch log: all sightings of Halichoerus grypus, Phoca vitulina, Phocoena phocoena with GPS position, behaviour, and reaction to vessel. Deliverable: appended to each survey report. Time: zero additional vessel time; Olle trains observers.

### Water quality (physical-chemical and biological)

**T-W1. CTD casts and nutrient profiles**
At 3–5 stations (site centre, shoreward, and 2 km offshore reference), deploy a calibrated CTD (Sea-Bird SBE 19plus or equivalent) with sensors for conductivity, temperature, depth, dissolved oxygen, fluorescence (chl-a proxy), and turbidity. Collect water samples at surface, pycnocline, and near-bottom for total nitrogen, total phosphorus, Secchi depth, suspended particulate matter (SPM), and chlorophyll-a (filtered, fluorometric). Minimum: 4 sampling occasions (February, May, August, November). Lab: SMHI's marine chemistry lab (Norrköping) or Eurofins Miljö (Lidköping), accredited for SS-EN ISO 10260 (chlorophyll), SS-EN ISO 11905 (nitrogen), SS-EN ISO 6878 (phosphorus).

**T-W2. Phytoplankton and zooplankton net hauls**
At the main CTD station, collect a vertical Bongo net haul (200 µm mesh) for zooplankton and a phytoplankton bottle sample. Identify to genus level; note cyanobacteria bloom risk (Nodularia spumigena, Aphanizomenon flos-aquae are the key summer toxin producers in the Baltic). Occasions: May, July, September. Lab: SLU Aqua plankton group or Marint centrum Simrishamn (they hold a regional zooplankton identification contract).

### Noise baseline

**T-N1. Ambient underwater noise baseline — hydrophone deployment**
Deploy a calibrated broadband hydrophone (Cetacean Research Technology H1a or equivalent, flat response 10 Hz–100 kHz) at the site for 2 × 6-week deployments covering low-activity period (January–February) and high-activity summer shipping/fishing period (June–July). Record in duty-cycle mode (5 min/hour minimum). Deliverable: 1/3-octave band spectra, statistical distribution (L10, L50, L90), background noise percentiles for use in construction noise impact model. Lab/contractor: DHI Sverige (they run the Trafikverket ferry corridor noise monitoring and have calibrated systems) or Akustikfirman (Stockholm).

### Seasonal and interannual coverage requirements

**T-S1. Year-2 repeat of T-H2 and T-W1**
Any benthic macrofauna baseline requires at minimum two years to capture inter-annual variability (Baltic hypoxia events, mild vs. ice winter). A second year of macrofauna grabs and CTD profiles is necessary before the MKB can claim a statistically valid pre-installation baseline.

## 3. Suggested sequencing

**Mandatory seasonal windows that drive the schedule**

The survey programme cannot be sequenced freely. Four hard constraints dominate:

- T-Bi1 (spring migration) is only possible April 10 – May 20. Miss it, wait a year.
- T-M1 (grey seal aerial count) requires March (pup/ice haul-out) or August; neither can be substituted.
- T-H1 and T-H3 (benthic habitat mapping, dive surveys) require June–August for visibility and vegetation at peak biomass. Cannot be conducted in winter.
- T-W2 (phytoplankton July cyanobacteria) requires July–August.

These constraints mean the minimum MKB-credible survey programme spans at minimum two full calendar years.

**Pre-grant, low-commitment activities (start immediately)**

The following tasks have low financial exposure and high information value; they should begin before grant confirmation because they are data-gathering rather than field mobilisation and because the seasonal window may pass:

- T-Bi4: Desk data request to GOF and Nabben — costs nothing, takes a few weeks.
- T-S1 (year 1 benthic grabs + CTD): If H2 2027 start is realistic, 2026–2027 field seasons must be the baseline years; the first round of T-H2 and T-W1 should start in the summer of 2027 at the latest, meaning a 2026 pre-baseline visit if possible.
- T-M2 (C-POD deployment): The sooner the C-PODs are in the water, the more months of baseline data accumulate before the MKB submission deadline.
- T-N1 (noise baseline): Deploy in January–February 2027 if funding is confirmed by then; the low-noise winter baseline is the most important season to capture.

**After grant confirmation (expensive, risk-exposed)**

- T-B1/B2/B4 (MBES, SSS, sub-bottom) — vessel mobilisation cost ~200–350 kSEK per campaign; justify against confirmed project budget.
- T-M1 aerial seal survey — requires contracted fixed-wing aircraft and observer team, ~80–120 kSEK per flight.
- T-Bi2 radar deployment — radar hire 6–8 weeks ~150 kSEK.

**Which surveys must be complete before MKB is drafted**

The MKB cannot be drafted without: completed T-H1 (benthic habitat map), two rounds of T-H2 (macrofauna grabs, two seasons), T-B1 (bathymetry), one full 12-month T-M2 (C-POD record), T-Bi1 and T-Bi2 from at minimum one migration season each, T-W1 from all four seasonal profiles, and T-N1 (noise baseline). This translates to a minimum 18–24 month lead time before the MKB can be submitted for Länsstyrelsen review.

## 5. Open questions and things to verify

**Natura 2000 sites within 10 km — to be confirmed**
The site at 57.844°N, 18.797°E must be cross-referenced against the Swedish Natura 2000 register at Naturvårdsverket (naturvardsverket.se/skyddad-natur/naturvards-registret). Based on the known geography: SE0340148 Gotlands nordkust (SCI/SPA for limestone coast, seabirds, seals) is a high-probability overlap. SE0340066 Norska träsk (wetland SCI) is further inland. The precise site boundary shapefiles must be downloaded and overlaid with the project footprint including the cable route, before the initial Länsstyrelsen dialogue. Assumption here: at least one Natura 2000 site boundary is within 2 km; the project should plan for a full Appropriate Assessment.

**Local bird monitoring stations with time series covering the site**
GOF's Nabben station at Fårö's northern tip is the dominant local time series. The assumed distance from Nabben to the project site is approximately 12–15 km. This is close enough for the Nabben migration data to be quantitatively relevant for a flight-line model, but far enough that site-specific counts (T-Bi1, T-Bi2) are still required. Olle should verify whether GOF has any secondary count points between Fårö and Storugns with any years of data.

**Nordkalk's existing environmental monitoring at Storugns**
Nordkalk's quarrying and port operations at Klinthagen/Storugns are operated under an environmental permit (tillstånd) from Länsstyrelsen Gotland. This permit will have associated monitoring conditions (villkorsövervakning) covering dust, noise, blasting vibration, and receiving water quality. The monitoring reports are public documents (offentliga handlingar) available from Länsstyrelsen. Olle should file a begäran om utlämning of Nordkalk's most recent annual monitoring reports. If their monitoring network covers water quality at the FPV site coordinates, this is a potentially 5–10 year historical baseline that would otherwise cost 500–800 kSEK to establish from scratch.

**Ongoing scientific projects that could piggyback cost-sharing**
- HELCOM COMBINE monitoring programme: Sweden's national COMBINE station (Gotland Basin, BY15, at 57.30°N 20.03°E) is too far east to piggyback directly, but the programme vessels transit through the area and may accommodate supplementary CTD casts on request to SMHI.
- SLU Aqua's national coastal fish and benthic monitoring: SLU Aqua runs standardised Baltic coastal monitoring transects at several Gotland locations. If any monitoring station is within 10–15 km of the site, their historical benthic data is directly usable as a reference series for T-H2 comparisons.
- University of Gothenburg's Department of Marine Sciences has ongoing projects on Baltic hard-bottom ecology (Fucus decline, charophyte distribution) that could provide species-identification support and methodological alignment.
- Stockholm University Baltic Sea Centre: publishes annual Baltic Sea synthesis reports; their nutrient and eutrophication data sets are publicly downloadable and should be used to contextualise the T-W1 findings.

**Ice regime — microclimate at the specific site**
SMHI's ice service (Isavdelningen) holds satellite and observation records of ice cover for the Baltic coast. The specific site at 57.844°N is just outside the typical fast-ice zone in most years but can be enclosed by drift ice. The FPV and concrete breakwater design must be validated against the 1-in-50-year ice extent from SMHI's gridded ice records. This is a design question that the environmental baseline programme should flag, but the actual structural response is an engineering deliverable, not an ecological one.

**What assumptions might be wrong**
- The 130 m diameter breakwater is assumed to be the operative footprint for benthic impact assessment. If the mooring spread is wider (catenary anchor legs in 20–30 m of water may spread 150–300 m from the structure), the benthic survey scope increases substantially.
- The site is assumed to be outside existing Natura 2000 boundaries. If a boundary overlaps, the project may need a full habitats regulation article 6(3) Appropriate Assessment rather than just a standard MKB. This could add 12–18 months to the regulatory timeline.
- Winter access to the site for surveys is assumed to be feasible from Storugns harbour. If Nordkalk's port is not available for charter, vessel mobilisation must come from Slite or Fårösund, adding 1–2 hours transit per survey day.
