## 1. Industrial-liaison problems and risks

**1.1 Permit-fatigue and re-opening risk**
Nordkalk completed a grinding, multi-year defence of the Klinthagen extraction permit. The Mark- och miljööverdomstolen ruling of June 2026 granted 30 years of continued extraction, but the case was contested by Naturvårdsverket, Länsstyrelsen Gotland, Region Gotland, Naturskyddsföreningen and Urbergsgruppen — and as of late 2026 the decision has been appealed to Högsta domstolen by Naturskyddsföreningen and Urbergsgruppen. Nordkalk's legal team and facility management are exhausted and acutely sensitive to anything that gives opponents a new hook. A FPV demonstration adjacent to their operation will immediately be read by local NGOs as "more industrial pressure on northern Gotland's coastal zone". Any misstep in how SUREWAVE/Sunlit Sea presents itself to the public before Nordkalk has had a chance to own the narrative internally will be treated as a threat, not an opportunity. The first rule of engagement: let Nordkalk set the pace of any public statement.

**1.2 Vessel traffic density and navigation conflict**
Storugns handles approximately 400 vessel calls per year and around 2.7 million tonnes of cargo annually, predominantly limestone and quicklime to steel mills and cement plants around the Baltic. Wagenborg alone moves over 1 million tonnes on behalf of Nordkalk through this port. Vessels up to 23,000 DWT with 9.0 m draught use the west pier. Approach channels and turning basins are not wide. A catenary-moored FPV array with a 130 m diameter footprint creates a fixed obstruction in waters where large bulk carriers manoeuvre. The exact coordinates — 57.844°N, 18.797°E — need to be overlaid against Sjöfartsverket's Storugns port approach chart and any declared fairways before any other technical work proceeds. If the FPV array is on or near the approach line, the entire site may be untenable without a significant mooring redesign.

**1.3 Blasting schedule and vibration propagation**
Klinthagen quarry is an active open-cast operation. Blasting occurs on a regular schedule — typically weekly or fortnightly cycles in large limestone operations of this type — and generates ground vibration that propagates through rock into adjacent water bodies. For a catenary-moored floating structure, seismic-equivalent impulse loads during blasting events are a structural fatigue question, not merely a comfort question. There is no public data on measured vibration levels at the water surface adjacent to Storugns. Nordkalk may have vibration monitoring data from their environmental permit compliance, which would be the starting point; if not, a measurement campaign would be needed before structural designers can sign off. This needs to be scoped early because it is the kind of data that takes months to accumulate properly.

**1.4 Limestone dust deposition on PV panels**
Limestone operations are chronic particulate emitters — calcitic dust from blasting, crushing, conveyor transfer points and truck movements. Storugns has invested in sweeping machines, asphalted areas and sprinkling systems, but in a Baltic onshore wind the plume from the Klinthagen side and the terminal loading area will periodically reach adjacent sea surface to the northeast. PV panel efficiency loss from limestone dust is a documented problem at quarry-adjacent solar in the Mediterranean and South Asia; the Baltic version is less studied but the physics are the same. High-pH calcium carbonate deposits are mildly abrasive and difficult to rinse in rain. For an FPV system with panels at low tilt near sea level, rain self-cleaning is already less effective than for land-mount. Dust deposition rates, wind-rose data and panel-cleaning protocols need to factor into the yield model and maintenance plan.

**1.5 Port operational safety perimeter**
Nordkalk's Storugns port has its own port rules registered with Sjöfartsverket (Port Rules, Nordkalk Storugns Port, Gotland, rev. 201231). These define restricted zones, vessel handling procedures and coordination requirements. Any floating structure within or near the port area must be cleared against these rules. Nordkalk's own port operations team — not just the corporate sustainability function — needs to be consulted. The port rules also establish pilotage and boarding requirements that affect how service vessels for the FPV array would operate in the same water area.

**1.6 Radio-frequency and radar interference**
Port operations, vessel navigation, and the Storugns facility use VHF marine radio, AIS and radar systems. A large metallic floating structure — aluminium modules over approximately 13,000 m² — has a non-trivial radar cross-section. A structural health management platform with wireless sensors adds RF emissions to the environment. Swedish Maritime Administration and the facility's own navigation systems need assurance that the FPV array does not create a radar shadow or AIS ghost. This is a solvable technical problem but it requires early coordination with Sjöfartsverket and Nordkalk's port authority.

**1.7 Freshwater and process-water interactions**
Limestone quarrying at this scale involves groundwater management: pumping to keep the quarry dry, with discharge to surrounding water bodies. Nordkalk Storugns has environmental permit conditions on water discharge quality and volumes. The FPV installation does not consume water, but it alters local surface hydrology (shading, evaporation suppression) and any anchor or mooring installation involves seabed interaction. The project team needs to understand the existing discharge permit conditions to demonstrate that FPV does not interfere with Nordkalk's compliance envelope. The 30-year extraction permit ruling specifically mentioned groundwater conditions and required water-shortage compensation provisions.

**1.8 Lighting footprint and night-sky coordination**
Nordkalk's Storugns facility operates extended hours — loading is scheduled Monday–Thursday 06:00–22:00 and Friday–Sunday 06:00–19:00. The FPV array's navigation lighting (required by maritime rules for a fixed structure), any structural health monitoring indicator lights, and potential contractor lighting during installation will add to the night-sky signature of what is already a large industrial site. In the context of the ongoing Klinthagen permit dispute, additional light pollution arguments could be raised by NGOs. Coordinate night-lighting specifications with the facility's own environmental commitments.

**1.9 PPA structure with a continuously operating industrial load**
A behind-the-meter PPA with Nordkalk Storugns is commercially attractive on paper: large, stable industrial load, operations effectively 24/7, incentive under CSRD Scope 2 reporting to demonstrate renewable sourcing. However, the contractual structure is non-trivial. Nordkalk's Storugns production does not modulate output to match solar generation — they run crushing, conveying and kiln operations on their own schedule. A 1 MWp FPV generates a variable output profile peaking at midday summer. The PPA structure must address curtailment (if grid export is limited), avoided-cost pricing versus market pricing, and take-or-pay provisions. Since SigmaRoc acquired Nordkalk in 2021, energy procurement decisions above a certain threshold are likely made at group level, not facility level — the facility platschef cannot commit Nordkalk to a multi-year PPA without corporate sign-off.

**1.10 Nordkalk's CSRD and Scope 2 reduction position**
SigmaRoc, as a UK-listed group now owning Nordkalk, faces CSRD and TCFD reporting obligations. Nordkalk itself has published sustainability targets including a goal of fossil-free production by 2040. Scope 2 electricity emissions reduction through a demonstrable renewable energy off-take would be directly useful to SigmaRoc's annual sustainability reporting. This is the commercial opening — but it also means that any PPA negotiation will be reviewed by SigmaRoc Group's sustainability and finance teams in Edinburgh and/or Helsinki, not only by Storugns facility management. Know which level to pitch to first.

**1.11 Operational staging and jetty access**
The Storugns port has significant loading capacity and handles large vessels. For FPV installation, the ability to stage float modules, anchors and breakwater elements at or near Storugns would dramatically reduce logistics costs compared to bringing everything from the Swedish mainland. However, any arrangement to use Nordkalk's berths, hardstanding or warehousing, even temporarily, creates a contractual relationship that Nordkalk may want to avoid pre-grant — it implies formal endorsement of the project at a time when the Klinthagen appeal to HD is still live.

**1.12 Reputational calculus: extraction narrative versus innovation narrative**
Nordkalk has spent years being cast by opponents as a threat to Gotland's natural environment. Hosting a renewable energy demonstration on the water adjacent to their facility gives them a genuine counter-narrative: the same company that extracts limestone for the steel industry is enabling clean energy innovation. This is a real reputational asset for SigmaRoc's stakeholder communications. The risk is the flip side: if the FPV project itself attracts controversy (EIA process, bird and bat impacts in Baltic coastal zone, visual landscape), Nordkalk gets dragged into that controversy too. Their communications team will want assurance that SUREWAVE/Sunlit Sea's own stakeholder process is watertight before they publicly associate.

**1.13 Who controls the water surface**
The coordinates sit in what may be allmänt vattenområde (public water area) adjacent to Nordkalk's fastighet. Swedish water area ownership is complex — shoreline fastigheter may extend into the water, but use rights for fixed installations require antingen fastighetsägarens medgivande or a separate nyttjanderättsavtal. If the water area belongs to Nordkalk's fastighet, the project needs their consent regardless of any public permit. If it is allmänt vattenområde, the project still needs consent from adjacent fastighetsägare for mooring anchors that touch the seabed within their lantmäteri-registered area. This is not a theoretical risk — getting this wrong has killed projects that were otherwise fully permitted.

**1.14 Nordkalk's own permit consultants and their position**
The Klinthagen permit case was fought by Nordkalk with specialist legal and environmental consulting support. Those same consultants know the Gotland political environment, the NGO landscape and Länsstyrelsen's sensitivities better than almost anyone else. If Nordkalk's permit team comes to see the FPV project as a potential complication — something that creates a new environmental baseline or a new procedural hook — they will advise facility management to keep distance. Conversely, if Nordkalk's environmental consultant can be brought to see FPV as an environmental positive that complements the Klinthagen permit's habitat-restoration conditions, they become an inadvertent advocate. Early quiet contact at the right consultant level is worth mapping.

**1.15 Emergency response coordination**
Storugns is an industrial site with its own emergency response plan under Swedish AFS regulations for quarrying and port operations. A maritime emergency response — fire on the FPV array, anchor failure, vessel collision with the float structure — would involve Sjöfartsverket, MSB, and local fire services, in an area where Nordkalk's own emergency resources and access routes are present. There must be a clear deconfliction of emergency response procedures, so a FPV incident does not trigger Nordkalk's facility emergency protocol and vice versa.

## 3. Suggested sequencing

The single most important principle here: Nordkalk must never feel surprised. A company that has just survived a grinding permit fight is on high alert for anything that arrives without warning and creates new obligations or new press.

**Phase 0 — Pre-grant, desktop only (months 1–4 from now)**
Do everything that does not require Nordkalk's involvement: T-NKL-01 (water tenure), T-NKL-02 (nautical chart overlay), T-NKL-04 and T-NKL-06 (contact identification), T-NKL-05 (SigmaRoc sustainability contact), T-NKL-14 and T-NKL-15 (energy and Scope 2 desk research). This phase costs nothing and does not expose Nordkalk to any new commitment. It also means that when Olle walks in the door at Storugns, he knows more about their situation than they expect.

**Phase 1 — Quiet initial engagement, pre-grant (months 3–6)**
The first platschef contact (T-NKL-07) and preparatory brief (T-NKL-08) should happen before the grant application is submitted in December 2026, but only after Phase 0 is complete. The purpose is to have a face on the file at Storugns who knows the project exists, understands it is a long way from commitment, and has not been surprised by reading about it somewhere. This contact should be quiet — not a formal meeting request on letterhead, but a phone call followed by a short note. Keep the Klinthagen HD appeal in mind: this is not the moment for any public association.

**Phase 2 — First site visit and operational data gathering, post-grant application but pre-decision (months 6–9)**
The first face-to-face meeting at Storugns (T-NKL-09) can happen after the grant application is submitted. By this point SUREWAVE/Sunlit Sea has a concrete public document (the EIC application) to show, which professionalises the conversation. Request operational data (T-NKL-10, T-NKL-11, T-NKL-12, T-NKL-13) in this phase. These data requests ask for something real from Nordkalk, but they are framed as scientific input, not commercial commitment.

**Phase 3 — Post-grant confirmation (H1 2028 if funded)**
Only after grant confirmation do the heavyweight engagements happen: PPA concept note (T-NKL-16), operational boundary agreement negotiation (T-NKL-19), port staging feasibility (T-NKL-21), emergency response coordination (T-NKL-20). These all imply real resources and real commitment from Nordkalk. Nordkalk's facility manager cannot approve a PPA or an operational agreement under their own authority — these require SigmaRoc Group involvement. Grant confirmation also changes Nordkalk's calculus: a funded project with a consortium behind it is a partner worth engaging commercially, where a grant-stage proposal is a speculative enquiry that can be quietly ignored.

**Pacing caution:** Storugns facility management runs a 24/7 quarrying and shipping operation. They are not a professional stakeholder engagement partner. Do not send more than one substantive communication per month in Phase 1. In Phase 2, one site visit and a follow-up data request is the right rhythm for 3–4 months. The single largest risk is making Nordkalk feel that a renewable energy start-up is creating administrative burden on top of an already difficult permit environment.

**Klinthagen HD appeal status and Nordkalk's communications posture**
The Klinthagen judgment of June 2026 has been appealed to Högsta domstolen by Naturskyddsföreningen and Urbergsgruppen. HD's decision on whether to grant prövningstillstånd (leave to appeal) could come at any point in 2026–2027. Until that decision is known, Nordkalk's external communications posture will remain defensive. The moment HD either declines prövningstillstånd (case closed, permit confirmed) or grants it (back to full legal uncertainty), Nordkalk's willingness to be publicly associated with new co-development changes significantly. Track this case and time any co-marketing approach to the HD prövningstillstånd decision.

**Whether Nordkalk would accept being named as a project partner in dissemination materials**
This is the specific question that the EIC application may want answered — a named industrial host/partner strengthens a demonstration project application. Given the HD appeal status, the answer in December 2026 is almost certainly "not yet, and only as a host site, not as an industrial partner with commitments". The application may need to describe Nordkalk as a potential industrial collaborator under non-disclosure, without naming them, if the relationship has not progressed far enough by submission date.
