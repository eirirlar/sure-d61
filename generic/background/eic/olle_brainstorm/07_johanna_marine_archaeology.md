## 1. Archaeology and cultural heritage problems and risks

**1.1 Legal framework: KML 2 kap. and the 1850-year rule**
All shipwrecks that sank before 1850 are automatically protected as fornlämningar under Kulturmiljölagen (KML, SFS 1988:950) 2 kap. 1 §, regardless of whether they appear in any register. That 1850 threshold is absolute: a merchant vessel that went down in 1849 during a winter storm off northern Gotland is a protected cultural monument the day it is found. Any seabed disturbance — driven anchor piles, drag anchor installation, chain scour, dredging, cable trenching — that affects a protected fornlämning without prior tillstånd from Länsstyrelsen is a criminal offence under KML 2 kap. 12 §. For a foreign-headquartered consortium the reputational and project-delay risk of triggering a KML violation is severe.

**1.2 Fornsök coverage is incomplete for marine sites**
RAÄ's Fornsök database (app.raa.se) is the statutory baseline and must be queried before any marine construction permit application. However, marine registration in Fornsök significantly lags terrestrial coverage. Swedish marine archaeology capacity has historically been concentrated at SMTM/Vrak and Stockholms Universitet, and systematic survey of coastal Gotland waters is incomplete. A "clean" Fornsök result for the 57.844 N / 18.797 E area does not mean the site is archaeologically clear; it means no survey has confirmed remains there yet. This distinction must be made explicit in the Environmental Impact Assessment (EIA) and in RAÄ consultation.

**1.3 Baltic wreck density: the numbers are not reassuring**
Conservative estimates put 9,000+ kulturmiljölagen-protected wrecks on the Baltic seabed; other estimates run to 100,000 total wrecks across all periods. In Gotland waters the density is particularly high because northern Gotland lies astride the main Stockholm–Hanseatic–Eastern Baltic shipping corridor that was active from the Viking Age through the 20th century. The maritime traffic funnel between Gotland's northern tip, Fårö, and the Gotland Deep generated centuries of losses: navigational hazards, storms, wartime sinkings. A 130 m diameter footprint in shallow coastal water at a historical trafficked headland, with 10–15 anchor-pile positions spread across an area much larger than the float array itself, statistically intersects this density.

**1.4 The "Vasa effect": exceptional preservation and correspondingly high research value**
The same brackish, low-oxygen, low-temperature conditions that preserved the Vasa in Stockholm harbour for 333 years operate throughout the Baltic. The key factors are absence of the wood-boring shipworm Teredo navalis (which cannot survive the low salinity), near-anaerobic bottom sediments in even relatively shallow water, and cold temperatures. Wooden hulls from the Hanseatic period — cogs, hulks, kraayers — and from the 18th and 19th centuries that have settled into Gotland seabed sediment can retain original timber, cargo, personal effects, and organic remains in a state that would be archaeologically impossible anywhere in the open North Sea or Atlantic. This makes a wreck encountered during this project not merely an inconvenience to route around but a potentially significant scientific monument, with corresponding RAÄ, museum, and public attention.

**1.5 Mooring installation is the primary archaeological risk, not the FPV platform**
The concrete floating breakwater and PV float array themselves rest on the water surface; they do not penetrate the seabed. The archaeological risk comes entirely from the mooring system: driven piles, drilled rock anchors in the limestone seabed, or drag anchors deployed with installation vessels. Each anchor position is a point of direct seabed penetration. Catenary chain scour over time can disturb a metre-wide corridor along the chain arc between anchor and buoy. For a 130 m diameter installation with perimeter mooring, this could mean 12–20 anchor positions, each with a scour corridor extending 30–50 m shoreward or seaward. The total area at risk from chain movement is substantially larger than the hull footprint visible on a site plan. Mooring layout must be developed in close coordination with the archaeological anomaly map — not designed first and then checked.

**1.6 Storugns / northern Gotland coastal archaeology**
The shoreline zone around Storugns and northern Gotland is archaeologically rich on land as well as in water. The region has documented prehistoric coastal settlement associated with raised-beach ridges (strandvallar) from post-glacial land uplift (Gotland is still rising at approximately 2 mm/yr). Stone labyrinths (trojaborgar) appear at several coastal headlands in northern Gotland and have uncertain but possibly medieval or post-medieval origin as navigation markers or ritual sites. Fossil shorelines are visible as ridge sequences running parallel to the coast, and prehistoric settlement scatters are documented along them. The cable landing route from the FPV array to shore crosses this coastal zone and requires its own walkover assessment — terrestrial as well as intertidal.

**1.7 Visby UNESCO World Heritage Site: buffer zone uncertainty**
The Hanseatic Town of Visby is inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List (property reference 731, inscribed 1995). The inscribed property is 104.3 ha covering the medieval walled town. UNESCO documentation notes that no formal buffer zone has been demarcated, though one was recommended in early review cycles. Visby is located on the western Gotland coast, approximately 90 km south-southwest of the project site. At that distance, a direct buffer-zone trigger is unlikely for the FPV installation itself. However, the EIA narrative must address the landscape and maritime setting of Gotland as a whole — Gotland's cultural landscape designation (riksintresse kulturmiljö) is broad — and any marine finds that speak to Hanseatic trade routes to and from Visby acquire heightened significance under the UNESCO Outstanding Universal Value statement, which specifically mentions Visby's role as a medieval trading node on Baltic sea routes. A significant Hanseatic wreck found within the project footprint would attract international attention and UNESCO scrutiny beyond the normal Swedish procedure.

**1.8 Kappelshamn and Fårö Sund: nearby documented wreck clusters**
The shallow waters around Kappelshamn (approximately 30 km northwest of the project coordinates) and the Fårö Sund channel to the northeast are among the more densely documented wreck areas in northern Gotland. Fårö Sund has historically been a sheltered anchorage and a navigation choke point; vessels caught in Baltic storms sought shelter there, some didn't make it. Kappelshamn was a significant limestone-export harbour from the 19th century onward with associated small-vessel losses. The project site at 57.844 N / 18.797 E sits in coastal water between these two wreck-dense areas. The prior probability of encountering an unregistered wreck during a thorough geophysical survey of this area is higher than the Baltic baseline.

**1.9 20th-century wrecks: not just medieval**
Cultural heritage protection under KML applies to the pre-1850 cutoff, but later wrecks have a separate archaeological and regulatory status. 20th-century losses — fishing vessels, cargo steamers, vessels from both World Wars — are relevant to the EIA under miljöbalken and may also be protected under foreign nations' legislation if they are warships (German, Russian, or British naval losses in the Baltic). A German submarine or minesweeper from WWII resting in this area would not be a KML-protected fornlämning in the Swedish sense, but its presence would raise operational complications (munitions, diplomatic notification under international conventions) that go well beyond the archaeological domain. The geophysical survey programme must be designed to detect these targets as well as pre-1850 wrecks.

**1.10 Limestone seabed: sub-bottom profiler limitations**
The seabed in northern Gotland coastal water is predominantly biogenic limestone (the same formation Nordkalk quarries at Klinthagen, a few kilometres from the project site). Limestone reflects acoustic energy strongly, which reduces the penetration depth of sub-bottom profiler (SBP) systems compared to clay or sandy seabeds. Buried wrecks under a thin limestone rubble cover may be harder to detect with standard parametric SBP. The survey contractor must be briefed on the substrate and may need to adjust frequency or use a CHIRP system optimised for hard-bottom conditions. This affects survey cost and interpretation confidence.

**1.11 The chance-find procedure and construction halt**
KML 2 kap. 10 § requires that if an ancient cultural monument is discovered during construction work, activity must cease immediately and the find reported to Länsstyrelsen. For a marine construction project this means that if an installation vessel's anchor system or pile driver encounters a wreck, operations stop. The lead time between discovery, RAÄ/Länsstyrelsen assessment, and a decision on whether to allow continued work is typically 4–8 weeks minimum and can extend to several months if a major monument is involved. In a shallow Baltic site with a narrow weather window for marine installation (April–September at most), a mid-season halt is a serious programme risk. Contracts with installation contractors must include this contingency.

**1.12 Local fishermen as an intelligence source**
Commercially active and recreational fishermen in northern Gotland waters have decades of observational data on seabed obstructions — snagged nets, anchor hold problems, unexplained sonar returns — that never reach Fornsök. Fishermen in the Storugns / Kappelshamn area will have operational knowledge of where the wrecks and obstructions are. This knowledge is typically not formalised and is not admissible as the cultural heritage survey itself, but it is invaluable for targeting the geophysical survey efficiently and for directing diver inspection to high-probability anomaly zones. Engaging local fishing community representatives before committing to a survey track plan is standard good practice and should be done early.

**1.13 Nordkalk Klinthagen proximity**
Nordkalk operates a limestone quarry at Klinthagen, approximately 5 km from the project site, with a marine terminal for bulk export. Quarry permitting and harbour construction activities will have generated environmental and archaeological assessment material. The Länsstyrelsen Gotland archive should hold any archaeological clearance surveys from Nordkalk operations. These may provide useful baseline sonar data for the coastal seabed immediately adjacent to the project area and should be obtained through an information request before commissioning new survey work from scratch.

**1.14 Länsmuseet Gotland's role**
Länsmuseet Gotland (now part of Gotlands Museum) holds the regional archaeologist function for Gotland and has its own marine archaeology competence, including records of wreck documentation from local diving and survey activity not always fully reflected in central Fornsök. Any serious marine archaeology programme on Gotland requires early engagement with Länsmuseet, both to access their non-Fornsök records and because Länsstyrelsen Gotland's kulturmiljöenhet routinely involves Länsmuseet in assessment procedures. Bypassing them and dealing only with RAÄ centrally is procedurally inefficient and tends to result in supplementary information requests that slow the process.

**1.15 Survey-to-clearance timeline is longer than most project managers expect**
A bankable kulturhistorisk inventering for a marine project of this scale typically involves: desk-based assessment (4–6 weeks), initial RAÄ / Länsstyrelsen consultation and scope agreement (4–8 weeks), mobilisation and execution of geophysical surveys (2–4 weeks depending on weather and vessel availability), anomaly reporting and diver inspection programme (4–12 weeks depending on anomaly count), draft report and Länsstyrelsen review (8–12 weeks), and final clearance or conditional approval. Total elapsed time from project decision to go-ahead for mooring installation: 9–18 months is realistic, 6 months is optimistic only if no significant anomalies are found and weather cooperates. This timeline needs to be in the EIC proposal's Gantt from the start.

## 2. Archaeology and heritage tasks for Olle

### Desk-based assessment

**T-A1. Fornsök database search**
Query RAÄ Fornsök (app.raa.se/fornsok) for all registered fornlämningar — marine and terrestrial — within a 5 km radius of 57.844 N / 18.797 E. Download georeferenced records as GeoJSON or shapefile. Flag all marine entries with "okänt läge" (uncertain position) within 10 km as requiring positional verification in the field survey. Deliverable: annotated map of registered cultural remains. Time: 1 week. Dependencies: none. Counterparty: RAÄ Fornsök public database.

**T-A2. Kulturmiljöregister and riksintresse search**
Query the Kulturmiljöregister for riksintresse kulturmiljö (national interest cultural environment) designations covering northern Gotland coastal zone. Identify whether the project area or the cable landing route falls within or adjacent to a riksintresse area, which would elevate the significance threshold in the EIA. Deliverable: map overlay with riksintresse boundaries. Time: 1 week. Dependencies: none. Counterparty: RAÄ / Länsstyrelsen Gotland.

**T-A3. SMHI and Sjöfartsverket historical maritime chart review**
Source historical nautical charts (19th-century and early 20th-century Swedish Admiralitetskarta, from Sjöhistoriska museet or the national archives Krigsarkivet) covering northern Gotland coastal water. Historical charts sometimes mark known wreck positions, shoals named after lost vessels, and anchor grounds. Deliverable: annotated digital copies noting any cultural heritage indicators. Time: 2 weeks. Dependencies: none. Counterparty: Sjöhistoriska museet / Riksarkivet.

**T-A4. Maritime history literature and SMTM/Vrak report archive**
Request access to SMTM/Vrak's internal archive of marine archaeological reports from Gotland waters. SMTM maintains a database of all licensed marine archaeological investigations in Sweden. Search for any previous sonar or diver survey reports covering the 57.84 N / 18.80 E area. Deliverable: bibliography and summary of prior survey coverage. Time: 2 weeks. Dependencies: none. Counterparty: SMTM/Vrak, Stockholm.

**T-A5. Länsmuseet Gotland records request**
Contact Länsmuseet Gotland (Gotlands Museum, marinarkeologisk avdelning) and request their internal records of wreck documentation, local amateur diver reports, and any systematic coastal survey data for northern Gotland. Ask specifically about the area from Storugns to Kappelshamn and the offshore zone east of the Fårö Sund approach. Deliverable: summary memo with any non-Fornsök wreck positions received. Time: 2–3 weeks. Dependencies: none. Counterparty: Länsmuseet Gotland / Gotlands Museum.

**T-A6. Local fishermen knowledge gathering**
Organise meetings with 3–5 active commercial fishermen and long-term recreational fishermen operating in northern Gotland coastal waters (Kappelshamn Fiskehamn is the natural focal point). Ask about net snags, anchor problems, sonar anomalies they have encountered. Map the reported positions to the closest degree of precision obtainable. Deliverable: informal map of reported obstructions with qualitative notes; these are survey-targeting inputs only, not formal inventory. Time: 2 weeks (scheduling-dependent). Dependencies: T-A5 for initial contacts. Counterparty: Kappelshamns Fiskareförening or equivalent local body.

**T-A7. Nordkalk Klinthagen archaeological clearance request**
Submit an information request to Länsstyrelsen Gotland's archive and to Nordkalk for any archaeological clearance surveys associated with Klinthagen quarry and its marine terminal. Relevant because the terminal is close to the project area and prior surveys may cover overlapping seabed. Deliverable: copies of any relevant survey reports. Time: 2 weeks (archive response time). Dependencies: none. Counterparty: Länsstyrelsen Gotland (diarium), Nordkalk.

### RAÄ and Länsstyrelsen consultation

**T-A8. Initial written consultation with Länsstyrelsen Gotland kulturmiljöenhet**
Submit a formal samrådsunderlag (consultation memo) describing the project, its footprint, mooring system, cable route, and construction method to Länsstyrelsen Gotland's kulturmiljöenhet. Request a formal scoping opinion specifying what cultural heritage investigation they require before a tillståndsansökan can be accepted. Deliverable: samrådsunderlag document (5–10 pages + maps); Länsstyrelsen response letter. Time: 4–6 weeks to receive response after submission. Dependencies: T-A1, T-A2. Counterparty: Länsstyrelsen Gotland, kulturmiljöenheten.

**T-A9. RAÄ / Vrak specialist consultation**
Request a pre-application meeting with RAÄ Kulturmiljöavdelningen and/or SMTM/Vrak's marine archaeology unit to discuss the survey scope for this type of installation in northern Gotland waters. RAÄ can give informal guidance on the survey standard they would expect before recommending that Länsstyrelsen grant clearance. Deliverable: meeting notes, agreed scope memo. Time: 3–4 weeks to arrange. Dependencies: T-A8. Counterparty: RAÄ Kulturmiljöavdelningen, SMTM/Vrak.

### Geophysical survey programme

**T-A10. Side-scan sonar and magnetometer survey**
Commission a geophysical survey using towed side-scan sonar (minimum 1 m resolution; 100–300 kHz dual-frequency) and a towed proton precession or fluxgate magnetometer. Survey coverage: the entire mooring footprint plus a 200 m buffer. Primary target: all ferrous and large-mass anomalies that could indicate wreck material. Recommended contractors: MMT Sweden AB (Gothenburg, market leader in Scandinavian marine survey), GeoSurveys AB, or DHI Marine Survey. Deliverable: processed sonar mosaic and magnetometer anomaly map. Time: 2–4 weeks weather-dependent (survey season: May–September). Dependencies: T-A8 scope agreement, vessel procurement lead time typically 3–4 months. Counterparty: survey contractor + Länsstyrelsen scope approval.

**T-A11. Sub-bottom profiler (SBP) survey**
Run a CHIRP sub-bottom profiler along a dense grid across the mooring footprint to detect buried organic material (timber hulls below the sediment surface) and characterise seabed stratigraphy. Brief contractor on the hard-limestone substrate to ensure appropriate system selection (parametric or CHIRP rather than boomer/sparker). Deliverable: SBP profile sections and interpreted stratigraphy map. Time: combined with T-A10 if same vessel. Dependencies: T-A10. Counterparty: same survey contractor.

**T-A12. Multibeam bathymetric survey**
Collect high-resolution multibeam bathymetry (0.5 m grid or finer) across the project footprint. Bathymetry reveals surface wreck mounds, scour features, and seabed relief that aids mooring design and provides context for anomaly interpretation. Essential input to mooring design team. Deliverable: DTM (digital terrain model) + hillshade render of seabed. Time: combined with T-A10/A11 if same vessel. Dependencies: T-A8. Counterparty: survey contractor.

### Anomaly investigation

**T-A13. Anomaly classification and prioritisation**
After receiving geophysical data, produce an anomaly catalogue classifying each return as: high probability wreck / possible wreck / probable geological or modern debris / no action required. Present to Länsstyrelsen Gotland for agreement on which anomalies require diver inspection before mooring design is finalised. Deliverable: anomaly classification report. Time: 4–6 weeks post-survey. Dependencies: T-A10, T-A11, T-A12. Counterparty: Länsstyrelsen Gotland.

**T-A14. Licensed diver inspection of priority anomalies**
Commission diver inspection of all anomalies classified as high-probability or possible wreck under T-A13. Divers must be licensed marine archaeologists (or working under the supervision of one with RAÄ diver licence) to record any wreck material. Video + photogrammetry documentation. Deliverable: diver inspection report with assessment of each anomaly's cultural heritage significance and KML status. Time: 2–4 weeks depending on anomaly count and sea conditions. Dependencies: T-A13. Counterparty: licensed marine archaeology diving company (e.g., Vrak-licensed contractor, Maritime Archaeology Sweden, or equivalent).

### Shoreline and cable-landing archaeology

**T-A15. Coastal walkover survey — cable landing route**
Commission a terrestrial / intertidal walkover by a licensed field archaeologist covering the proposed cable landing corridor from the low-water mark to the landward termination. Document any prehistoric raised-beach ridge features, possible settlement indicators, trojaborg sites, or inscribed/marked stones within 50 m of the corridor. Deliverable: walkover survey report. Time: 1–2 weeks fieldwork, 2 weeks reporting. Dependencies: cable route is fixed (needs to be confirmed before survey). Counterparty: Länsmuseet Gotland or another licensed archaeologist.

### Integration with project design

**T-A16. Mooring footprint overlay — iterative with mooring design team**
When anomaly map (T-A13) and mooring preliminary design are both available, overlay them and identify conflicts: any anchor position that falls within 10 m of a confirmed or probable wreck. Work with Sunlit Sea / SINTEF mooring design team to shift pile positions to avoidance zones. Deliverable: revised mooring layout with documented avoidance rationale. Time: 1–2 weeks per design iteration. Dependencies: T-A13, mooring design input. Counterparty: Sunlit Sea / SINTEF mooring engineer.

**T-A17. Mitigation plan**
Draft a formal mitigation plan for submission with the tillståndsansökan: primary strategy is avoidance for all confirmed wrecks; for any wreck where avoidance is geometrically impossible, define the scope of archaeological recording and Länsstyrelsen-approved disposition (removal of artefacts to designated repository, photogrammetric recording before disturbance, etc.). For pre-1850 wrecks any physical intervention requires separate tillstånd under KML 2 kap. 13 §. Deliverable: mitigation plan document. Time: 3–4 weeks. Dependencies: T-A14, T-A16. Counterparty: Länsstyrelsen Gotland (for approval).

**T-A18. Chance-find procedure document for installation phase**
Draft a site-specific chance-find procedure for all contractors operating in the project area during installation: define reporting chain, stop-work authority, Länsstyrelsen emergency contact, documentation protocol. Ensure it is included in marine installation contract specifications. Deliverable: procedure document (3–5 pages). Time: 1 week. Dependencies: T-A17. Counterparty: Länsstyrelsen Gotland (notification); installation contractor.

## 3. Suggested sequencing

**Phase 1 — Before mooring design is frozen (months 1–10)**
The desk-based assessment (T-A1 through T-A7), fishermen knowledge gathering (T-A6), and initial RAÄ/Länsstyrelsen consultation (T-A8, T-A9) must all be completed before mooring layout is finalised. These tasks together define what "safe zones" for anchor placement exist and which parts of the footprint are uncharacterised. Geophysical surveys (T-A10 through T-A12) must also precede mooring freeze. This is non-negotiable: a mooring layout that has been fixed in a project agreement and then requires redesign because an anchor pile lands on a wreck costs far more in time and legal fees than running the survey early.

The one timing constraint that is often underestimated: geophysical survey vessels with the right equipment (dual-frequency SSS + magnetometer + CHIRP SBP + multibeam, all on one mobilisation) have limited availability in the Baltic, and survey windows in shallow exposed coastal water off Gotland are realistically May–August. Booking a survey vessel for a specific window typically requires 4–6 months advance lead time. If the project intends to survey in summer 2027, vessel procurement must begin no later than November–December 2026 — i.e., immediately after the EIC proposal submission.

**Phase 2 — Before tillståndsansökan is submitted (months 6–14)**
Anomaly classification (T-A13) and diver inspections (T-A14) must be complete and reported before the formal permit application is submitted. Länsstyrelsen will not accept an application without an inventory that they consider tillräcklig. Attempting to submit before inspection results are in typically results in a completeness request (föreläggande om komplettering) adding 3–6 months.

**Phase 3 — Before construction contract is placed (months 10–16)**
The mooring overlay (T-A16), mitigation plan (T-A17), and chance-find procedure (T-A18) should be finalised before the installation contract is placed, so the contractor's obligations and stop-work triggers are included in the tender documents rather than added post-award. The shoreline walkover (T-A15) should happen once the cable route is fixed but before any civil work at the cable landing.

**Seasonal constraints**
Side-scan sonar surveys in exposed shallow coastal water require sea state of Beaufort 3 or less for reliable tow-fish control; northern Gotland coastal water is exposed to northeast and east swells. Operationally, May–July is the optimal window, with August as contingency. September onwards the Baltic storm season begins and survey work becomes unreliable. Diver inspection is possible May–September; visibility at 10–20 m depth in northern Gotland water is typically 5–10 m in summer, acceptable for photographic documentation. Avoid survey mobilisation for October–April.

## 4. Key Swedish institutions and marine archaeology contacts

**Riksantikvarieämbetet (RAÄ)**
The central government agency responsible for the cultural heritage regulatory framework. RAÄ operates Fornsök and the Kulturmiljöregister, provides sector authority guidance to Länsstyrelsen, and sets standards for archaeological investigation (including marine). RAÄ Kulturmiljöavdelningen is the first-call expert body for interpretation of KML for a novel project type. Contact: RAÄ Visby (regional presence) or central Kulturmiljöavdelningen in Stockholm.

**Länsstyrelsen Gotland — Kulturmiljöenheten**
The operative permitting authority for all archaeological matters on Gotland. Issues and conditions tillstånd under KML for any activity that risks affecting fornlämningar. For this project, Länsstyrelsen Gotland kulturmiljöenhet is the single most important counterparty: they define what survey standard is required, review the inventory report, and grant or refuse the clearance on which mooring installation depends. The 2026 guidance note they published on landscape and cultural environment assessment for large energy facilities on Gotland (Länsstyrelsen Gotland 2026-06-04 press release) suggests they are actively developing the framework for renewable energy projects — a positive signal.

**Länsmuseet Gotland / Gotlands Museum (Marinarkeologisk avdelning)**
Regional museum with marine archaeology competence and extensive local records. Essential early-engagement partner. They often act as Länsstyrelsen's preferred subcontractor for cultural heritage assessments on Gotland or are consulted informally before Länsstyrelsen issues a formal scope opinion. Address: Strandgatan 14, 621 56 Visby.

**Statens Maritima och Transporthistoriska Museer (SMTM) — Vrak, Museum of Wrecks**
National authority for Swedish marine cultural heritage. Holds the central archive of all licensed marine archaeological investigations in Sweden, maintains the marine fornlämning register, and conducts its own research surveys. Key contact for accessing prior survey reports from Gotland waters and for expert consultation on the significance of wreck finds. Address: Djurgårdsbrunnsvagen 24, Stockholm.

**Sjöhistoriska Museet**
Part of SMTM. Holds historical nautical charts, ships' logs, and maritime archive material relevant to identifying historical traffic patterns in northern Gotland waters.

**MARIS — Maritime Archaeological Research Institute, Södertörn University**
Academic centre for Baltic marine archaeology with particular strength in wood-preservation science and underwater survey methodology. Useful for peer-review of the survey contractor's anomaly interpretations and for expert witness support if a significant wreck is found and heritage-significance assessment is contested.

**Stockholms Universitet — Department of Archaeology and Classical Studies (marine archaeology)**
Academic marine archaeology group with Baltic field experience. Publication track record on Baltic wrecks from medieval and early-modern periods; useful for literature baseline and for expert consultation on Hanseatic-period wreck typology.

**Marine geophysical survey contractors**
- MMT Sweden AB (Gothenburg): market-leading Baltic survey contractor; operates multibeam, SSS, SBP and magnetometer systems; has prior experience with cultural heritage survey specifications in Swedish waters.
- GeoSurveys AB: Swedish independent contractor with Baltic offshore survey work.
- Marin Mätteknik AB: smaller operator, good Gotland-area track record.
- DHI Marine Survey (Danish, operates in Baltic): strong environmental and geophysical capability.

**Licensed marine archaeology diving contractors**
Specific firms that hold RAÄ diving licences for marine archaeological work in Sweden: check RAÄ's current list of authorised operators. Kontiki Marine AB and affiliated operators have been active in Gotland waters. Vrak/SMTM can provide a current referral list.

**Local Gotland maritime heritage associations**
Gotlands Maritima Sällskap and local diving clubs (Gotlands Dyksällskap and affiliated clubs) maintain informal wreck documentation and photographic archives. These are non-statutory sources but valuable for targeting the formal survey.
