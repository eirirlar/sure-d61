# Image critique: inadvertent military/strategic leakage

Audience: Forsvarsmakten HR / line management, Gotland. Purpose of images: support a civilian leave-request pitch. The threshold for rejection is low — if the image looks like Olle is going to do something quasi-military, the leave gets refused on the grounds that the project conflicts with his duties or character of work.

---

## Image 1 — Projektet i korthet (isometric FPV overview)

### 1. Defence/military leakage risks

1. **Breakwater units read as defensive perimeter** — the phrase "ring of pale grey concrete floating breakwater units" combined with "anchor chains descend into clear water" creates a composition that image models routinely render as a mine-barrier or anti-access obstacle. Probability: medium. Reader sees: underwater protection/denial infrastructure rather than wave energy management.

2. **Isometric overhead tactical framing** — the "viewed from above and slightly to the side" specification is structurally identical to how tactical maps, naval operations diagrams and military briefing graphics are rendered. Models have extremely strong priors to produce an aerial-surveillance aesthetic when given this perspective combined with sea water and a rectangular grid. Probability: high. Reader sees: intelligence diagram of a maritime installation.

3. **"Rectangular grid, 50 by 20 metres"** — the explicit military-style dimensions read like a hardened installation footprint rather than a civilian product. Models associate specific metric footprints with engineering corps site plans. Probability: low-medium. Reader sees: technical military site layout.

4. **"Dark-blue solar panels" + "pale concrete grey" + "muted colour palette"** — this exact palette (dark blue, grey, pale sand) is shared between Horizon Europe technical reports and NATO/defence infrastructure diagrams. Without strong civilian colour anchors, the model may tip the render toward the latter. Probability: medium. Reader sees: grey-blue naval installation graphic.

5. **"No text overlay in the image itself" instruction** — this is reasonable, but models tend to fill the visual space vacated by labels with more "serious" aesthetics; the absence of consumer-product cheerfulness pushes the render toward technical/operational modes. Probability: low. Reader sees: unremarkable, but slightly cold.

6. **Anchor chains** — anchor chains are a strong maritime/naval icon. Even in an isometric illustration, rendered chains descending into water may produce nautical-chart or anchor-inventory aesthetics. Probability: low-medium. Reader sees: marine engineering installation, possibly naval supply infrastructure.

### 2. Civilian framing recommendations

- Replace "isometric cutaway viewed from above and slightly to the side" with "architect's perspective illustration as if standing on a nearby dock at eye level, looking at the installation from the side at roughly 30 degrees above horizontal" — this keeps technical clarity but kills the surveillance-overhead composition.
- Specify warm amber/golden tones in the sky and warm reflections on the water; explicitly say "the colour palette references a Swedish clean-energy product brochure, warm and inviting, not a technical operations document."
- Replace "pale grey concrete floating breakwater units" with "white polymer floating pontoon modules, similar in shape to recreational marina dock floats." This shifts the material register from military-engineering to marina-industry.
- Replace "thin anchor chains" with "yellow nylon mooring lines." Nylon lines in yellow/orange are civilian marina equipment; chains are naval/port.
- Add "the sky is rendered in warm pastel blues and whites, more like an IKEA catalogue outdoor scene than an engineering diagram."

### 3. Vibe anchors to add

- "In the lower right corner a small red rowing boat or kayak is moored to the breakwater, suggesting recreational access."
- "A wooden information sign with a QR code and a small Sunlit Sea logo stands on a floating platform at the edge of the installation — the kind of information panel found at a nature reserve."
- "A single white sailing dinghy passes in the background, far from the installation, suggesting this is a shared civilian waterway."
- "The overall aesthetic is that of a Swedish energy company's investor brochure — clean, warm, optimistic, not technical or institutional."

## Image 2 — Platsen på Gotland (aerial coastline)

### 1. Defence/military leakage risks

1. **"High, oblique aerial photograph"** — this is the exact framing used in military reconnaissance photography, UAV surveillance footage, and strategic site assessment imagery. Nordic coast + oblique aerial + industrial zone is one of the strongest military-leakage combinations possible. Probability: high. Reader sees: drone reconnaissance of a logistics or strategic infrastructure installation.

2. **"Limestone handling terminal with conveyor belts and a small cargo pier"** — industrial cargo piers in a Baltic coastal setting render extremely strongly as military logistics or naval supply terminal aesthetics. Gotland specifically has existing military significance in Nordic strategic context; any Gotland coastal military reader will be primed to read a Baltic pier as strategically relevant. Probability: high. Reader sees: potential dual-use or strategic logistics facility.

3. **"A bulk vessel is moored"** — a single large vessel moored at an isolated pier with no other civilian context (shops, people, cars, campsites) reads as naval or coast-guard supply. Probability: medium. Reader sees: supply vessel or patrol craft moored for strategic purposes.

4. **"Pale, slightly weathered grey-white cliffs"** — the Gotland limestone coast is recognisable to any Swedish military reader and carries specific strategic associations. The cliffs + aerial + industrial zone combination will be read as surveillance of a real strategic location. Probability: medium. Reader sees: a real militarily significant site on Gotland photographed from above.

5. **"A few seabirds in flight"** — seabirds in flight in an aerial shot are a classic cinematic shorthand for surveillance footage trying to appear incidental. A trained military reader will notice this as a camouflage device. Probability: low. Reader sees: staged surveillance footage.

6. **"No military presence of any kind" phrasing in prompt** — as noted in the brief, the model attends to "military" and renders military signals unconsciously. The negation actively primes the failure. Probability: medium. Reader sees: whatever the model generates after "attending" to military framing.

7. **"Cinematic drone photography" style instruction** — "cinematic drone" is a commercially available aerial videography aesthetic that is visually nearly identical to military/intelligence drone footage. The instruction pushes the render toward that exact aesthetic. Probability: high. Reader sees: professional surveillance or reconnaissance imagery.

### 2. Civilian framing recommendations

- Replace the aerial framing entirely. A ground-level or low-horizon postcard-style photograph of the Gotland coast is less militarily legible and equally effective for establishing place. Consider: "A bright summer postcard photograph taken from a low clifftop or coastal path, looking out across a Baltic bay."
- If aerial is necessary for the slide's purpose, replace "high, oblique aerial" with "low-altitude view from a light aircraft window at roughly 200 metres, banking slightly" — this is tourist/sightseeing framing rather than reconnaissance framing.
- Replace "limestone handling terminal with conveyor belts" with "a small-boat harbour with sailboats moored in rows, a wooden boathouse, and a café terrace with colourful umbrellas visible at the waterline."
- Replace "bulk vessel moored" with "two or three small white sailing boats at anchor in a bay, a kayak rental sign on the shore."
- Replace "cinematic drone photography" with "Swedish Tourism Board promotional photography, bright and inviting, the aesthetic of a Visit Gotland travel brochure."

### 3. Vibe anchors to add

- "A red-painted wooden boathouse (Swedish röd stuga style) at the water's edge."
- "A family on a coastal path in summer clothes with bicycles."
- "A hand-painted directional sign pointing toward a nature reserve."
- "Colourful beach huts or camping tents in a clearing near the coast."
- "A small café or kiosk with outdoor seating visible near the shore."
- Any of these unmistakably anchors the coastline as civilian recreational; none can be mistaken for strategic infrastructure.

## Image 3 — Hur tekniken fungerar (ground-level sea view with FPV)

### 1. Defence/military leakage risks

1. **"Pale concrete floating breakwater units — rounded, barge-shaped"** — a "barge-shaped" concrete float on open water renders very strongly as a military pontoon bridge, a temporary landing barge, or harbour-defence module. This is the highest-risk phrase in this description. Probability: high. Reader sees: military pontoon equipment or amphibious landing infrastructure.

2. **"Glassy and reflective" water + "Nordic late-afternoon sky"** — this combination produces exactly the aesthetic of professional maritime surveillance photography: flat calm water at golden hour, photographed for documentation purposes. Image models render this with a documentary-cold quality that reads as operational rather than promotional. Probability: medium. Reader sees: site survey photography for official purposes.

3. **"The coastline is just visible as a thin pale strip on the far horizon"** — a horizon-scan composition with a barely visible coast is identical to the framing of coast-watch or maritime patrol imagery. Probability: medium. Reader sees: an image taken by someone conducting surveillance of a maritime zone, with the coastline as a reference point.

4. **"Documentary rather than promotional"** — this instruction directly solicits the aesthetic of authoritative documentation, which models align with official/governmental/military registers. The instruction should be reversed. Probability: medium. Reader sees: a documentary image associated with official or institutional photography.

5. **"Dark-blue grid of about forty rectangular PV panels laid flat on the water, visually similar to a very low floating platform or pontoon dock"** — the "pontoon dock" simile is dangerous; the model is likely to render the installation looking like a military pontoon dock rather than a solar product. Probability: medium-high. Reader sees: floating military platform.

### 2. Civilian framing recommendations

- Replace "barge-shaped" with "capsule-shaped, similar to buoys found in a marina or aquaculture installation." Marina buoys are unambiguously civilian; barges are not.
- Replace "documentary rather than promotional" with "the style of a solar energy product brochure — clear, optimistic, showing the product working well in good conditions. Think Vattenfall or IKEA solar product photography."
- Remove the "coastline just visible as a thin pale strip on the far horizon" framing instruction. Instead anchor the far distance with clearly civilian elements: "In the far distance, the white sails of a few sailing boats are just visible on the horizon."
- Specify warm tones explicitly: "The image has warm golden late-afternoon tones that give the water a honey-amber glow near the panels — clean-energy promotional photography, not industrial documentation."
- Change "dark-blue grid" to "navy-blue and white grid" to introduce a lighter, less militarily coded colour.

### 3. Vibe anchors to add

- "A single yellow mooring buoy marked with a small 'Sunlit Sea' logo floats at the edge of the installation."
- "A paddleboarder or kayaker passes 50 metres from the installation, emphasising the recreational shared-waterway context."
- "Seagulls sitting on the breakwater modules, the way they would sit on a marina pontoon."
- "Warm reflections of the solar panels in the water glow amber-orange, suggesting clean renewable energy at work."

## Image 4 — Olles roll (civilian coordinator on jetty)

### 1. Defence/military leakage risks

1. **Man in dark softshell jacket, from behind, looking out to sea on a jetty** — this is the single highest-risk composition across all four images. It is the precise visual archetype of intelligence/coast-guard/surveillance photography: the observer on the edge of territory, looking out at a maritime zone. Models will render this in cold grey-blue tones with a professional-surveillance quality regardless of the civilian instruction. Probability: very high. Reader sees: coast guard, naval officer, intelligence operative surveying a maritime target.

2. **"Three-quarter view from behind the man's left shoulder"** — the from-behind shoulder composition is standard military observation photography. A uniformed person shot from this angle is unambiguous; a civilian in dark softshell shot from this angle is still military-coded. Probability: high. Reader sees: official observer at a strategic vantage point.

3. **"Holding a notebook and a mobile phone"** — this specific combination of objects in a maritime surveillance composition is literally what coast-guard or border-force spotters carry. A notebook on a jetty is not an office prop; it reads as a log. Probability: high. Reader sees: coast-guard spotter logging vessel movements or monitoring an installation.

4. **"Dark softshell jacket, grey trousers, sturdy walking boots"** — this exact outfit is the standard civilian-contractor uniform in defence and coast-guard contexts. It is worn by private maritime security personnel, defence contractor site surveyors, and harbour-master assistants. Probability: high. Reader sees: defence contractor or coast-guard civilian auxiliary.

5. **"Low stone jetty on a Baltic coastline"** — stone jetties on Baltic coasts read as military/coast-guard infrastructure in Nordic image model training data. A concrete or stone jetty (as opposed to a wooden boardwalk or marina dock) pushes toward official infrastructure aesthetics. Probability: medium. Reader sees: official maritime infrastructure, border/coast access point.

6. **"Slightly overcast day" + "diffuse, soft Baltic daylight, muted blue-grey palette"** — the muted blue-grey palette with overcast light is the dominant palette of documentary photography of official/governmental activities. Combined with the other signals, it completes the intelligence-operative aesthetic. Probability: medium. Reader sees: cold official imagery, not civilian product photography.

7. **"The image should read as local project coordinator doing routine site liaison"** — "site liaison" and "routine" are again bureaucratic/official framings. Models may render the tone of the image as governmental or military-auxiliary rather than private-sector civilian. Probability: low-medium. Reader sees: someone in an official or quasi-official role.

8. **"No uniform, no high-visibility vest, no safety helmet, no military element whatsoever"** — the model attends to "uniform", "military element", producing ghost signals. Probability: medium. Reader sees: inadvertent uniform-adjacent cues in the render.

### 2. Civilian framing recommendations

This image needs a fundamentally different composition. The current design is not salvageable with incremental changes — the from-behind-on-jetty trope is too deep-seated in model training data to overcome with prompt negations.

See the Specific Redesign Recommendations section below.

### 3. Vibe anchors to add

Any of these, depending on the replacement composition chosen:

- "A reusable coffee cup with a lid on the table in front of him, the kind from a local cafe."
- "A small electric bicycle parked against a fence in the background."
- "A local Gotland newspaper or a tourist map folded to show the northern coastline."
- "A calendar open to show a project timeline with Swedish-language month names."
- "Bright red geraniums on a windowsill behind him."
- "A children's drawing or hand-written thank-you card pinned to the noticeboard behind him, suggesting community engagement work."

### 4. Image 4 — Specific redesign recommendations

The man-on-jetty-from-behind composition must be abandoned entirely. Four alternative compositions that serve the slide purpose (communicating Olle's role as civilian community coordinator) while reading unambiguously civilian:

**Option A — The community hall briefing.**
Olle (middle-aged, wearing a colourful fleece or casual shirt, no dark softshell) is seated at the end of a long wooden table in a Swedish bygdegård, looking at a laptop screen with a small projected slide showing a diagram of the FPV installation. Three or four local residents sit around the table with coffee cups. A hand-drawn floor plan of the coastline is on the table. The setting is unambiguously a volunteer civic meeting — wooden walls, a Swedish flag bunting, a notice board with community event flyers. This composition is impossible to read as military.

**Option B — The coastal path walking conversation.**
Olle is walking along a rocky coastal path side by side with a woman in a bright yellow raincoat (representing a Länsstyrelsen or municipality contact). They are both looking at a tablet screen she is holding. In the background, a calm bay is visible, with the FPV installation barely visible as a small feature on the water. The yellow raincoat is a crucial civilian anchor — it is civilian safety clothing, not dark softshell. Walking together reads as collaborative, not surveillance.

**Option C — The farm office map consultation.**
Olle is leaning over a large printed map on a plain office desk, pointing to a location with a pen. A woman colleague or local authority contact sits opposite him. Coffee cups, a binder with "Projekt Gotland" visible on the spine, and a phone showing a project management app fill the desk. No water visible. This composition deliberately removes the sea entirely; Olle's role is planning and coordination, not observation.

**Option D — The marina café conversation.**
Olle is sitting at an outdoor table at a small harbour café in summer clothes (casual linen shirt, light trousers). He is facing the camera at roughly three-quarter angle. On the table: a coffee, a laptop open to a spreadsheet, and a printed project schedule. In the background, small white sailing boats are moored, and the FPV installation is visible across the bay. The facing-the-camera or three-quarter angle is crucial — it removes the surveillance trope, which requires the from-behind perspective.

Of these, Option A (community hall) is the most robustly civilian and has the lowest leakage risk. Option D (marina café) is the most visually attractive but requires careful framing to avoid producing a "harbour-watch" composition if the model places Olle too close to the water.

## Worst-case render gallery

**Image 1 worst case.** The model renders a dark-grey hexagonal platform structure surrounded by a ring of angular anti-wave concrete caissons, connected by chains, viewed from directly above against a dark sea — the composition reads as a NATO sea-mine-denial barrier with solar cells as camouflage. The defensive-perimeter breakwater ring and the overhead tactical perspective combine to produce this result. The single most effective prevention: change the perspective from overhead to a 30-degree-above-horizon side view, and replace "ring of concrete breakwater units" with "a loose cluster of round white polymer foam buoys forming a loose perimeter."

**Image 2 worst case.** The model renders a high-altitude grey-toned aerial of a rocky coast with a single large ship moored at a military-style pier, no people, no civilian colour, angular industrial buildings that resemble bunkers, and what appears to be a coastguard monitoring station on the clifftop. This image, shown in a Forsvarsmakten briefing room, could halt the leave request immediately. The single most effective prevention: replace the "high oblique aerial + limestone handling terminal + bulk vessel" composition entirely with a ground-level Visit Gotland tourist photograph showing a harbour with sailboats and a café.

**Image 3 worst case.** The model renders a flat rectangular grey pontoon floating on a gunmetal-grey Baltic sea under an overcast sky, surrounded by barge-shaped grey floats — the scene looks like an amphibious military pontoon deployment exercise photographed for an after-action report. The water is flat and cold; there are no civilian context signals. The single most effective prevention: replace "barge-shaped, weathered, concrete" with "capsule-shaped white polymer buoys, marina-grade" and add a kayak or paddleboarder in the foreground to unmistakably anchor the recreational register.

**Image 4 worst case.** The model renders a man in a dark navy jacket standing at the end of a stone pier from behind, holding a black notebook, looking out at a grey Baltic horizon where a flat dark rectangular structure sits on the water. The image is indistinguishable from a coast-guard intelligence photograph of a maritime infrastructure installation. A Forsvarsmakten line manager reviewing this slide in a leave-request meeting would read it as evidence that the project involves some form of maritime monitoring or surveillance work, and would refuse the leave to avoid conflict with military duties. The single most effective prevention: abandon the composition entirely and use Option A (community hall briefing) in which Olle is facing the camera, seated indoors, surrounded by civilian community members and coffee cups.
