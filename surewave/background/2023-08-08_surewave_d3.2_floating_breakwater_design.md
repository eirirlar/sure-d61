Executive summary

The study focuses on investigating the behavior of floating breakwaters composed of precast elements connected through flexible connections. The investigation is carried out in offshore areas, considering real possible wave spectra and wind speeds provided by project partner SIS. The main criteria for evaluation include wave breaking or attenuating effect, dynamic behavior, internal load carrying capacity, and force characteristics and transmission to couplings. The breakwater design has been optimized through coupled aero-hydrodynamics with the aim of maximizing the photovoltaic (PV) area for cost-effectiveness. Numerous correlations found in existing literature, designed to estimate the transmission coefficient, have been thoroughly examined and discussed. These correlations\' outcomes have been compared with the outcomes of high-fidelity Computational Fluid Dynamics (CFD) simulations and it is presented here.

# Introduction

## General

This deliverable is related to the technical ***Objective O1 -- "Novel floating PV design for offshore extreme conditions"*** and to ***Challenge 1 -- "Dimensioning of the Floating breakwaters including connections and mooring to protect FPV system against severe load conditions"***.

## Description of deliverable

The activities performed in this memo are associated with task 3.2. The design parameters of the floating breakwater are presented. The main result includes plots and drawings for the optimized breakwater solution.

The relationships between reflection and transmission, the dynamic behaviour, and the force transfer are also presented for various realistic wave spectra and breakwater cubature.

## Description of related tasks

The description of the task is provided here which is taken from the SUREWACE Annex Part B.

#### T3.2 -- Overall design of the external breakwater 

As precast elements, they are limited in size and are therefore connected to each other with flexible connections based on preliminary sizes and geometries of the modular and perimetral breakwaters as shown in Section 1.2.1. Following the Beaufort scale for wind speeds, the behaviour of floating breakwaters with variable dimensions at real possible wave spectra in offshore areas will be investigated with regards to the following criteria:

- Wave breaking or attenuating effect (proportion of reflection and transmission -- critical link from the breakwater optimization through coupled aero-hydrodynamics in subtask 5.2.3)

- Dynamic behaviour

- Internal load carrying capacity

- Force characteristics and transmission to couplings

To get the most benefit from the system versus the costs, the PV area should be as large as possible. Therefore, all studies are intended to maximize this area.

The task directly interacts with the necessary anchoring & mooring (T3.3), with the required material properties and design of breakwater shell and inner core (T4.1 and T4.2) and with the wave loading and overtopping (T5.2).

# Input

## Wave conditions

The wave conditions used in the design of the breakwater are specified in deliverable \"D2.1 -- Use case scenario basis for typical, rough location\" with the following additional assumptions:

The upper boundary of the significant wave height for a certain wave peak period is either

- The steepness criteria according to DNV-RP-C205 \[1\] or

- The 50 years contour line, if the steepness criteria line exceeds the contour line.

Approximately at a wave peak period of $T_{p} = 7\ s$, the steepness criteria line exceeds the 50 years contour line.

Accordingly, peak period to wave height combinations are chosen for further investigation.

#### Steepness criteria

The DNV-RP-C205 report \[1\] defines the steepness criteria with $S_{p} = 1/15\$for $T_{p} \leq 8\ s$, with $S_{p} = 1/25$ for $T_{p} \geq 15\ s$, and a linear interpolation in between. The significant wave height is subject to the wave peak period as per the following equation \[1\]:

  ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  $$H_{s} = \frac{S_{p} \bullet g \bullet T_{p}^{2}}{2\ \pi} = \frac{g \bullet T_{p}^{2}}{30\ \pi}$$   (2‑1)
  ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- -------

  ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Using this equation, wave peak period to wave height combinations are set with intervals of 0.5s (see [Table 1](#_Ref142296961)). #### Deep water conditions

It is assumed that there is sufficient water depth at all locations to allow deep-water conditions to be applied. Any influence of the seabed on the waves is not considered. Therefore, the wavelengths are calculated with the following equation for deep-water conditions:

  ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
  $$L = \frac{g}{\omega} \bullet T = \frac{g}{2\pi} \bullet T^{2}$$   (2‑2)
  ------------------------------------------------------------------- -------

  ---------------------------------------------------------------------------

With:

  --------------------------------------------------------------------------
  L      ...   Wave length (m)
  ------ ----- -------------------------------------------------------------
  g      ...   Acceleration of gravity (m/s²)

  T      ...   Wave period (s)
  --------------------------------------------------------------------------

#### Translation of peak period to wave height combinations into JONSWAP sea state spectrum

In order to characterize the waves as realistically as possible, the peak period to wave height combinations are translated into a sea state spectrum according to JONSWAP. As in the North Sea the harshest conditions are expected, the mean value for the peak enhancement factor is used with $\gamma = 3,3$ \[3\]. Correlating wind speeds and fetch lengths are to be determined which are used as input variables for the breakwater simulations. In deliverable D2.2, these parameters were calculated with the following equations:

  ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  $$\frac{g \bullet H_{s}}{U^{2}} = 1,6 \bullet 10^{- 3} \bullet \left( \frac{g \bullet x}{U^{2}} \right)^{\frac{1}{2}}$$    (2‑3)
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- -------
  $$\frac{g \bullet T_{p}}{U} = 0,2857 \bullet \left( \frac{g \bullet x}{U^{2}} \right)^{\frac{1}{3}}$$                      (2‑4)

  ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

With:

  ------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  $$H_{s}$$   ...   Significant wave height (m)
  ----------- ----- ------------------------------------------------------------
  $$U$$       ...   Wind speed in 10 m height (m/s) 🡪 $U = U_{10}$

  x           ...   Fetch length (m)

  g           ...   Acceleration of gravity (m/s²)
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------------

However, these equations are not recommended by Bishop \[4\], as they can lead to highly overestimated wave parameters. If the wind speed $U_{10}$ is used, the equations correspond to those in the publication by Hasselmann \[5\]. The fetchlength Values can also be taken from [Figure 6](#_Ref140759064).

## Material properties

### Conventional construction with polystyrene and concrete

The conventional and widely used construction method for floating concrete pontoons and breakwaters comprises an outer shell of reinforced concrete and a core of expanded polystyrene: EPS (see [Figure 7](#_Ref142297404), [Figure 8](#_Ref142297405), and [Figure 9](#_Ref142297407)). The buoyancy is realized by the polystyrene, while the concrete shell protects the core from mechanical impacts and UV radiation. EPS is watertight therefore a concrete bottom slab is not required.

The following material properties are considered for conventional constructions:

- Density of reinforced concrete $\sim\ 2,5\ t/m³$

- Density of expanded polystyrene $\sim\ 15\ kg/m³$

- Water absorption of expanded polystyrene \< 2,0 Vol.-%

The development of new circular materials runs parallel to this work package in WP4. In the first design iteration, conventional construction materials (reinforced concrete and expanded polystyrene) have been utilized. To enhance load transfer and distribution, transverse walls have been incorporated within the FB, and the edges of the polystyrene core have been chamfered. (see [Figure 7](#_Ref142297404), [Figure 8](#_Ref142297405), and [Figure 9](#_Ref142297407)).

### Construction with a new circular material solution

Preliminary new circular materials for the outer shell in Task T4.1 and for the core in Task 4.2 have been proposed. Since the tasks are not finished yet, preliminary results from Task 4.1 and Task 4.2 are used with a forecast of expected final results.

The following material properties are considered for new circular materials:

- Density of reinforced light weight concrete $\sim\ 1,9\ t/m³$

- Density of light weight concrete $\sim\ 300\ kg/m³$

- Water absorption of light weight concrete \> 20,0 Vol.-%

The light weight concrete\'s (LWC) water absorption capacity would lead to negative floatability. To counteract this, an additional bottom slab is utilized to prevent water from being absorbed into the core (see [Figure 10](#_Ref142298009), [Figure 11](#_Ref142298010), and [Figure 12](#_Ref142298011))

### Weight limits as a boundary for FB dimensions

The FB is to consist of pontoons of modular design. To ensure that these can be manufactured without relatively high machinery costs, their weight is to be limited. The upper weight limit used in the analysis is set to approx. 120 tons as this weight can still be handled by mobile cranes. Some models with higher weights have been simulated for comparison only.

## Geometries

With reference to deliverable D2.2, chapter 3.2.3 \[7\]. As described, the transmission is influenced by the following items:

- Incident waves (significant height, period, length)

- FB geometry (width, draft, shape)

- FB mechanical properties (centre of gravity, total mass, moment of inertia)

- FB mooring properties (mooring spring stiffness, mooring damping)

The wave parameters are defined in Chapter [2.1](#wave-conditions). The mechanical properties are given automatically by the construction method with the outer shell and inner core. Similar conditions are considered for all different cross sections. The mooring is initially neglected to be able to determine the actual influence of the breakwater cross section.

Under consideration of these boundary conditions, the effect of the width-draft-ratio and of the shape (cross section) can be directly derived or evaluated. In principle, it needs to be confirmed that the cross section of the conventional design (FB with two keels) is more effective in terms of transmission than other cross sections. In addition, the most effective width-draft ratio was to be determined, considering the conditions of Chapter [2.2.3](#weight-limits-as-a-boundary-for-fb-dimensions).

# Simulations with FLOW-3D HYDRO

## General

The evaluation of FB efficiency with regards to transmission was done with simulation software FLOW-3D Hydro. This software is a complete CFD modelling solution for the civil and environmental engineering industry. FLOW-3D Hydro is an ideal tool to analyse the performance of complex wave and structure interactions such as hydrodynamic loading on coastal infrastructure, wave transmission, overtopping, and floating structure dynamics. \[8\]

## Set-up

The following conditions are used as simulation input:

- 600 s simulation duration with step size of 0.1 s

- Active physics models:

  - Gravity and non-inertial

  - Moving objects

  - Turbulence and viscosity

## Approach to evaluate results

The evaluation of FB efficiency in terms of transmission are assessed with the ratio between wave generation (boundary, X-Min, fluid elevation) and free surface elevation of the history probe behind the FB -- see diagram 1 and 2 in [Figure 31](#_Ref141090165).

The volume of the fluid should remain constant over the simulation period. But due to program internal simulation inaccuracies, the fluid volume and thus also the elevation of the water level fluctuates (diagram 3 in [Figure 31](#_Ref141090165)). Since the history probe reports the free surface elevation in relation to the water level, the elevations need to be adjusted to account for the deviation between the fluid volume at the respective time against the fluid volume at the simulation start.

![[]{#_Ref141090165 .anchor}Figure 31 - Example of FLOW-3D Hydro results printout](../images/2023-08-08_surewave_d3.2_floating_breakwater_design/image36.png){width="6.686805555555556in" height="6.686805555555556in"}

The water level at a certain point in time of the simulation can be derived from the following equation:

  ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  $${WL}_{raw}(t) = \frac{\left( V_{Fluid,raw}(t) - V_{FB,draft} - V_{End\ wall} \right)}{\left( X_{Max} - X_{Min} \right) \bullet \left( Y_{Max} - Y_{Min} \right)}$$   (3‑1)
  ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- -------

  ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

With:

  --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  $${WL}_{raw}(t)$$      ...   Water level (raw data) at a certain time of the simulation (m)
  ---------------------- ----- ---------------------------------------------------------------------
  $$V_{Fluid,raw}(t)$$   ...   Volume of fluid (raw data) at a certain time of the simulation (m³)

  $$V_{FB,draft}$$       ...   Volume of the FB below the water line (m³)

  $$V_{End\ wall}$$      ...   Volume of the wall at boundary mesh X-Max (m³)

  $$X_{Max}$$            ...   Max. position of boundary mesh in X-direction (m)

  $$X_{Min}$$            ...   Min. position of boundary mesh in X-direction (m)

  $$Y_{Max}$$            ...   Max. position of boundary mesh in Y-direction (m)

  $$Y_{Min}$$            ...   Min. position of boundary mesh in Y-direction (m)
  --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The following [Figure 32](#_Ref141102927) shows the surface elevation of the history probe against the water level without adjustment.

![[]{#_Ref141102927 .anchor}Figure 32 - Surface elevation of history probe against water level](../images/2023-08-08_surewave_d3.2_floating_breakwater_design/image37.png){width="6.69375in" height="3.147222222222222in"}

The water level can be aligned with the following equation:

  -----------------------------------------------------------------------
  $${WL}_{aligned}(t) = mean\left( {WL}_{raw}(T_{p}) \right)$$    (3‑2)
  --------------------------------------------------------------- -------

  -----------------------------------------------------------------------

With:

  ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  $${WL}_{aligned}(t)$$                      ...   Aligned water level at a certain time of the simulation (m)
  ------------------------------------------ ----- -----------------------------------------------------------------------
  $$mean\left( {WL}_{raw}(T_{p}) \right)$$   ...   Mean value of water levels (raw data) over a period = wave period (m)

  ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

![Figure 33 - Surface elevation of history probe against aligned water level](../images/2023-08-08_surewave_d3.2_floating_breakwater_design/image38.png){width="6.69375in" height="3.147222222222222in"}

The adjusted surface elevation of the history probe can be determined with the following equation:

  -------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  $${FSE}_{adjust}(t) = {FSE}_{raw}(t) - {WL}_{aligned}(t) + {WL}_{0}$$   (3‑3)
  ----------------------------------------------------------------------- -------

  -------------------------------------------------------------------------------

With:

  ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  $${FSE}_{adjust}(t)$$   ...   Adjusted free surface elevation of the history probe at a certain time of the simulation (m)
  ----------------------- ----- ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  $${FSE}_{raw}(t)$$      ...   Free surface elevation (raw data) at a certain time of simulation (m)

  $${WL}_{aligned}(t)$$   ...   The aligned water level at a certain time of the simulation (m)

  $${WL}_{0}$$            ...   The water level at the start of simulation (m)
  ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The adjusted free surface elevation at the history probe is shown against the adjusted water level in [Figure 34](#_Ref141104857).

![[]{#_Ref141104857 .anchor}Figure 34 - Adjusted surface elevation at history probe against the adjusted water level](../images/2023-08-08_surewave_d3.2_floating_breakwater_design/image39.png){width="6.6875in" height="3.1534722222222222in"}

The incident surface elevation at the wave generation (boundary X-Min) is not related to the water level. Therefore, the incident surface elevation can be compared directly with the adjusted surface elevation of the history probe.

The comparison is shown in following [Figure 35](#_Ref141105344).

![[]{#_Ref141105344 .anchor}Figure 35 - Adjusted surface elevation at history probe against the surface elevation at wave generation](../images/2023-08-08_surewave_d3.2_floating_breakwater_design/image40.png){width="6.69375in" height="3.147222222222222in"}

# Theoretical approaches to determine transmission

## General

There are many publications on numerical and experimental investigations of floating breakwaters of various shapes and sizes. To be able to predict the efficiency of floating breakwaters for specific sea states, many predictive formulas from test results have been developed. In the following chapters, the best-known approaches are presented as a comparison to the results of FLOW-3D Hydro.

## Formula of MACAGNO

Macagno \[9\] proposed a formula for transmission prediction for a box-type FB with the following conditions:

- rigid structure

- fixed structure

- no overtopping

- linear wave theory

Since the theoretical approach of a rigid, fixed body is less complicated, the influence of FB motion was neglected by Macagno, which might be sufficient for many cases but will not represent the realistic behaviour of the FB and the transmitted wave \[10\].

The transmission coefficient according to Macagno is determined with the following formula \[9\]:

  ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  $$C_{t,M} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{1 + \left\lbrack k \bullet W_{FB} \bullet \frac{\sinh{k \bullet h}}{2 \bullet \cosh(k \bullet h - k \bullet d)} \right\rbrack^{2}}}$$   []{#_Ref140486622 .anchor}(4‑1)
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ ---------------------------------

  ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

With:

  ------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  $$C_{t,M}$$   ...   Transmission coefficient according to Macagno \[9\]
  ------------- ----- ----------------------------------------------------------
  $$k$$         ...   Wave number (rad/m) $k = \frac{2 \bullet \pi}{L}$

  $$W_{FB}$$    ...   Width of FB (m)

  $$h$$         ...   Water depth (m)

  $$d$$         ...   Draft of FB (m)
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The results of the Macagno equation for the chosen FB are shown in [Figure 43](#_Ref141858136).

## Extension of MACAGNO formula

Equation [(4‑1)](#_Ref140486622) provides an estimate of the anticipated wave transmission without accounting for dynamic aspects, which is good enough at this stage. However, for more comprehensive analyses, it is imperative to incorporate dynamic effects, such as the impact of the natural period of FB oscillation and the influence of FB motion \[10\].

Ruol, Martinelli, and Pezzutto \[11\] have proposed a correction of Macagno's formula accounting for dynamic effects and a Π-shaped FB.

  -----------------------------------------------------------------------
  $$C_{t} = \beta(\chi) \bullet C_{t,M}$$                         (4‑2)
  --------------------------------------------------------------- -------

  -----------------------------------------------------------------------

With:

  -----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  $$C_{t,M}$$       ...   Transmission coefficient according to Macagno \[9\]
  ----------------- ----- -----------------------------------------------------------------
  $$C_{t}$$         ...   Transmission coefficient according to Ruol et. al \[11\]

  $$\beta(\chi)$$   ...   Correction function (-) as per equation [(4‑5)](#_Ref140497039)
  -----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Macagno's formula [(4‑1)](#_Ref140486622) is based on four variables. Ruol, Martinelli, and Pezzutto \[11\] introduce a combination of these variables, indicated by $\chi$, which is shown as a simplification of $\frac{T_{p}}{T_{h}}$.

  -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  $$\chi \approx \frac{T_{p}}{T_{h}} \approx \frac{T_{p}}{2 \bullet \pi} \bullet \sqrt{\frac{g}{d + 0,35 \bullet W_{FB}}}$$   []{#_Ref140496408 .anchor}(4‑3)
  --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- ---------------------------------

  -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

With:

  -----------------------------------------------------------------------------
  $$T_{p}$$    ...   Wave peak period (s)
  ------------ ----- ----------------------------------------------------------
  $$T_{h}$$    ...   Natural period of heave oscillation (s)

  $$d$$        ...   Draft of FB (m)

  $$W_{FB}$$   ...   Width of FB (m)
  -----------------------------------------------------------------------------

Since Macagno's formula refers to regular waves, it is recommended to replace the peak period $T_{p}$ by the mean period $T_{m}$, assuming $T_{m} \cong \frac{T_{p}}{1.1}$ \[11\]. Accordingly, equation [(4‑3)](#_Ref140496408) yields to:

  -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  $$\chi \approx \frac{T_{p}}{1.1 \bullet T_{h}} \approx \frac{T_{p}}{2.2 \bullet \pi} \bullet \sqrt{\frac{g}{d + 0,35 \bullet W_{FB}}}$$   (4‑4)
  ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- -------

  -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

To fit the ratio between experimental and numerical transmission coefficient, a correction function $\beta$ is proposed by the authors \[11\] as a function of $\chi$, considering the boundary conditions for very long and very short waves resulting in $\beta = 1$ for $\chi > 1,3$ and $\chi < 0,3$.

  ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  $$\beta(\chi) = \frac{1}{1 + \left( \frac{\chi - \chi_{0}}{\sigma} \right) \bullet e^{- \left( \frac{\chi - \chi_{0}}{\sigma} \right)^{2}}}$$   []{#_Ref140497039 .anchor}(4‑5)
  ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- ---------------------------------

  ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

where $\chi_{0} = 0,7919$ (with 95% confidence interval 0,7801; 0,8037) and $\sigma = 0,1922$ (0,1741; 0,2103).

The validation of the Ruol, Martinelli, and Pezzutto model is based on test results of wave transmission published in the studies of Martinelli et al. \[12\], Gesraha \[13\], Koutandos, et al. \[14\], Cox et al. \[15\], and Peña et al. \[16\].

## Formula of WIEGEL

A linear theory was developed by Wiegel \[17\] to predict the wave heights transmitted under a rigid, vertical, thin barrier extending from above the water line to a depth. Wiegel postulated that the power transmitted by a wave between the bottom of the vertical barrier and the seabed would remain unchanged if the structure were absent in that location. \[10\].

Wiegel established the following equation:

  ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  $$C_{t,W} = \sqrt{\frac{2k \bullet (h - d) + \sinh{2k \bullet (h - d)}}{\left( \sinh{2kh} \right) + 2kh}}$$   (4‑6)
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- -------

  ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

With:

  --------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  $$C_{t,W}$$   ...   Transmission coefficient according to Wiegel \[17\]
  ------------- ----- ------------------------------------------------------------
  $$k$$         ...   Wave number (rad/m) $k = \frac{2 \bullet \pi}{L}$

  $$h$$         ...   Water depth (m)

  $$d$$         ...   Draft of FB (m)
  --------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Kriebel and Bollmann \[18\] developed the modified power transmission theory based on the theory of Wiegel and they included the effect of partial wave reflection.

The following equation was developed:

  -----------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  $$C_{t,W,adjust} = \frac{2 \bullet C_{t,W}}{\left( 1 + C_{t,W} \right)}$$   (4‑7)
  --------------------------------------------------------------------------- -------

  -----------------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Approach of COX

Cox \[19\] developed a theoretical relationship to estimate the performance theory for a single floating wave attenuator. The following equations shall describe this relationship \[20\]:

  -----------------------------------------------------------------------
  $$C_{t,C} = C_{b} \bullet K_{t}$$                               (4‑8)
  --------------------------------------------------------------- -------

  -----------------------------------------------------------------------

With:

  ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  $$C_{t,W}$$   ...   Transmission coefficient according to Cox \[19\]
  ------------- ----- --------------------------------------------------------------
  $$C_{b}$$     ...   Float width correction factor

  $$K_{t}$$     ...   Wave power transmission coefficient for a thin profile float
  ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The float width correction factor can be calculated as follows:

  ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  $$C_{b} = \frac{2\omega\left( 1 + \left( \frac{2\omega W_{FB}}{L} \right)^{2} \right)}{\left\lbrack 2 + \left( \frac{2\omega W_{FB}}{L} \right)^{2} \right\rbrack}$$   (4‑9)
  ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- -------

  ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

With:

  --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  $$\omega = 2\pi/T$$   ...   Circular wave frequency (rad/s)
  --------------------- ----- ----------------------------------------------------------
  $$W_{FB}$$            ...   Width of FB (m)

  $$L$$                 ...   Wave length (m)
  --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The wave power transmission coefficient for a thin profile float yields to:

  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  $$K_{t} = \frac{2 \bullet P}{(1 + P)}$$                         (4‑10)
  --------------------------------------------------------------- --------

  ------------------------------------------------------------------------

With:

  --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  $$P = \frac{\left\lbrack \frac{4\pi \bullet (h - d)}{L} + \sinh\left( \frac{4\pi \bullet (h - d)}{L} \right) \right\rbrack}{\left\lbrack \frac{4\pi \bullet h}{L} + \sinh\left( \frac{4\pi \bullet h}{L} \right) \right\rbrack}$$   (4‑11)
  ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- --------

  --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
  $$h$$   ...   Water depth (m)
  ------- ----- -------------------------------------------------------------
  $$d$$   ...   Maximum draft of attenuator (m)

  $$L$$   ...   Wave length (m)
  ---------------------------------------------------------------------------

## FOUSERT / VAN TOL Correlation for dynamic behaviour

### General

The publications of Fousert \[21\] and van Tol \[22\] investigate the dynamic behaviour of the FB and their effects on the wave generation behind the FB on the lee side. They examine FB\'s potential movements separately and their impact on leeward waves.

A free-floating body is subject to six degrees of motion with reference to its centre of gravity.

![Figure 46 - Degrees of freedom for a floating body](../images/2023-08-08_surewave_d3.2_floating_breakwater_design/image48.png){width="6.659410542432196in" height="4.461805555555555in"}

Assuming very long floating breakwaters, surge, pitch, and yaw are not that important and these can be neglected. In the present report, sway, heave, and roll are only considered.

The equations of motion representing a harmonic oscillation are as follows:

  -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  Sway (2)    $$y = y_{a} \bullet \cos\left( \omega \bullet t + \epsilon_{y\zeta} \right)$$             (4‑12)
  ----------- ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- --------
  Heave (3)   $$z = z_{a} \bullet \cos\left( \omega \bullet t + \epsilon_{z\zeta} \right)$$             (4‑13)

  Roll (4)    $$\phi = \phi_{a} \bullet \cos\left( \omega \bullet t + \epsilon_{\phi\zeta} \right)$$    (4‑14)
  -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

With:

  ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  $$\epsilon_{n\zeta}$$   ...   Phase angle
  ----------------------- ----- ----------------------------------------------------------
  $$\omega = 2\pi/T$$     ...   Circular wave frequency (rad/s)

  $$n_{a}$$               ...   Motion amplitude
  ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

There is an interaction of the FB with the surrounding water and additional conditions need to be defined to determine the interference of the FB with the water particles. Fousert \[21\] describes these conditions as follows:

- The *continuity or Laplace equation* hypothesizes the incompressibility of the fluid.

- The seabed boundary condition assumes that there is no exchange of vertical energy at the seabed, making the vertical velocity of water particles at the seabed zero. In deep-water conditions, waves are not affected by bottom friction.

- The *free surface dynamic boundary condition* assumes the pressure at the free water surface ($z = \zeta$) to be equal to the atmospheric pressure.

- The *free surface kinematic boundary condition* specifies the vertical velocity of a water particle at the free surface of the fluid to be identical to the vertical velocity of the fluid itself.

- The *kinematic boundary condition of the oscillating body surface* describes the velocity of a water particle at a point at the surface of the body to be equivalent to the velocity of this (watertight) body point itself.

- The *radiation condition* states that at the distance, *R*, from the oscillating body becomes large; the potential value tends to zero.

- The *symmetric or anti-symmetric condition* explains that the potential equations can be simplified when the FB has a symmetric centre line.

The FB cannot be considered as a mechanic mass-spring system, since the surrounding water affects the total mass, the spring stiffness, and the damping of the system.

The hydrodynamic added mass and damping parameters are not constant because they depend on the motion amplitude and the frequency.

The natural frequency of a hydrodynamic system can generally be described by the following equation:

  --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  $$\omega_{n} = \sqrt{\frac{k_{m} + c_{nn}}{m + a_{nn}}}$$       []{#_Ref129076687 .anchor}(4‑15)
  --------------------------------------------------------------- ----------------------------------

  --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

With:

  -----------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  $$\omega_{n}$$   ...   Directional natural frequency (1/s)
  ---------------- ----- ------------------------------------------------------------
  $$k_{m}$$        ...   Spring stiffness of the mooring line (kg/s² or N/m)

  $$c_{nn}$$       ...   Hydrodynamic spring stiffness (kg/s² or N/m)

  $$m$$            ...   Structural mass (kg)

  $$a_{nn}$$       ...   Hydrodynamic added mass (kg)
  -----------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The FB in undamped conditions is subject to resonance when the exciting wave frequency is close to the natural frequency of the system.

The resonance peak can be changed by changing the mass or the spring stiffness of the system because these changes directly modify the natural frequency.

- The increased mass of the system 🡪 resonance peak shifts to lower frequencies

- Increased spring stiffness 🡪 resonance peak shifts to higher frequencies

### Sway motion

Sway is the horizontal motion due to incoming waves with horizontal components perpendicular to the longitudinal axis of the FB. Sway will generate a wave that is radiated to the leeward side as well as to the seawards \[21\].

The following parameters apply to sway motion equations:

  -----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
       $$\zeta_{a}$$      ...   Incoming wave amplitude at the seaside of the FB (m)
  ----------------------- ----- -----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
         $$y_{a}$$        ...   Displacement in the y-direction (m)

           $$k$$          ...   Wave number (rad/m)

        $$\omega$$        ...   Circular wave frequency (rad/s)

           $$d$$          ...   Draft of FB including keels (m)

         $$d_{1}$$        ...   Draft of FB buoyancy body excluding keels (m)

        $$L_{FB}$$        ...   Length of the FB (m)

        $$W_{FB}$$        ...   Width of the FB (m)

           $$m$$          ...   Structural mass of FB (kg) 🡪 $m = \rho_{w} \bullet L_{FB} \bullet W_{FB} \bullet d_{1}$

         $$A_{s}$$        ...   Water plane area of FB section (m²) 🡪 $A_{s} = L_{FB} \bullet W_{FB}$

         $$A_{w}$$        ...   Water displacement area of the FB (m³) 🡪 $A_{w} = L_{FB} \bullet W_{FB} \bullet d_{1}$

        $$a_{22}$$        ...   Hydrodynamic added mass for sway motion (Ns²/m = kg)

        $$b_{22}$$        ...   Hydrodynamic sway damping (Ns/m = kg/s)

        $$c_{22}$$        ...   Hydrodynamic spring stiffness in horizontal direction (kg/s² or N/m)

        $$k_{my}$$        ...   Mooring spring stiffness in horizontal direction (kg/s² or N/m)

        $$d_{my}$$        ...   Mooring damping in the horizontal direction (Ns/m = kg/s)

        $$\alpha$$        ...   Mooring line angle

   $$\epsilon_{y\zeta}$$  ...   Phase shift of sway motion (deg.)

         $$\rho$$         ...   Density of water (kg/m³)

  -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- --------

  -----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The ***amplitude*** ***of the sway motion*** can be described as follows:

  -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- --------

  -----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

and depending on the point of time:

+=====================================================================================================================================================================================================================================================================================================================================================================================================+========+

The ***phase shift*** can be determined with following equation:

  -----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

with $0 \leq \epsilon_{y\zeta} \leq 2\pi$

  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  $$\omega_{n,2} = \sqrt{\frac{k_{my} + c_{22}}{m + a_{22}}}$$    (4‑20)
  --------------------------------------------------------------- --------

  ------------------------------------------------------------------------

The mooring spring stiffness is mainly governed by the type of the mooring line. For a prestressed mooring line, the horizontal portion of the mooring force can be determined by the cosine of the mooring line angle α:

  ------------------------------------------------------------------------

  --------------------------------------------------------------- --------

  ------------------------------------------------------------------------

### Heave motion

Heave is the vertical motion due to incoming waves acting on the floating body. Heave will generate a wave that is radiated to the leeward side as well as to the seawards. The leeward-sided wave has a phase shift related to the incoming wave. This phase shift is subject to the structural dimensions, the wave frequency, and the mooring stiffness \[21\].

The following parameters apply to heave motion equations:

  ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
       $$\zeta_{a}$$      ...   Incoming wave amplitude at the seaside of the FB (m)
  ----------------------- ----- ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
         $$z_{a}$$        ...   Displacement in the z-direction (m)

           $$k$$          ...   Wave number (rad/m)

        $$\omega$$        ...   Circular wave frequency (rad/s)

           $$d$$          ...   Draft of FB including keels (m)

         $$d_{1}$$        ...   Draft of FB buoyancy body excluding keels (m)

         $$d_{2}$$        ...   Depth of the keels below the buoyancy body (m)

        $$L_{FB}$$        ...   Length of the FB (m)

        $$W_{FB}$$        ...   Width of the FB (m)

           $$m$$          ...   Structural mass of FB (kg) 🡪 $m = \rho_{w} \bullet L_{FB} \bullet W_{FB} \bullet d_{1}$

         $$A_{s}$$        ...   Water plane area of FB section (m²) 🡪 $A_{s} = L_{FB} \bullet W_{FB}$

         $$A_{w}$$        ...   Water displacement area of the FB (m³) 🡪 $A_{w} = L_{FB} \bullet W_{FB} \bullet d_{1}$

        $$a_{33}$$        ...   Hydrodynamic added mass for heave motion (Ns²/m = kg)

        $$b_{33}$$        ...   Hydrodynamic heave damping (Ns/m = kg/s)

        $$c_{33}$$        ...   Hydrodynamic spring coefficient in vertical direction (kg/s² or N/m) 🡪 $c_{33} = \rho \bullet g \bullet A_{s}$

         $$C_{3}$$        ...   Vertical wave pressure distribution coefficient 🡪 $C_{3} = 1 - \left( \frac{k}{W_{FB}} \right) \bullet e^{- kd_{1}}$

        $$k_{mz}$$        ...   Mooring spring stiffness in vertical direction (kg/s² or N/m) 🡪 $k_{mz} = k_{m} \bullet \sin\alpha$

        $$d_{mz}$$        ...   Mooring damping in the vertical direction (Ns/m = kg/s) 🡪 $d_{mz} = d_{m} \bullet \sin\alpha$

        $$\alpha$$        ...   Mooring line angle

   $$\epsilon_{z\zeta}$$  ...   Phase shift of sway motion (deg.)
  ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

#### Heave motion equations

The following equation describes the ***amplitude of the vertical wave force***:

  -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  $$F_{a,z} = \zeta_{a} \bullet e^{- kd_{1}} \bullet \sqrt{\left( c_{33} \bullet C_{3} - a_{33} \bullet \omega^{2} \right)^{2} + \left( b_{33} \bullet \omega \right)^{2}}$$   (4‑28)
  ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- --------

  -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ --------

  ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

and depending on the point of time:

+----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------+--------+
| $z(t) = z_{a} \bullet \sin\left( \omega t + \epsilon_{z\zeta} \right)$                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                             | (4‑30) |
|                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                    |        |
| $$z(t) = \zeta_{a} \bullet \left( e^{- kd_{1}} \right) \bullet \sqrt{\frac{\left( c_{33} \bullet C_{3} - a_{33} \bullet \omega^{2} \right)^{2} + \left( b_{33} \bullet \omega \right)^{2}}{\left\lbrack \left( k_{mz} + c_{33} \right) - \left( m + a_{33} \right) \bullet \omega^{2} \right\rbrack^{2} + \left\lbrack \left( d_{mz} + b_{33} \right) \bullet \omega \right\rbrack^{2}}} \bullet \sin\left( \omega t + \epsilon_{z\zeta} \right)$$ |        |
+====================================================================================================================================================================================================================================================================================================================================================================================================================================================+========+

The ***phase shift*** can be determined with the following equation:

  ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  $$\epsilon_{z\zeta} = \arctan\left( \frac{- m \bullet b_{33} \bullet \omega^{3}}{\left( c_{33}C_{3} - a_{33}\omega^{2} \right) \bullet \left\lbrack \left( k_{mz} + c_{33} \right) - \left( m + a_{33} \right)\omega^{2} + \left\lbrack \left( d_{mz} + b_{33} \right)\omega \right\rbrack^{2} \right\rbrack} \right)$$   (4‑31)
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- --------

  ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

with $0 \leq \epsilon_{y\zeta} \leq 2\pi$

The ***heave natural frequency*** can be considered as a two-dimensional problem with a breakwater length of $L_{FB} = 1\ m$. The natural frequency of a heave system can be derived from the general term []{.mark}[(4‑15)](#_Ref129076687) with the following equation:

  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  $$\omega_{n,3} = \sqrt{\frac{k_{mz} + c_{33}}{m + a_{33}}}$$    (4‑32)
  --------------------------------------------------------------- --------

  ------------------------------------------------------------------------

  --------------------------------------------------------------- --------

  ------------------------------------------------------------------------

# Summary

This report investigated the efficiency of floating breakwaters in terms of wave transmission. This has been carried out for different types/cross sections of breakwaters and with different approaches. Theoretical approaches of existing publications by different authors were compared with the results of numerical simulations. Although there are variations between the results from the different approaches, they tend to show similar results.

The main conclusions of the investigations are as follows:

- There is no generally applicable approach to calculate the wave transmission of floating breakwaters. The existing approaches from the literature are always based, at least partially, on the results of numerical simulations or physical model tests. Differences between the approaches are mainly due to different assumptions regarding the consideration of input variables such as degrees of freedom of motion, dynamic behaviour, or natural frequencies.

- The most detailed formulation was carried out by Fousert \[21\], because he considers the most input variables or parameters. However, in the high-frequency range, Macagno \[9\] including adjustments from Ruol, Martinelli, and Pezzutto \[11\] also delivers good results with a less complex calculation approach.

- Results of numerical simulations show that there are effective and less effective FB cross sections. If we take into account the FB\'s producibility and transportability, all cross sections have minimal to no impact within a specific wave period range (between 5 and 7 seconds). During larger wave periods, the FB essentially follows the motion of the waves.

Accordingly, the resulting anchoring forces are also lower (for Task T3.3). The results of T3.2 and T3.3 are the input for T3.4 and T5.2.

- The new circular material was considered with interim results of Tasks T4.1 and T4.2. The model needs to be rechecked or confirmed after the final results are available.

- The connections for joining the breakwater elements are not yet investigated. Especially in the case of oblique or longitudinal wave action, the connections must be capable of absorbing or transmitting the forces caused by movements (for Task T5.3).
