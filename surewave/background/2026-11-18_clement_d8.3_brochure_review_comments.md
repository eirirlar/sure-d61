# SUREWAVE D8.3 Technical Brochure — review comments from Clement Germany

Reviewer: Thomas Pehlke (Dipl.-Ing., structural engineer, Clement Germany GmbH). Date: 2026-11-18. Eleven margin comments on the September 2026 issue of the brochure. Figure numbers refer to that issue.

Covering emails, same day:

- First email: the comments focus mainly on the breakwater section. The graphics differ from the design as it was determined, and that is where most of the comments are.
- Second email (to the consortium): avoid technically questionable content; there is no need to show many measurements. Four points matter:
  1. The pontoon cross-section does not narrow towards the bottom.
  2. The anchorage is a slack catenary with long, shock-absorbing chains, not a taut line.
  3. The breakwaters have transverse interior walls; otherwise the forces would not be absorbed.
  4. The breakwaters have a deep draft and low freeboard.

  These reveal no secrets, because the information is already in the text. Seven CAD graphics were sent as input for Figures 3, 4, 7 and 9.

## Margin comments

| # | Location | Comment |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Fig 2, integrated-system cutaway | State that the illustration is a non-to-scale system illustration. |
| 2 | Fig 3, plant layout | Should be more precise. The outer pontoons are trapezoidal, the mooring lines are further away, and the diameter dimension should be placed correctly. See the drawings sent by email. |
| 3 | Section 2, site-class wave heights | 0.6 / 0.5 / 1.6 m are the median significant wave heights (50 % of sea states higher, 50 % lower), not the significant wave heights. This should be stated; otherwise it is unclear why the Mediterranean, with the lower value, is mid-condition. Fifty-year significant wave heights are 4.4 m, 7.0 m and 12.3 m (D2.1 p. 10). |
| 4 | Fig 4, three site classes | Generally good. The breakwater cross-section should be adjusted and the unit should float deeper. The anchoring looks taut; it should be a catenary with a pile anchor, not a concrete block. Correct the wave heights per comment 3. |
| 5 | Section 3, attenuation claim (">40 % of wave energy in the 1–3 s band") | The transmission coefficient is about 40 % in that range, so the wave attenuation is about 60 %. |
| 6 | Section 3, "Keels raise performance for longer wave periods without a proportional weight penalty" | Keels increase the draft without adversely affecting stability or metacentric height. |
| 7 | Fig 5, dimensioned three-view drawing | Wrong. Use the figures from D3.2 p. 12. |
| 8 | Section 3, chain diameters (54 / 95 / 95 mm) | Amended after D5.1. Nominal mild / mid / harsh = 90 / 66 / 70 mm. Including the 16 mm allowance: 107 / 87 / 87 mm, because of available chain sizes. |
| 9 | Section 3, "corrosion allowance sized for a 40-year design life, refreshed at mid-project chain replacement in the 50-year economic model" | Questions whether this should be mentioned. The 40 years is twice the originally assumed 20-year PV module life. A larger corrosion allowance would reach 50 years without replacement. |
| 10 | Fig 7, catenary mooring per site | Generally good, but the chain should look more like a catenary. One part of the chain always lies on the seabed; this is needed for the damping effect. |
| 11 | Fig 9, three-material cross-section | Resembles neither the full-scale unit nor the prototype. The catenary attachment point is on the outside, the connections to the transition element are on the inside, and in longitudinal section there are no attachment pontoons at the ends. |
