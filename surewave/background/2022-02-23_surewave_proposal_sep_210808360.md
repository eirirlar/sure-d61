Abstract *

SUREWAVE will develop and test an innovative concept of Floating Photo-Voltaic (FPV) system consisting of an external floating
breakwater structure acting as a protection against severe wave-wind-current loads on the FPV modules, allowing increased
operational availability and energy output, thus unlocking the massive deployment of Offshore FPV. It will be focused on the research
for securing optimal behaviour at aero & hydrodynamic and structural integrity level of the external breakwater, the internal FPV
modular structure, the connections, the mooring and anchoring and the whole FPV system, complying with mechanical, electrical
(maximizing energy output) and cost-efficiency requirements, ensuring high lifetime of critical components, high reliability of the
system and easy, quick and cost-efficient, construction, installation and O&M of the whole system.
This will require:
1) the development of novel circular concrete material solutions for the breakwater, cost-effective, easy to produce and with high
mechanical, physical and durability properties and low CO2 footprint.
2) the development of an advanced predictive computational modelling and simulation framework: coupled aero & hydrodynamic
modelling, Structural integrity modelling, material properties modelling and Structural Health Management to reduce CAPEX and
OPEX;
3) the design and implementation of an optimal testing and validation methodology for offshore FPV system to achieve TRL5,
including lab testing, basin-model testing and marine environment testing of critical components assuring high resilience to
corrosion and biofouling.
Maximum advantage of existing expertise will be taken from experts in: offshore wind floating structures, material and phenomena
modelling (SINTEF, MARIN, CEIT), design and installation of FPV in calm waters (SIS), floating solutions (CLEMENT), circular structural
and non-structural materials (ACC) and in social, environmental and economic assessment (IFEU).

1. EXCELLENCE
The European Offshore renewable energy strategy1 highlights the need for more established renewable energy
technologies, and to diversify the technology portfolio to take maximum advantage of the vast potential offered
by EU's seas to become climate neutral by 2050. However, not all offshore renewable technologies are in the same
stage of maturity. Floating Photo-Voltaic (FPV) is in the early R&D stage when it comes to open sea deployment
(Although already deployed in landlocked waters) and more development are needed. Experts have estimated that
the total worldwide capacity of FPV could reach 62 GW by 20302. The limited deployment of offshore FPV is caused
by the intrinsic characteristics of the marine environments (wind loads, wave loads, currents, corrosion, biofouling).
The specific design and structure of the FPV influence its output power generation, durability and investment cost3.
Thus, offshore FPV systems need to be studied in detail (At the design, components, materials and structural levels)
to ensure competitive offshore reliability, lifetime, easy installability and improved O&M, while considering
economic, environmental, and social barriers.

That is why SUREWAVE has seen a clear and huge opportunity in the research and development of a novel and
advanced offshore FPV solution able to unlock the massive offshore FPV deployment in the EU seas. It will take
maximum advantage of previous deep expertise accumulated from the offshore wind field to understand the
floating structures behaviour and how to model those (through SINTEF, MARIN, CEIT) combined with the high
knowledge of development and deployment of FPV in calm waters (SIS) together with floating solutions expertise
of CLEMENT and advanced structural and non-structural materials expertise of ACC. Technical expertise will be
complemented by environmental and socio-economic assessment (IFEU) to overcome non-technical barriers.

1.1.1 OBJECTIVES
The main objective of SUREWAVE is to develop and test an innovative concept of Floating Photo-Voltaic (FPV)
system consisting of an external floating breakwater structure made of new circular materials acting as a

environments with high wind (speed >25 m/s), current (>1.2 m/s) and wave (height >14 m), thus unlocking the
massive deployment of Offshore FPV at European and worldwide level.

SUREWAVE proposes to solve the main challenges of offshore FPV deployment in open sea by developing a solution

operational window and energy output while ensuring the structural integrity of the complete system in open sea.
Another advantage is that it will also minimise the future effort of adaptation of the internal FPV components and
intermodular connection systems, that won't need to be overengineered for increased survivability, thus
minimising future development costs of such systems.

PILLAR-1: R&D of the innovative breakwater concept for the Offshore FPV to be operated in harsh conditions.

The focus of first main pillar of SUREWAVE is to minimize the effect of the offshore extreme conditions of very high
wind (speed >25 m/s), current (>1.2 m/s) and wave (height >14 m) on the operability of the PV system. SUREWAVE
strategy is to enclose the fragile PV elements into a protective barrier, instead of over engineering the PV elements
itself. The external structural element's (protective barrier) main function is to protect the FPV modules by
efficiently minimizing and reducing the wave effect into the protected area. This will maximise the operational
window of the energy system and limited loads on the PV system will increase the lifespan of the system and
decrease the maintenance operations.

Moreover, limited loads on the PV system will increase the lifespan of the system and decrease the maintenance
operations. SUREWAVE's solutions for building this external protective barrier is to rely on the breakwater
technology (CLEMENT's main field of expertise) whose survivability is optimised for severe loads in terms of
dimensions, geometry, and materials. This solution has never been implemented in offshore scenario before. If the
idea of the concept is not to modify the modular PV modules itself which will be protected from the most severe
mechanical loads, improvements of the individual critical components of the internal FPV structure will be needed
in SUREWAVE: There is more specifically innovations needed in the design of mechanical joints between the
modules and the coupling between the breakwater line and the FPV modules for such a system to become
successful.

The SUREWAVE decision on basing the concept on the development of a protective novel offshore breakwater is
clearly justified considering the analysis of the offshore extreme conditions. It is estimated that the protection
from the breakwater will be maximised for wave periods distributed around 4 to 5s, which are the most detrimental
wave periods for the installation. Especially the high-frequency wave energy content is critical to the structural
integrity of the PV, as it may induce (i) high slamming loads on the PV through wave breaking and (ii) large
overturning motions of the PV, leading to large inter-connector loads as well as the risk of large wind lift loads when
the bottom of the PV panels emerges from the waterline.

Therefore, the floating breakwater will be designed to break or to attenuate the high-frequency wave energy to an
acceptable limit. It is however not the intention of this project to damp the longer waves period (swell waves).
SUREWAVE will rely on the high flexibility of the FPV system which will allow the structure to follow the largest
wave period. Moreover, the probability of occurrence of such wind and wave events is expected to be very low,
maybe once a year or even less frequently.

SUREWAVE approach to increase the durability of the FPV system and its integrity relies on a protective line of

modular conception makes it easy to be stacked into a common shipping container/ barge deck, enabling rapid
deployment, and covering large area on the water. One critical point of every modular design, including SIS
solutions are the flexible connections between the modules (Challenge 1) which will be specifically improved in

The design and dimensioning of the external breakwater including anchoring and mooring systems is key to the
concept (Pillar-1) and will thus be carried out to provide optimal wave damping frequency range to attenuate the
locally generated wind waves (WP3). The global shape of the breakwater perimeter (Circular, square or others as
described in Pillar-1 and shown in Figure 1-6), but also its geometry (optimal shape, see Figure 1-5), weight and
dimensions are design parameters to be optimised (Challenge 1). The Beaufort scale of wind speeds will be followed
with variable dimensions at real possible wave spectra in offshore areas, aiming to tackle: the wave breaking or
attenuating effect (proportion of reflection and transmission), the dynamic behaviour, the internal load carrying
capacity and force characteristics and transmission to couplings.

The anchoring and mooring systems will be dimensioned according to the definitive shape of the individual
breakwater modules and of the global perimetral shape of breakwater. The anchoring solution chosen will influence
the distribution of the loads between the breakwater, mooring and modules. Particular attention will be paid to
excessive forces on the mooring attachment points and snapping-forces on the mooring attachment points/lines

PHASE 3) Offshore FPV ­ TESTING AND VALIDATION

The third phase of SUREWAVE aims to mature the offshore FPV technology up to TRL5 using a double assessment
approach: Technical assessment and integral sustainability assessment:

The technical testing and validation of the SUREWAVE offshore FPV solution will be assessed through the
implementation of the WP6 of the work plan (Pillar-1 and 2). The main WP objective is to make the technical
validation of the global Floating PV solution developed in WP3, and the model tools developed in WP4 and WP5.
This Validation will include lab testing (through advanced hydrodynamic test bench facilities of MARIN and
structural laboratory at SINTEF) and sea-exposed validation of the concrete element for long term degradation and
biofouling assessment to mature the solution to a TRL5, considering harsh conditions. In addition, this technical
assessment aims to achieve and validate a robust and reliable simulation framework that will allow to optimize
the FPV solution thus having a better understanding of the device's real-life performance allowing a safe reduction
in the over-engineering of devices' specifications, lowering the cost at the time than reducing investment risks.

SUREWAVE will define and deploy an advanced sensing and monitoring solution to allow adequate
implementation of the basin tests and for the long-term validation. The monitoring system will enable to gather
data during the testing phase, which will be used to validate the models and SHMS developed in WP5. The
monitoring system will be capable of detecting failures on critical points of the structure so that the designer can
optimize the properties of the material and/or the geometry of the structure for future improved designs.

The critical mechanical connections will be assessed in SUREWAVE to ensure that the ultimate strength
performance of the identified critical mechanical joints (connections between two breakwater components,
connections between breakwater and solar modules, connections between solar modules) have sufficient
structural capacity. Furthermore, fatigue performance testing will be carried out to validate the fatigue life
expectancy considering the load amplitude values obtained from structural analysis tools developed in WP5.

The hydrodynamic performance of the combined external breakwater and internal FPV modules in both
operational and survival sea states (High frequency slamming loads and breaking waves) will be validated by means
of model tests in a wave basin at MARIN advanced facilities. The detailed description and dimensions along with
the test campaigns are given in WP6.

Finally, to complete the technical testing and validation phase, SUREWAVE will validate the long-term behaviour
of the concrete breakwater solution (Pillar-2) under normal operating condition (ACC test site) testing of
biofouling and corrosion of the rebars of the concrete breakwater prototype built within WP4 on which the
advanced sensing and structural health monitoring will be deployed to provide valuable data for the models and
methodologies developed in WP5 as well as insight on the behaviour of the technical solutions developed in WP3
and WP4.

In parallel an accurate integral sustainability assessment will be performed, through WP7, to solve the potential
non-technical barriers and facilitating a high-replicable deployment of the solution into the real offshore energy
world. This analysis is composed of 4 main approaches: Environmental, Economic, Social and integrated approach.

The first step will be the definition and implementation of the mass and energy balance considering materials used,
energy consumed for the whole life-cycle (production of components, pre-assemblies, transport, installation, O&M
and decommissioning) both for the SUREWAVE FPV solution as well as for a baseline system.

Then once available the mass-energy balances, to ensure the minimization of the environmental impact, a Life-
Cycle Assessment (LCA) of the developed solution assuming the potential full-scale future deployment into the
marine environment will be done, so that the ultimate objectives of the project for increased clean energy
generation, GHG emissions reduction, and minimization of product footprint in-land (production) and offshore
(installation, O&M, decommissioning) are duly accomplished.

Secondly, to achieve a high economic impact to ensure a feasible adaptation of the solution by the market, an
accurate methodology will be defined and implemented for calculating the LCoE of the SUREWAVE solution,
conceived to ensure replicability in different sea basins searching for economic feasibility not only the application
in harsh environments but also analysing routes of SUREWAVE easy adaptations for high replicability potential on
less demanding conditions (e.g. Mediterranean sea).

Then an integral social Life Cycle Assessment (sLCA) will be carried out to analyse social impacts of the researched
activities on a variety of stakeholders including competition and synergies with other sea activities (i.e. fishing, fish-
farms, maritime navigation, tourism-leisure) to identify critical risks early on ensure social acceptance.

Once the environmental, economic and social aspects have been duly analysed and assessed, a final step of
integrated sustainability assessment will be performed by integrating these 3 aspects through the use of a multi-

sea basins, allowing a safe reduction in the over-engineering of the offshore FPV devices´ specifications.

The SHSM platform will be developed to integrate the novel breakwater solution for offshore extreme conditions
with the internal FPV structures and considering different parametrizable mechanical connections (anchoring,
mooring, internal modules connection) towards an evaluation of the structural health integrity for all the use cases
based on AI. Specifically, this platform would provide a whole framework, which is able to test and validate the
customized models, and to size different components. This simulation platform will focus on compromises
between structural integrity, sustainability and cost-efficiency to optimise durability/cost performances.

* SUREWAVE Global Solution: A complete offshore FPV system to better operate in deep and high-demanding
waters, enabling the solar PV sector to maximize affordable solar energy in the oceans under a feasible, durable,
reliable and cost-effective solution. This solution will be marketed through SIS. Other partners will be compensated
with the payment of licenses/ royalties for the results that contribute to the solution. The large-scale product is
expected to be a plant about 50MWp, with a power peak of 150W/m2 (e.g. 578m square or 652m diameter).

A preliminary product evolution strategy is defined: 1) SUREWAVE R&D (mid-2022 to mid-2025); 2) 25MW pilot
demonstration offshore, hopefully under umbrella of HORIZON EUROPE (From 2025 to 2027); 3) Commercialization
& contracts´ awards (from 2026 and beyond); 4) 1st commercial products deployment (from 2028 to 2030); 5)
Multiple SUREWAVE solution operating (from 2032 and beyond).

The preliminary market approach that sets the exploitation scope of the project:

 Total Addressable Market (Total market for SUREWAVE) In the 1st commercialization phase (2030-2032), the
   consortium aims to commercialize the product in offshore PV plant projects for "mid-extreme conditions"
   locations in the North and Baltic seas (covering The Netherlands, Germany, Denmark, Sweden, Finland, Poland
   and less demanding sites of UK or Norway) and Atlantic (France, Portugal). In a 2nd commercialization phase
   SUREWAVE will reach by 2035 the specific extreme harsh conditions of the North Sea as well as Norwegian and
   Celtic seas as well as less demanding sea basins (e.g. Mediterranean) with SUREWAVE customized design.

 Serviceable Available Market (Target market for SUREWAVE) The product that SUREWAVE develops will be
   suitable both for extreme sea conditions with very high wind (speed >25 m/s), current (>1.2 m/s) and wave
   (height >14 m) loads and for less demanding marine conditions (Atlantic, Mediterranean, etc). Considering the
   worldwide of 62GW2 expectations by 2030 and considering that EU advances in a competitive position
   acquiring a 10% of the global market, the estimated serviceable market could be of 6.2 GW.

 Serviceable Obtainable Market (% of the target market) The market for the offshore FPV is quite uncertain
   with several large competitors with reasonable mature offerings. However, the market is still growing and offers
   room for innovative new commers. It is aimed to obtain a market share of 20%, meaning an installed power by
   year 2032 of 1,240 MW hence applying the product to 25 offshore FPV farms (considering SUREWAVE solution
   scaled-up to 50MW per farm with solar array surface of 3.33e6m2 and power peak of 150W/m2).

Task 4.1 Research and design of new circular material solutions for the structural shell of the breakwater

resistivity, corrosion potential (Ecorr), carbonation, biofouling, self-healing and abrasion resistance.

standard- and inhouse test methods, which allow to adapt the setup to specific requests. SINTEF has access to a

wide range of servo-hydraulic machines for testing depending on the load level expected, ranging from a few kN

(Dartec) to 1000kN (Schenk-Instron) as well as temperature chambers and a workshop which can machine any

type of specific test setup if required. It is also planned on using advanced strain measurements technique (DIC)

and cameras which will provide detailed information about the localised deformation of the joints.

Task 6.3: Critical components Fatigue performance (L: SINTEF; P: SIS, CEIT, CLEMENT; Duration: M20-M28):

Task 6.3 aims at investigating the Fatigue life expectancy of the critical connections defined in task 6.2. to this

end, fatigue analyses will be performed on the three mechanical joints. The load amplitude and failure modes for

the fatigue evaluation will be given by the structural analyses developed in WP5 task 5.3 and 5.4, But it is

anticipated that tension for different amplitudes will be the main failure mode and thus the main test

configuration (2 different load amplitudes are planned to be tested to give a rough SN curve for each connection).

Fatigue bending will also be envisaged for the breakwater modules connections (Made of rope) and the

connection between break water and solar modules depending on the connections design chosen in WP3. A

minimum of three repetitions should be made per connections assessed and test configurations (Leading to 18

fatigue tensions tests 12 bending tests). Fatigue tests will be performed using high frequency magnetic resonance

fatigue test machines available such as Amsler 50kN or Instron 100kN depending on the load amplitude targeted

and the design of the mechanical connections which will be developed in WP3.

Task 6.4: Basin system validation (L: MARIN; P: SIS, SINTEF, CEIT; Duration: M13-M26): The hydrodynamic

performance of the combined breakwater and PV modules in both operational and survival sea states is validated

by means of model tests in a wave basin at MARIN. The wave basin is 220 m

long, 4 m wide and has a 3.6 m water depth; the flap-type wave maker is

able to generate waves up to 0.7 m wave height. A segment of the

breakwater and PV field including interconnectors will be geometrically

reproduced at a model scale of approximately 1:20. The scale model will be

instrumented to measure motions, accelerations, mechanical joints loads,      FIGURE 3-3: QUASI 2D SETUP

and high-frequency slamming loads due to breaking wave impacts in operational & survival sea states. Figure

3-3 shows a quasi-2D setup of the model test in the wave basin with breakwater covering the width of the flume.

The basin test data will be used to tune and validate the hydrodynamic optimization in WP5 and to demonstrate
the TRL5 of the final PV field design. Two test campaigns are foreseen: 1) An initial concept of the combined
breakwater-PV configuration will be tested to measure wave overtopping the breakwater and their impacts onto
the PV field as essential data for the numerical model in T5.2; 2) At final stage, the fully developed FPV field
including breakwater and interconnectors will be reproduced and validated in operational and survival sea states.
Task 6.5: Long-term degradation of the concrete component exposed in harbor (L: ACC; P: CEIT, SINTEF,
CLEMENT; Duration: M20-M36): The task aims at validating the long-term behaviour of the concrete breakwater
solution developed in WP4. Two main mechanisms are responsible for long term degradation of the concrete in
marine environment: biofouling deposition and corrosion degradation of the rebars. Using sensing technology
developed in task 6.1, long term degradation will be assessed on a small-scale concrete breakwater prototype
(1m wide, 0.5m height, 2m length) at the R&D maritime environment demo site of ACC in Port of Gijón (Spain).
During this testing, the monitoring systems will provide valuable data for the models developed in WP5 as well
as insight on the behaviour of the overall technical solutions developed in WP3 and WP4.

An integrated sustainability assessment of selected scenarios on the developed floating PV solutions will be

performed taking into account technological, environmental, societal and economic sustainability aspects. This

Duration: M1-M24): This task defines goal and scope questions, general definitions, settings as well as qualitative

and quantitative system and scenario descriptions for T7.2 to T7.6, covering several scenarios to be assessed,

The quantitative system description will in particular contain complete mass and energy balances of the systems

M32): The technological assessment will evaluate technological indicators of the systems and reference systems

defined in task 7.1. Indicators such as maturity, circularity/suitability for a circular economy, durability and risks

for accidents will be selected and evaluated to capture technological aspects (mainly risks and barriers but also

positive drivers) that can affect sustainability. This will result in a conclusive evaluation of all technological

indicators that consists mainly of semi-quantitative qualitative information.

Task 7.3: Environmental Assessment (L: IFEU; P: SINTEF, SIS, ACC, CLEMENT; Duration: M13-M32): A particular

focus will be on how to reduce GHG emissions while increasing the amount of renewable electricity by tapping

into marine surface area as a resource extending the land surface that can be used for PV, thus contributing to

the objectives of the Mission Healthy oceans. Screening LCAs will follow the guidelines of ISO 14040/14044 on

product LCA and taking into account the ILCD Handbook, EC Recommendation C/2021/9932 on PEF and the DNSH

principle. Potential local/regional environmental impacts of the planned marine installations, which are not

covered by LCA, will additionally be analysed using elements of an environmental impact assessment (EIA).

Task 7.4: Economic assessment (L: SIS; P: SINTEF, ACC, CLEMENT, IFEU; Duration: M19-M32): The LCoE

(considering yield, expected degradation, up-time, O&M, decommissioning) will be calculated for the global

coupled FPV solution resulting from WP3, and will also consider the reduction of the risks impacting on finance

and insurance costs as well as on the production costs owing to reduced GHG anergy footprints (based on a

current and future CO2 tariff predictions). The system will be simulated in different use-case locations (heavily

impacting yield, component and O&M) hence illustrating the economic viability and replicability potential.

Task 7.5: Social assessment (L: IFEU; P: SIS; Duration: M19-M32): This task will assess social sustainability aspects

thus fully integrating SSH into the sustainability assessment framework. It will be based on the UNEP guidelines

(2020) for social LCA and methods from social impact assessment (SIA). Employment, income, gender, rural

development, inclusiveness, labour rights, health and safety, competition about local use of the marine space.

Task 7.6: Integrated assessment of sustainability (L: IFEU; P: SINTEF, SIS; Duration: M25-M36): This task will

integrate the results from the previous analyses (Tasks 7.2 to 7.5) through a multi-criteria evaluation SW for the

integrated assessment of sustainability covering all sustainability parameters chosen and linking them to UN

sustainable development goals (UN-SDG). This is based on the ILCSA methodology (integrated life cycle

sustainability assessment). Sensitivity analyses will identify parameters crucial for sustainability of the systems

to show optimization potentials. From these analyses, conclusions and recommendations to all relevant

stakeholder groups such as developers, industry and policy makers will be derived.
