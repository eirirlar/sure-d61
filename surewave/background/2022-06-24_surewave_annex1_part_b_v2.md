# Excellence

The **European Offshore renewable energy** **strategy**[^1] highlights the need for **more** established **renewable energy** technologies, and to **diversify** the technology portfolio to take maximum advantage of the **vast potential offered by EU's seas** to **become climate neutral by 2050**. However, not all offshore renewable technologies are in the same stage of maturity. **Floating Photo-Voltaic (FPV) is in the early R&D stage when it comes to open sea deployment** (Although already deployed in landlocked waters) **and more development are needed**. Experts have estimated that the total worldwide capacity of FPV could reach 62 GW by 2030[^2]. The **limited deployment of offshore FPV** is caused by the intrinsic characteristics of the marine environments (wind loads, wave loads, currents, corrosion, biofouling). The specific design and structure of the FPV influence its output power generation, durability and investment cost[^3]. Thus, offshore FPV systems need to be studied in detail (At the design, components, materials and structural levels) to ensure competitive offshore reliability, lifetime, easy installability and improved O&M, while considering economic, environmental, and social barriers.

That is why **SUREWAVE has seen a clear and huge opportunity** in the research and development of a novel and advanced **offshore FPV solution able to unlock the massive offshore FPV deployment in the EU seas**. It will **take maximum advantage** of previous deep **expertise accumulated from the offshore wind** field to understand the floating structures behaviour and how to model those (through SINTEF, MARIN, CEIT) combined with the high **knowledge** of development and deployment of **FPV in calm waters** (SIS) together with **floating solutions expertise** of CLEMENT and advanced **structural and non-structural materials** expertise of ACC. Technical expertise will be complemented by environmental and socio-economic assessment (IFEU) to overcome non-technical barriers.

**SUREWAVE aims to solve the main challenges of offshore FPV** by developing a solution **adapted** to the **most critical sea states**, being able to **mitigate** the harsh conditions for the FPV.

## Objectives and ambition

### objectives

The **main objective** of SUREWAVE is to **develop and test an innovative concept** of Floating Photo-Voltaic (**FPV**) system consisting of **an external floating breakwater** structure made of **new circular materials acting as a protection against severe wave loads on the** FPV structure itself, allowing **increased operational availability** and **energy output**. Such a concept is able to **operate in all the European sea-basins including open-sea very harsh environments** with high wind (speed \>25 m/s), current (\>1.2 m/s) and wave (height \>14 m), thus **unlocking the massive deployment of Offshore FPV at European and worldwide level**.

The overall project achievement will be accomplished by addressing the following **specific objectives**:

Technical objectives to address the most relevant **TECHNICAL CHALLENGES** for the implementation of the **SUREWAVE OFFSHORE FPV SOLUTION**:

**O1-NOVEL FLOATING PV DESIGN FOR OFFSHORE EXTREME CONDITIONS**:

- **Research and design on novel breakwater concept** with a large wave attenuation effect, as external element for the **protection and attenuation** of the wind, waves, and currents harmful **to the internal FPV** elements whilst ensures that **overall structure** is safely and non-destructively conducting associated forces into the anchorage, **not impairing the functionality of the PV-systems**.

- Research and optimization of the **coupling design** between **external breakwater and internal FPV**, including innovative **mechanical joints designs** to ensure a robust and flexible design and optimal structural behaviour.

- **Optimizing anchoring and** **mooring of the full FPV system** including it in the whole breakwater design as a critical element affecting its structural behaviour owing to extreme offshore conditions.

- **Improved** **energy connection systems** through routing and collection of the DC homeruns and through the physical interface between floating solar installation and the breakwater.

**O2-NEW CIRCULAR CONCRETE MATERIALS DEVELOPMENT**:

- Develop and test innovative **circular concrete materials solutions** for the external protective breakwater**,** enabling **reduced environmental impacts** with lower CO~2~ footprint, ensuring **structural integrity** in severe marine environment (**very high wind** speed \>25m/s, **current** \>1.2m/s and **waves** height \>14m) and improved **material durability** through corrosion and biofouling resistance.

- **Light weight solution** with **reduced production & installation** **costs** and easy to cast and transport.

**O3-PREDICTIVE COMPUTATIONAL MODELLING & SIMULATION FRAMEWORK DEVELOPMENT COMBINING**:

- Improved motion and loading prediction of full FPV system through a high fidelity **coupled aerodynamic and hydrodynamic** modelling tools.

- **Structural integrity modelling** tool to assess the structural integrity of the most critical parts of the FPV solution by means of advanced finite element techniques.

- **Artificial Neuronal Network (ANN)** based **material properties modelling tool** for optimization of the circular concrete material composition.

- **Structural Health Management System (SHMS)** to ensure the safe, **reliable and durable operation** of the FPV solution **in service** and to **e**stablish **inspection, maintenance and replacement periods** by combining structural health monitoring, corrosion management and fatigue assessments and thereby reducing CAPEX and OPEX.

**O4-ADVANCED TESTING AND VALIDATION FRAMEWORK FOR OFFSHORE FPV** technologies with a double purpose:

- To ensure **high level of techno-economic feasibility** of the solution **reducing degradation and failure** rates while ensuring high availability of system operation offshore in harsh conditions (**maximizing energy output**).

- To achieve **high level of fidelity of the simulation framework** developed validated by advanced lab testing allowing a safe reduction in the over-engineering of devices' specifications.

Non-technical objectives to **ensure a feasible, sustainable and highly-replicable DEPLOYMENT OF THE OFFSHORE FPV SOLUTION INTO THE REAL OFFSHORE ENERGY WORLD**. SUREWAVE will consider the following aspects:

**O5-SOCIETAL BENEFITS** **MAXIMIZATION** while avoiding potential negative impacts of offshore FPV solution via:

- **Social sustainability assessment** including social life cycle assessment (sLCA) to identify critical risks early on.

- **Societal engagement**: Identify stakeholders (e.g. in fishing, navigation, tourism) and take their needs e.g. on **spatial planning** into account in designing a compatible solution that receives public acceptance.

- Creating **new businesses and job opportunities** for on-site shore assembly, transport, installation, O&M.

**O6-ENVIRONMENTAL IMPACT MINIMIZATION**: SUREWAVE will massively extend the area suitable for large scale PV installation supporting the EU in powering a decarbonized economy in time for the 1.5°C goal.

- A specific **Life Cycle Assessment** (LCA) and elements of **Environmental Impact Assessment** (EIA) will ensure that targeted GHG savings are really achieved throughout the supply chain by circularity and that no significant harm is done to the marine ecosystem or any other planetary boundary in addition to the climate.

- A maximized **contribution to the objectives of the Mission Healthy Oceans** by-design, aiming to integrate with the maritime economy promoting circular, low-carbon, multi-purpose use of marine space.

**O7-HIGH ECONOMIC IMPACT** to ensure a feasible and scalable uptake of the solution by the market thanks to:

- **Reducing Levelized Cost of Energy (LCoE)** to around **54 €/MWh**, making **offshore FPV a competitive renewable energy** (in line with SET Plan targets for offshore wind[^4], e.g. 35-45 €/MWh for fixed, 62-106 €/MWh for floating).

- Ensuring **high replicability** of the solution, also in less demanding conditions (e.g. Mediterranean Sea).

- **Reducing investment risk** thanks to smart structural health monitoring and predictive computational tools for ensuring structural integrity to reduce failure and to increase installability and maintainability.

**O8-HIGH EXPLOITABILITY**: In order to achieve best pathways for the commercialization of the global solution a definitive **business model and definitive business plan** for exploitation of the solution will be defined. The plan will **include specific Offshore Renewable Energy market complexity**, IPR, regulatory framework, market up-take barriers, and fostering the massive replication into the offshore energy sector.

To this end, **SUREWAVE will take the maximum advantage of the existing knowledge** on: offshore energies, structural, aerodynamic & hydrodynamic modelling, monitoring, predictive tools, advanced FPV systems, new circular materials potential, simulation, testing and lab facilities (see [Table 1‑1](#_Ref93576330)).

**[SUREWAVE overall ambition]{.underline}:** The major compositions of an FPV system ([Figure 1‑2](#_Ref96350629)) include a floating structure, a mooring line, anchoring, PV module, inverter, and electrical connection. Multiple FPV installations have been deployed in many countries with a total power installed of almost 2,500 MW[^5]. However, all of them are in freshwaters, lakes, ponds, dams and reservoirs[^6]. To date, although FPV technology has been installed onshore, it is **still novel for offshore** areas which present several disadvantages (exposure to waves, wind loads, salinity, and aquatic species, all of which affect the life cycle and efficiency of energy production). There are few studies related to the FPV systems in offshore/marine environments, however, these studies do not consider the effect of harsh environment on the performance and operation of the FPV system[^7] where high levels of fatigue, stress and corrosion are expected. In order to address these challenges, SUREWAVE **proposes a novel and breakthrough concept** for **cost-effective and low maintenance Offshore FPV**. The project will start at **TRL2-3**. The FPV solution will achieve an intermediate **TRL3-4** with the design, development and preliminary testing of the materials and breakwater design and its advanced simulation under the modelling tools developed for the structural integrity assessment. An iterative process of design, simulation, testing and improvement will be carried out to ensure a robust and reliable maturity achievement of the solution. Finally, technical validation of some of the critical components of the system as well a scaled model of the FPV system in emulated offshore conditions will lead the concept to **TRL4-5.**

| **Ultra-High-Performance Fibre-Reinforced Concrete (UHPFRC**): 1) optimized packing density of the matrix, with no coarse aggregates; 2) ductility enhancement by introduction of fibers to obtain strain-hardening behaviour in tension; 3) compressive strength in the range of 120 or more MPa (very much higher than the conventional reinforce concrete -- 35-60 MPa); 4) ability to self-heal and seal cracks during their lifetime thanks to its own autogenous healing capacity and to the addition of crystalline waterproofing admixtures; and 5) high durability is achieved due to of high compactness and low w/b, therefore, the total amount of pores in the microstructure is very low (from 3 to 5 %) and they are non-interconnected scale. This feature gives them quite exceptional performance in terms of durability: water porosity, air permeability, freeze-thaw cycles resistance and chloride-ion diffusion factors are greatly reduced compared to ordinary or HPC. In addition, the fibers contribute to the ability of delaying crack propagation in the hardened concrete[^11]. UHPFRC is increasingly being used in structural elements subjected to dynamic loads, for instance in offshore applications or bridge constructions and recent H2020 projects (e.g. [LORCENIS](https://cordis.europa.eu/project/id/685445/es) or [ReSHEALience](https://uhdc.eu/)) are researching on UHPRFCs for coastal and offshore energy applications. However, **UHPFRC has some drawbacks** that will be addressed and overcome in SUREWAVE Project: 1) **Higher shrinkage** than conventional concrete, which can be the cause of micro- and macrocracking, and could limit the range of applications; 2) **Higher CO2 footprint** due to the high amount of cement required; 3) **Higher cost** due to the high amount of cement and steel fibers. Depending on the cement and fibre content, material costs for the concrete per m^3^ is expected to be -5 to 10 times higher than standard concrete.                                |

| SUREWAVE Project aims to reduce the high amount of cement necessary in **UHPFRC** (from 850 kg cement/m3 of concrete to 350 kg/m3) by substituting it with a combination of ground granulated blast furnace slag (GGBFS) and fly ash (class F), both industrial waste materials. In both cases, also silica fume is used (a co-product from the production of silicon or ferrosilicon metal). Studies show that larger amounts of cement (up to 60 %) can be replaced in UHPFRC by fly ash and GGBFS without any loss of mechanical performance[^12]. Several aspects will be improved at once thanks to this substitution: **Sustainability** of the concrete (Cement is the main contributor to the CO2 footprint), its **durability** both for the pozzolanic effect and for the shrinkage reduction. ## Methodology

### CONCEPTS & MODELS UNDERPINNING THE PROJECT

As introduced before, SUREWAVE aims to research, develop, and test an advanced offshore FPV solution taking advantage of the existing expertise in offshore energy structures (ACC, CLEMENT), FPV landlocked waters (SIS), and huge potential of material and structural predictive modelling (SINTEF, CEIT), able to unlock the deployment of FPV in any kind of offshore scenario.

As introduced before, SUREWAVE aims to research, develop, and test an advanced offshore FPV solution taking advantage of the existing expertise in offshore energy structures (ACC, CLEMENT), FPV landlocked waters (SIS), and huge potential of material and structural predictive modelling (SINTEF, CEIT), able to unlock the deployment of FPV in any kind of offshore scenario.

SUREWAVE proposes to solve the main challenges of offshore FPV deployment in open sea by developing a solution that can **smoothly be adapted to the most critical ocean conditions scenario**, being able firstly to **attenuate the harsh conditions** for the FPV and secondly to **resist** **to the quick variability of diverse mechanical** (aerodynamic & hydrodynamic) and **degrading conditions** of marine environment. To have a clear understanding of such technical challenges, first it is necessary to have an overview of the **main technical concepts** of the proposed solution (see [Figure 1‑1](#_Ref94196590)). SUREWAVE concept is based on the development on an **innovative external floating breakwater structure** made of **new circular material solutions** to **attenuate and minimize the adverse effects of open-sea harsh conditions** for the FPV structure present inside. It is very **inspired from harbour construction** where the ships are protected from sever sea states by a protective barrier. This unique design philosophy will allow FPV to increase operational window and energy output while **ensuring the structural integrity** of the complete system in open sea. Another advantage is that it will also minimise the future effort of adaptation of the internal FPV components and intermodular connection systems, that won\'t need to be overengineered for increased survivability, thus minimising future development costs of such systems.

The concept underpinning SUREWAVE is laying on three pillars ([Figure 1‑4](#_Ref96350354)):

- [Pillar-1]{.underline}: R&D of the **innovative breakwater** concept for the Offshore FPV to be operated in harsh conditions.

- [Pillar-2]{.underline}: Development of **sustainable new circular materials** with improved techno-economic-environmental performance

- [Pillar-3]{.underline}: I**ntelligent predictive modelling tools** to **ensure an optimal structural integrity, reliability, durability, and** improved **O&M.**

**[PILLAR-1:]{.underline}** R&D of the **innovative breakwater concept** for the Offshore FPV to be operated in harsh conditions.

The focus of first main pillar of SUREWAVE is to minimize the effect of the offshore extreme conditions of very high wind (speed \>25 m/s), current (\>1.2 m/s) and wave (height \>14 m) on the operability of the PV system. SUREWAVE strategy is to enclose the fragile PV elements into a protective barrier, instead of over engineering the PV elements itself. The external structural element\'s (protective barrier) main function is to protect the FPV modules by efficiently minimizing and reducing the wave effect into the protected area. This will maximise the operational window of the energy system and limited loads on the PV system will increase the lifespan of the system and decrease the maintenance operations.

Moreover, limited loads on the PV system will increase the lifespan of the system and decrease the maintenance operations. SUREWAVE\'s solutions for building this external protective barrier is to rely on the **breakwater technology** (CLEMENT\'s main field of expertise) whose survivability is optimised for severe loads in terms of dimensions, geometry, and materials. This solution has never been implemented in offshore scenario before. If the idea of the concept is not to modify the modular PV modules itself which will be protected from the most severe mechanical loads, improvements of the individual **critical components of the internal FPV** structure will be needed in SUREWAVE: There is more specifically innovations needed in the design of **mechanical joints** between the modules and the **coupling** between the breakwater line and the FPV modules for such a system to become successful.

The SUREWAVE decision on basing the concept on the development of a protective novel offshore breakwater is clearly justified considering the **analysis of the offshore extreme conditions**. It is estimated that the protection from the breakwater will be maximised for wave periods distributed around 4 to 5s, which are the most detrimental wave periods for the installation. Especially the high-frequency wave energy content is critical to the structural integrity of the PV, as it may induce (i) high slamming loads on the PV through wave breaking and (ii) large overturning motions of the PV, leading to large inter-connector loads as well as the risk of large wind lift loads when the bottom of the PV panels emerges from the waterline.

Therefore, the floating breakwater will be designed to break or to attenuate the high-frequency wave energy to an acceptable limit. It is however not the intention of this project to damp the longer waves period (swell waves). SUREWAVE will rely on the **high flexibility of the FPV system** which will allow the structure to follow the largest wave period. Moreover, the probability of occurrence of such wind and wave events is expected to be very low, maybe once a year or even less frequently.

[**PILLAR-2**:]{.underline} Development of sustainable new circular materials with improved techno-economic impact and reduced environmental footprint

![[]{#_Ref96413470 .anchor}Figure 1‑7: Illustartion of the inner core and outer shell structure of a break water with different material solutions investigated in SUREWAVE](media/image7.png){width="3.0055555555555555in" height="1.729861111111111in"}

Concerning the breakwater properties, it is conceived to be **a high performance sustainable** product, with **low environmental footprint**, and combining **corrosion and** **biofouling resistance. Its high mechanical properties should make it** highly-efficient at damping critical aero-hydro-dynamic loads while ensuring very **high lifespan**. In addition, it should be **easy to cast**, **easy to produce and transport** and **cost-efficient**. To combine all these characteristics into a novel structural element not developed for such conditions before, SUREWAVE will propose **new circular concrete material** solutions. The breakwater can be mainly decomposed in two parts ([Figure 1‑7](#_Ref96413470))[^27]: an external structural shell and an internal non-structural inner core. It is the combination of these two parts that gives a very strong and lightweight component (A technology similar to sandwich materials). Within SUREWAVE, it is aimed at optimising both parts of the breakwater.

On one hand, several **structural** circular material solutions will be researched for the structural shell, mainly analysing Ultra High Performance Concrete (UHPC) and Light weight aggregates concrete (LWAC). The aim is to use recycled aggregates and to replace part of the cement with granulated blast furnace slag (GBFS) or fly ash, as well as to boost corrosion and biofouling resistance. In addition, to boost their durability, these materials will have the ability to self-heal and seal cracks during their lifetime thanks to its own autogenous healing capacity and to the addition of crystalline waterproofing admixtures.

On the other hand, **non-structural cellular lightweight** cement-based materials will be researched for the inner core with special focus on developing low CO~2~ and waterproof lightweight inorganic formulations including waste materials such as fly ash and slags.

[**PILLAR-3**:]{.underline} Development of the **intelligent predictive computational modelling t**ools (serving both for the design phase and for the structural health management for optimal O&M), to ensure an optimal **structural integrity**, reliability and durability of the Offshore FPV.

The last pillar of SUREWAVE is the **structural integrity** as the main driver of the future offshore FPV. The **offshore structures integrity** is critical because of high probability of component failure with higher maintenance costs. This O&M issue is the main cost driver of the offshore energies which makes the **critical difference between offshore FPV and landlocked FPV**. That is why, the **main focus** will be to highly secure an optimal structural behaviour and **structural integrity of the FPV system as a whole**.

The breakwater system by itself does not guarantee that the whole structural health is controlled and behaving properly specially in some sea sates where the damping effects of the breakwater are limited. In that case, both the breakwater integrity and the inner connections might be subjected to severe loads. In addition, the corrosion effects on the whole system are one of the main causes of offshore long term structural failure. Over time both **critical phenomena** (**corrosion and aero-hydrodynamic extreme conditions**) will affect the offshore PV.

To this end, SUREWAVE aims to **develop and** **establish a predictive computational modelling and simulation framework** to design, predict and ensure structural integrity and increased lifespan of the offshore FPV system as a whole, which includes several modelling modules and computational steps:

- Advanced **material properties modelling tool** for the circular concrete materials, including regression ANN empirical-based model to predict the mechanical properties for different types of aggregates. It will predict compressive strength of the concrete for given density levels of LWA, strength to density ratios and porosities.

- Novel **aero-hydro-dynamic simulation** tool **coupling aerodynamic and hydrodynamic** effects as an integral effects scenario. ### the overall methodology

The methodology followed in the project can be split into **three main phases, each connected to a WP structure and tasks** (see [Figure 3‑1](#_Ref95388283)):

**PHASE 1) Offshore FPV -- GLOBAL FRAMEWORK DEFINITION**

This phase will be carried out through the implementation of the **WP2** (General framework definition) and also with task 8.1 (Societal engagement) to avoid social barriers. It aims at establishing a basis, general framework, specifications and base case concepts of the global FPV solution to give a common and coherent **starting point** for the main technical developments to be addressed in phase 2 \"technology development\". In addition, the requirements for installation, operation, maintenance, and decommissioning will be also considered in this phase.

The First step in establishing use-case scenarios is the selection of a few actual locations, which are suitable for the deployment of FPV system, and from which metocean data will be collected and forecasts models estimated. The locations will have an impact on the establishment of the worst-case scenarios and thus on the main requirements potentially affecting the structural integrity of the solution. It will also give indication to the estimation of the potential power production as well as potential preliminary use-case transport, installation, O&M framework (available facilities, equipment, costs, timing, etc.). These locations will be carefully selected to represent a variety of European sea conditions and analysed to define a set of characteristic use cases scenario, global requirements and critical aspects to be taken into account.

The site selection will enable to accurately **establish** both the **technical specifications to solve the identified technical challenges (Challenge:1-5)** and the **basis of the non-technical requirements** to solve the potential **barriers at social, economic, and environmental** level as well as to successfully addressing other potential barriers as **regulatory or standardisation** among others, to ensure future feasible scale-up, deployment and replication.

Furthermore, a **preliminary study** **of the critical components** will be carried out in Phase 1 mainly focused on: 1) Breakwater (as external protective-damping structural element); 2) Anchoring/mooring; 3) Mechanical joints/connexion elements; 4) Energy connection systems; as well as an analysis focusing on the **integration requirements** among the different key elements involving **transversal disciplines** (**electric system, transport, installation, O&M, decommissioning**) but also with **specific emphasis on specifying the needs on the mechanical joining of the breakwater and FPV array** as a critical aspect potentially affecting both the structural system behaviour and also the cost of the solution. The results of this stage will be the **reference point** for the other two phases at development and validation level, but also for the analysis of exploitation plan and business strategy for the SUREWAVE deployment. **Offshore FPV -- TECHNOLOGIES DEVELOPMENT**

**PHASE 2) Offshore FPV -- TECHNOLOGIES DEVELOPMENT**

SUREWAVE approach to increase the durability of the FPV system and its integrity relies on a protective line of breakwaters around FPV modules from Sunlit Sea but could potentially be applied to other floating PV concepts. Thus, the main focus of the project is not the full optimisation of the Sunlit Sea solar PV module, although it was selected for good reasons: The SIS **concept** is a very flat solar solution (minimizing wind drag) based on individual modules (Made from pressed, rolled marine grade aluminium, see [Figure 1‑10](#_Ref95999879)) assembled into prefabricated complete strings. Its light, aluminium shell design makes it very resistant to external loads and to corrosion. Its modular conception makes it easy to be stacked into a common shipping container/ barge deck, enabling rapid deployment, and covering large area on the water. One critical point of every modular design, including **SIS** solutions are the flexible connections between the modules (**Challenge 1**) which will be specifically improved in SUREWAVE by means of structural analyses (**WP3**, **WP5**) and validation in **WP6**.

The **design and dimensioning of the external breakwate**r including **anchoring and mooring systems** is key to the concept (**Pillar-1**) and will thus be carried out to provide optimal wave damping frequency range to attenuate the locally generated wind waves (**WP3**). The global shape of the breakwater perimeter (Circular, square or others as described in **Pillar-1** and shown in [Figure 1‑6](#_Ref94779431)), but also its geometry (optimal shape, see [Figure 1‑5](#_Ref94778643)), weight and dimensions are design parameters to be optimised (**Challenge 1**). The Beaufort scale of wind speeds will be followed with variable dimensions at real possible wave spectra in offshore areas, aiming to tackle: the wave breaking or attenuating effect (proportion of reflection and transmission), the dynamic behaviour, the internal load carrying capacity and force characteristics and transmission to couplings.

**The anchoring and mooring** systems will be dimensioned according to the definitive shape of the individual breakwater modules and of the global perimetral shape of breakwater. The anchoring solution chosen will influence the distribution of the loads between the breakwater, mooring and modules. Particular attention will be paid to excessive forces on the mooring attachment points and snapping-forces on the mooring attachment points/lines and anchors in case of rigid motions of the FPV array due to changing wind direction. This research will be supported by the advanced **coupled aero-hydrodynamic** **analysis and design optimization tool** (**Challenge 4**) developed in **WP5** **(Pillar-3)** which will give accurate estimations on the forces distribution and local motions of the system. These analyses consist of three steps: 1) **aerodynamic assessment** of the breakwater in relevant inflow conditions (wind speed, direction, turbulence level) of the complete FPV system; 2) **hydrodynamic assessment** to characterize the wave-related hydrodynamic loads distinguishing between severe (**survival scenario**) and mild-moderate sea states (**operational scenario**). Their probability of occurrence and the impact on the breakwater, internal FPV modules and mooring lines will be assessed; 3) **Coupled aero-hydrodynamic** analysis and design optimization under DAKOTA optimization toolkit to accurately determine the required gap between the breakwater and the PV panel modules in worst-case scenarios.

![[]{#_Ref96082359 .anchor}Figure 1‑11: Typical Composition of K-Ultra-High Performance Concrete (K-UHPC)](media/image12.png){alt="Chart Description automatically generated" width="1.7409722222222221in" height="1.648611111111111in"}

The breakwater itself will endure severe loads as a protective barrier (Wave slamming and pressure load from the waves are two main load scenarios). Particular attention will thus be placed on **optimising its durability** (Structural integrity and resistance to long term degradation in marine environment). This will be obtained through the development of innovative new **circular concrete** solutions constituting the core and the shell of the breakwater structure (**Pillar-2**). The new circular material solutions will be developed in **WP4**, based on preliminary specifications on the breakwater and global floating PV design in WP3 and on the pressures and forces estimated by the models in WP5. The selection of the optimal solution at the end of the project will be a combination of alternative options of structural shell solutions with the inner core solutions. Concerning the new circular material solutions for the **structural shell of the breakwater (Challenge-2)**, the strategy followed in SUREWAVE relies on analysing a set of new circular mixes designs based on low CO2 structural concrete formulations of two different approaches: Ultra High-Performance Concrete (see example of composition in [Figure 1‑11](#_Ref96082359)[^28]) and Light weight aggregates concrete. This research will be supported by the implementation of an efficient computational modelling tool for concrete material properties in WP5, using promising ANN Model Order Reduction techniques to overcome **(Challenge-5)** and enabling to investigate a wider variety of compositions than the standard experimental-only based approach. For the **inner core of the breakwater,** a non-structural cellular lightweight cement-based material will be investigated as replacement to the non-sustainable EPS material (**Challenge 3**). Special focus will be put on developing **low CO2 and waterproof lightweight inorganic formulations**, stable in marine environment, including in the formulation waste materials such as fly ash and slags and waterproofing agents. Although individual analysis and testing of their mechanical, physical and durability properties will be done, special emphasis will be put on the **durability assessment of the interface** between the shell and the inner core, which will be critical to assure later mechanical integrity of the breakwater component to be developed. For the preliminary validation of the new material solution SUREWAVE will **cast a small prototype** of the breakwater to be **tested at lab** scale TRL4, with a reduced scale of **1 m (wide), 0.5 m (heigh), 2 m (length**). It will be further validated at harsh environmental conditions in the "PHASE 3" (see below) achieving a TRL5 in WP6.

A last critical point to be addressed in the technical development phase is the **connections between the different components** of the FPV system (**Pillar-1**), in particular the connections point between the PV modules and breakwater line (**Challenge 1**) to ensure safe, reliable, and durable operation**.** SUREWAVE strategy to solve that issue is to perform **Structural integrity assessments (Pillar-3)** of the most critical parts of the structure using a combination of analytical, numerical, and advanced finite element techniques. In particular, strength and fatigue assessments of these components will be performed through **advanced Fracture Mechanics techniques** and FEA taking into account the predicted working conditions (corrosion, environmental effects, nodal forces/displacements) and required specifications and standards.

Besides, SUREWAVE will develop a user-friendly **Structural Health Management System (SHMS)** by combining numerical modelling and structural monitoring (**Challenge 6**). For this purpose, fatigue crack propagation algorithm for simplified 2D and 3D crack fronts will be developed and integrated into FEA simulation. A comparative validation of the computational tools will be done to calibrate and validate the developed modelling and simulation tools using the data produced by targeted validation campaigns (WP4 for the breakwater circular material properties and WP6 for the whole system).

**PHASE 3) Offshore FPV -- TESTING AND VALIDATION**

The third phase of SUREWAVE aims to mature the offshore FPV technology up to TRL5 using a double assessment approach: **Technical assessment** and **integral sustainability assessment**:

The technical testing and validation of the SUREWAVE offshore FPV solution will be assessed through the implementation of the **WP6** of the work plan (**Pillar-1 and 2**). The main WP objective is to make the technical validation of the global Floating PV solution developed in WP3, and the model tools developed in WP4 and WP5. This Validation will include lab testing (through advanced hydrodynamic test bench facilities of MARIN and structural laboratory at SINTEF) and sea-exposed validation of the concrete element for long term degradation and biofouling assessment to mature the solution to a TRL5, considering harsh conditions. In addition, this technical assessment aims to achieve and validate a **robust and reliable simulation framework** that will **allow to optimize the FPV solution** thus having a **better understanding of the device's real-life performance** allowing a safe **reduction in the over-engineering** of devices' specifications, lowering the cost at the time than reducing investment risks.

SUREWAVE will define and deploy an **advanced sensing and monitoring solution** to allow adequate implementation of the basin tests and for the long-term validation. The monitoring system will enable to gather data during the testing phase, which will be used to validate the models and SHMS developed in WP5. The monitoring system will be capable of detecting failures on critical points of the structure so that the designer can optimize the properties of the material and/or the geometry of the structure for future improved designs.

**The critical mechanical connections** will be assessed in SUREWAVE to ensure that the **ultimate strength performance** of the identified critical mechanical joints (connections between two breakwater components, connections between breakwater and solar modules, connections between solar modules) have sufficient structural capacity. Furthermore, **fatigue performance** testing will be carried out to validate the fatigue life expectancy considering the load amplitude values obtained from structural analysis tools developed in WP5.

The **hydrodynamic performance** of the **combined external breakwater and internal FPV modules** in both operational and survival sea states (High frequency slamming loads and breaking waves) will be **validated** by means of **model tests in a wave basin** at MARIN advanced facilities. The detailed description and dimensions along with the test campaigns are given in WP6.

Finally, to complete the technical testing and validation phase, SUREWAVE will validate the **long-term behaviour of the concrete breakwater** solution (**Pillar-2**) under **normal operating condition (ACC test site) testing of biofouling and corrosion** of the rebars of the **concrete breakwater prototype** built within WP4 on which the advanced sensing and structural health monitoring will be deployed to provide valuable data for the models and methodologies developed in WP5 as well as insight on the behaviour of the technical solutions developed in WP3 and WP4.

In parallel an accurate integral sustainability assessment will be performed, through **WP7**, **to solve the potential non-technical barriers** and facilitating a high-replicable deployment of the solution into the real offshore energy world. This analysis is composed of 4 main approaches: Environmental, Economic, Social and integrated approach.

The first step will be the definition and implementation of the **mass and energy balance** considering materials used, energy consumed **for the whole life-cycle** (production of components, pre-assemblies, transport, installation, O&M and decommissioning) both for the **SUREWAVE FPV solution** as well as for a **baseline system**.

Then once available the mass-energy balances, to ensure the **minimization of the environmental impact**, a Life-Cycle Assessment (**LCA**) of the developed solution assuming the potential full-scale future deployment into the marine environment will be done, so that the ultimate objectives of the project for increased clean energy generation, GHG emissions reduction, and minimization of product footprint in-land (production) and offshore (installation, O&M, decommissioning) are duly accomplished.

Secondly, to achieve a **high economic impact** to ensure a feasible adaptation of the solution by the market, an accurate methodology will be defined and implemented for **calculating the LCoE** of the SUREWAVE solution, conceived to ensure replicability in different sea basins searching for economic feasibility not only the application in harsh environments but also **analysing routes of SUREWAVE easy adaptations for high replicability** potential on less demanding conditions (e.g. Mediterranean sea).

Then an integral **social Life Cycle Assessment** (sLCA) will be carried out to analyse social impacts of the researched activities on a variety of stakeholders including competition and synergies with other sea activities (i.e. fishing, fish-farms, maritime navigation, tourism-leisure) to identify critical risks early on ensure social acceptance.

Once the environmental, economic and social aspects have been duly analysed and assessed, a final step of **integrated sustainability assessment** will be performed by integrating these 3 aspects through the use of a multi-criteria evaluation SW tool for the integrated assessment of sustainability covering all sustainability parameters chosen and linking them to UN sustainable development goals (UN-SDG).

Finally, through **WP8** SUREWAVE will maximize impact, including specific communication and dissemination activities targeting both scientific and industry stakeholders, as the project is expected to have a strong impact in both areas. A robust exploitation plan will also be elaborated to ensure and maximize the successful uptake of the results. It will include the development of a business plan for the replication of the results thanks to the Structural Health Management System integrating a complete simulation framework useful as a design and dimensioning tool for any offshore FPV design, lowering the design costs and de-risking the design and implementation of the solution.

## Project's pathways towards impact

### contribution of project results to the topic outcomes

***[Topic Outcome 1]{.underline}**: Improved overall lifetime, reliability, installability, operability and maintainability of marine substructures, mechanical joints and energy connection systems for ocean energy devices and/or offshore floating PV to reduce degradation and failure rates and thus investment risk.*

[Lifetime]{.underline}: SUREWAVE will improve the **overall lifetime** not only considering the offshore extreme conditions but also in comparison with land-locked waters FPV which is below 20 years[^30]. SUREWAVE will put **the offshore FPV** lifetime over **20 years achieving the rates of ground mounted solar**[^31], through three main ways:

- 1\) **Robust mechanical connections** will be specifically designed in the project and validated by use of fatigue testing to ensure reasonable lifetime of these critical parts up to **20 years** with low maintenance, **and flexible** enough to **avoid critical failure** of the components.

- 2\) **The FPV design mainly using corrosion/aging resistant materials** (Al/concrete/Polymers) with low degradation in offshore conditions (expected durability of the components is **around 20 years**).

- 3\) **Improved numerical models** with reduced uncertainties provides optimal estimate of load and stress distribution in the component leading to optimized component design with **reduced failure rate (by 10%**) and i**mproved remaining useful fatigue lifetime by 25%** of the different elements of the FPV.

[Reliability]{.underline}: Specifically, reliability will be improved by:

- 1\) The **solar panel flat-low design** directly in contact with water reduces the potential damages (by 70%) caused by lifting forces caused by surface wind and Aluminium solar panel shell structure very resistant to external loads and un-submersible to maximize availability and generation capacity

- 2\) **Decreased wind and wave exposure** (up to 30% decrease intensity) thanks to **optimal structural** attenuating design of the breakwater shape **through advanced predictive computational modelling and simulation** tools (both for the material properties and for the whole structural integrity), and thanks to the shielding effect of breaking wave on the solar modules leading to a reduced lifting force, slamming load and potential damages.

- 3\) **Concrete breakwater extremely resilient** to severe mechanical loads and to physical degradation in water environment (corrosion & biofouling).

[Installability]{.underline}: It will be drastically reduced thanks to:

- 1\) The breakwater concept which unlocks the **possibility to moor the solar array directly to the breakwater**. The following assumptions have been made: One tugging operation for a bundle of four elevated seaworthy offshore FPV rafts (for instance Moss Maritime or Solar Duck). Assuming 400 Wp solar panels. This results in 76 tugging operations for deploying 10 MWp FPV installation. By employing the SUREWAVE solution and using a seagoing barge, the number of tugging operations are reduced to 3, meaning a **96% reduction** of tugging.

- 2\) easy to mould and **port-site casting** of the external **concrete breakwater** thus lowering logistic cost by 30%.

- 3\) **internal modular and foldable FPV** light weight system of PV modules easy to install modular breakwater reducing the transportation and logistics cost by 30%.

- 4\) **Energy connection systems** easy to install (40% less time) by routing DC homeruns on breakwater, reducing need for cable tray floats and allows for combining the operation of mooring and DC homerun routing.

[O&M]{.underline}: SUREWAVE ensures high-efficient **operability** and drastic reduction of **maintenance tasks** thanks to:

- 1\) ease-to-use-app SHMS providing accurate structural integrity diagnosis reducing maintenance, inspection and replacement periods and preventing and reducing the occurrence of critical failures.

- 2\) improved resilience to mechanical loads and physical marine degradation thanks to novel external protective breakwater made of specifically targeted concrete formulation to increase corrosion and biofouling resistance.

- 3\) Smart mechanical **connection design** enabling quick and easy replacement if critical failure occurs.

The SUREWAVE achievements concerning the reduction of degradation and failure rates as well as the high reliability and availability of the system energy output will lead to a **direct** **reduction of** **offshore FPV investment risk**. Besides, the achievements in terms of O&M will lead to reduction of **O&M variable and fixed costs** (insurance, etc), thus increasing the credibility and acceptance of the solution by the potential offshore FPV project developers.

***[Topic Outcome 2]{.underline}**: Better understanding of the device's real-life performance allowing a safe reduction in the over-engineering of devices' specifications*

The **Structural Health Management System toolkit** will serve not only for the O&M of the FPV system in service, but it will be also conceived to be used as an easy-to-use-app for having an **optimal and accurate understanding of the FPV system real-life behaviour**, allowing for the offshore FPV **design, sizing, dimensioning, modelling and simulation** to enable the **quick, accurate, efficient and easy replication and customization** of the offshore FPV system based in the novel SUREWAVE concept. This SHSM tool will allow to assess the combination of optimal designs of internal FPV components, mechanical joints, external breakwaters for a specific or a combination of required offshore conditions not only for worst scenarios but in middle and good scenarios for application in other sea basins, allowing a **safe reduction in the over-engineering of the offshore FPV devices´ specifications**.

The SHSM platform will be developed to integrate the novel breakwater solution for offshore extreme conditions with the internal FPV structures and **considering different parametrizable** mechanical connections (anchoring, mooring, internal modules connection) towards an **evaluation of the structural health integrity** for all the use cases based on AI. Specifically, this platform would provide a whole framework, which is able to test and validate the customized models, and to size different components. This simulation platform will **focus on compromises between structural integrity, sustainability and cost-efficiency** to **optimise durability/cost** performances.

***[Topic Outcome 3]{.underline}**: Reduction of LCOE in line with the SET Plan targets (actions should clearly justify estimated LCOE at project start and end, using a recognised calculation methodology)*

SUREWAVE will reduce FPV **CAPEX** mainly with the circular concrete breakwater concept, and other aspects:

- [Reduction of design & fabrication costs]{.underline}: 1) internal FPV strings from pressed, rolled marine grade Aluminium with reduced material usage and integrating recyclable and pawnable material **reducing cost by 20%** in comparison with current FPV; 2) external **breakwater with up to 30% cost reduction** owing to use of waste aggregates and reduction of 60% of cement; 3) **40% reduction of design time/cost** of the structural specifications of internal FPV, coupling system and external breakwater including engineering of PV field, thanks to accurate insights of the detailed aero and hydrodynamic calculations from the simulation toolkit.

- [Reduction of the transport & installation costs]{.underline}: 1) individual FPV strings able to be stacked into a common shipping container and rapidly deployed into the water, mechanically connected together **reducing 30% logistic cost**; 2) **breakwater** made of circular concrete mixes **allowing cast on port**, **reducing 30% logistic cost**; 3) Mooring the array directly to breakwater allowing a **reduction of 40%** of the **anchoring & mooring costs**.

SUREWAVE will reduce **OPEX** thanks to the structural integrity assured by the computational modelling toolkit:

- [Reduction of failures and maintenance]{.underline}: 1) **Avoid 70% of internal FPV system failure** thanks to strings using corrosion/aging resistant materials 2) **Avoid 100% critical failure** for **fatigue crack of breakwater**; 3) **Avoid 80% of critical failure** of the **mechanical connections and mooring** lines thanks to the capacity of non-linear wind and (breaking) wave impact loading simulations for different breakwater and PV field configurations.

- [Reduction of insurance costs]{.underline}: The high reliability on the system thanks to solid combination of the design, simulation and in-service predictive O&M provided by the SHMS is expected to **reduce insurance costs by 20%.**

- [Reduction of reparation frequency works]{.underline}: Thanks to an accurate prediction of the Maintenance, inspection and replacement periods provided by the SHMS a **reduction of 30% on the O&M travel expenses** will be achieved.

SUREWAVE improves the **generable energy** output of the FPV system offshore thanks to several approaches:

- [Reduction of downtimes of the global system]{.underline}: **Securing the structural integrity of the system**, thanks to the SHMS allowing accurate structural design, guarantees at least a reduction of **50% of downtimes in operational sea-states** and allows for a quick recovery of the availability of the system without critical failure during the low frequent survival sea-states (14 m. waves). This means an average increase of **20% of the energy output**.

- [Increasing energy efficiency]{.underline}: 1) **increase of 5%** in the system efficiency is expected thanks to the internal FPV modules flat design **maximizing cooling** of PV panels limiting diffusion driven processes; 2) potential **increase of 5%** of available time of generation by optimizing the hydrodynamic behaviour **minimizing splash zone** inside FPV thanks to the breakwater attenuating effect on wave-current-wind conditions.

Deployed FPV in land-locked waters has created an opinion reluctant scenario of key biodiversity actors with uncertain level of impact into the ecosystems.                                        **In T7.3**, SUREWAVE will carry out a detailed and credible **LCA** that will ensure: (1) low-carbon circular materials; (2) reduction of raw material consumption and over-engineering 3) less number of components replaced, increase useful life and reduction of failure risk through predictive models & in-service monitoring.

  [Expensive & Complex Offshore O&M]{.underline}: Renewable offshore plants project operators and maintainers are reluctant to operate non-reliable and/or expensive, uncertain or over-maintained systems owing to the big impact of offshore O&M on system profitability.                                                                                              The **SHMS (developed in WP5**) will allow the end-user to determine the remaining life of the critical parts in service, and consequently, to optimize inspection and replacement periods for these components, in this way reducing maintenance cost by over 20%.

  [Investment risk]{.underline}: There has not yet been developed proper certification standard. Only DNV has established a recommended practice for FPV (DNV RP0584) not applicable for offshore FPV. The safety and quality norms, (IEC 61251 and IEC 61730) are not designed for the extreme offshore. Therefore, the cost of capital and insurance costs are high.   The SUREWAVE combined solution **contributes to added predictability** and bespoke components designed for the offshore FPV industry. It is expected that higher predictability regarding **degradation**, **failure modes** **and safety** will reduce both the costs of capital and insurance. This alone has a large impact on LCOE, see section on LCOE calculations in "Topic Outcome 3".

  [Cultural-social]{.underline}: Lack of public buy-in of FPV technology due to visual impacts and competing uses of water bodies could stall project development. Previous negative experiences with RES projects may lead to an unfavourable public opinion on FPV^43^.                                                                                                SUREWAVE will show a credible integrated sustainability assessment (**WP7**) (co-relating environmental, economic and social aspects) together with specific dissemination and communication and social engagement to maximize offshore FPV acceptance and avoid unfounded concerns.

  [Regulatory]{.underline}: Uncertainty about water rights may delay FPV project development and increase costs. Lack of interagency cooperation and coordination may stall FPV deployment. Lengthy, expensive, and unclear environmental approval processes for FPV systems can make projects less financially appealing.                                               SURWAVE will engage (through **Task 2.4**) policymakers and financial institutions to increase awareness of FPV systems that can lead to increased support for investing in R&D and deployment projects and that will raise sufficient background, in FPV and its benefits to allow policymakers to design effective policies and regulations.

## Measures to maximise impact - Dissemination, exploitation and communication

### impact strategy

Dissemination, Exploitation, and Communication Master Plan (DECMP, D8.1) will identify key messages specific for different target groups, i.e., the ocean energies research community, potential end-users (energy utilities, offshore energy project developers-promoter); structural integrity tools users, engineers, circular material researchers and students; associations and other interest groups. The objectives of this impact strategy are: **Goal 1: Raising awareness and informing** stakeholders on results and proposed approaches for technology providers and early adaptors in the offshore energy sector. **Goal 2: Engaging in a dialogue** with stakeholders, to foster exploitation opportunities both for commercialization and further research based on SUREWAVE solutions. **Goal 3: Transferring knowledge** to partners and stakeholders. **Goal 4: Increasing acceptance** of the proposed technical solutions, in particular the potential environmental & cost benefits. **Goal 5: Replicating the SUREWAVE solutions** beyond the project´s lifetime based on suitable business models and exploitation pathways.

**[Dissemination strategy]{.underline}**: SUREWAVE will identify (1) relevant journals, topics and events to publish its results to reach a highly relevant audience. The dissemination activities will include presenting and publishing the results in (2) the selected Open Access outlets and conferences, providing open data available for stakeholders and engaging with target groups, (3) collaborating with EU community and engagement in sister projects and organizations to amplify the visibility and impact of the project. For example, the consortium will organize a webinar on *Sustainable offshore floating PV systems for harsh sea waters*; the evaluation of these actions (4) through the review of citations, impact factor of journals, altimetric and feedback forms during events.

**[Exploitation strategy]{.underline}**: The project will identify (5) Key Exploitable Results (KERs), target markets and potential users and customers, and develop the IP management plan. **[Communication strategy]{.underline}**: After the (8) identification of target audiences, communication channels, type of materials and the formation of key messages of the project, SIS will produce (9) original content including journalistic articles, press releases, videos, infographics, fact sheets, a technical brochure and social media posts. ### Exploitation Strategy 

The exploitation strategy will be coordinated by SIS with a solid background of commercializing FPV solutions in ocean energy sector. The core innovation of SUREWAVE will be exploited as a whole **offshore FPV solution** based on an innovative external floating structure of breakwater using new circular materials which together to the development of optimal structural integrity management tools will ensure the feasible and cost-effective installation of solar PV power massively in offshore deep and high-demanding waters. In addition, secondary exploitable results will be analysed to assess their potential as future products and services. To that end, a step-by-step exploitation strategy will allow defining the exploitation routes based on which the project will work on:

- **Joint Exploitation Plan**: A common exploitation plan will be defined according to the position of each partner within the value chain and the existing background and foreground generated. This plan will articulate the main synergies among the individual exploitation plans through the definition of a global action framework for commercialization. This plan will also consider non-commercial pathways such as scientific, political or social.

- **Individual Exploitation Plan**: Each consortium member will build its individual exploitation plan according to its own exploitation strategy (as shown in [Table 2‑5](#_Ref89155083)). To reach both outcomes, SUREWAVE will apply the following approach: SIS will be in charge of identifying the key market needs (in T8.4). SIS with the collaboration of the rest of partners, will work in the identification and definition of the different exploitation agreements. Once this is set and after thoroughly analyse the IPR, the different commercialization and replication roadmaps for the Key Exploitable Results of the project will be defined as part of the exploitation methodology. In that way, SUREWAVE will ensure the sector uptake and maximize the impact of the achievements.

The **exploitation methodology** includes **5 steps: 1) Identification of key results and deployment of IPR management strategy** considering the nature of the background and foreground of the partners. **2) Evaluation of business potential of the results identified**, will be analysed through SWOT analysis. **3) Business model generation for those results with a high business potential** through well-known methodologies such as Lean-Canvas or Value-proposition Canvas. **4) IPR analysis to define IP mechanisms required to protect the foreground produced**. **5) Business/Exploitation plan development** considering the results achieved and partners´ exploitation strategies:

**[\* SUREWAVE Global Solution]{.underline}**: **A complete offshore FPV system** to better operate in deep and high-demanding waters, enabling the solar PV sector to maximize affordable solar energy in the oceans under a feasible, durable, reliable and cost-effective solution. This solution **will be marketed through SIS.** Other partners will be compensated with the payment of licenses/ royalties for the results that contribute to the solution. The **large-scale product** is expected to be a **plant about** **50MWp**, with a power peak of **150W/m^2^** (e.g. **578m square** or **652m diameter**).

A **preliminary product evolution strategy** is defined: 1) SUREWAVE R&D (mid-2022 to mid-2025); 2) 25MW pilot demonstration offshore, hopefully under umbrella of HORIZON EUROPE (From 2025 to 2027); 3) Commercialization & contracts´ awards (from 2026 and beyond); 4) 1^st^ commercial products deployment (from 2028 to 2030); 5) Multiple SUREWAVE solution operating (from 2032 and beyond).

The **preliminary market approach** that sets the exploitation scope of the project:

- [Total Addressable Market (Total market for SUREWAVE)]{.underline}🡪 In the 1^st^ commercialization phase (2030-2032), the consortium aims to commercialize the product in offshore PV plant projects for "mid-extreme conditions" locations in the **North and Baltic seas** (covering The Netherlands, Germany, Denmark, Sweden, Finland, Poland and less demanding sites of UK or Norway) and **Atlantic** (France, Portugal). In a 2^nd^ commercialization phase SUREWAVE will reach by 2035 the specific extreme harsh conditions of the North Sea as well as Norwegian and Celtic seas as well as less demanding sea basins (e.g. Mediterranean) with SUREWAVE customized design.

- [Serviceable Available Market (Target market for SUREWAVE)]{.underline}🡪 The product that SUREWAVE develops will be suitable both for extreme sea conditions with very high wind (speed \>25 m/s), current (\>1.2 m/s) and wave (height \>14 m) loads and for less demanding marine conditions (Atlantic, Mediterranean, etc). Considering the worldwide of 62GW^2^ expectations by 2030 and considering that **EU advances in a competitive position** acquiring a 10% of the global market, the estimated **serviceable market could be of 6.2 GW**.

- [Serviceable Obtainable Market (% of the target market)]{.underline}🡪 The market for the offshore FPV is quite uncertain with several large competitors with reasonable mature offerings. However, the market is still growing and offers room for innovative new commers. It is aimed to obtain a market share of **20%**, meaning an **installed power by year 2032** of **1,240 MW** hence applying the product to **25 offshore FPV farms** (considering SUREWAVE solution scaled-up to **50MW** per farm with solar array surface of 3.33e6m^2^ and power peak of 150W/m^2^).

The target clients finally operating such plants are mostly energy utilities and/or ESCOs, but direct clients can also be purchasing entities involving public and private investors and promoters, so intrinsic peculiarities will be considered (e.g. procurement rules), and with the possibility of alternative ways of making them using the SUREWAVE floating PV solution (e.g. licensing or royalties using design, etc). Deep analysis and business model strategies will be done in task 8.4 to ensure maximum exploitation of the SUREWAVE global solution.

**[Strategy for the management of intellectual property]{.underline}**: The strategy for IPR Management is set up in the Consortium Agreement (CA). The overall IPR strategy is to ensure that partners are free to fully exploit their market position. Hence, **SUREWAVE has a policy of protecting IP where possible**, increasing the likelihood of market uptake, and ensuring a smooth handling of the individual IPR of the involved partners in view of transfer agreements. It is agreed that **foreground** will be the property of the beneficiary carrying out the work generating that foreground. Where several beneficiaries have jointly generated foreground and where their respective share of the work cannot be ascertained, they will have joint ownership of such foreground. They will establish (**in task 1.4**) an agreement regarding the allocation and terms of exercising the joint ownership. Beneficiaries will also define the **background needed** for the purposes of the project development in a written agreement and, where appropriate, may agree to exclude specific background. A preliminary analysis has been performed to identify results and its ownership (see [Table 2‑5](#_Ref89155083)). The following categories of IP are distinguished in the project:

- **AI, ML and software modelling tools resulting from the project (foreground)**, which will be protected by means of secret. IPR transfer agreements will be discussed in task 1.4

- **Datasets used to train the AI and ML systems**: The owners of the datasets will have advantageous commercial conditions (to be discussed in T1.4) for future commercial versions.

- **Design of physical elements** as the new circular material mixes, breakwater detailed engineering design or the global FPV system engineering design, which commercial IP conditions will be discussed in task 1.4.

Finally, the DMP will include research data management in the research output management. It will be ensured that data that will be produced in the project context and not for to commercial exploitation or access restrictions. It will be made available as open data under **Creative Commons license CC BY-NC (Attribution-Non-Commercial)**.

### overall structure of the work plan, including graphical presentation of the components showing how they inter-relate

The following figure shows SUREWAVE Work Package structure, leaderships and relation between work packages, and how the floating PV solution will be prepared, integrated and demonstrated along the work packages:

![[]{#_Ref95388283 .anchor}Figure 3‑1: pert diagram of the work plan](media/image15.png){alt="Interfaz de usuario gráfica, Diagrama Descripción generada automáticamente" width="6.658783902012249in" height="3.757474846894138in"}

To achieve SUREWAVE overarching goal and its specific objectives, the work plan of **36 months** is divided into eight Work Packages (WP). Besides the cross-cutting WPs, i.e., WP1, dedicated to project management and WP8 (Dissemination, Communication, Exploitation & Societal Engagement), the work plan can be grouped into three main blocks of WPs:

- The first block (**WP2**) is dedicated to the preliminary **global framework definition** of the floating PV solution based in the breakwater concept. The results will be general specifications for: the development of the global floating PV system, the new materials and the modelling and simulation tools.

- The second block (**WP3, WP4 and WP5**) is dedicated to the **research and technologies development** of the floating PV solution (global system integration, breakwater design, new circular concrete material solutions, computational modelling and simulation tools for quick and accurate determination of material properties ensuring structural integrity and lifetime of the solution).

- The third block (**WP6 and WP7**) entails the **testing and validation** at technical, social, economic and environmental level of the solution to achieve the TRL5 for the novel SUREWAVE floating PV solution.

# ETHICS SELF-ASSESSMENT

## 

A new Section 4 has been created in the DoA Annex I Part B. Ethics self-assessment text included in the proposal part A has been copied in this section.

## Ethical dimension of the objectives, methodology and likely impact

This project will involve humans for carrying out non-technical studies of social research.

On the one hand, within Task 8.1 specific activities will be carried out to engage "social" stakeholders from the very beginning of the project: local marine industries, citizens, associations, an any specific potential targeted marine locations suitable for future exploitation of the floating PV solution. The aim will be start analysing and defining social needs/requirements by-design (with knowledge exchange to the global framework definition in WP2) to ensure that the floating PV design will be compatible with other sea activities (i.e. fishing, fish-farms, maritime navigation, tourism-leisure or other), also considering the spatial planning aspects. Several workshops with specific social actors will be done to discuss on the project's concept to ensure social aspects consideration in the solution as well as to ensure social awareness and acceptance of the developed offshore FPV solution for harsh ocean conditions. The conclusions will be reported in D8.1 and will be considered for the social analysis once the solution is developed and tested within WP7.

On the other hand, task 7.5 will assess social aspects of all scenarios as defined in Task 7.1. The social assessment will be mainly based on the UNEP guidelines (2020) for social life cycle assessment of products and organizations (sLCA) based on data on costs and working hours provided by Task 7.4. Where necessary, the methods from social impact assessment (SIA) will be used complementarily. The methodology of the assessment will be adjusted to the necessity of the integrated sustainability assessment. Among the effects investigated are aspects such as employment, income, rural development, inclusiveness, labour rights, health and safety, competition about resources etc. This will be complemented by an analysis of stakeholders' views documented in literature on other marine renewable energy installations that are already in the realisation stage to be able to identify potential barriers early on.

## Compliance with ethical principles and relevant legislations

The consortium is aware of ethical rules and standards. In relation with the involvement of humans for social research issues concerning the societal engagement task (Task 8.1) as well as task 7.5 for the social assessment for analysing the societal impact of the project research activities.

Specific consent and acceptance for participating in the social analysis interviews, workshops or any other activity will be properly asked to any human involvement.

In addition, the project is aware about the potential use of personnel data affecting social assessment on effects such as employment, income, rural development, inclusiveness, labour rights, health and safety, competition about resources etc.

This will be done in full compliance of the main legislation and directives and more specifically the Directive 95/46/EC & Directive 2002/58/EC of the European parliament regarding issues with privacy and protection of personal data and the free movement of such data, and the EU General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR). In addition, the project will fully comply with specific national legislation of the countries involved in the project.
