**EXECUTIVE SUMMARY**

The document herein reports the activities carried out in Task 4.1 and Task 4.2 concerning the development and characterization of novel circular concrete solutions for the breakwater. The breakwater is mainly decomposed into two parts: an external structural shell and an internal non-structural inner core. On one hand, two different concrete solutions for the shell were developed; lightweight aggregate concrete (LWAC) and high-performance concrete (HPC). On the other hand, non-structural cellular lightweight concrete was researched for the inner core.

Chapter 1 covers the deliverable with an introduction and outlining the objectives of the deliverable.

Chapter 2 describes the work performed in Task 4.1 *Design of new circular material solutions for the structural shell*.

Chapter 3 describes the work performed in Task 4.2 *Design of new circular material solutions for the inner core*.

The findings presented in Chapter 4, Results, and the concluding remarks in Chapter 5 demonstrate that, across all examined types of concrete, an optimal solution was chosen for each of the concrete solutions, aligning with specified technical requirements while considering sustainability and circular economy aspects.

#  Introduction

This report deals with the use of novel circular solutions for floating breakwater, both for the structural shell and for the non-structural inner core. Floating breakwaters are utilized as a protection against short-period wind and waves both for natural or artificial such as ship induced waves in semi sheltered areas such as lakes, rivers, etc. or near-shore. The most common type is the box shape or pontoon type breakwater (as shown in [Figure 1](#_Ref169254095) ), usually manufactured with a shell of conventional concrete and with an inner core of Expanded Poly-Styrene (EPS) with high cost and high CO~2~ footprint. Therefore, there is research needed to replace EPS materials with more sustainable solutions. Considering this, one of the goals of the SUREWAVE project is to develop High Performance Concrete (HPC) or Lightweight Aggregate Concrete (LWAC) for the outer shell and also substitute EPS with Cellular Lightweight Concrete.

![P268#y1](media/image4.png){width="4.756944444444445in" height="2.6993055555555556in"}

[]{#_Ref169254095 .anchor}Figure 1 Schematic of Box type pontoon

The buoyancy of this kind of breakwater is realized by the lightweight core. The density of the EPS is between 15 and 30 kg/m³. Therefore, the concrete developed for the core should be as light as possible in order to keep the amount of additional weight as low as possible. That is why cellular lightweight concrete was investigated for the inner core of the breakwater.

Moreover, in conventional pontoon type breakwater constructions, the polystyrene has a water absorption of less than 2 Vol.-%. The outer shell in these cases is only on top and on the sides. This water absorption cannot be achieved with the inner core of cellular concrete. Therefore, the core must be protected on the bottom as well. Accordingly, the shell is not only the structural part but also the waterproof layer. The related concrete must be very dense and as light or thin as possible to save some weight. That is why lightweight aggregate concrete (LWAC) and high-performance concrete (HPC) were investigated for the structural shell.

# New circular material solutions for the structural shell of the breakwater

## Requirements

The requirements in terms of compressive strength, density and consistency for the two types of concrete for the structural shell were:

[Lightweight Aggregate Concrete (LWAC)]{.underline}:

- Compressive strength after 28 days: 45 MPa

- Dry density after 28 days: ≤1900 kg/m^3^

- Consistency: minimum flow class F4 (490 mm diameter)

[High performance concrete (HPC)]{.underline}:

- Compressive strength after 28 days: 120 MPa

- Dry density after 28 days: 2350-2500 kg/m^3^

- Consistency: minimum class F4 (490 mm diameter)

## Lightweight aggregate concrete (LWAC) 

LWAC was one of the two options to design a circular solution for the structural shell. In order to design a circular LWAC solution, work was conducted in a stepwise process. In the first step, eight reference trial mixes were developed using natural aggregates while considering the specified requirements for compressive strength and dry density. The materials used were: CEM I 52.5 R as binder, quartzite aggregates as natural normal weight aggregates, expanded clay (Laterlite0F0F0F[^1]) as natural lightweight aggregate and one superplasticizer additive from Sika (SikaViscoCrete 90NG). Details of each reference mix (materials and fraction) are included in [Table 1](#_Ref148699176).

In the second step, screening tests were conducted to identify the optimal mix design that produced satisfactory results in terms of consistency, density and compressive strength. Sample 8 was selected as a reference mix design to design a circular LWAC solution because it fulfilled the technical requirements set for compressive strength and density. Subsequently, ten trial mix dosages were designed to replace natural aggregates with secondary aggregates (see [Figure 2](#_Ref153441948)) to develop a circular LWAC solution. Natural normal-weight aggregates were partially replaced by recycled concrete aggregates from the Construction of Demolition Waste (CDW) and black steel slags from steel production. Expanded clay was replaced by Minolite1F1F1F[^2] lightweight aggregate.

![](media/image8.png)

[]{#_Ref153441948 .anchor}Figure 2. Recycled and artificial aggregates

Details of circular mix recipes partially replacing natural aggregates with secondary aggregates are displayed in [Table 2](#_Ref153442796). In this case, two additives were required when recycled concrete aggregates were utilized, a superplasticizer (SikaViscoCrete 90 NG) plus a plasticizer (Sikament 3003) to get a good consistency of concrete. The water-to-cement ratio (mass of water/ mass of cement) was set to 0.35 in all the mix recipes.

## Cellular Lightweight Concrete 

Cellular concrete is an ultra-light concrete masonry product. It can weigh as little as 1/5 of ordinary concrete due to its distinct cellular structure, which comprises millions of tiny pockets of trapped air. It is a combination of cement, lime, water, and fine-grained sand, fly ash can be a preferred addition instead of fine grain materials. The low specific weight of cellular concrete is about nearly 350 to 800 kg/m^3^, and it has good thermal properties. The chemical reaction of aluminum paste makes it a porous shape having mild weight and enhanced insulation properties, distinguishing it from other lightweight concrete formulations.

Twenty-six different trial mixes were cast to design an optimal circular cellular concrete solution with a density between 300 and 400 kg/m^3^. The materials used were: CEM I 42.5R/SR5 as a binder, fly ash class F, lime, and gypsum as reactive additions, quartzite sand as natural fine aggregate, expanded clay (Laterlite) as natural lightweight aggregate, Minolite as recycled lightweight aggregate from glass waste, aluminium powder as air-generating agent and sodium hydroxide (solid or solution) to active the reaction and to generate air within the concrete matrix.

Details of trial mix recipes from Sample 1 to Sample 9 are shown in [Table 4](#_Ref154144350) below. Natural sand was used as aggregate from Sample 1 to Sample 6, Expanded clay was utilized as natural lightweight aggregate in Sample 7 and Sample 8 and both types of aggregates were used in Sample 9. Sodium hydroxide solution 5M was added in Sample 7.

# Results

The test methods applied for the characterization of the developed concrete solutions were selected according to the requirements set out.

On one hand, for both LWAC and HPC, tests were conducted on the consistency of fresh concrete and the compressive strength and density of hardened concrete at 2, 7, and 28 days. The early-age strength of the trial mixes was assessed because the pontoon-type breakwaters are produced in series at a precast plant, requiring sufficient strength to allow for early demoulding.

On the other hand, the compressive strength and density of the cellular concrete trial mixes were tested.

Results of the LWAC and HPC trial mixes developed for the structural shell of the breakwater are shown in sections 4.1 and 4.2, respectively.

Results of the cellular concrete trial mixes developed for the inner core of the breakwater are shown in section 4.3.

# Conclusions

Deliverable 4.1 summarises the results obtained by the SUREWAVE team involved in Task 4.1 and Task 4.2 -- *New circular material solutions for the structural shell and the inner core of the breakwater* (ACCIONA, SINTEF and CLEMENT).

Two concrete solutions for the structural (outer) shell were investigated: lightweight aggregate concrete (LWAC) and high-performance concrete (HPC). For the non-structural inner core, cellular lightweight concrete was developed.

The concrete solutions were developed through a step-by-step process. First, reference trial mixes were created using primary ingredients and then characterized. Next, screening tests were conducted to identify the optimal reference mix design that produced satisfactory results. Once the optimal reference formulation was obtained for each concrete solution, circular concrete mixes were designed by replacing primary ingredients (natural aggregates and cement) with secondary ingredients (secondary aggregates, fly ash (FA), and ground granulated blast-furnace slag (GGBS)). Secondary aggregates from three types of waste were used: recycled concrete aggregate from concrete, Minolite lightweight aggregate from glass, and black steel slags from steel production.

For each investigated material, the main outcomes are summarised in the following. In general, fresh and hardened concrete properties have been tailored to match the requirements.

**Two materials were identified for the outer shell:**

**[1) LWAC]{.underline}**

- LWAC was one of the alternative solutions for the structural shell.

- Eighteen trial mixes of LWAC were designed.

- The requirements set for this type of concrete were minimum consistency class F4 of fresh concrete, minimum compressive strength 45MPa, and density equal to or less than 1900 kg/m^3^ at 28 days of age.

- Sample 17 mix recipe was selected as the optimized circular LWAC solution with a consistency of 455mm, 49MPa of compressive strength and 1898 kg/m^3^ of density at 28 days of age. These results comply with the requirements.

- Sample 18 was designed using the same recipe as sample 17 but using green cement with a lower carbon footprint. Results indicated that although the compressive strength was increased significantly (68 MPa), so did density (near 2000 kg/m^3^).

- 100% of natural lightweight aggregates (expanded clay) were replaced by Minolite recycled lightweight aggregate in both samples 17 and 18.

- 20% by volume of natural coarse aggregate was replaced by recycled concrete aggregate in both samples 17 and 18.

**[2) HPC]{.underline}**

- The other alternative to be developed for the shell of the breakwater was HPC.

- Two trial mixes of HPC were cast. We utilized our in-house (ACC) expertise to create the initial reference material. Building upon this foundation, we formulated a second mix with circularity as the central focus.

- The established requirements for this type of concrete were minimum consistency class F4 of fresh concrete and compressive strength of 120MPa at 28 days of age.

- Sample 2 mix recipe was selected as the circular HPC solution with 128MPa of compressive strength at 28 days of age and a consistency of fresh concrete of 665mm. Results indicated that requirements were met.

- 18% by weight of cement was replaced by by-products GGBFS and FA in sample 2.

**One material is identified for the inner core:**

**[Cellular concrete]{.underline}**

- Twenty-six different trial mixes were cast to design an optimal non-structural circular cellular concrete solution for the inner core of the breakwater structure, aiming at a density around 300 kg/m^3^.

- Sample 26 mix recipe was selected as the optimal cellular concrete solution with a density of 365 kg/m^3^.

- This circular cellular concrete mix was developed replacing 100% of natural lightweight expanded clay aggregates by Minolite lightweight aggregates from recycled glass.

- 80% less cement than non-structural normal concrete was used (25kg/m^3^ vs minimum 150kg/m^3^).

- Solid Sodium hydroxide was used to activate the release of hydrogen in the cellular concrete matrix.

- The curing process was set as follows: autoclave for 5-6 hours at 122°C and after curing process according to ASTM C495 standard.

In the report, we have proposed two alternative materials for the inner core and one material for the outer core of the floater pontoon. The structural properties of the floating pontoon depend on the combination of materials used for both the inner core and outer shell, along with reinforcement. While these individual materials meet the necessary requirements for designing the circular breakwater, a conclusive decision on which materials and combinations (HPC or LWAC) to use for the outer shell would require casting a component and assessing durability. Similarly, how much cellular concrete is required for the inner core will be assessed during the casting and testing of the component.

In the next step, first, we will cast the component and after that, we will assess the durability of the chosen optimal concrete mix designs for the breakwater's shell. The amount of cellular concrete to maintain the structural behavior and floatability will be estimated. Additionally, we'll evaluate the mechanical performance of the interface between the outer shell and the inner core. This evaluation is crucial to ensure the mechanical integrity of the breakwater component, which will be further developed in Task 4.4. The results of these characterization tests will be documented in Deliverable D4.2, focusing on the development, validation, and laboratory testing of the breakwater prototype.
