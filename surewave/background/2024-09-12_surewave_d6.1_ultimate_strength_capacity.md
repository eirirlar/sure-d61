**EXECUTIVE SUMMARY**

The document herein reports the activities carried out in Task 6.2 concerning tests performed in SINTEF laboratories to characterise the failure modes and strength capacities of three critical connection systems developed and used in the FPV concept developed in the SUREWAVE project: The Poly Urethane (PU) hinges between solar modules, the cable systems between the breakwaters and the newly developed connections system between the breakwater and the solar modules.

Section 1 covers the deliverable with an introduction and outline of the objectives of the deliverable.

Section 2 focuses on the evaluation of the mechanical properties and strength of the PU hinges.

Section 3 focuses on the evaluation of the strength of the steel wire systems between the breakwaters.

Section 4 focuses on the evaluation of the strength of the connection systems between the breakwaters and modules.

The report ends with conclusions in section 5.

#  Introduction

The report summarises the work done in task 6.2 of the SUREWAVE project. The aim of the task was to assess the ultimate strength of the critical components of the floating PV system defined in WP3, to check the survivability of the connection systems present in the FPV farm during extreme scenarios (storm conditions). These are the weak points of the system with highest failure rate probability. The task has thus focused on assessing the ultimate strength of the following components (see [Figure 1](#_Ref170808604) [Figure 19](#_Ref171416360) and [Figure 25](#_Ref176770038)):

- The steel cable connections between two breakwater components and used by Clement (made of steel rope and rubber hinge),

- The special connections between breakwater and solar modules that were designed in the project,

- The connections between solar modules: PU hinges.

For each connection, the main failure modes have been identified. To assess ultimate strength, the connections were tested until failure to determine the maximum load. Specifically, for the PU hinges, additional tests were conducted to evaluate the stiffness of the components in each direction, which was crucial for the assessments in WP5, and the lab basin tests in task 6.4.

Given that two of the mechanical connections in this project are custom-designed (combining materials and joint technology), SINTEF utilized both standard and in-house test methods, allowing for setup adaptations to meet specific requirements. Custom test setups were also machined to fit the specimens in the lab equipment. Advanced strain measurement techniques (DIC) and cameras were employed during some tests, providing detailed information on localized deformation in the joints.

# Mechanical behaviour and strength of PU hinges between modules

## Experimental matrix 

The first component assessed in the task 6.2 is the PU hinges developed by SUNLIT Sea to connect the solar modules together. Two hinges are used per module sides, for a total of 8 connection points per module, see [Figure 1](#_Ref170808604).

The campaign to characterize the mechanical behaviour of the PU hinges is summarised in [Table 1](#_Ref170814787) below. ## Tensile tests on ISO527b specimens

The ISO527 is a standard that defines the specimen shape and machining procedure recommended for testing of polymeric and plastics materials[^1]. During the tests, the strain and displacements in the specimens were monitored using the DIC technique. To this end, a speckle pattern was painted on each of the specimens, and its evolution was followed by a Basler camera, see [Figure 2](#_Ref170815862). The acquisition system recorded images at a 2 Hz frequency with an average resolution of 900×2980. The acquisition system of the camera was synchronized with the tensile machine data acquisition system. All recorded images were post-processed using the eCorr software[^2]. The objective of the DIC is to provide full-field strain measurement during the test, using a digital grid, see [Figure 3](#_Ref170816332), left. To compare the results on the different specimens, the strain was calculated using the same virtual extensometers, that are defined along the full gauge length of the specimens (see [Figure 3](#_Ref170816332), right).

This enables to have access to local strain field measurements. Note that the curves presented in the results sections have been post-processed and retreated to account for the offsets that can occur during the initial positioning of the machine.

From the extensometer, the displacements, elongation and strain can be computed.

$$\frac{l - l_{0}}{l_{0}}$$

$$\sigma = \frac{F}{A}$$

$$\varepsilon_{l} = \ln\frac{l}{l_{0}}$$

### Iso527b tensile tests results 

The results of the tensile tests are given in [Figure 5](#_Ref170896434). The material exhibits typical elastomeric highly nonlinear behaviour, with a very high elongation (or strain to failure), around 300-350%, and a strong strain hardening behaviour until failure. This is even more visible on the stress vs strain curves depicted on the right side of [Figure 5](#_Ref170896434). Finally, the rate effects on this material are found to be minimal within the tested strain rate range, as the curves at different strain rates show very similar behaviour.

The apparent modulus, defined as $E = \frac{\sigma}{\varepsilon}$, evolves with deformation as depicted by the blue curves in [Figure 5](#_Ref170896434) (right), and on the left picture in [Figure 6](#_Ref170898201), from around 20 to 70 MPa: it first increases until a strain of about 0.05, then decreases to reach a plateau until a strain of about 0.7 and increases again until final failure.

# Conclusions

The deliverable focused on reporting the results obtained from the experimental campaign performed in task 6.2 to assess the ultimate strength of three critical connections present in the FPV solar system developed in the SUREWAVE project. From the results, the following conclusions can be made:

[For the Poly Urethane (PU) hinges connecting the solar modules together, there are a few critical points that requires attention:]{.underline}

- The Hinges were found **very deformable**, due to the Polyurethane elastomer used. This can potentially lead to **hinges loosen up from the module** fixtures during high wave motions.

- They have a **Relatively low ultimate strength (Average 3.5 kN**), while a load of around 8-10kN are expected from the work carried out in WP5[^7].

- They have a **very low compressive strength**, due to buckling, which may lead to **collision between modules in operation** and high snap loads.

- They exhibit very high shear and torsion deformation indicating that tension is the only failure mode to consider during operation.

Following the points highlighted during the testing of the PU hinges, it is highly **recommended to redesign these hinges, to at least meet the requirements in terms of ultimate** **strength**. A full redesign could also be made in order to avoid the problem of collision that can arise from the low compressive strength.

[In the case of the steel wires connecting the breakwater]{.underline}:

- The ultimate strength was found to be 402kN, **in agreement with the rated strength of 450kN**

- Two failure modes were observed: Failure of the steel twins in main rope or loosen of the steel wire from its fixation socket

Overall, the steel wire system meets the expectations in terms of mechanical performances, and **the system seems to be robust and reliable** for its function.

[For the tailored designed connection between the modules and the breakwater :]{.underline}

- The ultimate strength was found to be 41 kN, **in agreement with the requirements from D3.3**

- The failure is always observed in the ropes, at the contact area with the steel bracket, due to tearing, or in the main rope body.

Overall, the new connection **is very robust and operates at the desired load levels.** It is also very easy to increase the connection strength by choosing a stronger rope if needed. Finally, some improvement could be made to the bracket's geometry, to smooth the sharp edges and avoid tearing during contact with the rope. This will also increase the ultimate strength capacity of the connection system.
