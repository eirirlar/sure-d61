# Competitors and substitutes – nearshore and offshore floating solar (desk research, 14 November 2026)

Scope: competitor and substitute scan for a business plan for a wave-tolerant, modular, nearshore floating solar product (0.3–10 MW, Nordic fjords and harbours first, then Baltic and Mediterranean). Desk research of about 30 web searches and fetches on 14 November 2026. Every claim carries a source tag [S#] resolved in section 5, marked P (primary: company, official body, project owner) or S (secondary: trade press, aggregators). Search-engine summaries were used to locate sources; where only a summary was available and the underlying page was not opened, this is noted as "via search summary". Items marked "unknown" were not found in this pass, not confirmed absent.

## 1. Key findings

1. The European offshore/nearshore floating solar field is still entirely at pilot stage in late 2026. The largest European marine units are about 0.5–1 MW (BlueNewables/Naturgy at Valencia, Oceans of Energy at Hollandse Kust Noord, SolarDuck Merganser). No European supplier has a commercial multi-MW marine reference in operation [S9][S12][S15][S6].
2. The best-known North Sea commercial pilot was withdrawn. CrossWind (Shell/Eneco) reported on 1 May 2026 that the Oceans of Energy system at Hollandse Kust Noord (about 1,400 panels, installed August 2025) suffered "thermal incidents" shortly after installation, traced to a manufacturing defect in a second connector type; the system was returned to IJmuiden and the innovation project concluded [S9, P]. The current business-plan table still lists this as an assembled 0.5 MW array and should be updated.
3. Fred. Olsen 1848 (BRIZO) is the closest Nordic competitor on the "exposed nearshore" positioning. It holds a DNV Statement of Conformity (DNV-RP-0584) for a 3 MW BRIZO island at Hs up to 3.5 m, gusts to 65 m/s, current 0.38 m/s, and aims to have the product qualified for deployment in the first half of 2027; it is updating documentation to DNV-ST-C108 and DNV-ST-E309 [S5, S][S4, P]. It runs pilots at Risør (124 kW), Ramskjær (Norway) and EDP's Alto Rabagão reservoir in Portugal [S5][S6].
4. BRIZO targets utility-scale nearshore, large lakes and hydropower reservoirs, i.e. the same adjacent segment (wave-exposed inland water) the business plan names for its EIC Transition demonstrator [S5, S]. Expect head-to-head competition there from 2027.
5. Ocean Sun (Oslo-listed) has the widest installed reference list of the Nordic marine players (Norway, Asia, Maldives, Canary Islands 270 kWp, Brazil 300 kWp first power October 2026), but is financially thin: secondary summaries report cash of NOK 6.7 million at end Q1 2026 and NOK 21.1 million at 30 June 2026, with H1 2026 operating loss of NOK 10.3 million [S1][S2][S3, S]. Its strategy is a capital-light licensing model (ACEN MoU for Asia, May 2026) [S3].
6. BlueNewables (Tenerife) with Naturgy has the largest Mediterranean marine unit: two PV-bos platforms of 64 m × 41 m, 600 modules each, 500 kW each, 1 MW total at the Port of Valencia, funded by IDAE/NextGenerationEU; first unit arrived 13 July 2026 and an October 2026 report describes the full 1 MW as installed and in testing [S12, P][S13, S][S14, S]. The concept is a large raised catamaran built by a shipyard, with no published cost.
7. SolarDuck has repositioned towards offshore oil and gas: an "Offshore Floating Power Hub" for subsea tiebacks and CCS, backed by a €3.2 million Dutch RVO subsidy with MARIN (June 2026) [S17][S18]. Its 5 MW hybrid demonstrator at RWE's Hollandse Kust West VII was due in 2026; no 2026 confirmation of operation was found. Last disclosed equity round €15 million (December 2023; Katapult Ocean, Green Tower, ETF Rotterdam, Invest-NL) [S19, P]. Its 540 MW Calabria wind–solar hybrid (120 MWp solar) is targeted for 2028 [S20].
8. Asia is at utility scale, but mostly not floating. CHN Energy's 1 GW Dongying project, 8 km offshore Shandong, is fixed-pile steel truss, fully grid-connected around end 2025, total investment about CNY 8.1 billion (about USD 1.16 billion, implying roughly USD 1.1–1.2/W including a 100 MW/200 MWh battery) [S27][S28, S]. A 1.8 GW offshore PV project at Changli, Hebei, is under construction [S26, S].
9. Genuinely floating seawater projects in China are small: Sinopec Qingdao (put into use 1 July 2025, reported as about 7.5 MW, factory-scale all-seawater floating PV) [S26]; CIMC Raffles 400 kWp semi-submersible (2023) and a 100 kW bamboo-based platform at Yantai (August 2026) [S24][S25]. Taiwan's HEXA Renewables Changhua plant (373 MWac, about 440 MW DC, Ciel & Terre floats, commissioned November 2024) is the largest "offshore floating" plant, but is coastal/intertidal-type water leased from government rather than open sea [S29][S30].
10. Inland FPV majors are moving towards harsher water but have not launched marine products. Ciel & Terre is working with Principia to certify Hydrelio as "heavy duty" and launched Fusio (October 2025, certified 2026) for GW-scale inland [S21]. Sungrow FPV has DNV mooring verification and an offshore R&D programme since 2019, but no named offshore floating reference was found [S23]. Isigenere and Zimmermann remain freshwater-focused [S31][S32].
11. New entrants with a direct nearshore claim appeared in 2025–2026: Landatu Solar (Basque, founded 2022) tested a 12 m × 12 m stretched-membrane/mesh SEAMOD demonstrator in the Port of Bilbao and is preparing a funding round [S33][S34]; Reverlast (Helsinki) markets 100 kWp, 100 m-long modules made from decommissioned wind-turbine blades for Hs up to 4 m, 0.5–15 km offshore, but lists no pilot [S35, P]. HelioRec (France) has port pilots (Brest 25 kW, Oostende) and a Shoreham Port MoU (April 2025) [S36].
12. Island resort markets are a proven niche served by Swimsol (Austria): 2.4 MW at Cheval Blanc Randheli (April 2026) and 1,037 kWp at Fari Islands (2025), with over 50 Maldives resort islands on its solar [S37][S38]. Ocean Sun also sells into this niche (Soneva Secret, January 2026) [S1].
13. Heavy offshore concepts set the upper bound on design waves but at high structural mass: Saipem/Moss Maritime XolarSurf (designed for waves up to 8 m, full-scale prototype in the Trondheim fjord, 2025 concept work for subsea and aquaculture power) [S10][S11]; SINN Power Ocean Hybrid Platform (claimed Hs 12 m) has moved commercially to vertical bifacial floating PV on German lakes (October 2025) [S39].
14. The recurring failure mode in 2025–2026 is electrical and balance-of-system reliability at sea (Oceans of Energy connector fire) rather than structural collapse. This supports positioning on standard modules, proven marine electrical components and DNV-ST-C108/E309 compliance. Few dedicated floating-solar insolvencies were found; the clearest consolidation signal is Thames Water's parent winding up its floating-solar subsidiary Trinzic Operations (37 redundancies) [S40, S]. Wave energy neighbours (Wello) failed after device losses at sea [S40].
15. No competitor publishes a price or cost per watt for a marine floating system. The only cost anchor found is the Chinese pile-mounted figure in item 8. Competition will therefore be decided on documented references, DNV compliance and quoted EPC price per project.

## 3. Detailed profiles

### 3.1 Ocean Sun (Norway)

- Concept: hydroelastic membrane stretched in a floating ring; panels lie on the membrane, which follows the waves and transfers heat to the water.
- Design conditions: designed for sheltered to moderate conditions. The EU BOOST project (coordinated by Ocean Sun, PLOCAN partner) deployed a 270 kWp offshore demonstrator at Tazacorte, La Palma, opened December 2023, to certify the technology for non-sheltered sites; a site-specific DNV design verification is reported [S7, S][S8, S]. No final offshore certification outcome was found.
- References: Soneva Secret, Maldives, completed January 2026 [S1, P via Euronext/Inderes]; 300 kWp R&D plant with UAPÊ in Pindamonhangaba, Brazil, first power 21 October 2026 [S1]; 200 kW Magat reservoir, Philippines, with GCL-SI [S1, S].
- Pipeline: non-binding MoU with ACEN-Silverwolf for utility-scale projects in Asia (May 2026) [S3, S]; earlier agreement with MP Quantum for Greece and Cyprus (2021, 2–4 MWp pilot then larger), no build confirmed [S20b, S].
- Funding: Euronext Growth Oslo listed. Secondary summaries: Q1 2026 operating income NOK 2.1 m, net loss NOK 6.4 m, cash NOK 6.7 m; H1 2026 operating income NOK 4.0 m, operating loss NOK 10.3 m, cash NOK 21.1 m at 30 June 2026, no interest-bearing debt [S3, S]. Licensing revenue small (NOK 0.3 m in Q3 2025) [S3]. NOK 8 m public funding for the EROS project on wave, salinity and soiling effects (February 2026) [S2, P].
- Business model: capital-light licensing plus resort projects [S3].
- Strengths vs a wave-tolerant modular nearshore product: longest operating record at sea in Norway and Asia, low material use, listed visibility. Weaknesses: limited wave tolerance of a membrane concept, modules mounted flat on membrane (non-standard), thin balance sheet, revenue still mostly grants.

### 3.2 Fred. Olsen 1848 – BRIZO (Norway)

- Concept: pretensioned fibre-rope mesh; each floater has four anchoring points and loads go directly to the moorings rather than through neighbouring floats. HDPE rope locks, PE pipes, bridles (8–10 per mooring line) and surface buoys; floating walkway carries cabling and string inverters; tension buoy for reservoirs with large level swings [S5, S].
- Design conditions: DNV Statement of Conformity to DNV-RP-0584 for a 3 MW island, Hs up to 3.5 m, gusts 65 m/s, current 0.38 m/s, 10 cm marine growth; caption refers to targeting sites with waves up to 7 m (inconsistent with the SoC) [S5]. DNV news item July 2026 describes an independent technical review of design methodology, hydrodynamic loads (from scale-model tests at SINTEF), structural behaviour and test procedures [S4, P].
- References: 124 kW pilot at Risør, Norway, deployed 2024 [S6, P]; tension-buoy pilot at EDP Floating PV Lab, Alto Rabagão, Portugal, early 2026 [S5][S16]; Ramskjær pilot in Norway, new tension buoy to be installed fall 2026 [S5].
- Plans: documentation to DNV-ST-C108 and DNV-ST-E309; product qualified and ready for deployment in H1 2027 [S5]. Target segments: utility scale, nearshore, large lakes, hydropower reservoirs [S5].
- Funding: part of the privately held Fred. Olsen group; no external round found.
- Business model and prices: not published.
- Strengths: strong owner, DNV-reviewed design basis, offshore wind heritage, tow-out of a pre-assembled island. Weaknesses: mooring-intensive design (each float moored), installation needs pretensioning and limited water-level variation unless tension buoys are used, no commercial plant yet.

### 3.3 SolarDuck (Netherlands)

- Concept: elevated triangular platforms with modules several metres above the sea; array connected by flexible connectors.
- References: 0.5 MWp Merganser pilot with RWE at the North Sea Farmers test site [S15, S]; 80–100 kW unit in Tokyo Bay central breakwater area for Tokyu Land's eSG project, installed around May 2024 [S22, S]. MARIN station-keeping tests of a 54-platform array [S17].
- Pipeline: 5 MW hybrid demonstrator at RWE Hollandse Kust West VII (due 2026; operation not confirmed) [S19]; 540 MW Calabria hybrid with Green Arrow Capital and New Developments (120 MWp floating PV, target 2028) [S20, S].
- Funding: €15 m in December 2023, Invest-NL €5 m of it [S19, P]; €3.2 m RVO subsidy with MARIN (June 2026) for the Offshore Floating Power Hub [S18, P].
- Strategy 2026: subsea tiebacks, CCS and remote offshore assets; OTC 2026 paper claims competitiveness with umbilicals at 10–30 km [S17, S].
- Strengths: offshore wind partnerships, high wave clearance, Japan foothold. Weaknesses vs nearshore modular: heavy structure per kWp, offshore marine operations, focus moving away from coastal industry power.

### 3.4 Oceans of Energy (Netherlands)

- Concept: modular floaters, arrays assembled in port, towed out and connected by dynamic export cable [S9].
- Hollandse Kust Noord: contract with CrossWind (Shell/Eneco) in 2023, about 1,400 panels (sources differ, 0.5–1 MW), tested in IJmuiden spring 2025, installed offshore August 2025. Thermal incidents shortly after installation; a manufacturing defect in a second connector type; system returned to port; project concluded, partners to share lessons (CrossWind, 1 May 2026) [S9, P].
- Funding: about €30 m reported, plus early grant and seed rounds [S41, S]. Coordinated EU joint industry project with 15 partners (RINA) from February 2024 [S41].
- Assessment: reputational setback for the only commercial North Sea contract; no next project announced. Shows that marine electrical BoS is a critical risk.

### 3.5 BlueNewables (Spain)

- Concept: PV-bos raised catamaran platforms, 64 m × 41 m deck, 600 modules, 500 kW each; panels high above sea surface [S13, S].
- Project: 1 MW (2 × 500 kW) at Port of Valencia with Naturgy, Alusin Solar (structure), San Enrique shipyard in Vigo (build), coordinated by the Port Authority; expected about 1,500 MWh/year; IDAE grant under Renmarinas Valenciaport (NextGenerationEU). First unit "Paiporta" arrived 13 July 2026 for testing outside the southern breakwater [S12, P][S13]. October 2026 report: full installation in testing [S14, S].
- Plans: global expansion through steelmakers, ports, industrial partners and shipyards [S13].
- Strengths: utility partner, port host, shipyard supply chain. Weaknesses: very large rigid units, shipyard-built steel cost, no published design wave.

### 3.6 HelioRec (France)

- Concept: floating platforms using recycled plastic, aimed at ports and dense coastal areas.
- References: 25 kW Port of Brest (October 2023, inspected June 2025; 7 m tidal range); Port of Oostende pilot under DUAL Ports [S36, S]. MoU with Shoreham Port, UK (April 2025) [S36]. STEWART project and EU-backed project with partners (2025) [S36].
- Funding: First Imagine! investment (April 2023) [S36]. Amounts unknown.
- Assessment: small-unit port niche competitor; scale and wave tolerance unproven.

### 3.7 SINN Power (Germany)

- Ocean Hybrid Platform (wave, wind, PV), claimed design for Hs 12 m and 27 m/s, modules up to 20 kW, modular to 10 MW; SOcean containerised platform up to 40 kWp tested at Heraklion [S39, S, mostly 2020–2021 sources].
- October 2025: commissioned a lake plant with vertically mounted floating panels, plans to extend to open sea [S39].
- Assessment: commercially active on inland vertical FPV; no marine commercial plant.

### 3.8 Saipem / Moss Maritime – XolarSurf (Italy/Norway)

- Concept from Moss Maritime, cooperation with Equinor from March 2020 for near-coastal floating solar; designed for waves up to 8 m [S10, S]. Earlier Equinor/Moss pilot off Frøya (80 m², raised 3 m) passed trials [S11, S].
- Full-scale XolarSurf prototype tested in the Trondheim fjord for about one year; 2025 concept work on powering subsea facilities and a combined solar–wind–battery system; evaluation for nearshore aquaculture [S10][S11, Moss brochure 2026, P].
- Assessment: credible offshore engineering, aimed at oil and gas and aquaculture rather than small industrial PPAs. Adriatic hydrogen hub concept unconfirmed in timing.

### 3.9 Swimsol (Austria)

- SolarSea rigid floating platforms in reef-sheltered tropical lagoons. 2.4 MW at Cheval Blanc Randheli (reported April 2026), claimed saving USD 1.5 m per year; 1,037 kWp Fari Islands (2025); 1,080 kWp Taj Exotica; over 50 Maldives resort islands use Swimsol solar (roof and sea combined) [S37, S][S38, P].
- Funding and ownership: unknown in this pass.
- Assessment: proves willingness to pay for marine FPV where diesel is the alternative; conditions (tropical lagoon) differ from Nordic fjords (ice, cold, storms).

### 3.10 Ciel & Terre (France)

- Hydrelio inland pontoons; Fusio for GW-scale (launched October 2025, certified 2026); WattRack (December 2025); first full-scope EPC in Italy (June 2026) [S21, S].
- Marine: collaboration with Principia to certify Hydrelio as heavy-duty for harsh conditions (date not shown) [S21]. Supplied floats for HEXA's Changhua plant extension [S29].
- Assessment: the most likely inland incumbent to move into sheltered coastal water; partner in SuRE (per business plan).

### 3.11 Sungrow FPV (China)

- Largest floating-structure supplier; Cirata 192 MW (Indonesia), DNV verification of mooring design, SGF-TS30M launched at Intersolar May 2025, 4.99 MW Carmen Copper mine (August 2025); offshore FPV R&D since 2019 and a co-located wind–solar demonstration in progress (location unknown) [S23, S].
- Assessment: price setter for inland floats; potential fast follower in nearshore if a standard emerges.

### 3.12 Isigenere / Isifloating (Spain) and Zimmermann (Germany)

- Isigenere: >100 installations, HDPE and cork floats (EDP Alqueva 5 MW), Seaflex moorings; says offshore would need a different structural design (2022) [S31, S].
- Zimmermann ZIM Float: freshwater "solar boats", about 20 % water coverage, wind tolerance 160 km/h average and 220 km/h gusts [S32, S].
- Neither has a marine product found in this pass.

### 3.13 Other European entrants

- Landatu Solar (Spain): SEAMOD modular stretched-membrane and mesh with outer buoyancy frame, 12 × 12 m demonstrator at Santurtzi dock, Port of Bilbao; validation complete March 2026; funding round in preparation [S16, S][S33, P][S34, S].
- Reverlast (Finland): standardised ~100 kWp modules, 100 m × 5 m, 30 t, from decommissioned wind blades; 1 MW = 12 modules (about 120 × 100 m); up to 3 MWh battery per MWp; Hs up to 4 m, depth up to 50 m; claims 5–6 months from site confirmation to first power; no reference listed [S35, P].
- Tractebel (Engie) SEAVOLT with DEME and Jan De Nul: large floating metal frame; Belgian coast test site planned summer 2023 with Ghent University; patent pending [S42, S]. No 2026 status found.
- Heliofloat: grouped in a 2023 Marine Structures review with Swimsol and SolarDuck as large-float concepts tested in rough seas [S43, S]; no current activity found.

### 3.14 Asia

- CHN Energy (Guohua Energy Investment): 1 GW fixed-pile offshore PV 8 km off Dongying, Shandong, about 1,223 ha, first units November 2024, full grid connection around end 2025, 1.78 TWh/year expected, CNY 8.1 bn (about USD 1.16 bn), 100 MW/200 MWh storage, integrated with aquaculture [S27, S][S28, S].
- Changli, Hebei: 1.8 GW offshore PV demonstration under construction; developer not confirmed [S26].
- Sinopec Qingdao Refining: China's first factory-scale all-seawater floating PV, in use 1 July 2025 (about 7.5 MW) [S26, S].
- CIMC Raffles: 400 kWp semi-submersible (four units) delivered April 2023 [S24, S]; 100 kW bamboo-based "Jilin-2" platform at Yantai supplying an ammonia/hydrogen/methanol demonstration, August 2026, plans for MW scale [S25, S].
- CNOOC, Huaneng, CTG: no floating marine project found in this pass (CNOOC has reported offshore PV on platforms historically; not verified here).
- Taiwan: HEXA Renewables (I Squared Capital) Changhua, 373 MWac (pv magazine: about 440 MW), 347 ha of government-leased coastal plots, commissioned November 2024; phase 1 181 MWac by Chenya Energy 2020; extension with Ciel & Terre [S29, P][S30, S].
- Japan: Tokyo Bay eSG demonstrator (Tokyu Land, SolarDuck, Kyocera Communication Systems, Everblue), 80–100 kW, 2024; safety assessment presented at JSCE 2026 [S22].
- Korea: no 2025–2026 marine FPV result found in this pass (Saemangeum is a reclaimed tidal area; not verified).

## 4. Gaps

- No published prices, cost per watt or LCOE for any European marine floating system. Only Chinese pile-mounted capex found.
- BRIZO business model, pricing and the size of Ramskjær pilot not published.
- SolarDuck HKW VII 5 MW status in 2026 not confirmed; sources conflict on 5 MW vs 520 kWp.
- Ocean Sun financials taken from secondary summaries (Quartr, allmind); primary quarterly reports not opened. Cash jump between Q1 and Q2 2026 not explained (likely a share issue; unverified).
- Saipem XolarSurf prototype date not confirmed (Saipem page returned 403).
- Swimsol, HelioRec, SINN Power, Landatu, Reverlast funding amounts unknown.
- Patents: not searched systematically (only SEAVOLT "patent pending" and Isigenere "patented floats" noted). A Espacenet search on BRIZO, Ocean Sun and SolarDuck families is recommended before IP claims in the plan.
- Korea, CNOOC, Huaneng, CTG marine FPV not covered; Baltic-specific competitors (Poland, Baltic states, Sweden, Finland) beyond Reverlast not found.
- Sinopec Qingdao capacity (7.5 MW) is from search summaries, not opened page.
