# Business model and finance desk research – nearshore floating PV

Prepared 2026-11-14 as background for the SUREWAVE KER1 business plan draft. Perspective: economics, business models and financing. About 30 web searches and fetches. Primary = issuer's own publication (report, filing, press release); secondary = trade press or third-party summary of it. Search snippets were AI-summarised by the search tool; numbers marked "secondary" should be checked against the original before they go into a deliverable.

## 1) Key findings

- Floating PV on calm artificial water cost USD 1.29/Wdc for a 10 MWdc fixed-tilt US system in Q1 2021, versus USD 1.03/Wdc for ground-mount: a premium of USD 0.26/Wdc (25 %), driven by about 300 % higher structural cost (floats and anchoring). LCOE about 20 % above ground-mount. (NREL, Ramasamy and Margolis, TP-7A40-80695, Oct 2021, https://docs.nlr.gov/docs/fy22osti/80695.pdf – primary)
- The same NREL report gives float cost by system size: USD 0.40/Wdc at 2 MW, 0.36 at 5 MW, 0.30 at 10 MW, 0.20 at 50 MW; European-made HDPE floats shipped to the US USD 0.22–0.90/Wdc. Floats are 75–85 % of structural BOS. (NREL 2021, ibid. – primary). This is the closest public benchmark for the €0.30–0.40/W kit target.
- Wood Mackenzie (2024) puts the floating capex premium over ground-mount at USD 0.13–0.15/W, narrowed by module prices below USD 0.10/W. (Wood Mackenzie analyst H. Kanwar, quoted via pv magazine / CleanTechnica, Nov–Dec 2024, https://cleantechnica.com/2024/12/11/floating-solar-power-to-rise-to-77-gigawatts-by-2033/ – secondary)
- Ground-mount benchmarks: IRENA 2024 total installed cost for utility PV in Europe about USD 779/kW, global weighted average USD 691/kW; global utility PV LCOE USD 0.043/kWh. (IRENA, Renewable Power Generation Costs in 2024, June 2025, https://www.irena.org/Publications/2025/Jun/Renewable-Power-Generation-Costs-in-2024 – primary; figures via pv magazine https://www.pv-magazine.com/?p=310775 – secondary)
- Fraunhofer ISE (2024) puts ground-mounted PV LCOE in Germany at 4.3–9.2 €ct/kWh (press reports 4.1); PV plus battery 6.0–10.8 €ct/kWh; floating PV not covered. (Fraunhofer ISE, LCOE study, July 2024, https://www.ise.fraunhofer.de/content/dam/ise/en/documents/publications/studies/EN2024_ISE_Study_Levelized_Cost_of_Electricity_Renewable_Energy_Technologies.pdf – primary)
- Academic estimates for open coastal / offshore FPV in Northern Europe are far above the reference plant's €0.062/kWh: about €250/MWh in Iberia rising to about €650/MWh in northern Norway (Martinez and Iglesias, Solar Energy, 2024, https://research.ucc.ie/en/publications/mapping-of-the-levelised-cost-of-energy-from-floating-solar-pv-in/ – primary abstract, numbers via search summary); €257/MWh at Sørlige Nordsjø II and €287/MWh at Utsira Nord, 90 % interval €203–407/MWh, floats 51 % of capex (Renewable Energy vol. 249, 2025, https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/renene/v249y2025ics0960148125006433.html – secondary summary).
- Global offshore FPV assessment (Rodríguez-Gallegos et al., Energy and Environmental Science 18, 2025, DOI 10.1039/D5EE04460J) benchmarks its cost model against 2020–2025 tenders and finds LCOE below USD 0.10/kWh achievable in 34 % of offshore regions, best in Indonesia, Thailand, Malaysia; validation includes Norwegian sites. Capex figures not retrieved. (pv magazine, 5 Dec 2025, https://www.pv-magazine.com/2025/12/05/offshore-solar-could-achieve-lcoe-of-less-than-0-06-kwh-in-thailand-malaysia/ – secondary)
- Tracker suppliers with fully outsourced manufacturing earn 32–34 % gross margin: Nextracker FY2025 (to 31 Mar 2025) GAAP gross margin 34.1 %, revenue up USD 459.4 m (18 %), but cost of sales reduced by USD 224.9 m of US 45X credits (Nextracker 10-K FY2025, https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1852131/000185213125000021/nxt-20250331.htm – primary); Array Technologies 2024 GAAP 32.5 %, adjusted 34.1 %, with a net loss of USD 296.1 m (Array Q4/FY2024 release, https://www.nasdaq.com/articles/array-technologies-reports-fourth-quarter-and-full-year-2024-financial-results-9158 – secondary).
- Soltec is the cautionary case for vertical expansion into EPC and development: H1 2024 net loss about €126 m from impairments and EPC losses, credit line matured unrenewed, then a February 2025 decision to exit EPC and development and focus solely on trackers, followed by sale of development assets (€22.1 m) and a new shareholder. (Soltec press releases 27 Feb 2025 and 22 Jul 2025, https://soltec.com/en/press-room/soltec-will-focus-the-future-of-its-business-on-the-supply-of-solar-trackers-to-drive-its-recovery/ – primary; pv-tech, https://www.pv-tech.org/soltec-to-focus-exclusively-on-tracker-business-following-2024-losses – secondary)
- The closest Nordic FPV peer runs a licence model: revenue mainly from licence fees per Wp installed plus engineering (FEED) fees; it reported a bill of materials around USD 0.50/Wp for a standard 74 m ring (2020). Its May 2026 share offering targeted NOK 15–20 m at NOK 0.50 per share, which illustrates the funding difficulty of a listed licence-model FPV company. (Annual report 2024 via financialreports.eu, https://financialreports.eu/filings/ocean-sun/annual-report/2024/2544445/; Euronext notice 22 May 2026, https://live.euronext.com/en/products/equities/company-news/2026-05-22-ocean-sun-contemplated-offering – primary)
- Equity raised by marine-energy peers: SolarDuck €15 m (Dec 2023; Katapult Ocean, Green Tower, Energy Transition Fund Rotterdam, Invest-NL) (pv magazine press release, https://www.pv-magazine.com/press-releases/solarduck-secures-15-million-euro-funding-for-the-further-development-of-offshore-floating-solar-power/ – primary); CorPower Ocean (wave) Series B €53 m total led by Nordic Ninja, SEB Greentech, InnoEnergy, later joined by EIC Fund and Algebris, plus EIC Accelerator award of €2.5 m grant + €15 m equity (2025) (Ocean Energy Europe, https://www.oceanenergy-europe.eu/corpower-ocean-awarded-up-to-e17-5-million-in-eic-accelerator-funding-to-scale-up-wave-energy/ – secondary; ImpactAlpha, https://impactalpha.com/corpower-ocean-closes-e53-million-to-commercialize-ocean-wave-energy/ – secondary).
- Develop-own-operate with debt works at small scale in marine solar: Swimsol (Maldives, sea-based solar for resorts under PPAs priced below diesel) financed growth with a USD 14 m syndicated loan from responsAbility and OeEB (2021) and a USD 20 m facility (end 2024) for 18 projects / 16 MW. (responsAbility press releases, https://www.responsability.com/en/press-releases/responsability-provides-usd-20-million-facility-to-swimsol-for-solar-projects-in-the-maldives – primary)
- Develop-and-sell exists for inland FPV in Europe: BayWa r.e./GroenLeven sold a 46 MW floating project in Friesland to a local cooperative (Dec 2025); Q Energy sold 50 % of the 74.3 MW Les Îlots Blandin plant to Velto Renewables and raised over €50 m debt from Crédit Agricole Transitions & Energies and Bpifrance. (BayWa r.e., https://www.baywa-re.fr/en/news/baywa-r-e-successfully-completes-sale-of-largest-floating-pv-project-in-the-netherlands – primary; Solarplaza, 4 Aug 2026, https://www.solarplaza.com/resource/13527/article-where-floating-solar-creates-real-value-in-europe-2026/ – secondary)
- Price thresholds a Nordic plant must beat are low: LevelTen European solar PPA average €34.25/MWh in Q3 2025 (https://www.pv-tech.org/european-solar-ppa-prices-fall-below-35-mwh-q3-2025/ – secondary); KYOS 10-year solar capture price estimates (Feb 2025) SE3 about €50.6/MWh, SE4 €51.9/MWh (https://www.kyos.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/11/KYOS-PPA-report-Feb-2025.pdf – primary, column headers not verified); Norwegian power-intensive industry paid on average 42 øre/kWh in 2025 excluding taxes and grid tariff (SSB, https://www.ssb.no/en/energi-og-industri/energi/statistikk/elektrisitetspriser/article-for-electricity-prices/lowest-electricity-price-since-2020 – primary).
- Lender and insurer focus for FPV: key permits before debt; technical due diligence on extreme-weather assumptions, fatigue loads, anchoring, cable movement, warranties and maintenance access; insurers review mooring exposure, storm resilience and repair access (Solarplaza, Aug 2026, ibid. – secondary). Insurers describe FPV as unfamiliar to most domestic insurers at utility scale (WTW, May 2024, https://wtwco.com/en-us/insights/2024/05/surveying-the-risk-landscape-of-an-emerging-solar-sector – primary). The 2024 storm damage at the Omkareshwar reservoir plant in India days after start-up is the most cited recent failure (Insurance Thought Leadership, https://www.insurancethoughtleadership.com/commercial-lines/solar-power-comes-new-hazards – secondary).

## 2) Detailed notes

### 2.1 Capex benchmarks

NREL Q1 2021 floating PV benchmark (primary, downloaded and read via pdftotext). Base case: 10 MWdc, fixed tilt, US, artificial water body, wind load 38.85 m/s, snow 20 psf, depth 50 m, water-level variation 10 m, swell 1 m, module efficiency 19.9 %, float cost USD 0.30/Wdc. Results: FPV initial investment USD 12.9 m vs ground-mount USD 10.3 m for 10 MWdc (USD 1.29 vs 1.03/Wdc). Drivers: structural BOS about 300 % higher; contingency 5 % vs 3 %; extra 5 % shipping/handling on floats and anchors; site investigation higher; installation labour and equipment 50 % lower than ground-mount; no fencing/roads. Structural BOS is 25–30 % of total cost, floats 75–85 % of structural BOS. Shore anchors assumed 20 per MW below 30 MW. FPV O&M assumed equal to ground-mount O&M minus land lease (the report notes the industry disagrees: boats and divers versus no vegetation management). Yield assumption 1,527 vs 1,570 kWh/kWdc (Kansas) including a 3 % cooling gain. Real discount rate 5.1 %, debt fraction 71.8 %. Note: 2021 module and freight prices were high; absolute numbers are dated but structure ratios remain useful.

World Bank / SERIS (2019) floating solar market report: FPV capex about USD 0.8–1.2/Wp, slightly higher than or comparable with ground-mount; BOS plus mounting 39 % of capex for FPV vs 29 % for ground-mount in a 50 MW example. (Via ERIA appendix and PV-Tech summary, https://www.pv-tech.org/wp-content/uploads/legacy-publication-pdfs/9e59b1a3fb-crunching-the-numbers-on-floating-solar.pdf – secondary; original https://openknowledge.worldbank.org/handle/10986/32804.)

Vendor blogs give utility FPV at USD 0.65–1.10/Wp for 2025–2026 tenders and ground-mount at USD 0.60–0.80/Wp (surgepv.com, undated blog – secondary, low reliability). Marketplace floats listed at USD 0.12–0.15/W (Alibaba listing – not a project cost; scope unclear). Not used for conclusions.

Offshore FPV: the 2025 North Sea Renewable Energy paper attributes 51 % of capex to floats. Ocean Sun's 2020 presentation cited a BOM of about USD 0.50/Wp for a 74 m ring (secondary, via search summary of a financialreports.eu filing). TNO-era Dutch estimates around €354/MWh for offshore PV (pv magazine, Dec 2020, https://www.pv-magazine.com/2020/12/10/offshore-floating-pv-may-reach-maturity-in-2030 – secondary, attribution unclear) and €150/MWh for a Belgian coastal project (pv magazine, Sep 2021, https://www.pv-magazine.com/2021/09/03/off-shore-pv-project-with-lcoe-of-e0-15-kwh-off-the-belgian-coast – secondary). A Politecnico di Torino master's thesis (2025, not peer-reviewed) gives offshore FPV LCOE of about €70–82/MWh at 1 MW and €49–61/MWh at 20 MW (https://webthesis.biblio.polito.it/36067/1/tesi.pdf).

IEA PVPS Task 13 report T13-31:2025 (April 2025) covers inland and nearshore FPV yield, reliability and O&M; it gives no capex figures, says yield models for FPV are insufficient for reliable LCOE, and names monitoring/O&M automation as the cost lever. Global FPV capacity 7.7 GW at end 2023, almost 90 % in Asia. (https://iea-pvps.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/04/IEA-PVPS-T13-31-2025-REPORT-Floating-PV-Plants.pdf – primary; TaiyangNews summary – secondary)

NREL ATB 2024 utility PV: exact fixed O&M value not retrieved. Older NREL values: USD 19/kWac-yr (ATB 2020, tracking) and fixed-tilt O&M falling from USD 17 to 13/kW-yr 2017–2018 (Reuters Events, https://www.reutersevents.com/renewables/node/60975 – secondary).

### 2.2 Business-model evolution of comparable firms

- Trackers (Nextracker, Array): product suppliers with outsourced manufacturing, engineering and software attached, no plant ownership; gross margins 32–34 % in 2024–2025, but inflated by US 45X manufacturing credits. Derived from the 10-K: FY2025 revenue about USD 2.96 bn (FY2024 2.50 bn plus 459.4 m); the 45X reduction of USD 224.9 m equals about 7.6 percentage points of margin, so ex-credit gross margin is roughly 26–27 % (calculation, not a reported figure).
- Soltec: tracker supplier that added EPC and project development; 2024 revenue €326.3 m (+30.8 %), other activities with operating losses of €46.1 m, net loss reportedly above €200 m (Forbes Spain – secondary), then exit from EPC and development in 2025 and return to a €12.4 m profit in 2025 after restructuring (Forbes Spain, https://forbes.es/economia/897302/ – secondary). Lesson often drawn: EPC and development tie up balance sheet and bank guarantees that a supplier cannot carry in a downturn.
- Ciel & Terre (inland FPV leader): equipment plus design, anchoring design and supervision; anchoring and mooring delivered by preferred partners under its QC; floats produced by local blow-moulders/OEM partners near markets because float transport is costly; partnered with developers for large plants (440 MWp Taiwan with HEXA Renewables). Completed 820 MW in 280 projects by mid-2023. (TaiyangNews, https://taiyangnews.info/technology/ciel-et-terre-global-expansion-in-floating-pv-technology; pv-tech – secondary)
- Ocean Sun: licence fee per Wp plus FEED fees; small, lumpy revenue; repeated small equity raises (see key findings).
- Principle Power (floating wind): technology licensing plus lifecycle engineering services, letting clients contract the supply chain locally; majority owned by developers (Aker Offshore Wind and Ocean Winds at the time of ScotWind). (Principle Power services page and press, https://principle-power.vercel.app/services – secondary mirror)
- Oceans of Energy: designs, builds, installs and operates offshore solar; funded by early investors, project-based funding and commercial income; first commercial contract with the Crosswind JV for Hollandse Kust Noord (company website, https://oceansofenergy.blue – primary). No round sizes found.
- Swimsol: develop-own-operate under 20-year PPAs, offered turnkey or financed; debt-financed by impact lenders; about 50 MWp installed (secondary).
- BayWa r.e. / GroenLeven and Q Energy: classic develop-and-sell or sell-down of inland FPV to cooperatives and institutional investors (see key findings).

### 2.3 Financing landscape

Equity: SolarDuck €15 m (Dec 2023) after an earlier €4 m round; aggregator lists total funding USD 24.55 m (unverified). CorPower Series B €53 m total plus EIC €17.5 m blended (2025); a separate €32 m Series B1 reported Oct 2024 (secondary, may overlap the €53 m). Ocean Sun NOK 15–20 m offering May 2026. No disclosed rounds for Oceans of Energy or BlueNewables found.

EU instruments:
- EIC Accelerator: grant up to €2.5 m plus EIC Fund equity €0.5–10 m (larger in Challenges, e.g. CorPower €15 m). October 2025 cut-off: 61 companies selected from 121 interviewed (EIC, 17 Feb 2026, https://eic.ec.europa.eu/news/61-start-ups-and-smes-secure-eic-support-latest-round-eic-accelerator-2026-02-17_en – primary). January/March 2026 cut-offs: 38 companies, €90 m grants, €202 m provisioned for equity, 84 % eligible for blended finance (EIC, 15 Jun 2026 – primary). 2024 cut-off: 71 of 1,211 applicants.
- Innovation Fund small-scale projects: capex above €2.5 m up to €20 m, budget about €100 m in the 2025 call (IF25, closed 23 April 2026); IF24 selected 61 projects including floating solar and agrivoltaics (secondary summaries: TaiyangNews, Zabala, EY).
- BlueInvest: €75 m EIF fund-of-funds (2020); committed €45 m to Astanor Ventures and Blue Horizon Ventures (2021); portfolio example Gazelle Wind Power via Indico. No floating-solar holding found.
- EIB: frequent loans to ground-mount PV portfolios under InvestEU (e.g. €166 m to BNZ for 17 plants, 2024, https://www.eib.org/de/press/all/2024-341-investeu-eib-signs-eur166-million-green-loan-with-bnz-to-deploy-renewable-energy-plants-in-spain-italy-and-portugal – primary); no EIB floating-solar loan found. EBRD lent €9.1 m to a 12.9 MW reservoir FPV plant in Albania (2021, pv-tech – secondary).
- Norway: Enova support for floating solar specifically not found; Enova supports floating wind heavily (NOK 4.5 bn across 18 projects since 2007 per Storting written answer – secondary). Research Council of Norway 2025 energy research round includes floating PV projects (https://www.forskningsradet.no/nyheter/2025/416-millioner-til-energiforskning/ – primary).

Nordic investors active in ocean/solar: Katapult Ocean (Oslo; portfolio SolarDuck, Mocean, GKinetic, Gazelle, Triton Anchor; mandate includes solar, wave, tidal, anchoring and mooring) (Katapult UNEP FI report 2025, https://katapult.vc/wp-content/uploads/2025/08/UNEP-Report-2025-07-Katapult-Ocean.pdf – primary); Nordic Ninja and SEB Greentech (CorPower lead investors); Nysnø Klimainvesteringer (state climate investor, Norway link required, no 2024–2025 solar deal found); Equinor Ventures and Norrsken not checked.

Project finance for small FPV: no published debt terms for 1–10 MW FPV in the Nordics found. Generic solar: about 70/30 debt/equity (PV-Tech/Gallagher – secondary); NREL modelled 71.8 % debt. Inland FPV of 74 MW has been bank-financed in France (Crédit Agricole, Bpifrance). For marine solar, impact lenders (responsAbility, OeEB) have lent at portfolio level rather than per small plant.

### 2.4 Price thresholds

- European solar PPA average €34.25/MWh Q3 2025 (LevelTen); Nordic PPA prices fell 27 % in January 2025 (Pexapark – secondary); Swedish solar deal prices down 20.8 % year on year in Q1 2025 (LevelTen via pv-tech – secondary).
- KYOS solar capture-price estimates for 2026–2035: SE3 50.6 (P25 40.5), SE4 51.9 (41.6), SE1 12.9, Finland 33.5 €/MWh (assumed €/MWh; headers not verified).
- Norway: NO2 had the highest spot prices in 2025, peak monthly average 100 øre/kWh (February), Q3 2025 average 79 øre/kWh (SSB – primary). Power-intensive industry average 42 øre/kWh in 2025 excl. taxes and grid tariff (SSB – primary). Lowest fixed-price contract at one supplier 63.23 øre/kWh on 15 May 2026 (Norwegian government reply to ESA, https://www.regjeringen.no/contentassets/6feeba217ae742e2bce5d356eed89cc7/vedlegg-norway-price-for-electricity-15.06.26.pdf – primary; customer segment not specified). C&I energy sharing up to 5 MW per industrial area from 1 Jan 2026 (secondary).
- Conversions at about 11.7 NOK/EUR (my assumption): 42 øre/kWh ≈ €36/MWh; 79 øre/kWh ≈ €68/MWh; 63 øre/kWh ≈ €54/MWh.

### 2.5 Risks investors and insurers highlight

- Storm and extreme-wind survival, mooring and anchoring fatigue, cable movement (Solarplaza 2026; WTW 2024). Omkareshwar storm damage 2024.
- Yield uncertainty: FPV yield models lack reliable data (IEA PVPS T13-31:2025).
- Limited track record of structures and suppliers leads lenders to ask for more guarantees (pv magazine India long read, 2019 – secondary).
- Decommissioning: scrap value unlikely to offset removal cost for FPV (pv-tech technical paper – secondary).
- Insurance market hardening for solar generally (hail, Nat Cat), with higher premiums and deductibles (GCube via Insurance Business, Bloomberg/Insurance Journal Dec 2023 – secondary). No published FPV-specific loss statistics found.
- Permits before debt; grid connection; supplier balance sheet behind warranties (Soltec shows supplier credit risk).

## 3) Implications for pricing and model (analysis, not fact)

- Kit price €0.30–0.40/W sits inside the NREL range for calm-water HDPE floats at 2–10 MW (USD 0.30–0.40/Wdc, 2021) and inside the European-float band USD 0.22–0.90/Wdc. For a wave-rated nearshore product with hinges and mooring interfaces, this is a defensible early price. It is above the 50 MW calm-water level (USD 0.20/Wdc), so volume buyers will push towards €0.20–0.25/W.
- Internal consistency flag: the reference plant has floats plus breakwater at about €209/kWp (€0.21/W). A kit sale price of €0.30–0.40/W for floats alone is higher than that cost line, so either the economic study assumes series-volume cost without supplier margin, or the kit price would raise the plant capex above €900/kWp. The business plan should state which price the €900/kWp and €0.062/kWh assume. If €0.21/W is the full-margin price, the 30–40 % gross margin implies a kit cost of about €0.13–0.15/W.
- The 30–40 % gross-margin target matches tracker leaders (32–34 %), but their margins include US manufacturing credits worth about 7–8 points; ex-credit about 26–27 %. A 25–35 % range is a more conservative claim for a European subcontracted supplier.
- €900/kWp total capex is close to IRENA's 2024 European ground-mount average (USD 779/kW, about €720/kW at 1.08 USD/EUR) plus a premium in line with Wood Mackenzie's USD 0.13–0.15/W. It is far below academic open-sea estimates. The plan should say explicitly that the reference case is sheltered nearshore water, which explains the gap to the €200–650/MWh offshore literature.
- LCOE €0.062/kWh is the Mediterranean case. Against Nordic benchmarks (European solar PPA €34/MWh, SE3/SE4 capture about €50/MWh, Norwegian industry spot about €36/MWh) it does not compete as a wholesale or remote-PPA product. At Nordic yields (roughly half of Mediterranean), LCOE at the same capex would be roughly €0.10–0.12/kWh (rough scaling, not modelled). The Norwegian case therefore rests on behind-the-meter supply next to the load, where the buyer also avoids grid tariff and electricity tax, and on the land-scarcity argument; the plan should quantify the delivered-price comparison (energy + grid tariff + tax) rather than the spot price.
- Develop-operate-sell of 0.3–3 MW plants is proven for inland FPV and marine solar (BayWa r.e., Q Energy, Swimsol), but buyers have been cooperatives, institutional investors and impact lenders buying de-risked plants with records. Expect a valuation discount until two to three years of operating data exist, and expect lenders to want DNV design-basis assessment and an insurer on risk before debt.
- Moving to EPC around 2030 carries the Soltec risk: bank guarantees, working capital and fixed-price exposure. Ciel & Terre's partner model (local float production, partner-supplied mooring under QC) and Principle Power's licence-plus-engineering model are lower-capital alternatives worth naming as fallback paths.
- Financing path consistent with peers: EIC Accelerator blended (up to €2.5 m grant plus equity), Katapult Ocean / Nordic Ninja / SEB Greentech type ocean-energy investors for equity of €5–15 m at Series A/B; Innovation Fund small-scale (capex €2.5–20 m) fits a first nearshore demonstrator or a European casting line; portfolio-level impact debt rather than single-plant project finance for the first plants.
