# Customers and regulation – desk research for the KER1 business plan

Desk research, 14 November 2026. Perspective: the buyer (coastal industry, ports, developers) and the rules they operate under. Public sources only. Each claim carries source, date and URL, tagged [P] for primary (regulator, legislator, official agency, TSO, standard body, the organisation's own report) or [S] for secondary (trade press, consultancy, vendor blog, data aggregator). Law references are described, not quoted; they must be verified against the official text before use in a deliverable.

## 1. Key findings

1. Norway raised the licensing threshold for solar plants under the Energy Act to 10 MW installed capacity from 1 July 2025. Plants below 10 MW no longer need an NVE licence and are handled by the municipality under the Planning and Building Act. This covers the whole 0.3–10 MW product range (strictly: below 10 MW). [P] Regjeringen/Energidepartementet, "Endringar i lover og forskrifter frå 1. juli 2025", 24 June 2025, https://regjeringen.no/no/aktuelt/endringar-i-lover-og-forskrifter-fra-1.-juli-2025-fra-energidepartementet/id3111203
2. In Norwegian sea areas, a floating installation also needs a permit under the Harbour and Fairways Act (havne- og farvannsloven). The municipality handles this within its sea area (out to one nautical mile beyond the baseline) outside main and secondary fairways; Kystverket handles fairways. A permit cannot be granted against an adopted area plan, so municipal zoning (or a dispensation) comes first. [S, municipal guidance] Meløy kommune application form, https://www.meloy.kommune.no/globalassets/dokumentarkiv/plan-og-naring/havner-og-kaier/soknadsskjema.2.0.pdf ; [P] Kystverket decision, Kragerø fairway, https://www.kystverket.no/contentassets/2a91cd02456f4d88a106b51c51ad5f34/tillatelse---hfl---farledstiltak-i-inns-til-kragero---kragero-kommune---vestfold-og-telemark-fylke.pdf
3. Grid access is the binding constraint for Norwegian industrial growth: as of August 2026 Statnett had reserved 7,715 MW for consumption with a queue of 15,997 MW; stricter maturity requirements for keeping grid reservations took effect 1 July 2026. On-site generation behind the meter does not need new transmission capacity, which strengthens the case for local supply. [S] Mondaq, "Stricter maturity requirements for grid connections", 2026, https://www.mondaq.com/utilities/1842312/stricter-maturity-requirements-for-grid-connections ; [P] Statnett half-year report 2026, https://www.statnett.no/en/about-statnett/news-and-press-releases/news-archive-2026/half-year-report-2026-statnett-sf---increasing-the-pace-of-grid-development/
4. Norwegian industrial power prices sit far apart by zone: in Q3 2025 average spot was about 79 øre/kWh (NO2), 61 (NO1), 11 (NO3) and 3.5 (NO4) (SSB). The southern zones (NO1, NO2, NO5) are where on-site solar competes; NO3/NO4 are weak markets for it. Non-power-intensive industry paid 50.5 øre/kWh in Q2 2025 (excluding tax and grid tariff). [P] SSB, "Slightly higher electricity price in third quarter", https://www.ssb.no/en/energi-og-industri/energi/statistikk/elektrisitetspriser/article-for-electricity-prices/slightly-higher-electricity-price-in-third-quarter ; [P] SSB elkraftpris, https://www.ssb.no/elkraftpris/
5. Nordic PPAs are mature but slowed after the 2021–22 energy crisis; traditional power-intensive industry takes over half of disclosed Nordic PPA volume; tenors trend shorter; developers aim for at least 10 years to match debt. Pricing is rarely disclosed. [P] Nordic Energy Research / AFRY, "Power Purchase Agreements and their use in the Nordics", Nordic Energy Research 2025:05, November 2025, https://pub.norden.org/nordicenergyresearch2025-05/nordicenergyresearch2025-05.pdf
6. Norway has a state guarantee that covers offtaker default under PPAs for wood-processing, chemicals and metals industry (Eksfin power guarantee; PPAs of 7–25 years; offtaker at least 10 GWh/yr and at least 35 GWh total contract volume). A 1–10 MW solar plant producing roughly 1–9 GWh/yr is below this scale unless bundled. [P, via Nordic Energy Research 2025:05, above] ; [P] Eksfin, https://www.eksfin.no/no/produkter/kraftgaranti/
7. European PPA volumes fell in 2025 (Pexapark: 13.1 GW across 247 deals vs 15.3 GW in 2024; S&P: corporate PPAs down 35 % to 15 GW), but solar held up (4.2 GW in H1 2025, up from 3.9 GW). Corporate buyers sustained the market while utility PPAs fell 59 %. European PPA tenors range 5–25 years. [S] Balkan Green Energy News on Pexapark, https://balkangreenenergynews.com/pexapark-ppa-activity-in-europe-drops-in-first-half-of-2025/ ; [S] PV Tech, "Europe signed 4.2GW solar PV PPAs in H1 2025", https://www.pv-tech.org/europe-signed-4-2gw-solar-pv-ppas-in-h1-2025/ ; [S] S&P Global, 21 April 2026, https://www.spglobal.com/energy/en/news-research/blog/energy-transition/042126-clean-energy-procurement-held-up-in-2025-with-more-diversified-approaches ; [S] PV Tech, "EU PPA market defined by falling prices, corporate demand and political shifts", https://www.pv-tech.org/eu-ppa-market-defined-by-falling-prices-corporate-demand-and-political-shifts
8. CSRD scope was cut by the Omnibus I agreement (provisional deal 9 December 2025; Parliament adoption reported 16 December 2025): mandatory reporting now applies to EU undertakings with more than 1,000 employees and over EUR 450 million turnover, removing an estimated 80–90 % of previously covered companies. Scope 2 reporting pressure therefore falls mainly on large groups and their supply chains, not on mid-sized coastal industry directly. [S] Cooley, 10 December 2025, https://www.cooley.com/news/insight/2025/2025-12-10-eu-reaches-agreement-on-omnibus-i-impacting-csrd-and-csddd-compliance-for-us-companies ; [S] Clifford Chance, February 2026, https://www.cliffordchance.com/insights/resources/blogs/business-and-human-rights-insights/2026/02/omnibus-i-the-european-union-concludes-csddd-and-csrd-reforms.html
9. Port electrification is a regulated demand driver: under AFIR, TEN-T core and comprehensive maritime ports must supply shore power for at least 90 % of container and passenger ship calls by 1 January 2030; under FuelEU Maritime, container and passenger ships of 5,000 GT or more must connect at those ports from 2030. ICCT estimates the EU must triple or quadruple its 2023 installed shore power (309 MW in 51 ports). [S] ICCT working paper, October 2023, https://theicct.org/publication/shore-power-eu-oct23 ; [S] Lloyd's Register AFIR summary, https://www.lr.org/en/services/statutory-compliance/fueleu-regulation/alternative-fuels-infrastructure-regulation/
10. Germany: the Water Resources Act (WHG § 36(3)) bans solar plants on natural surface waters and limits them on artificial or heavily modified waters to 15 % coverage and at least 40 m from shore. The text names surface waters (oberirdische Gewässer) only and does not mention coastal waters; whether coastal and harbour waters are outside the rule needs legal confirmation. The Bundesrat asked for the area limits to be relaxed (resolution 26 September 2025). [P] WHG § 36, https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/whg_2009/__36.html ; [P] Bundesrat Drucksache 214/25 (Beschluss), https://dserver.bundestag.de/brd/2025/0214-25B.pdf ; [P] Fraunhofer ISE, "Floating PV: Ein Leitfaden für Deutschland", summer 2025, https://www.ise.fraunhofer.de/content/dam/ise/de/documents/publications/studies/Fraunhofer-ISE-Leitfaden-Floating-PV.pdf
11. In Germany, floating PV whose output is mainly self-consumed by a local business is treated as a privileged project in the outer zone (BauGB § 35) and follows a simpler planning path than grid-feeding plants; this favours the industrial on-site segment. [P] Fraunhofer ISE Leitfaden, 2025, section 5.5 (URL above)
12. Sweden: floating structures in water are vattenverksamhet under the Environmental Code (miljöbalken, chapter 11). Smaller works need a notification (anmälan) to the county board; larger works, or works affecting valuable nature or non-consenting parties, need a permit from the Land and Environment Court after consultation and often an EIA. [P] Länsstyrelsen Gotland, "Vattenverksamheter som behöver tillstånd", https://www.lansstyrelsen.se/gotland/miljo-och-vatten/atgarder-och-verksamheter-i-vatten/vattenverksamhet/vattenverksamheter-som-behover-tillstand.html
13. Bankability is the dominant buyer and lender concern. Lenders review extreme-weather assumptions, fatigue loads, anchoring, cable movement, warranties and maintenance access; insurers look at mooring exposure, storm resilience, electrical performance and repair access; lenders want key permits before committing debt. [S] Solarplaza, "Where floating solar creates real value in Europe", 4 August 2026, https://www.solarplaza.com/resource/13527/article-where-floating-solar-creates-real-value-in-europe-2026/ ; [P] World Bank/SERIS, "Where Sun Meets Water: Floating Solar Handbook for Practitioners", 2019, https://openknowledge.worldbank.org/handle/10986/32804
14. DNV published coordinated floating solar standards in May 2026: DNV-ST-C108 (structural design of floats, aimed mainly at sheltered inland and near-shore waters) and DNV-ST-E309 (station keeping, edition 2026-04), complemented by DNV-RP-0584. [P] DNV news, 2026, https://www.dnv.com/news/2026/dnv-launches-coordinated-standards-to-improve-safety-and-reliability-of-floating-solar/ ; [P] DNV-ST-E309 page, https://www.dnv.com/energy/standards-guidelines/dnv-st-e309-station-keeping-of-floating-solar-photovoltaic-systems/
15. An EU call fits the product directly: Horizon Europe HORIZON-CL5-2026-11-D3-14 "Improved system design for innovative PV applications" (EUPI-PV partnership) has an offshore/nearshore PV branch, EUR 30 million total, about EUR 7.5 million per grant, deadline 1 December 2026 (to be confirmed on the Funding & Tenders Portal). The Innovation Fund small-scale band (CAPEX EUR 2.5–20 million) is the route for a first commercial plant; the IF25 deadline (23 April 2026) has passed, and an SME-only small-scale call is indicated for 2027. [S] Accelopment, "Green Deal energy calls 2026", https://accelopment.com/blog/funding/green-deal-energy-calls-2026/ ; [S] euacc.ai topic page, https://www.euacc.ai/calls/HORIZON-CL5-2026-11-D3-14 ; [P] European Commission, IF26 SME call page, https://climate.ec.europa.eu/areas-action/eu-funding-climate-action/innovation-fund/calls-proposals/if26-sme-call_en

## 2. Detailed notes

### 2.1 Why coastal industry and ports buy on-site renewables

Corporate PPA market, Nordics

- Nordic Energy Research 2025:05 (AFRY), November 2025 [P]: PPAs are "a key tool for making projects bankable"; most common structures are fixed price for baseload or pay-as-produced volumes; utility PPAs and corporate PPAs dominate. AFRY's database holds about 80 TWh/yr of disclosed Nordic PPAs since 2013. Power-intensive industry offtakes over half of volumes (55 % in the chart, 2013–2025). Onshore wind is the largest technology; in Denmark most PPAs are solar. Norway has a long tradition of hydro utility–industry PPAs; corporate PPA activity in Norway stagnated in the 2020s after the pause in onshore wind licensing. Tenor trends shorter; developers aim for a minimum 10-year term to match debt; lenders have tightened bankability requirements. https://pub.norden.org/nordicenergyresearch2025-05/nordicenergyresearch2025-05.pdf
- Same report, tax fact box [P]: Norwegian resource rent tax limits the supply of PPAs from hydro; contract prices from new PPAs can be used for tax purposes under conditions in skatteloven (minimum 3 years, at least 150 GWh, power-intensive industry for hydro; new onshore wind 2024–2030). Not directly relevant to solar but explains why hydro PPA supply to industry is constrained.
- Eksfin power guarantee [P]: covers offtaker payment default under PPAs; eligible industries wood processing, chemicals, metals; PPA duration 7–25 years; offtaker at least 10 GWh/yr and at least 35 GWh total PPA volume. https://www.eksfin.no/no/produkter/kraftgaranti/ (as summarised in Nordic Energy Research 2025:05).
- BloombergNEF recorded no corporate PPAs in Norway in 2024 (as reported in search snippet of BNEF slides, May 2025) [S]: https://solar-media.s3.amazonaws.com/assets/RPR25/Speaker%20Slides/bnef_2025-05-16_Renewables%20Procurement%20and%20Revenues%20Summit.pdf . Not verified against the full slide deck.
- Pexapark (reported by Balkan Green Energy News) [S]: in January 2025 the Nordics had the largest PPA price decline in Europe (−27 %), driven by Nordic Cal26/Cal27 futures; in February 2025 the Nordics were the only region with a modest price increase. https://balkangreenenergynews.com/european-ppa-volumes-drop-in-january-pexapark
- Zeigo (Schneider Electric) PPA price reports [S]: Sweden and Finland saw very low wind PPA prices and wind cannibalisation in the 12 months to Q2 2025, with a slight rebound in Q3 2025. https://www.zeigo.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/08/2025-Q2-Zeigo-PPA-Pricing-Report-Executive-Summary.pdf ; https://www.zeigo.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/Q3-2025-PPA-Price-Report-Free-Preview.pdf

Corporate PPA market, Europe

- Pexapark full-year 2025 [S, via news]: 13.1 GW disclosed across 247 deals (2024: 15.3 GW); Spain 3.9 GW, Italy 1.8 GW, Poland 1.5 GW. H1 2025 solar 4.2 GW / 73 deals; German solar PPA volume fell 84 % year on year in H1 2025 on cannibalisation concerns. Pexapark EURO composite index (10-year pay-as-produced) about EUR 50–53/MWh in early 2025. https://balkangreenenergynews.com/pexapark-ppa-activity-in-europe-drops-in-first-half-of-2025/ ; https://www.pv-tech.org/europe-signed-4-2gw-solar-pv-ppas-in-h1-2025/
- S&P Global, 21 April 2026 [S]: European corporate PPA contracting fell 35 % to 15 GW in 2025; solar PPA prices rose while wind was stable. https://www.spglobal.com/energy/en/news-research/blog/energy-transition/042126-clean-energy-procurement-held-up-in-2025-with-more-diversified-approaches
- Pexapark tenor range 5–25 years, longest recorded 30 years (March 2025) [S]. https://www.pv-magazine.com/2025/04/18/pexapark-records-24-european-ppas-for-over-1-5-gw-in-march/
- Implication: buyers value price certainty, but solar PPA prices in markets with high solar penetration (Germany, Spain) are pressured by cannibalisation and negative-price hours. For on-site (behind-the-meter) supply the relevant benchmark is the delivered price including grid tariff and taxes, not the wholesale PPA price.

Scope 2 and CSRD

- Omnibus I [S]: CSRD scope narrowed to EU undertakings with over 1,000 employees and over EUR 450 million turnover; applies from financial years starting on or after 1 January 2027 (Cooley 10 December 2025; Clifford Chance February 2026; ESG Today). URLs in key finding 8; also https://www.esgtoday.com/eu-lawmakers-strike-deal-to-cut-sustainability-reporting-due-diligence-laws/
- Norway applies CSRD through the EEA; the national effect of Omnibus I in Norway was not checked (gap).
- Not found: a primary source on market-based Scope 2 accounting rules for on-site generation under ESRS E1 or GHG Protocol revisions. Gap.

Port electrification and shore power

- AFIR (Regulation (EU) 2023/1804) and FuelEU Maritime (Regulation (EU) 2023/1805) [S summaries]: 90 % of container and passenger ship calls at TEN-T ports to be served by shore power by 1 January 2030; ships of 5,000 GT or more must connect from 2030. Secondary sources: ICCT (October 2023), Lloyd's Register, Sustainable Ships. https://theicct.org/publication/shore-power-eu-oct23 ; https://www.sustainable-ships.org/rules-regulations/afir . Verify against EUR-Lex (https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2023/1804/oj and https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2023/1805/oj).
- Demand: ICCT estimates about 5.9 TWh at-berth energy demand in 489 major EU ports (2019 data), nearly 70 % in TEN-T ports; Sustainable Ships estimates 6–13 TWh/yr from 2030. [S] https://www.sustainable-ships.org/stories/2025/eu-shore-power-demand-2030
- Early movers: Kiel and Marseille-Fos report AFIR compliance years ahead of the deadline [S]. https://engine.online/news/kiel-achieves-afir-target-5-years-before-eu-deadline-70f5 ; https://www.hellenicshippingnews.com/marseille-fos-achieves-afir-target-4-years-before-eu-deadline/
- Norway: AFIR applies via the EEA (status of incorporation not checked). Enova's shore-power programme for existing vessels closed with a final deadline 1 December 2025; total state allocation to shore power ("landstrøm") for 2026 set at NOK 44.2 million after the revised budget, and Enova had a special NOK 100 million mandate for the Flåm shore-power plant in 2025. [P] Energidepartementet/KLD, supplementary assignment letter to Enova, 30 October 2026, https://www.regjeringen.no/contentassets/051d75e38a4743c3a29f2bb169a4443d/supplerende-oppdragsbrev-til-enova-etter-revidert-nasjonalbudsjett-2026-30.10.2026.pdf
- Implication: shore power raises peak demand at ports (MW-scale for cruise and ferries) and therefore grid-connection needs; a floating solar plant on port water reduces energy bought but does not remove peak-capacity needs without storage.

Industrial electrification in Norway

- Statnett Long-term Market Analysis 2024–2050 (February 2025) [P]: Norwegian consumption from about 140 TWh today to 180–260 TWh in 2050; base case about 220 TWh. https://statnett.no/globalassets/for-aktorer-i-kraftsystemet/planer-og-analyser/lma/long-term-market-analysis-2024-2050-executive-summary.pdf (figures as reported in search snippets; base case via MarketScreener https://marketscreener.com/news/latest/Norway-power-demand-may-rise-60-until-2050-grid-operator-42766328 [S]).
- Statnett half-year 2026 [P]: mature consumption applications about 3,500 MW in H1 2026 (up 2,000 MW year on year); over 600 MW new consumption and 100 MW new production connected in the past 12 months. URL in key finding 3.
- Statnett paused new data-centre reservations on 9 November 2026 until 1 August 2027 at the latest (about 800 MW connected, 4,200 MW reserved, 9,100 MW queued) [S]. https://www.enerdata.net/publications/daily-energy-news/statnett-pauses-new-grid-capacity-data-centres-norway.html
- Maturity assessment: local grid company for projects under 10 MW, regional for 10–99 MW, Statnett for 100 MW and above [S, Mondaq, URL in key finding 3].
- Elvia/THEMA, "Strømforsyningen 2035 – hva er det plass til av industri- og samfunnsutvikling", 11 August 2026 [P, not read]: https://www.elvia.no/siteassets/dokumenter/rapporter/stromforsyningen-2035---hva-er-det-plass-til-av-industri--og-samfunnsutvikling_thema-rapport-2026-11.08.pdf

### 2.4 Permitting

Norway

- Energy Act licensing: 10 MW threshold for solar from 1 July 2025; below that, municipal processing under the Planning and Building Act [P] (key finding 1). Previously the trigger was establishment of high-voltage installations above 1 kV [P, consultation notes] https://cms12.regjeringen.no/contentassets/5fd0b6da39b243859f1211a3abb9626f/horingsnotat-om-endring-i-energilovforskriften.pdf . Grid connection components (transformers, cables above certain voltage) may still need a facility licence under the Energy Act; not verified.
- Planning and Building Act: sea areas within the municipality are zoned in the municipal area plan; a floating plant needs a suitable zoning purpose or a dispensation, and likely a detailed zoning plan for larger plants. Not verified against a primary guide for floating solar (gap).
- Harbour and Fairways Act: works in the sea that may affect safety or passability need a permit; municipality within its sea area, Kystverket in main and secondary fairways; permit cannot conflict with an adopted area plan; case-handling fee per application [S/P sources in key finding 2]. Law text: https://lovdata.no/dokument/NL/lov/2019-06-21-70 (to be verified).
- Other likely consents: county governor (Statsforvalteren) for pollution/dredging if mooring works disturb seabed; Fiskeridirektoratet consultation; cultural heritage (marine archaeology) survey. Typical examples of Statsforvalteren permits for sea works: https://www.statsforvalteren.no/contentassets/93186e41c5f24f749b77858c9c26c8f2/myklebust-verft-utfylling---vedtak-og-tillatelse.pdf [P]. No published timeline for a floating solar permit in Norway was found (gap).

Sweden

- Miljöbalken chapter 11 (vattenverksamhet): notification to Länsstyrelsen for smaller works; permit from Mark- och miljödomstolen for larger works, after consultation (undersökningssamråd), Länsstyrelsen decision on significant environmental impact, and an EIA; court proceedings with announcement, comments, main hearing; appeal to MÖD [P] Länsstyrelsen Gotland (key finding 12). Thresholds for anmälan (in the förordning om vattenverksamheter) not extracted (gap).
- Precedent: Mark- och miljödomstolen granted a permit for a solar plant including vattenverksamhet in Eksjö kommun (LC Energi Skuru AB) [P] https://energirapportering.domstol.se/globalassets/filer/domstol/vaxjo_tingsratt/kungorelser-mmd/kungorelse-dom-i-miljomal-m-1936-25-ab-58.pdf
- Ground-mounted solar is normally not a permit- or notification-requiring environmentally hazardous activity, but consultation with Länsstyrelsen is required (Västra Götaland) [P] https://www.lansstyrelsen.se/vastra-gotaland/miljo-och-vatten/miljofarlig-verksamhet/provning-av-miljofarlig-verksamhet.html
- Timelines: not stated by authorities; court permits for vattenverksamhet commonly take more than a year (not sourced here; gap).

Germany

- WHG § 36(3) (key finding 10) [P]. Fraunhofer ISE notes FPV is currently assigned to UBA water-body type 99 "artificial lake" and that use of other water-body types is not foreseen in the regulatory framework [P].
- Required permits: water-law permit (Landeswasserbehörde) plus building permit; for grid-feeding plants usually land-use plan (Flächennutzungsplan) and development plan (Bebauungsplan); for mainly self-consumed power by a local business, privileged outer-zone project under BauGB § 35; for mining lakes, mining authorities are involved [P] Fraunhofer ISE Leitfaden 2025, chapter 5.
- Authorities at municipal and regional level are cautious and uncertain about procedures, costs and ecological impact (ISE interviews in project PV2Float) [P].
- Coastal and harbour waters: no primary source found on how WHG § 36(3) applies to coastal waters (Küstengewässer) or on federal waterway permits (Wasserstraßen- und Schifffahrtsverwaltung, strom- und schifffahrtspolizeiliche Genehmigung under the Federal Waterways Act). Gap; needs German legal check.
- Example: 15 MW gravel-pit project received a 25-year water-rights permit from Regierungspräsidium Karlsruhe [S] https://www.pv-tech.org/calls-for-simplified-permitting-to-support-growth-of-floating-solar-segment/
- Timeline example (France, inland): Les Îlots Blandin, 74.3 MW, development from 2019, construction September 2023, fully operational June 2025 [S] Solarplaza, 4 August 2026.

Lithuania

- No Lithuania-specific rule on floating solar permitting or water-surface leases found. The RED III transposition package (June 2025) simplifies renewable permitting generally [S]. The Kruonis pumped-storage floating pilot (EUR 235,000 grant from LVPA, 2019) found the systems unsuitable for strong water-level changes, eddies, currents and ice [P] Ignitis Gamyba, https://ignitisgamyba.lt/en/our-activities/business-development-and-innovations-projects/floating-solar-power-plant-in-kruonis-pshp/4538 . Gap: Lithuanian Water Law (Vandens įstatymas) and territorial planning rules for coastal/lagoon waters.

EU level

- RED III (Directive (EU) 2023/2413) [S summaries]: inside renewables acceleration areas, permit procedures for new plants not to exceed one year (two years offshore); outside, two years (three offshore); shorter deadlines for small installations below 150 kW. https://guidehouse.com/insights/energy/2023/renewable-energy-directive-three-key-measures ; Energy Community/BBH presentation, https://www.energy-community.org/dam/jcr:81dea03c-6fd8-40f4-a17c-eb5cf220a487/Dirk%20Hendricks.pdf . SolarPower Europe reports permitting delays persist [S] https://www.pveurope.eu/markets/eu-countries-fall-short-fast-track-renewables-rules . Verify articles 16a–16b on EUR-Lex: https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/dir/2023/2413/oj . RED III applicability in Norway via EEA not checked.
- Electricity Market Design reform, Regulation (EU) 2024/1747, toolbox to promote PPAs (guarantee schemes, barrier removal) [P, via Nordic Energy Research 2025:05].
- No EU guidance document specific to floating PV permitting found.

### 2.5 Buyer decision criteria and concerns

- Bankability: lenders review extreme-weather assumptions, fatigue loads, anchoring, cable movement, warranties, maintenance access; insurers review mooring exposure, storm resilience, electrical performance and repair access; lenders expect key permits before debt [S] Solarplaza, 4 August 2026 (vendor-adjacent, likely optimistic).
- Historic reasons for staying out of FPV: higher cost than ground-mount, bankability hurdles, lack of O&M track record [S] PV Tech technical paper, https://www.pv-tech.org/technical-considerations-to-ensuring-bankable-floating-pv-projects/
- Insurance: FPV considered new and unfamiliar by most domestic insurers, especially at utility scale; brokers play a key role [S] WTW, "Surveying the risk landscape of an emerging solar sector", May 2024, https://www.wtwco.com/en-gb/insights/2024/05/surveying-the-risk-landscape-of-an-emerging-solar-sector (page returned HTTP 429; content from search snippet). Fire precedent: 2020 electrical fire at the Queen Elizabeth II Reservoir damaged float connectors and sank modules [S] Marsh, https://www.marsh.com/en-gb/industries/energy-and-power/insights/how-floating-solar-technology-could-contribute-to-uk-net-zero-targets.html
- Technical and environmental: World Bank/SERIS handbook (2019) names electrical safety, anchoring/mooring and O&M as main hurdles; soiling (bird droppings), faster degradation of electrical components; slower financial close; more permitting obstacles; recommends a precautionary approach at coastlines and high-biodiversity areas [P] https://openknowledge.worldbank.org/handle/10986/32804
- Value drivers named by buyers in European cases: industrial self-consumption protects against wholesale volatility (Holcim Obourg 31 MW, commissioned March 2026, about 30 GWh/yr) [S] Solarplaza.
- Authorities' concern: ecological impact on water bodies drives cautious permitting (Fraunhofer ISE interviews) [P].
- Standards: DNV-ST-C108 and DNV-ST-E309 (May 2026) give lenders a reference design basis; ST-C108 targets sheltered inland and near-shore waters [P] (key finding 14).
- No structured survey of industrial buyers' decision criteria for floating or nearshore solar was found. The planned customer interviews remain the main evidence source.
