# Market size, growth and drivers – desk research for the KER1 business plan

Desk research, 14 November 2026. Perspective: market size, growth and drivers for nearshore and sheltered-coastal floating PV (FPV), with Nordic/Baltic focus. Builds on the existing "The market" section of the KER1 business plan draft, which already cites: SolarPower Europe GMO 2026–2030 (664 GW in 2025, 3 TW fleet), World Bank/SERIS 2019 (1.3 GWp end-2018; 404 GW on 1 % of man-made reservoirs), Wood Mackenzie Nov 2024 (1.7 GW new FPV in 2024, 77 GW by 2033, DE 2.2 / FR 1.2 / NL 1 GW by 2033), module prices below USD 0.10/W, NZIA 30 % non-price criteria.

Tags: "primary" = I read the source document itself; "secondary" = news report, summary or search-engine extract of the source.

---

## 1. Key findings (usable in the business plan)

1. Global floating PV reached at least 8.7 GW installed by end-2024, with major projects in China, India, Taiwan and Europe (IEA PVPS, Trends in Photovoltaic Applications 2025 – fact sheet, Dec 2025/Feb 2026, https://iea-pvps.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/Trends-2025-FS.pdf). Primary.
2. The same IEA PVPS series puts FPV at 7.7 GW at end-2023, up from just above 1.6 GW at end-2018; almost 90 % is in Asia and close to 50 % in China; the Netherlands and France are the largest markets outside Asia (7th and 10th largest FPV capacities worldwide) (IEA PVPS Task 13, Floating PV Power Plants: A Review of Energy Yield, Reliability, and Maintenance, report T13-31:2025, April 2025, https://iea-pvps.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/04/IEA-PVPS-T13-31-2025-REPORT-Floating-PV-Plants.pdf). Primary. Note: this gives about 1 GW/year of FPV additions 2023–2024, roughly 0.15 % of global PV additions.
3. Offshore FPV is still experimental: IEA PVPS Task 13 states that offshore FPV has "only a few pilot and demo projects with TRL levels between 3 and 5", while inland FPV is above TRL 8, and that IEC standardisation so far focuses on inland FPV (T13-31:2025, April 2025, URL as above). Primary. Task 13 explicitly defines nearshore FPV ("at sea, but close to the coastline") as a separate category from offshore, which supports positioning nearshore as its own segment.
4. Offshore solar has reached gigawatt scale in Asia, but on piles, not floats: CHN Energy's 1 GW HG14 project 8 km off Dongying, Shandong, was fully grid-connected in late December 2025 (about 1,223 ha, 2,934 pile-mounted steel platforms) (pv magazine, 29 Dec 2025, https://www.pv-magazine.com/2025/12/29/china-commissions-worlds-largest-1-gw-open-sea-offshore-solar-project/). Secondary. Jiangsu province has a plan for 27.3 GW of offshore solar across 60 projects, targeting 10 GW installed by 2027 and 15–20 GW by 2030 (Enerdata, https://www.enerdata.net/publications/daily-energy-news/chinas-jiangsu-province-unveils-273-gw-offshore-solar-development-plan.html, date not confirmed). Secondary.
5. European marine potential studies exist at national level. Spain: 4.45–6.48 GW of floating offshore solar compatible with maritime spatial planning, of which about 2.54 GW in the Mediterranean under the general-restrictions scenario (University of A Coruña, Journal of Cleaner Production; reported by pv magazine, 15 June 2026, https://www.pv-magazine.com/2026/06/15/spains-offshore-pv-potential-estimated-at-6-48-gw/). Secondary. Netherlands: TNO scenarios give 200 GWp of solar in the Netherlands in 2050, of which 25 GWp on inland water and 45 GWp at sea (pv-tech, https://www.pv-tech.org/netherlands-renewables-tender-pushes-offshore-solar-forward/, undated in results). Secondary.
6. Europe's inland FPV is constrained by rules that push the market towards larger and more exposed water: Germany typically applies a 15 % maximum surface coverage and a 40 m shoreline buffer (Solarplaza, Where floating solar creates real value in Europe, 4 Aug 2026, https://www.solarplaza.com/resource/13527/article-where-floating-solar-creates-real-value-in-europe-2026/). Primary (trade article read in full). Wood Mackenzie names permitting and environmental concerns as the main barriers, with some countries banning FPV on natural lakes or capping covered surface (pv-tech, 25 Aug 2023, https://www.pv-tech.org/floating-solar-can-play-a-role-in-some-eu-countries-energy-transition/). Secondary.
7. EU solar demand is large but slowing: the EU installed 65.1 GW in 2025 (first annual decline since 2016, -0.7 %), reaching 406 GW; SolarPower Europe's most-likely scenario gives 718 GW by 2030, short of the 750 GW target, with rising negative-price hours eroding revenues for standalone solar (SolarPower Europe, EU Solar Market Outlook 2025–2030 press release, 11 Dec 2025, https://www.solarpowereurope.org/press-releases/new-report-eu-hits-2025-solar-target-but-market-contraction-puts-2030-goal-at-risk). Primary. Implication: value shifts to behind-the-meter supply next to a load (the industrial-PPA model) rather than merchant export.
8. Module prices are at historic lows and no longer dominate system cost: TOPCon bifacial modules averaged EUR 0.116/Wp in Europe in October 2026 (sun.store PV Index); OPIS assessed imported TOPCon (>600 W) at EUR 0.109/Wp DDP, and European-assembled TOPCon at EUR 0.246/Wp (both reported around 20 Oct 2026; https://www.pveurope.eu/tags/pv-index and https://www.opis.com/blog/europes-solar-market-stable-prices-rising-pressure/). Secondary. IEA PVPS reports Chinese module prices fell more than 60 % from 2023 levels (Snapshot of Global PV Markets 2026 fact sheet, 2026, https://iea-pvps.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/05/Snapshot-2026-FS.pdf). Primary.
9. FPV carries a structure-driven capex premium over ground-mount: NREL found a USD 0.26/Wdc (about 25 %) premium for 10 MWdc fixed-tilt FPV (NREL, Floating Photovoltaic System Cost Benchmark, FY22, https://www.nrel.gov/docs/fy22osti/80695.pdf). Secondary (search extract). For offshore FPV, "preliminary industry discussions" put installation costs about 30 % higher per kW than ground-mount, driven by marine logistics and corrosion-resistant parts (Chen et al., Journal of Renewable and Sustainable Energy; pv magazine, 21 May 2026, https://www.pv-magazine.com/2026/05/21/offshore-vs-ground-mounted-pv-2/). Secondary. The same study modelled about 12 % higher lifetime yield for a 181 MW intertidal FPV plant than for a 100 MW ground-mounted plant in Taiwan.
10. Norway's power demand is set to grow strongly while land for solar is contested: Statnett's Long-term Market Analysis 2024–2050 (Feb 2025) projects consumption rising from about 140 TWh to 180–260 TWh in 2050 (base case 220 TWh; production 217 TWh, i.e. a small deficit) (MNI Markets summary, Feb 2025, https://mnimarkets.com/articles/norway-to-see-strained-energy-balance-variable-power-prices-1738584825911; MarketScreener/Reuters, https://marketscreener.com/news/latest/Norway-power-demand-may-rise-60-until-2050-grid-operator-42766328). Secondary (Statnett PDF could not be downloaded, TLS error). Statnett's KMA 2024–2029 gives +20 TWh consumption from electrification and new industry to 2029 in the medium scenario (https://statnett.no/globalassets/for-aktorer-i-kraftsystemet/planer-og-analyser/kma/kortsiktig-markedsanalyse-2024-2029.pdf). Secondary.
11. NVE states that grid scarcity and land-use conflict will limit ground-mounted solar in Norway before 2030: NVE had received ground-mounted applications totalling close to 1.4 GW but had licensed only three plants totalling 14 MW; meeting the 8 TWh-by-2030 political goal would need about 10 GW installed. NVE's own forecast is 4 TWh solar in 2030 and 9 TWh in 2040 (NVE, NVEs svar på oppdrag om solkraft og annen lokal energiproduksjon, ref. 202311163-63, 5 Feb 2024, https://www.nve.no/media/16752/notatet-nves-svar-paa-oppdrag-om-solkraft-og-annen-lokal-energiproduksjon.pdf). Primary. The note does not discuss floating solar.
12. Norway's solar market is small but growing: about 117 MW added in 2025 (NVE data), about 876 MW cumulative end-2025, passing 1 GW (1,003 MW per Elhub) in September 2026 (pv magazine, 13 Jan 2026 and 16 Sep 2026, https://www.pv-magazine.com/2026/01/13/norway-adds-117-mw-of-solar-in-2025/, https://www.pv-magazine.com/2026/09/16/norway-passes-1-gw-solar-milestone/). Secondary. Neighbours are far ahead: Denmark added a record 1.55 GW in 2025 (about 6.3–6.5 GW cumulative), Sweden 652 MW (about 5.4 GW cumulative) (pv magazine, 16 Feb 2026 and 30 Jan 2026, https://www.pv-magazine.com/2026/02/16/denmark-deploys-1-55-gw-of-solar-in-2025/, https://www.pv-magazine.com/2026/01/30/sweden-deploys-652-mw-of-solar-in-2025/). Secondary.
13. Southern Norwegian power prices, where most coastal industry sits, are at levels that make local solar relevant: Q4 2025 average spot prices in NO1, NO2 and NO5 were 74–82 øre/kWh (NO5 74 øre/kWh, up from 36 øre/kWh in Q3), while NO4 averaged 24.1 øre/kWh (NVE, Kraftsituasjonen fjerde kvartal 2025, https://www.nve.no/media/19773/kraftsituasjonen_2025q4.pdf). Primary. The 2025 national day-ahead average was about 0.42–0.445 NOK/kWh, up about 9 % on 2024 (euenergy.live, ENTSO-E data, https://euenergy.live/norway/2025). Secondary.
14. Hybridisation with hydropower is a documented European driver: Ember finds 25 GW of wind and solar could be added behind the meter of existing hydropower plants in seven EU countries without new grid capacity (about 18 % of the wind and solar expected there 2026–2030), and names floating solar as attractive because it avoids land-use conflicts; eligible hydro plants use only about 19 % of their grid connection on average (Ember, Power on tap, 21 July 2026, https://ember-energy.org/latest-insights/power-on-tap-eu-hydro-giants-can-add-25-gw-renewables-without-new-grids). Primary. JRC work estimates FPV on 337 EU hydropower reservoirs could produce 42.31 TWh covering 2.3 % of reservoir area (Kakoulaki et al., Renewable and Sustainable Energy Reviews, 2023, https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/rensus/v171y2023ics136403212200870x.html). Secondary.
15. Insurance and lending are tightening for new technology classes: Beazley reports underwriters are reassessing exposure to floating solar alongside batteries and offshore wind; lenders expect key permits before debt and review extreme-weather assumptions, fatigue loads, anchoring and cable movement (insurancebusinessmag.com on Beazley research, https://www.insurancebusinessmag.com/uk/news/breaking-news/energy-transition-risk-is-outpacing-insurance-capacity-beazley-research-finds-580262.aspx – secondary; Solarplaza, 4 Aug 2026, URL as above – primary). This supports the business plan's emphasis on DNV-reviewed design and measured reference plants.

## 2. Detailed notes by topic

### 2.1 Global and European floating PV capacity

- IEA PVPS Trends 2025 fact sheet (primary): "Floating PV: At least 8.7 GW global capacity by end of 2024. Major projects in China, India, Chinese Taipei, Europe." The same sheet lists FPV among dual-use segments that "will gain market share as land-use conflicts intensify". Global PV additions in 2024 were led by China (357 GW), EU (66 GW), USA (47 GW), India (32 GW).
- IEA PVPS Task 13 T13-31:2025 (primary): 7.7 GW end-2023; from just above 1.6 GW end-2018. FPV in Europe is mainly on quarry lakes, sandpit lakes, irrigation ponds and other man-made water bodies. The report covers inland and nearshore FPV only, and notes PVGIS has recently added meteorological data for nearshore locations up to 25 km offshore.
- Discrepancy to flag: the business plan cites World Bank/SERIS 1.3 GWp at end-2018; IEA PVPS Task 13 gives "just above 1.6 GW". Using the IEA PVPS 8.7 GW (end-2024) figure would be more current.
- IEA PVPS Snapshot 2026 fact sheet (primary): EU added 65.7 GW in 2025 to 406 GW cumulative; floating PV, agri-PV and solar-plus-storage "continued to expand"; no FPV number for 2025.
- Wood Mackenzie (secondary): Europe's FPV market "almost 150 MW" was cited as the second largest region (date of this figure unclear, likely 2023-era coverage, canalsolar.com.br); 2024 forecast 77 GWdc globally by 2033, APAC 57 GW (81 %). The Floating Solar Landscape 2025 report exists (https://www.woodmac.com/reports/power-markets-floating-solar-landscape-2025-150440107/) but is paywalled; I could not see its numbers.
- An earlier DNV estimate (2022) projected 7–11 GW FPV installed by 2025 from 2 GW in 2020 (oedigital summary, secondary); actual end-2024 was 8.7 GW, so that forecast was roughly met.
- European project scale examples (Solarplaza, primary): Les Îlots Blandin, France, 74.3 MW on former gravel pits (over EUR 50 million debt, full operation June 2025); Obourg, Belgium, 31 MW on a chalk quarry for Holcim (commissioned March 2026, about 30 GWh/yr, up to 15 % of the site's electricity by 2030); proposed 140 MW on a lignite mining lake in Slovenia. Italy offers a premium tariff for FPV; in France floating competes with ground-mount in the same tender system.

### 2.2 Offshore and nearshore floating solar

- Technology maturity: TRL 3–5 for offshore FPV (IEA PVPS Task 13, primary). Task 13 Report T13-39:2026 notes wave-flume experiments show modules can withstand substantial breaking-wave loads without visible damage, and flags snow, ice and seasonal floating/grounded transitions as structural challenges (iea-pvps.org summary via search, secondary) – relevant for Nordic sites.
- China: 1 GW HG14 pile-mounted project (Shandong, Dec 2025, secondary); Jiangsu 27.3 GW offshore solar plan (secondary); China's first factory-scale all-seawater floating PV project at Sinopec Qingdao Refining and Chemical, completed 1 July 2025 (secondary, search extract) – an industrial-host coastal FPV analogue.
- Netherlands: Oceans of Energy assembled a 0.5 MW array (Nymphaea Aurora) for Hollandse Kust Noord in June 2025; RWE's Hollandse Kust West VII bid included a 5 MW SolarDuck offshore FPV demonstrator with storage, planned for 2026 (pv-tech 2023 and Solarplaza 2026). In January 2025 the Dutch government reportedly decided not to include floating solar criteria in upcoming offshore wind tenders (search extract of rechargenews/pv-tech, secondary – verify before use). A 2020 DNV GL study for the Dutch government projected about 100 MW of North Sea floating solar by 2030 and 500 MW by 2035 (pv magazine, 10 Dec 2020, https://www.pv-magazine.com/2020/12/10/offshore-floating-pv-may-reach-maturity-in-2030, secondary).
- Spain: 4.45–6.48 GW potential under maritime spatial planning (secondary, above). Port of Bilbao marine demonstrator (Landatu Solar) tests waves, wind, corrosion and anchoring (Solarplaza, primary).
- Mediterranean islands: Malta published a National Policy for the Deployment of Offshore Renewable Energy in 2024 covering floating wind and solar, and ran a market consultation in January 2024 for a circa 50 MW floating solar project in territorial waters; 13 proposals were received (MaltaToday, https://www.maltatoday.com.mt/news/national/129371/13_proposals_for_floating_solar_farms_received_in_market_consultation_exercise_regulator_says; Chambers Renewable Energy 2026 Malta, https://practiceguides.chambers.com/practice-guides/renewable-energy-2026/malta). Secondary. The Port of Marsaxlokk is documented by the European Sea Ports Organisation as a floating solar port practice (https://espo.be/practices/the-port-of-marsaxlokk-in-the-development-of-float). Secondary, not read. Italy: an Italian offshore industry association paper estimated up to 1 GW of offshore solar in five years (offshore-energy.biz headline only; the article itself could not be retrieved). Unverified.
- Cost at sea: a Europe-wide coastal LCOE mapping (University of Plymouth-led, Solar Energy, 2024) gives roughly 400 EUR/MWh in the North Sea and 440 EUR/MWh in the Baltic for offshore FPV (search extract of https://research.ucc.ie/en/publications/mapping-of-the-levelised-cost-of-energy-from-floating-solar-pv-in/). Secondary – check the paper's assumptions before citing; these levels are far above Nordic industrial PPA levels and reflect open-sea designs, which helps argue for nearshore sites with partial protection.
- Co-location with offshore wind: Oceans of Energy claims solar between turbines could raise energy per sea area up to fivefold (SolarPower Europe news, https://www.solarpowereurope.org/news/world-s-first-commercial-project-offshore-solar-floats-between-wind-turbines, company claim, secondary). The EU BAMBOO project (2024–2027) develops a blueprint for offshore solar between wind turbines (Copernicus OOS2025 abstract, secondary). A Belgian study modelled hydrodynamic effects of elevated FPV inside an offshore wind farm (Frontiers in Marine Science, Oct 2025, https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fmars.2025.1674859/full). Secondary.

### 2.3 Nordic and Baltic specifics

- Norway demand: Statnett LMA 2024–2050 (Feb 2025): 140 TWh today to 180–260 TWh in 2050, base 220 TWh (secondary via MNI/MarketScreener). KMA 2024–2029: +20 TWh to 2029 in medium scenario from electrification and new industry, with industrial growth slower and later than earlier forecasts (secondary, search extract). Statnett/NVE base scenarios: +35–38 TWh by 2040 (Menon Economics for Offshore Norge, Oct 2025, https://www.offshorenorge.no/globalassets/dokumenter/naringspolitikk/rapporter/menon-economics_scenarier-for-kraftbalanse-mot-2040.pdf, secondary). Data centres: Norway's reserved capacity 3.5 GW and queue 5.4 GW (Argus, https://www.argusmedia.com/en/news-and-insights/latest-market-news/2806298-nordic-data-centres-to-support-power-demand-growth, secondary).
- NVE Q4 2025 (primary): industrial consumption fell slightly in NO1 and NO5 year-on-year, rose in NO4; data-centre growth in NO3. A new Aurland–Sogndal line from October 2025 increased exchange between NO3 and NO1/NO5. Norgespris (fixed 40 øre/kWh for households from 1 Oct 2025) does not apply to industry – industrial buyers remain exposed to spot prices, which supports fixed-price PPAs.
- Quarterly 2025 spot averages from NVE quarterly reports (search extract, secondary except Q4): Q1 NO1 74, NO2 78, NO5 64 øre/kWh; Q2 NO1 61.8, NO2 68.2, NO5 45.8; Q3 NO1 61, NO2 79, NO5 36; Q4 NO1 77, NO2 82, NO5 74 (Q4 range confirmed in primary). An unweighted average gives roughly 68 (NO1), 77 (NO2), 55 (NO5) øre/kWh for 2025 – my own approximation, not an official figure. Q1 2026 reportedly NO1 123, NO2 118 øre/kWh (secondary, unverified).
- Household prices: 141.7 øre/kWh incl. grid and taxes in 2025; energy-only 66.2 øre/kWh (SSB via search extract, secondary). SSB's latest article (16 Feb 2026) gives the household price 2 % lower than 2024 (primary). No industrial price series found.
- Norway solar targets: political goal 8 TWh by 2030 (~10 GW) versus NVE forecast 4 TWh (NVE, primary). Ground-mounted solar is limited by grid capacity and land-use conflict (NVE, primary). No NVE, Statnett or Enova study or target for floating solar was found. Equinor applied to NVE for a 1 MWp pilot floating solar plant for two years (NVE document search result, secondary) – evidence that NVE licensing practice for FPV exists.
- Sweden, Denmark, Finland, Baltics: Sweden 5.4 GW, Denmark 6.3–6.5 GW, Finland about 1.56 GW (IRENA estimate via TaiyangNews, secondary). Baltic end-2025 not found; at end-2023 Lithuania and Estonia each had about 1.1 GW and Latvia about 300 MW (secondary). Finland's solar project pipeline exceeds 26 GW (TaiyangNews, https://taiyangnews.info/markets/finland-solar-pv-project-pipeline-exceeds-26-gw, secondary). No Baltic Sea offshore/nearshore FPV potential study found.

### 2.4 Market trends: prices, capex, hybrids

- Module prices: see key finding 8. European-assembled modules cost about twice imports (OPIS, Oct 2026), which matters for NZIA-driven "resilience" auctions.
- Capex composition: in a 50 MW case, mounting (incl. floats) and balance-of-plant make up 39 % of FPV capex versus 29 % for ground-mount (PV Tech, Crunching the numbers on floating solar, https://www.pv-tech.org/wp-content/uploads/legacy-publication-pdfs/9e59b1a3fb-crunching-the-numbers-on-floating-solar.pdf, secondary). Wood Mackenzie-linked reporting puts the FPV capex gap at about USD 0.13–0.15/Wp (pv-tech, secondary, search extract – verify). India: floating tariffs about 33 % above ground-mount (Renewable Watch, 12 Feb 2026, secondary).
- Overcapacity: global module manufacturing capacity 1,405 GW/yr versus 726 GW production (IEA PVPS Trends 2025 fact sheet, primary) – prices likely to stay low; structure, mooring and installation remain the cost lever.
- Hydro hybrids: Ember 25 GW (primary). EDP: 5 MW floating at Alqueva, 70 MW awarded in Portugal's floating auction, 12 multi-technology hybrid sites in Europe incl. Poland (EDP and pv-tech, secondary). Lazer (France) hydro-reservoir FPV commissioned 2023 (Ember, primary). Relevant to the adjacent wave-exposed reservoir/lake segment.

### 2.5 Policy drivers

- EU Solar Strategy / REPowerEU: 750 GW target 2030 (SolarPower Europe, primary); NECP aggregate 701 GW (secondary).
- RED III renewables acceleration areas: Member States had to designate acceleration areas for at least one technology by 21 February 2026, with streamlined permitting inside them (EC enabling framework page, https://energy.ec.europa.eu/topics/renewable-energy/enabling-framework-renewables_en, secondary). Compliance is uneven: Oeko-Institut found 26 of 27 Member States missed the 21 May 2025 mapping deadline (Denmark the exception) (https://www.oeko.de/en/news/press-releases/eus-renewable-energy-directive-remains-a-catalyst-for-renewables-amidst-political-instability, secondary); CAN Europe (April 2026) reports designation "remains uneven" after the deadline (https://caneurope.org/app/uploads/2026/04/renewable-acceleration-areas--eu-policy-briefing.pdf, secondary). No evidence found that any Member State has designated marine/coastal acceleration areas for FPV specifically.
- NZIA non-price criteria: already in the business plan; European-made components carry a large price premium today (OPIS), so non-price criteria are the channel by which European-made floats and assembly gain weight.

### 2.6 Barriers documented

- Permitting: multi-domain approvals (energy, water use, spatial planning, environment); authorities assess coverage, shore distance, navigation, dam safety, water quality, biodiversity (Solarplaza 2026, primary). Natural-lake bans and coverage caps (Wood Mackenzie via pv-tech 2023, secondary).
- Technical/reliability: corrosion, biofouling, tides, waves, mechanical stress at joints of rigid structures, data transmission at remote sites (IEA PVPS Task 13, primary).
- Bankability: lenders want key permits before debt; due diligence on extreme weather, fatigue, anchoring, cables, warranties, maintenance access (Solarplaza, primary).
- Insurance: floating solar treated increasingly as a catastrophe-exposed class (Beazley, secondary); protection gap for new climate technologies (FERMA, Sept 2025, secondary); GCube/Tokio Marine "Known Unknowns" (April 2025) on insurability across Europe (secondary, not FPV-specific).
- Cost: open-sea LCOE estimates around 400–440 EUR/MWh in North Sea/Baltic (secondary, verify); offshore capex about 30 % above ground-mount (secondary).
- Revenue: rising negative-price hours erode merchant solar revenue in the EU (SolarPower Europe, primary) – favours behind-the-meter industrial supply.

## 3. Gaps / what could not be found

- No authoritative 2025 global or European FPV installed figure. Latest primary figure is IEA PVPS 8.7 GW end-2024. The Wood Mackenzie Floating Solar Landscape 2025 report is paywalled.
- No authoritative European FPV cumulative capacity for 2024 or 2025 (only an older Wood Mackenzie "almost 150 MW" figure of unclear date, which looks low against single plants of 74 MW and 31 MW now operating; do not use it).

Commercial market-research reports (Market Research Future: offshore floating solar USD 2.5 billion in 2024 to USD 12.0 billion by 2035; Mordor Intelligence: offshore/nearshore 35.5 % CAGR to 2030) are not transparent and should not be cited in the business plan.
- No JRC or European Commission study of EU-wide marine/nearshore FPV potential found. No North Sea-wide or Baltic potential in GW (only the 2020 DNV GL Dutch study and Spanish national study).
- No current DNV Energy Transition Outlook or BNEF figure for floating or offshore solar. DNV's 2022 "Future of floating solar" page gives no figures without the full report.
- Statnett LMA 2024–2050 PDF could not be downloaded (TLS handshake failure); industry-specific consumption numbers and Statnett's solar projection are therefore secondary only.
- No official 2025 annual average spot price per Norwegian price zone (only quarterly NVE values); no SSB industrial electricity price found.
- No public floating solar target or study from NVE, Statnett, Enova or the Swedish Energy Agency.
- No 2025 solar capacity figures for Estonia, Latvia, Lithuania.
