# Technology risk, standards and IP: desk research for the KER1 business plan

Date: 2026-11-14. Desk research of about 40 web searches and fetches. Sources are marked [P] for primary (standards body, patent office or register, peer-reviewed paper, official report) and [S] for secondary (trade press, law-firm commentary, company press release, aggregator). Patent legal status comes from Google Patents or other aggregators. Google Patents labels its own status an assumption, so check the EPO, USPTO or CNIPA register before relying on any status. Nothing here is a legal opinion or a freedom-to-operate opinion.

## 1. Key findings

- DNV published two FPV design standards in April/May 2026. DNV-ST-C108 covers the structural design of floats and DNV-ST-E309 covers station keeping (mooring). Both list "Edition 2026-04" and were announced on 14 May 2026. DNV-RP-0584 (2021-03, amended 2021-10) is now positioned as complementary system-level guidance, and an update was announced for June 2026 [P: DNV news 2026-05-14; DNV standard pages]. The draft's "May 2026" is correct for the announcement. The formal edition date is April 2026.
- DNV-ST-C108 requires safety/consequence classification, a minimum design-basis content, material selection and qualification, failure mechanisms of non-metallic materials, design and testing of non-metallic components exposed to solar radiation, a test programme, and coating/corrosion protection [P: DNV ST-C108 page]. This maps directly onto the PU float, PU hinge and aluminium plate of the product. The polymer qualification and UV route is where the review will focus.
- DNV-RP-0584 is scoped to sheltered inland water and is "applicable for near-shore". Offshore and harsher locations are explicitly out of scope, where it serves only as guidance [S: GlobalSpec/DNV-linked summaries of the RP]. The scope of the two new standards (nearshore vs offshore) was not stated on the public pages.
- IEC work on FPV is still in draft. IEC TS 62548-3 ED1 ("PV arrays – Part 3: Floating photovoltaic power plants", TC 82) was at the WD/CD stage, with CD forecast for 30 Oct 2026 [P: ISS national mirror of the IEC project]. IEC TS 63496 ED1 ("Floating photovoltaic power plants – Design guidelines and recommendations") was at CD stage in April 2025 [S: Genorma listing]. No document numbered IEC TS 63574 was found. The number given in the brief may be wrong.
- Third-party certification of FPV structures is emerging. Bureau Veritas issued what it called the first Type Certificate for a floating solar structure (membrane design, Norway-based competitor), covering design review, manufacturing assessment and type testing, with PV modules excluded (28 Nov 2025) [S: energy-pedia, company/BV release]. DNV completed a technical review of the rope-mesh concept from another Norwegian competitor in mid-2026 [S: NA Clean Energy / pv magazine 2026-07]. BV gave approval in principle to two offshore concepts [S].
- Documented FPV storm failures point to mooring and connection-chain failure, not float rupture. At Yamakura (13.7 MW, Japan, Typhoon Faxai, 9 Sep 2019) seven anchors failed on an under-anchored edge (68 anchors vs 107–133 on other edges). Resin bolts between floats then failed progressively, the array split into three parts and caught fire. Shintaku Tameike (2.4 MW) failed the same way two weeks later. METI noted that no floating-specific structural code existed at the time [S: pv magazine, 22 Feb 2020, citing the METI preliminary report].
- At sea, Typhoon Danas (6 Jul 2025) broke up an offshore floating platform field (about 1,400 platforms, 16 ha) off Pingtung, Taiwan, and scattered debris over more than 1 km. The developer cited the need for reinforced connections between platforms. The authority ordered debris removal and a repair plan before restart [S: Taiwan News, 7 Jul 2025]. Debris and permit consequences are an insurance and permitting risk on top of the asset loss.
- Peer-reviewed reviews (Huang et al., Ocean Engineering 322, 2025; Shi et al., Ocean Engineering 286; Liu et al., JMSE 12(11):1942, 2024) name wave-induced fatigue of connectors and moorings, salinity corrosion, biofouling and the lack of long-term field data as the main marine FPV risks [P, abstracts only]. The IEA PVPS Task 13 report (April 2025) includes a preliminary FMEA and says FPV standards are still being developed [P: IEA PVPS / Sandia SAND2025-04732R abstract].
- On polyurethane in seawater, polyester-based PU hydrolyses: chain scission is first-order with an activation energy of about 71 kJ/mol, and stress at break falls steadily (Taourit, Le Gac, Fayolle, Polym. Degrad. Stab. 201, 2022) [P]. Polyether PUs resist hydrolysis better but are more sensitive to oxidation [S: summary of Ifremer work]. Combined UV plus seawater degrades PU by photo-oxidation (RSC Environ. Sci.: Processes & Impacts, 2023, microplastic scale) [P]. The chemistry choice (polyether vs polyester, aliphatic vs aromatic, UV stabilisation) is a material qualification item under ST-C108.
- IEC TS 62788-7-2:2017 defines accelerated weathering exposures (steady irradiance, temperature, humidity) for polymeric PV materials. It was written for backsheets and encapsulants, and the IEC states that other materials are not verified. Thermal and wet/dry cycling are deferred to future parts [P: IEC webstore / VDE preview]. Using it for a structural PU float and hinge is a reasonable proxy, but it does not by itself qualify a load-bearing elastomer. A DNV reviewer is likely to want mechanical testing after ageing.
- Aluminium 5083 has low general corrosion in seawater (2.8 µm/year in one lab study) but is sensitive to pitting, crevice and galvanic corrosion when in contact with steel, stainless steel or copper. Temperature increases severity [P/S: Galati university journal; AMPP/NTNU AA5083/6082 work; secondary summaries]. Fasteners, mooring hardware and any stainless parts touching the bottom plate are the design risk.
- Biofouling at a Dutch North Sea FPV pilot fully colonised the floats within months: 72 taxa, about 11 % non-indigenous, with blue mussel dominant. No anti-fouling coating differed significantly from the others (Mavraki et al., Front. Mar. Sci., 28 Dec 2023) [P]. Fouling adds weight (over 2 kg/m² on a wave energy converter) and drag, and modelling suggests it may cut mooring fatigue life by about 20 % (Hooper, Armstrong, Vlaswinkel, accepted manuscript) [P].
- Environmental evidence for marine FPV is early but not negative. A 50 kWp North Sea pilot showed no clear deviation in water quality or seabed against a reference (Vlaswinkel et al., Sustainability 15(8):6533, 2023) [P]. North Sea modelling showed SST effects under 0.1 °C but reduced local current speed (Frontiers in Marine Science, 2025) [P]. Reviews flag shading of seagrass and benthos, chain scour, invasive-species stepping stones and wet-tow transfer of non-native species as permitting issues (Benjamins et al., RSER 2024; Hooper et al.) [P].
- The patent landscape on flexible and articulated FPV is active and crowded. Examples: a Norwegian maritime engineering firm (now Saipem) holds a granted NO patent, with an abandoned US and a pending EP family member, on rigid elements joined by flexible joints that change shape in waves (NO347181B1, EP4173134A1, priority 2020). SolarDuck has an EP family on pivotably connected triangular frames (EP4267455A1, register shows refused July 2026). Jiangsu University of Science and Technology holds an active US patent on wave-dissipating FPV units joined by rubber rings (US11858589B1, to 2043). There are several Chinese patents on breakwater-integrated FPV (CN116208066B, CN113718708A). This supports the patent attorney's prior-art finding. It also means FTO should be checked for claims on flexible inter-float joints and breakwater-plus-array arrangements [P: Google Patents records].
- A registered EU design (it replaced the Registered Community Design on 1 May 2025) costs €350 for the first design and €125 for each additional design in a multiple application. It is registered in days after a formalities-only check, with no novelty search. Protection lasts up to 25 years in 5-year terms (renewals €150/€250/€400/€700). Publication can be deferred up to 30 months, and there is a 12-month grace period after own disclosure [S: CMS expert guide, 28 Oct 2025; Hindles/Boult]. It does not cover Norway. Norway needs a national filing with Patentstyret or a Hague designation [P: Patentstyret]. Features solely dictated by technical function are excluded (CJEU C-395/16 DOCERAM, 2018) [P], which limits what a hinge design registration can protect.
- Trade-secret protection under the EU Trade Secrets Directive (2016/943) requires "reasonable steps" to keep information secret: NDAs, access control, marking and documentation. Reverse engineering of a product lawfully on the market is lawful acquisition unless contractually restricted [S: Carpmaels, Boehmert, Meissner Bolte commentary]. A hinge that is visible on a delivered kit is therefore weakly protected by secrecy. Casting recipes, process parameters and planning-tool rules are better candidates.

## 2. Detailed notes per topic

### 2.1 DNV floating PV standards

- DNV-RP-0584, "Design, development and operation of floating solar photovoltaic systems". Edition 2021-03, amended 2021-10, 152 pages. Covers the whole life cycle, from component to system: site conditions, energy yield, mooring and anchoring, floating structures, permitting and environmental impact. It is technology-neutral and gives functional requirements. Focus is on sheltered inland water and it is applicable nearshore. Offshore or harsher sites are out of scope, where it serves only as guidance [P: DNV standard page; S: GlobalSpec listing, marine technology press 2021]. DNV announced an update for June 2026. Whether it was published was not confirmed in this research.
- DNV-ST-C108, "Structural design of floats for floating photovoltaic systems". Edition 2026-04. Requirements are performance-based and scale with the consequence of float failure. Contents: safety and consequence categories, minimum design-basis content, material selection and qualification, failure mechanisms for non-metallic materials, design and safety factors for metallic and non-metallic parts, test programme and procedures, design and testing of non-metallic components exposed to solar radiation, coating and corrosion protection. Paywalled, through DNV Veracity Rules and Standards Explorer+ [P: DNV page].
- DNV-ST-E309, "Station keeping of floating solar photovoltaic systems". Edition 2026-04. Covers design loads, load combinations and analysis procedures. Risk assessment is FMECA-based and safety factors are calibrated with structural reliability analysis. Includes component and system-configuration requirements to reduce failure risk. Installation, inspection, O&M and decommissioning are excluded and stay in RP-0584 [P: DNV page and news 2026-05-14; S: pv magazine USA 2026-05-20, TaiyangNews].
- DNV's news release stresses common ground for "developers, owners, insurers and regulators". It cites a GMI estimate of the FPV market (USD 7.9 billion in 2026 to 9.2 billion by 2035) [P: DNV news]. That market figure is far below other forecasts and should not be reused without checking.
- A DNV press release from 2022 reports DNV verification of a design basis to RP-0584 for the company's earlier product generation [P: DNV news 2022]. This is relevant precedent for the planned design-basis assessment of Generation 2.
- Implications for the business plan: a design-basis assessment against ST-C108 and ST-E309 (with RP-0584 for system aspects) is now the credible bankability route. The polymer qualification under ST-C108 (UV, seawater, fatigue after ageing) is likely the longest-lead item, and the budget of about €0.15 million should be checked against the test scope.

### 2.2 IEC and other standards and certification

- IEC TS 62548-3 ED1, "Photovoltaic (PV) arrays – Part 3: Floating photovoltaic power plants". TC 82 Technical Specification. Stage 20.99 (WD approved for registration as CD) since Mar 2023, next stage (CD) forecast 30 Oct 2026. No publication date forecast [P: ISS Serbia mirror of IEC project 114592].
- IEC TS 63496 ED1, "Floating photovoltaic power plants – Design guidelines and recommendations". CD date April 2025 (an earlier listing showed June 2023). Scope: design and installation of grid-connected FPV plants, including array configurations, cable routing and selection, overcurrent protection [S: Genorma listing; BSI development project page could not be loaded]. These may be the same work item renumbered, or two parallel items. This was not resolved.
- No IEC TS 63574 was found in IEC-mirror or aggregator searches.
- National references: Singapore TR 100:2022 (FPV) [S: Singapore Standards eShop listing]; Korean and Chinese national standards are mentioned in the IEA PVPS report abstract [P].
- Certification schemes: there is no single FPV certification scheme. The routes in use are DNV Statement of Conformity / design-basis verification to RP-0584, DNV technical review, Bureau Veritas Approval in Principle and BV Type Certificate (first issued Nov 2025) [S: energy-pedia 2025-11-28; DNV 2020 and 2022 news; NA Clean Energy 2026].
- Bankability and insurance: lenders require independent technical due diligence on technology, design, construction and operation. For FPV this includes bathymetry and water-level checks [S: PV Tech technical paper by DNV authors, 2020]. Lenders set their own insurance requirements as a loan condition [S: Marsh Commercial]. No FPV-specific underwriting criteria were found from insurers. The renewables insurer formerly called GCube (now Tokio Marine GX) publishes claims reports, but no floating-solar-specific loss data was found.

### 2.3 Failure modes and incidents

- Yamakura dam, Chiba, Japan, 13.7 MW, Typhoon Faxai (No. 15), 9 Sep 2019. Mean wind 41 m/s, gusts 57.5 m/s nearby. About two-thirds of the plant was destroyed and parts burned. Per the METI preliminary report: seven anchors loosened on the southern edge (68 anchors vs 107–133 on other edges). Mooring was attached only to the outer floats. Inner floats were joined by resin bolts, so load concentrated on the first bolt in each row and bolts failed in a chain. The array split into three parts and the northern half was blown away. The edge lost its water ballast, the array curled up and damaged equipment short-circuited into fire. The design wind load (JIS C 8955, minimum 38 m/s) was exceeded only marginally. The float supplier increased safety margins [S: pv magazine, M. Willuhn, 22 Feb 2020]. The plant was rebuilt in smaller sections and back in operation by Oct 2021 [S: oneindia/other secondary]. The METI final report was not located.
- Shintaku Tameike, Kyushu, Japan, 2.4 MW, Typhoon No. 17, Sep 2019. Same mechanism, with bolt collapse sending the array to shore [S: pv magazine 2020].
- Pingtung, Taiwan, offshore floating platforms (about 1,400 platforms on 16 ha, no modules installed yet), Typhoon Danas, 6 Jul 2025. Platforms were driven ashore and debris spread over more than 1 km. About 60 % were recovered. The developer said the gaps between platforms need reinforced connections. Authorities required debris removal and a repair plan before restart [S: Taiwan News 2025-07-07].
- Reported survivals (company claims, not independent): a membrane demonstrator on Magat Dam (Philippines) through two typhoon seasons; a 16 MW plant at Zhanjiang (China) through Super Typhoon Capricorn (2024); a US system through Hurricane Milton (2024) [S: offshore-energy.biz, CleanTechnica].
- Lessons that carry over to a hinged nearshore array: (1) the mooring and connection chain is the critical path, and progressive failure across inter-float joints is the documented mechanism; (2) segmentation limits the consequences of a single failure; (3) uplift and edge curling under wind matter for low-freeboard floats; (4) fire risk follows mechanical failure; (5) debris liability affects permits. The product's full-edge flexible hinge and replaceable hinge directly address (1) and (2). The business plan can say so, but it needs test evidence on hinge fatigue as well as ultimate load.
- Literature on hinged FPV: hinged connections are widely preferred for flexibility, but short waves cause relative motion between modules. In harsh seas this leads to hinge buckling [S: secondary summary of Cranfield-hosted papers; full text not read].
- Reviews: Huang et al., "Developing reliable floating solar systems on seas: a review", Ocean Engineering 322 (2025) 120525 [P]. Shi et al., "Review on the development of marine floating photovoltaic systems", Ocean Engineering 286, 115560 [P]. Liu et al., "Review of Recent Offshore Floating Photovoltaic Systems", JMSE 12(11):1942 (2024), doi 10.3390/jmse12111942 [P]. IEA PVPS T13-31-2025, Selj, Wieland, Tsanakas et al., April 2025, doi 10.69766/KDYA8846, also SAND2025-04732R, doi 10.2172/2562745, with an FMEA, degradation mechanisms and stress-testing needs [P]. Only abstracts and landing pages were read, not full texts.

### 2.4 Materials durability: PU, elastomers, aluminium, biofouling

- Polyester PU in seawater (40–90 °C): Fickian water uptake, then ester hydrolysis with first-order chain scission (Ea about 71 kJ/mol). Stress at break falls continuously. Strain at break rises, then drops once crosslink density falls below about 1.25 kg/mol (Taourit, Le Gac, Fayolle, Polymer Degradation and Stability 201 (2022) 109971, doi 10.1016/j.polymdegradstab.2022.109971) [P]. The Arrhenius law allows lifetime extrapolation from accelerated tests, which is a useful method to cite in the test plan.
- Polyether PU: better hydrolysis resistance, weaker against oxidation. One study of commercial polyether PUs immersed up to two years at 50–100 °C reported full tensile retention, with FTIR signs of hydrolysis [S: search summary of Ifremer-group papers; exact citation not verified].
- UV plus seawater: polyester TPU, polyether TPU and crosslinked PU all degraded by photo-oxidation under simulated sunlight in seawater. 350 MJ/m² UV was about 15 months outdoors at 31° N (RSC Environmental Science: Processes & Impacts, 2023, doi via d2em00415a) [P]. The study looked at microplastics, so transfer to bulk parts is uncertain.
- Aromatic vs aliphatic isocyanates: aliphatic yellows less. One Croatian study found aromatic PU elastomers kept their mechanical properties better despite discolouring [S: bib.irb.hr record; full text not read]. Outdoor PU seals lose tensile strength and elasticity and crack under prolonged sunlight [S: Emerald, Rubber/Seal journal 2025, abstract]. Conclusion: colour change is not a proxy for structural loss. Qualification should measure tensile, tear and fatigue properties after ageing.
- IEC TS 62788-7-2:2017 (TC 82): accelerated weathering exposures (steady simulated irradiance plus temperature plus humidity) for polymeric materials in PV modules. Intended to be referenced by other standards and used in R&D. Validated for backsheets and encapsulants only. Annexes give climate configurations and benchmark solar doses (2000–2015). Cyclic stresses are deferred. An amendment was in development [P: IEC webstore; VDE preview]. A float/hinge programme would typically add seawater immersion, thermal cycling, mechanical fatigue after ageing and possibly ISO 4892 xenon or ISO 11507 UV-fluorescent exposures. This is an inference, not a cited requirement.
- Aluminium 5083 in seawater: good general resistance, prone to pitting, crevice and intergranular corrosion. Galvanic corrosion occurs when coupled to carbon steel, stainless steel or copper alloys. Severity increases with temperature [P/S: AMPP conference paper "AA5083 and AA6082 exposed to seawater – effect of temperature and potential" (NTNU); Galati university journal "Corrosion behavior of Al5083, 316L and A681 in seawater" (2.8 µm/year potentiodynamic); secondary reviews]. A commercial blog figure (<0.025 mm/year) has no primary citation and should not be used. Salt-spray rates (about 0.2 mm/year after 30 days) are not comparable with immersion data. Sensitisation of 5xxx alloys (beta-phase precipitation above about 65 °C, which causes intergranular attack) is a known marine issue but was not researched here. Sunlit plates under panels can get hot, so this should be checked.
- Biofouling: at the Brouwersdam pilot (Dutch North Sea, 4–7 m depth), floats 17–428 days old were mostly fully colonised. 72 taxa were found, about 11 % non-indigenous, with Mytilus edulis and Obelia bidentata dominant. Four coatings (Intersleek, PPDura, Finsulate, Pato) showed no statistically significant difference. Biomass grew with time (Mavraki et al., Front. Mar. Sci. 10, 28 Dec 2023, doi 10.3389/fmars.2023.1223766) [P]. Hooper, Armstrong and Vlaswinkel (Plymouth, Lancaster, Oceans of Energy; accepted manuscript, Lancaster eprint 148346; journal not verified in this session) report fouling over 2 kg/m² at the waterline of a wave energy converter. Fouling increases drag and hydrodynamic loads, may affect cable heat dissipation, likely enhances corrosion fatigue, and modelling suggests about 20 % shorter mooring fatigue life. Splash-zone films can reduce light to panels. Knowledge gaps exist for novel materials [P]. Relevance: fouling mass on hinges and bottom plates reduces freeboard (6 cm design freeboard per project facts). Fouling on the PU and aluminium interfaces is an O&M cost item.

### 2.5 Environmental impact (permitting)

- Vlaswinkel, Roos, Nelissen, "Environmental observations at the first offshore solar farm in the North Sea", Sustainability 15(8):6533 (2023): 50 kWp, 400 m². No clear trend or deviation in water quality or seabed vs an open-sea reference. Bird use of the platform observed [P].
- Frontiers in Marine Science (2025, doi 10.3389/fmars.2025.1674859): modelled FPV inside a Belgian offshore wind farm. SST effect under 0.1 °C, significant reduction in current speed near the floats. Described as a first step to a full EIA [P, provisionally accepted when indexed].
- Benjamins et al., "Potential environmental impacts of floating solar photovoltaic systems", Renewable and Sustainable Energy Reviews (2024). Groups impacts into shading, hydrodynamics and air–water exchange, energy emissions, benthic effects, mobile species and social acceptability, and compares marine with freshwater evidence [P; abstract via search, Tethys page blocked].
- Rocha et al., Environmental Research: Infrastructure and Sustainability (Jan 2024): systematic review of 422 evidence items for freshwater (283 physical, 96 chemical, 43 biological). Reduced evaporation is best documented, and chemical and biological effects are poorly understood [P].
- Hooper et al. (accepted manuscript): marine insights from freshwater do not transfer directly. Risks: anchoring and cable impacts, sediment resuspension during installation, shading of seagrass and coral, scour from slack chains at low tide, invasive-species spread especially via wet-tow from ports, change from soft-sediment to hard-substrate communities. Benefits: possible reduced turbidity, negligible effect on net primary production in one modelling study [P].
- Company monitoring claims (Oceans of Energy: no negative impact in more than four years) are self-reported [S].
- Implications for permitting: expect requests for baseline seabed and seagrass surveys, mooring footprint and chain-scour mitigation, and a biofouling and invasive-species plan (including tow route and cleaning). Small harbour and industrial sites with already-modified seabed are favourable.

### 2.7 EU design registration and trade secrets

- Registered EU design (REUD, since 1 May 2025, replacing the RCD under Regulation (EU) 2024/2822): filing fee €350 for one design. From 1 May 2025, €125 for each additional design in a multiple application (previous tiers were €175 and €80). Formalities-only examination (is it a design; public policy and morality), no novelty or individual-character search. Can be registered within days. Deferment of publication up to 30 months. 12-month grace period for the applicant's own disclosures. Term of 5 years, renewable to 25 years. Renewal fees €150, €250, €400, €700, with a 25 % surcharge for late renewal. Expiry now falls on the anniversary date of filing [S: CMS expert guide, 28 Oct 2025; Hindles; Boult Wade Tennant; Mondaq; EUIPO Communication 3/24 listed but not read].
- Geographic gap: an EU design does not cover EEA/EFTA states such as Norway. Norway needs a national application to Patentstyret or a Hague international registration designating Norway (Norway in Hague since June 2010). Six-month priority from a Norwegian filing is possible [P: Patentstyret, "Design registration in other countries"].
- Validity limits: features solely dictated by technical function are excluded. The CJEU (C-395/16 DOCERAM, 8 Mar 2018) held that the existence of alternative designs is not decisive. The question is whether technical function was the only factor determining the appearance [P: CJEU judgment via BAILII; S: Gowling, Bird & Bird]. A "must-fit" interconnection exclusion also exists, but it was not researched in this session. For a hinge whose shape is engineered for load and flexibility, a design registration may be vulnerable or narrow. The float's overall appearance is a better design candidate. Unregistered EU design protection (short term, copying only) exists, but its duration was not verified in this session.
- Trade secrets (Directive (EU) 2016/943; implemented in Norway by the 2020 Trade Secrets Act): protection requires that the information is secret, has commercial value because it is secret, and is subject to reasonable steps to keep it secret. Practical steps cited by commentators: confidentiality instructions, NDAs with suppliers and casting subcontractors, access restriction, technical and organisational measures, and documentation of the measures for evidence. Independent discovery and reverse engineering of a product lawfully obtained are lawful, but reverse engineering can be restricted contractually with licensees and suppliers [S: Carpmaels; Meissner Bolte 2018; Boehmert; Osborne Clarke]. Implication: casting with subcontractors in Asia today and in Europe later needs NDAs and a no-reverse-engineering clause. The hinge geometry becomes public once kits ship.
