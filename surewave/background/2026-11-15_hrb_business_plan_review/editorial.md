# Editorial review – HRB business plan, KER1 Sunlit Sea CONNECT

Reviewed file: `surewave/deliverables/2026-11-14_surewave_hrb_business_plan_ker1.md` (draft of 14 November 2026). Reviewer role: senior editor, business and EU-funding documents. Reader assumed: META Group mentor, later investors; English as a second language.

## 1. Overall assessment

The plan is factual, well sourced and mostly free of selling language, and the organisation and product sections open with their main point. The market chapter is the weak part: "Sector", "Stage of development" and "Role of innovation" read as a research digest (third-party numbers on China, Taiwan, Spain, the Netherlands, Norwegian price zones) and push the company's own story – a 6.75 MWp named pipeline and a wave-tested hinge – far down the page. Several facts are stated three to five times (the €0.30–0.40/W price, 8–12 % engineering fee, the 37 kN hinge result, the failed North Sea pilot, the customer list, the DNV standards), and some sentences weaken the company without need (the factory closure, the patent decision, "several times higher still", "no survey was found"). There are no bold-in-body violations, but internal references remain ("sections 2–5", "KER1", "the financial annex" four times, "Booster Intellectual Assets Management service", "Prototype 4"), Norwegian terms are unexplained (øre/kWh, NO1/NO2/NO5, Enova, havn), and the product is named five different ways. Cutting the market chapter by about 40 % and fixing the ten items below would make the plan read as a business plan rather than a background study.

## 2. Top 10 edits, ranked

### 1. Market chapter: lead with the company's market, cut third-party detail

Location: "The market" → "Sector" (paragraphs 2–4), "Stage of development and competitive nature" (Asia bullet) and "Role of innovation and technology".

Problem: four dense paragraphs of global, EU, Asian and national statistics before the reader learns which market Sunlit Sea addresses and how large it is for the company. Detail such as "1.8 GW under construction in Hebei", "373 MWac plant in Changhua", "USD 1.16 billion including storage", Spain's 4.5–6.5 GW and Dutch 45 GWp scenarios, and Denmark/Sweden installed capacity is source-dumping: none of it changes a decision in the plan. The bottom-up view – the one number that matters to an investor – is buried at the end of "Stage of development".

Proposed fix: open "Sector" with a three-sentence summary, keep one paragraph each for floating solar and for Norway, and move the bottom-up pipeline paragraph to directly after it, under its own heading "Market size". Suggested opening:

> Sunlit Sea sells floating solar systems for coastal water next to industry and ports. Floating solar is a small, fast-growing part of the solar market (8.7 GW worldwide at the end of 2024, IEA PVPS), and almost all of it is on calm inland water. In Europe, rules that limit floating solar on inland water and the lack of free land near industry are moving the segment towards the coast, where few suppliers have a product designed for waves.

Cut: the Asia bullet down to one sentence ("In Asia, offshore solar is built at utility scale, mostly on piles in shallow water, for local markets."); the Spain and Dutch scenarios; the Denmark/Sweden comparison; the SolarPower Europe 718 GW / 750 GW sentence. In "Role of innovation", keep the module price, one NREL sentence and the SUREWAVE cost split; drop "early industry estimates put installation costs about 30 % above ground-mount" (unsourced and vague).

### 2. Remove internal framing from the title block and references to the "financial annex"

Location: title line "Business plan — KER1 Sunlit Sea CONNECT …" and subtitle "Horizon Results Booster G2M input · Sunlit Sea AS · sections 2–5, 14 November 2026"; "Pricing strategy" ("The financial annex will detail unit economics…"); "Sales forecast" table ("to be set in the financial annex", three cells) and the sentence below it.

Problem: "KER1", "G2M input" and "sections 2–5" are programme-internal; an investor reading a forwarded copy will not know what they mean. "The financial annex" is a companion-document reference; if the annex is not attached, three empty forecast cells and a promise are what the reader sees, which reads as unfinished.

Proposed rewrite of the header:

> Business plan – Sunlit Sea CONNECT, floating solar for nearshore and sheltered coastal water
>
> Sunlit Sea AS · November 2026 · Prepared with the Horizon Results Booster (go-to-market service)

If the mentor needs the KER label, put it once in a short line: "Key exploitable result 1 of the SUREWAVE project". For the forecast, fill the 2028 and 2030 cells with indicative ranges (even wide ones) or remove the "Indicative revenue" column for those rows and add one sentence: "Financial projections (income statement, cash flow, balance sheet) are provided separately." Delete the "will detail unit economics" bullet in Pricing strategy.

### 3. Collapse the repeated price and fee statements into one place

Location: "Selling prices and customer portfolio" (para 1), "How Sunlit Sea makes money" (items 1–2), "Pricing strategy" (Benchmark bullet).

Problem: "€0.30–0.40 per watt for early projects" appears three times and "8–12 % of project capital cost" twice. The reader meets the same number in the product chapter and twice in the business model chapter.

Proposed fix: keep the full price statement once, in "Pricing strategy". In "Selling prices and customer portfolio" (a heading required by the template), write one short line: "Target prices are set out under Pricing strategy." In "How Sunlit Sea makes money", keep the four streams without figures, or keep the figures there and refer to them from Pricing strategy – but only one of the two. Recommended: figures in "How Sunlit Sea makes money" (the first place a business reader looks), and Pricing strategy explains how they were set.

### 5. Fix the inconsistent "utility-scale" claim and the TRL story

Location: "Mission" ("brings utility-scale solar power to coastal water"); TRL statements in "Objectives", "Current and future products" table, "Development phases and investment" table.

Problem: the plants in the plan are 0.35–3.2 MWp and the mission promises utility scale; a mentor will notice. TRL appears in four places with three values (4–5 now, 5 at design freeze, 6 after Skien), which is correct but hard to follow because each mention gives a different piece.

Proposed fix: Mission – "Sunlit Sea brings solar power to coastal water, so that industry, ports and communities that have run out of land can produce renewable electricity next to where they use it." State the TRL path once, in the product table's status cell or in a single sentence under "Current and future products": "Generation 2 is at TRL 4–5 today, reaches TRL 5 at design freeze in mid-2027 and TRL 6 for sheltered sites after the first winter at Skien havn (2028)." Elsewhere refer only to the milestone, not the number.

### 6. One name for the product, one for the unit of capacity

Location: throughout.

Problem: the same thing is called "Sunlit Sea CONNECT", "Generation 2", "the platform", "the floating system", "the floating solar system", "the kit" and "the product". "Floating solar" and "floating PV" alternate. Capacity alternates between kWp, MWp, kW, MW and MWac, sometimes for the same plant class ("10 MW-class supply contract", "0.3–10 MW plants", "pipeline of 6.75 MWp"). A non-native reader may think these are different things.

Proposed convention (add a one-line definition at first use in "Sunlit Sea CONNECT"):

> Sunlit Sea CONNECT (Generation 2) is the floating platform: floats, hinges and mooring interfaces. It is supplied as a kit; the panels are bought separately.

Then use "CONNECT" for the product, "Generation 2" only when contrasting with Generation 1, "kit" only for the delivered package, and "floating solar" (not "floating PV") except inside source titles. Use MWp/kWp for Sunlit Sea plants and contracts ("10 MWp-class", "0.3–10 MWp"), and keep competitors' units as reported but add "(AC)" or "(peak)" only where it matters. Write "per watt" in prose and "/W" only in tables, consistently.

### 7. The North Sea pilot failure appears three times and reads opportunistic

Location: "Technology risks and qualification" (last para: "The main setback at sea in recent years, a North Sea pilot withdrawn in 2026…"); "Stage of development" (Europe bullet: "withdrawn in 2026 after thermal incidents"); "Competitors" table (Oceans of Energy row).

Problem: three mentions of a competitor's failure, one of them used as evidence for Sunlit Sea's design, comes across as point-scoring. The point (use proven marine electrical components) is a design choice and can stand without it.

Proposed fix: keep the fact once, in the Competitors table. Rewrite the last paragraph of "Technology risks":

> Industry experience points to two main failure paths at sea: moorings and float-to-float connections in storms, and electrical connectors. The full-edge hinge spreads the connection load along the whole float edge, and the electrical design uses proven marine components.

Delete the HKN sentence from the Europe bullet.

### 9. Shorten long sentences and explain acronyms in "Barriers" and "Pricing strategy"

Location: "Barriers to entry and development" → Permits bullet (one bullet, four jurisdictions, 110+ words); "Pricing strategy" → Ceiling bullet; "Role of innovation" (NREL sentence).

Problem: sentences of 40–60 words with nested clauses are hard for a second-language reader. Acronyms "EPC", "TOPCon", "Scope 2", "IPP", "behind the meter", "capture prices" are used without explanation; EPC is used in the scale table before it is expanded under Sales channels.

Proposed rewrite of the Permits bullet as three short sub-points:

> - Permits. Norway: plants below 10 MW have needed no national licence since 1 July 2025; the municipality decides under the Planning and Building Act, and a permit under the Harbour and Fairways Act is also needed. Sweden: floating structures are water operations under the Environmental Code. Germany: solar is banned on natural inland water and limited on artificial water (15 % coverage, 40 m from shore); the rules for coastal and harbour water are being clarified.

Ceiling bullet, split:

> - Ceiling: the buyer's alternative. At Sunlit Sea's target sites there is no land for ground-mounted solar, so the alternative is grid power or a power purchase agreement with a remote plant. Wholesale solar prices are low (about €34/MWh for European solar power purchase agreements in the third quarter of 2025). A coastal plant competes instead on the delivered price at the site (energy plus grid tariff and taxes) and on access to power without waiting for a grid connection.

Expand at first use: "EPC (engineering, procurement and construction)" in the scale table; "TOPCon" can be dropped ("imported modules"); "Scope 2 emissions" → "emissions from purchased electricity"; "behind the meter" → "used on site without passing through the public grid" once, then the short form.
