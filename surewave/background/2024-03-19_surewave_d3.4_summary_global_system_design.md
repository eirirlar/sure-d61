**EXECUTIVE SUMMARY**

As described in the task 3.5 definition "Design of coupled breakwater & floating PV and global system design", The deliverable provides the global design description of the floating solar farm, and the methodology used to define it. The first part of the report is summarizing the methodology used to study and assess critical components. This includes tests such as: Wave tank testing, laboratory tensile tests, laboratory shear tests and basin model tests. The report also describes the methodology behind the design of the floating breakwater and the anchoring & mooring. Based on a set of criteria, configurations for the full installation are assessed. The analysis mentioned in this report are fully available in the deliverables 3.1 "SUREWAVE D3.1 Design description of internal floating PV components", 3.2 "SUREWAVE D3.2 Floating breakwater design" and 3.3 "2023-12-08 - SUREWAVE Deliverable 3.3 final" where the coupling of the external breakwater and internal FPV module is assessed and discussed in regard to movement of the array under certain weather condition.

The circular installation configuration is selected and fully described in a global system summary, main focus of this report, as described in the task 3.5 definition. Included are number of components (bill of material), 3D designs and assembly and deployment requirements. This report discusses the finalized designs for both the concrete breakwater and FPV, along with potential enhancements for critical components. Additionally, these components are engineered to withstand varying wind, wave, and current conditions in exposed locations.

The report allows for a holistic view of the system and gives the reader a possibility to estimate the cost of such installation at a Mega Watt size and at a smaller size (420kWp). In parallel of the cost being assessed, the component summary of this report will be available to the WP7 for setting the project solution scenario (mass-energy balance).

# Introduction

#### Background and context

This deliverable aims to describe the project\'s coupled solution. It is done through a use-case scenario, previously identified as part of deliverable 2.1 - Use-case scenario definition, covering mild, moderate and severe sea states. The global system design will be the main focus of this report and will address the severe sea state scenario, where wind, waves, and currents will vary. Furthermore, the description of connection systems between breakwater and solar modules, a critical part of the installation, is detailed in this deliverable.

#### Objectives of the global system design

In order to conduct a comprehensive evaluation of technological risks, barriers, sustainability and environmental challenges, as well as social impact, the defined use-cases will contribute to WP7, specifically focusing on Life Cycle Assessment. The approaches developed within SUREWAVE project facilitates precise estimations within the project, equipping researchers with essential performance indicators such as installation dimensions, power production sizing, and the quantity of components to deploy and maintain.

#### Relationship with other tasks and work packages

The development of the novel FPV concept is done iteratively with the breakwater design and prototyping covered in task 4.4. Previous work completed in deliverable 2.2:Requirements for surface mooring system between FPV array and breakwater, and 2.3 High level understanding of the system general loads is central to the recommendations and propositions made. # Methodology: Design methodology and approach

## 2.1 Floating solar panel (FPV)

Sunlit Sea is developing a technology solution for floating photovoltaic (FPV). The solution, denoted "SLS FPV", consists of a photovoltaic (PV) panel sealed to an aluminium float. This gives a fully built-in solution lying flat on the water surface. To obtain the required energy output several floats are connected together in a large matrix, see in [Figure 1](#_Ref161653574).

![[]{#_Ref161653574 .anchor}Figure 1 : 3D model of SLS's 14x14 105kWp installation in Haugaland](../images/2024-03-19_surewave_d3.4_summary_global_system_design/image3.png){alt="P361#yIS1" width="5.905511811023622in" height="3.087399387576553in"}

This is a product designed for prefabrication and intended for use in all major markets and geographical regions (worldwide). The concept has primarily been validated in landlocked systems or protected areas such as dams and nearshore environments. The SLS FPV solution isn\'t tailored to particular locations or extreme offshore conditions. Instead, it adheres to a set of general design criteria used to evaluate structural integrity. Consequently, specific Metocean criteria for each site must be evaluated and confirmed to align with the designated design criteria.

In the scope of the WP2, D2.1, a set of different offshore locations have been discussed. [Table 1](#_Ref161653717) provides an overview of those locations.

In order to address those criteria, the D2.3 presented a high-level understanding of the general loads on the system and provided preliminary requirements for chosen critical components, and integration requirements between the breakwater and FPV system.

Most importantly, the general following analyses were discussed to estimate the loads:

- Aerodynamic analysis

- Hydrodynamics analysis

- Structural analyses (including connection- and mooring-point strength analysis)

- Connector strength analysis for connectors between individual FPV units.

In parallel to the theoretical explanation of the different loads applied on the FPV system, a set of field tests have been performed (in chronological order):

- Wave tank testing in Stadt towing tank (NO) \[1\]

- Lab tensile test of FPV-FPV connectors (hinges) in SINTEF (NO)

- Shear test of FPV-FPV connectors in SINTEF (NO)

- Basin test of a scaled model in MARIN Wageningen (NL)

Those tests originally planned to answer a set of other tasks within the scope of the SUREWAVE project. However, these tests have also influenced decisions in the design process of critical components, particularly the FPV -FPV connectors.

The wave tank tests in STT are described as a full scale testing of the SLS FPV solution \[1\]. Those tests have allowed us to set a first level of understanding of the behaviour of the FPV matrix under a set of sea states (waves). The different sea states performed during the experiments are listed in Table 2, and Figure *4* presents the sneak peak of the FPV during the tests.

![P393#yIS1](../images/2024-03-19_surewave_d3.4_summary_global_system_design/image8.png){width="5.905511811023622in" height="1.809767060367454in"}

Table 2: description of the different waves used in testing at STT

The movements of sets of panels (1x1, 1x3, 1x6 and 1x9) were monitored and compared to simulations from Orcaflex \[1\]. The findings indicated that the performance of the FPV aligned with the simulation. This suggests that the predicted behaviour of the FPV, as modelled in the simulation, accurately reflects its actual performance in lab-scale environments. These tests not only are useful for validating the accuracy of numerical simulations of FPV assemblies but also serve as a benchmark for refining and improving future simulations in case of discrepancy between numerical models and experiments. By comparing the results of physical tests with those obtained through simulations, we can redefine the models to better predict the behaviour of FPV assemblies in various conditions. Overall, these tests play a crucial role in advancing both the theoretical understanding and practical application of FPV technology.

![P397#yIS1](../images/2024-03-19_surewave_d3.4_summary_global_system_design/image9.png){width="5.61980971128609in" height="4.8060236220472445in"}

One of the most critical components of the fully assembled system was estimated to be the FPV - FPV connector, named "hinge". Indeed, the connections between the floats are of particular interest to this research project. Hinge loads between two or more floats will pose specific challenges that need to be addressed to ensure the stability and longevity of our floating structure. The hinge systems must be able to withstand extreme weather events, strong currents, and variable water depths, and ensure the integrity and stability of the floating structure under different conditions.

It was then obvious that a thorough evaluation of the component on a design level as well as on a material level had to be done. While the design is extensively described in D3.1 "Design description of internal floating PV components", the material used to construct the hinges had yet to be completely described in terms of mechanical characteristics: Stiffness, Young's modulus, Poisson's ratio.\
It was therefore thought that a set of mechanical tests, described in the table 3, at SINTEF would be relevant to pursue in the right direction.

Tests of a 5x3 FPV matrix were performed as showed in Figure 7, with and without shelter of an external floating breakwater (FBW). Results showed that the PV floats move horizontally in the same phase when the wavelength exceeds the length of the FPV system, but shorter waves result in relative motions between floats and hinge buckling in harsh seas. The FBW reduces hinge loads as it attenuates the high-frequency wave energy that largely drives relative motions between PV floats. Details and full explanations are available in \[2\].

The different tests have helped move in the right direction, giving a lot of attention and resources into the design of the FPV-FPV connectors known as the hinges, while trying to keep the production process costs minimal for financial reasons.

## 2.2 Floating Breakwater (FB)

In the D2.2 a summary of the investigations of critical components have been formulated. This resulted in technical requirements that are expressed as basic design criteria regarding required force transmission, moveability, geometric boundary conditions, material properties, dynamic behaviour, and durability requirements.

Based on the same use case mentioned before, the proposed analysis process of PIANC \[3\] and the calculation process of Fousert \[4\], the design development of the FB is considered according to the following diagram, represented in Figure 8:

![P427#yIS1](../images/2024-03-19_surewave_d3.4_summary_global_system_design/image15.png){width="4.643395669291339in" height="5.386104549431321in"}

Figure 8: Methodology employed to design the Floating breakwater

The requirement specifications coming from the analysis of D2.2 concluded that the Floating breakwater had to respect 3 axis of development:

#### Force transmission: 

Globally, loads from wind, waves, and currents act on the assembly. These loads must be safely transferred into the seabed. It is intended that the connection between the anchor or anchors and the assembly will be made directly via FB, as this is also where the largest proportion of the actions occur. Nevertheless, forces still act within the system (FB & FPV), which then must be transferred to the anchoring via the FB.

The connection should not only have an anchoring effect but should also keep the distance between both components (FB and FPV) constant or at a minimum distance to be defined. Thus, there is the possibility of an opposing motion of FB and FPV when acted upon by waves and phase shifting of the incident wave to the transmitted wave occurs.

#### Moveability:

The connection between the Floating Breakwater (FB) and Floating Photovoltaic (FPV) systems should exclusively distribute design loads from the FPV uniformly to the FB and onward to the anchorage, without transmitting any additional moments. A rigid, unhinged connection could directly transfer roll movements, potentially diminishing or eliminating damping effects.

Consequently, further analysis is necessary to determine whether the connection should be configured as a tension, compression bar, or both, considering the independent movements of each component and their interactions. Additionally, damping or elastic properties may need to be incorporated if deemed necessary.

#### Geometric boundaries:

For small-period waves, the transmission must be reduced to a minimum. It must be investigated which wave spectrum is harmful to the FPV or up to which spectrum a wave attenuation must take place. Accordingly, the geometry of the FB can be defined, using the preliminary approach, mentioned earlier, which needs to be confirmed numerically and experimentally.

In addition, the dimensions of the individual wave breaker elements are basically not limited, but with increasing size, the total weight will be the limiting criterion. In this respect, the minimum size must be selected so that the required wave attenuation takes place, but at a maximum so that the total weight remains in a transportable and handleable condition. The catamaran type has the most effective characteristics in terms of wave attenuation. This type consists of two keels or floats that are firmly connected to each other at a defined distance as shown in Figure 9. Either the buoyancy is realized directly by the keels or floats or additionally also by the connections between them.

![P441#yIS1](../images/2024-03-19_surewave_d3.4_summary_global_system_design/image16.png){width="5.905511811023622in" height="2.8643799212598426in"}

Figure 9: Catamaran cross typical section

## 2.3 Anchoring & Mooring 

The floating breakwaters must be securely anchored to the offshore site. Environmental effects from wind, waves and currents acting on the floating breakwaters are transferred to the ground or seabed via the anchorage.

It is therefore necessary to examine how the anchorage must be designed, not only in terms of economic efficiency, but in terms of technical feasibility as well. The Anchoring and mooring being in direct relation of the general layout involving FB, a first task was to come up with a relevant layout of FB.

A methodology based on rating criterions was developed in D3.3. This involved considering a set of different FB disposition and their effect on:

- Distribution of wave energy in terms of transmission and reflection

- Concentration of forces with regards to pontoon connections

- Anchorage

- Design and production (pre-fabrication)

- Transport and installation

- Costs

- Use of space

- Maintenance

The circular layout was identified as the preferred option for the following main reasons:

Advantages

- The wave forces always act on the round shape with a time delay, resulting in less movements and forces.

- Waves with a shorter period are reflected in different directions, creating a diffuse wave pattern. This minimises the interaction between the incoming wave and the reflected wave.

- The concentration of forces is also minimised and reduced to the anchorage points. Due to the small angles between the pontoons, the force distribution in the connections between the pontoons is uniform.

- The anchorage can be scaled and adapted to local conditions.

- Compared to other layouts, the circular arrangement requires the fewest pontoons.

- The external breakwater pontoons are all of the same shape and size, which avoids the increased cost of changing formwork.

- Relative costs are low due to the small number of different pontoons and the small total number of pontoons.

Disadvantages

- The gables of the pontoons are not perpendicular to the sides. This makes the gables more difficult to form. However, as all FB pontoons can be the same, this disadvantage is almost negligible.

- The square PV-panels must be adapted to the round shape. This results in offsets for which the connection between the FB and FPV must be individually adjusted.

- In order to be able to carry out maintenance work and ensure accessibility to the PV-panels, corridors must be set up at regular intervals. Due to the round shape, setting up these corridors is also more complex than with straight layouts.

# Global solution of SUREWAVE FPV

## 3.1 Overall design

The SUREWAVE project aims to develop a comprehensive solution for offshore floating photovoltaic (FPV) systems integrated with breakwater structures. The global solution involved several tasks that are serving the overall design process of the assembly:

**Task 3.1:** Design of internal individual floating PV components, focusing on optimizing operation in offshore conditions. This includes designing flat, aluminum modules, analyzing global FPV farm design, improving corrosion behavior, maximizing cooling, and implementing smart sensing.

**Task 3.2:** Design of external breakwater structures to attenuate waves and withstand dynamic forces. The design considers wave breaking, dynamic behavior, load carrying capacity, and force transmission.

**Task 3.3:** Improvement of mooring line and anchoring systems to securely anchor floating breakwaters to offshore sites. This task considers economic efficiency and technical feasibility, investigating various anchorage methods and their advantages/disadvantages.

**Task 3.4:** Development of connection systems between breakwaters and solar modules, ensuring a constant distance between components and uniform dynamic behaviour.

**Task 3.5:** Design of the coupled breakwater and floating PV system and the global system design. This involves analysing the array\'s position and shape maintenance during varying weather conditions, considering compressive and snapping forces, as well as finalising the overall design including 3D modelling, bill-of-materials, assembly, and installation requirements.

While the methodology to address those tasks (Task 3.1 to Task 3.4) has been summarised in the previous part. Here, as a part of Task 3.5, we will focus on the description of the complete system by integrating all the previous activities performed as part of the Task 3.1 to Task 3.4.

### 3.2.2 Transition elements

In the SUREWAVE project, a connection is planned between the FB and the FPV, which transfers the forces from the FPV via the FB and its anchorage into the seabed.

## 4.3 Floating breakwater

The process for the deployment of the floating breakwaters involves incorporating specific details relevant to SUREWAVE \'S approach, materials, and environmental considerations. Here\'s how the process can be tailored to fit the project:

### 4.3.1 Design and Construction

**Construction:**

SUREWAVE floating breakwaters are constructed off-site, utilizing precast reinforced concrete modules. This approach facilitates transport and assembly at the deployment site. The use of Ultra-High-Performance Fibre-Reinforced Concrete (UHPFRC) and Light Weight Aggregate Concrete (LWAC) is explored for their advantages in durability and environmental impact. For more information on the design of the FB, refer to the D3.2:​Floating breakwater design from WP3.

### 4.3.2 Pre-Deployment Checks

**Quality Assurance:**

Emphasizing the importance of sustainable and durable construction, SUREWAVE \'S floating breakwaters undergo rigorous quality checks, focusing on their ability to withstand marine environments with minimal ecological footprint.

**Permits and Compliance:**

The project seeks all necessary permits, ensuring compliance with environmental regulations and emphasizing the development of environmentally sustainable breakwater solutions.

### 4.3.3 Transportation to Deployment Site

**Method Selection:**

The precast modules of SUREWAVE \'S breakwaters are transported to the deployment site, considering the most efficient and environmentally friendly methods based on their size and the distance

**Preparation for Transport:**

Special attention is given to securing the breakwaters for transport, ensuring they are protected against damage and ready for assembly upon arrival.

**Navigation Planning:**

A comprehensive route plan is carefully designed ted to prioritize environmental preservation and minimize any potential impact on marine life throughout the transportation process. This requires thorough consideration of factors such as avoiding sensitive ecosystems, adhering to designated marine routes, and implementing measures to mitigate noise pollution or disturbances that could affect marine habitats. By carefully planning the transportation route, the project aims to uphold ecological integrity and ensure the wellbeing of marine ecosystems along the journey.

### 4.3.4 Deployment Preparation

**Positioning:**

Utilizing maritime engineering expertise, the breakwaters are accurately placed at the designated location, ensuring they meet the protection requirements of the FPV systems. Through advanced techniques and specialized knowledge, including hydrodynamics and structural analysis, engineers ensure that the FB are well positioned to protect against the extreme loads.

**Anchoring:**

In addition to precise placement, anchoring plays a pivotal role in securing both the breakwaters and FPV systems in their designated locations. The anchoring system design in section 3.1 will be utilized to ensures stability and resilience against varying environmental conditions, including strong currents and turbulent waves. A thorough seabed investigation will be performed to determine a suitable anchoring solution.

**Connection:**

Both electrical and mechanical connections will be designed to ensure the connection system withstand offshore environments. An integration of solar panels, inverters, and electrical distribution systems will be performed. Similarly, a mechanical connection system will ensure both FB and FPV are secured and able to withstand the loads.

### 4.3.5 Post-Deployment Activities

**Inspection and Adjustment:**

Initial inspections confirm the proper function of the breakwaters, with adjustments made as necessary to optimize environmental compatibility and protection efficiency.

**Monitoring:**

Ongoing environmental monitoring assesses the impact on marine life and informs any needed adjustments or maintenance to ensure the breakwaters remain an effective and sustainable solution.

### 4.3.6 Maintenance

Maintenance practices emphasize sustainability, with inspections focused on ensuring the longevity and environmental performance of the breakwater structures.

This adapted procedure highlights the SUREWAVE project\'s focus on sustainability, environmental responsibility, and integration with offshore FPV systems, requiring close coordination among engineers, environmental scientists, and maritime logistics experts.

## 4.4 Safety and compliance requirements

The safety and compliance requirements involve considering the unique aspects of integrating floating photovoltaic (FPV) systems with floating breakwaters. Here\'s how the general safety and compliance requirements can be adapted to the SUREWAVE installation:

### 4.4.1 Design and Construction

**Structural Integrity:**

Both the FPV units and the floating breakwaters must be designed to withstand the offshore environment\'s challenges, including significant wave action, wind, and potential loads from marine growth. The design of mechanical connections between the FPV units and the breakwater requires special attention to ensure load transfer without causing damage.

**Material Safety:**

The use of materials for both the FPV units and the breakwaters should prioritize durability, corrosion resistance, and minimal environmental impact. The Sunlit Sea FPV model 1, for example, uses an aluminium float for its robustness and longevity in marine conditions.

**Buoyancy and Stability:**

The integration of the FPV system with the floating breakwaters must maintain adequate buoyancy and stability, considering the added complexity of connections and the dynamic forces at play.

### 4.4.2 Electrical Safety

Electrical Codes: Compliance with relevant electrical codes (e.g., IEC standards) is crucial, especially given the unique challenges of combining electrical installations with maritime structures.

Waterproofing and Corrosion Protection: Extra care must be taken to waterproof all electrical components and protect against corrosion, a significant concern in marine environments.

Grounding and Lightning Protection: Given the increased exposure in offshore settings, robust grounding and lightning protection systems are even more critical to safeguard the installation and personnel

### 4.4.3 Environmental Compliance

**Environmental Impact Assessment (EIA):**

The EIA for SUREWAVE installations should cover the potential impacts of both the FPV system and the floating breakwaters on the marine environment.

Permitting and Biodiversity Protection:

Obtaining the necessary permits requires detailed plans for minimizing impacts on marine life, including considerations for how the floating structures might affect local ecosystems.

### 4.4.4 Operational Safety

**Access, Signage, and Lighting:**

Safe access for maintenance is crucial, with additional considerations for emergency response given the offshore context. Signage and lighting must be designed for visibility in maritime conditions.

**Monitoring and Maintenance:**

Regular monitoring should include structural integrity, electrical safety, and environmental impact, with maintenance plans addressing the unique challenges of offshore operations.

### 4.4.5 Decommissioning

**End-of-Life Plan:**

Plans for decommissioning must consider the removal of both the FPV system and the floating breakwaters, ensuring materials are disposed of or recycled in an environmentally responsible manner.

Incorporating these tailored safety and compliance requirements into the SUREWAVE project ensures that the integration of FPV systems with floating breakwaters addresses the specific risks and regulatory standards applicable to offshore installations.

# Conclusions

This report summarised the global system that is investigated in the scope of the SUREWAVE project.

It aims to develop an integrated offshore floating photovoltaic (FPV) system with breakwater structures to optimize operation in offshore conditions. The design process encompasses several key tasks: designing internal FPV components to maximize cooling, corrosion resistance and endure nonfiltered waves from the FB. Designing of external breakwater structures to attenuate waves, improved mooring and anchoring systems, and connection systems between breakwaters and solar modules to ensure uniform dynamic behaviour.

The comprehensive design includes analysing the FPV array\'s positioning and shape maintenance during varying weather conditions, with a use case on the harsh sea state weather condition.

The project\'s global design approach reflects a holistic strategy to create a sustainable and resilient offshore FPV installation, adaptable to the challenges of harsh marine environments​​.

Global design in its current state allows for an in depth understanding of what the full installation is intended to be made of. The WP5 & 6 will focus on testing and validating the architecture proposed.\
As we advance on the development of the SUREWAVE project, many components from the global system design will be further tested and confirmed.

Furthermore, some critical components such as the hinges (FPV - FPV), the FPV - TE and FB - TE connectors are expected to be improved.
