**EXECUTIVE SUMMARY**

This deliverable presents the revised Intellectual Property Rights (IPR) Management Plan for the Horizon Europe project **SUREWAVE: Structural Reliable Offshore Floating PV Solution Integrating Circular Concrete Floating Breakwater**. It updates Deliverable D1.4 and describes how intellectual property was identified, attributed, protected, monitored, and managed during project implementation.

The SUREWAVE Consortium Agreement remained the principal framework governing ownership of project Results, access rights to Background and Results, confidentiality, joint ownership, dissemination, and post-project use. Detailed exploitation strategies, business models, and commercialisation pathways are addressed separately under WP8. This document focuses on ownership, access rights, protection of confidential knowledge, and dissemination review.

The IPR framework was adapted to the technical organisation of SUREWAVE. Each partner was primarily responsible for developing Results within its area of expertise, while other partners contributed through requirements, testing, modelling, measurements, validation, and technical feedback. This modular structure allowed most Results to be associated with clearly identifiable generating partners and reduced the risk of unclear ownership.

The principal Results included circular concrete solutions developed by ACCIONA, floating breakwater designs developed by CLEMENT, FPV components and modified hinge designs developed by Sunlit Sea, aerodynamic assessment and mechanical testing performed by SINTEF, hydrodynamic modelling and wave basin testing performed by MARIN, and structural integrity, fatigue, corrosion-monitoring, and structural health management methods developed by CEIT.

The development of the FPV hinge system illustrates how the IPR framework operated in practice. Sunlit Sea refined the hinge design based on mechanical, structural, and hydrodynamic assessments performed by SINTEF, CEIT, and MARIN. The project distinguished between ownership of the component design and ownership of the testing, modelling, and validation methods. Such technical contributions did not automatically create joint ownership unless they formed an inseparable intellectual part of a specific Result.

A structured dissemination review and non-objection procedure was applied throughout the project. Scientific publications, conference contributions, public deliverables, datasets, Zenodo records, and communication materials were circulated among consortium partners before public release. Confidential information, potentially protectable Results, commercially sensitive details, and ownership or attribution concerns were addressed before dissemination.

Approved outputs were published through scientific journals, conferences, the SUREWAVE website, and the SUREWAVE Zenodo Community. Detailed material formulations, proprietary calculations, source code, complete numerical models, training data, and other commercially sensitive information remained under the control of the corresponding owners. Public disclosure did not transfer ownership, and applicable copyright, database rights, licences, confidentiality obligations, and attribution requirements remained in force.

No major ownership or access-right dispute requiring formal escalation was reported to project management. Future protection, licensing, demonstration, or commercial deployment remains the responsibility of the relevant Result owners and may require ownership confirmation, protection assessment, Freedom to Operate analysis, or additional post-project access agreements.

Overall, the IPR framework established in Deliverable D1.4 and the Consortium Agreement effectively supported technical collaboration, clear partner responsibilities, controlled dissemination, Open Science implementation, and protection of commercially valuable project Results.

# INTRODUCTION  {#introduction .SUREWAVE}

This document constitutes the revised Intellectual Property Rights Management Plan for the Horizon Europe project **SUREWAVE: Structural Reliable Offshore Floating PV Solution Integrating Circular Concrete Floating Breakwater**. It builds upon the initial IPR Management Plan established in Deliverable D1.4 and reports how intellectual property was identified, attributed, protected, monitored, and managed during project implementation.

The primary purpose of this deliverable is to assess the practical implementation and effectiveness of the IPR management framework established through the SUREWAVE Consortium Agreement. The document addresses ownership of project Results, access rights to Background and Results, confidentiality, protection of commercially valuable knowledge, management of potentially jointly generated Results, and review of information before public disclosure.

Detailed exploitation strategies, business models, target markets, commercialisation pathways, and future deployment plans are outside the scope of this document. These matters are addressed under WP8, particularly in Deliverables D8.1 and D8.5. The role of this IPR Management Plan is to describe how ownership, protection, access rights, confidentiality, and dissemination review supported the exploitation activities managed under WP8.

The Consortium Agreement remained the principal contractual framework governing:

- ownership of project Results;

- access rights to Background and Results;

- confidentiality and non-disclosure obligations;

- individual and joint ownership;

- review of publications and other dissemination activities;

- protection and use of project-generated intellectual assets; and

- post-project access to Background and Results.

The definitions and procedures concerning Background, Results, Sideground, ownership, joint ownership, access rights, and confidential information were established in Deliverable D1.4 and the Consortium Agreement. These provisions remain applicable and are not repeated in detail in this revision.

The present document therefore covers:

- IPR governance and responsibilities;

- identification and monitoring of project Results;

- ownership and disclosure status of the principal Results;

- adaptation of the IPR framework to the technical organisation of SUREWAVE;

- management of partner-specific and potentially jointly generated Results;

- dissemination review and non-objection procedures;

- the balance between IPR protection and Open Science;

- post-project access considerations; and

- remaining IPR actions.

#  Implementation of the IPR Management Strategy During the Project  {#implementation-of-the-ipr-management-strategy-during-the-project .SUREWAVE}

> The procedures established in Deliverable D1.4 and the SUREWAVE Consortium Agreement were applied throughout the project to identify, document, protect, manage, and disclose project Results in a controlled manner. This chapter describes how the IPR framework was implemented in practice and adapted to the technical organisation and collaborative activities of the project.
>
> Detailed exploitation strategies, business models, target markets, commercialisation pathways, and deployment plans are addressed separately under WP8, particularly in Deliverables D8.1 and D8.5. The present chapter focuses on ownership, partner contributions, Background, access rights, confidentiality, potential joint ownership, protection measures, and public disclosure.
>
> The implementation of the IPR framework followed the principles below:

- Results remained owned by the partner that generated them, subject to the provisions of the Consortium Agreement.

- Background remained the property of the partner introducing it into the project.

- Joint ownership was considered only where two or more partners made inseparable intellectual contributions to the same Result.

- Technical requirements, testing, validation, modelling, measurements, and specialist feedback did not automatically create joint ownership.

- Access provided for project implementation did not automatically include unrestricted rights for post-project commercial use.

- Ownership of a technology was distinguished from ownership of the methods, models, software, or procedures used to test or validate that technology.

- Confidentiality, patentability, commercial relevance, and future use were considered before public disclosure.

- Publications and other public outputs were subject to consortium review and the applicable non-objection procedure.

## 4.1 Identification and Monitoring of Results

> Each partner remained responsible for identifying the Results generated through its project activities and for informing the relevant Work Package leader and project management when a Result had potential scientific, technical, or commercial value.
>
> Results were assessed with regard to:

- the partner or partners responsible for their generation;

- the Background required for their development or use;

- whether individual partner contributions could be separated;

- whether potential joint ownership needed to be considered;

- confidentiality and possible protection requirements;

- suitability for scientific publication or public dissemination;

- dependencies on another partner's Background or Results;

- post-project access requirements; and

- relevance to the Key Exploitable Results monitored under WP8.

> The consortium used technical deliverables, test reports, numerical models, datasets, design revisions, meeting records, publications, and WP8 exploitation reporting to maintain traceability of partner contributions.
>
> The purpose of this monitoring was not to transfer ownership between partners, but to ensure that:

1.  the origin of a Result could be identified;

2.  contributions from other partners could be documented;

3.  confidential information could be protected;

4.  disclosure decisions could be taken before publication; and

5.  any future ownership or access questions could be addressed using project records.

## 4.2 Management of Background and Access Rights

> Background introduced into the project remained the property of the partner that owned or controlled it. The use of Background for carrying out SUREWAVE activities did not convert that Background into project-generated Results.
>
> This principle was particularly relevant to:

- the original floating photovoltaic technology and pre-existing hinge designs introduced by Sunlit Sea;

- modelling tools and computational methods used by MARIN, SINTEF, and CEIT;

- material-development knowledge and processes introduced by ACCIONA; and

- pre-existing engineering and design knowledge applied by CLEMENT.

> New developments generated specifically through the project were treated as Results. Where a new Result incorporated one partner's Background, ownership of the Background remained unchanged. Ownership of the new Result was assessed according to the intellectual contributions made during the project.
>
> Access rights granted for project implementation were distinguished from rights required for post-project commercial use. The fact that one partner received access to another partner's Background, model, data, material properties, or design information for carrying out a project task did not automatically provide unrestricted rights to use that information after the project.
>
> Where future research, demonstration, licensing, or commercial deployment requires access to another partner's Background or Results, the relevant conditions will remain subject to the Consortium Agreement and, where necessary, a separate licence, supply, service, collaboration, or joint-development agreement.

## 4.3 Adaptation to the Technical Structure of SUREWAVE

> SUREWAVE was organised around complementary but largely distinguishable areas of partner expertise. Each partner was primarily responsible for developing a particular technology, material, component, numerical method, testing approach, or software-related output.
>
> Other partners contributed requirements, environmental conditions, material properties, measurements, testing, modelling, or validation. These contributions enabled technical integration without automatically combining independently generated Results into jointly owned foreground.
>
> The practical approach consisted of the following:

1.  The originating partner developed the principal technology, design, material, model, method, or tool.

2.  Contributing partners supplied defined technical inputs, data, assessments, testing, or validation.

3.  Partner contributions were documented through project tasks, deliverables, design revisions, meeting records, test reports, datasets, numerical studies, and publications.

4.  Ownership was assessed separately from the right to access or use another partner's Result.

5.  Potential joint ownership was considered on a Result-by-Result basis.

6.  Public disclosure proceeded only after the dissemination review and non-objection procedure.

> The principal technical domains were allocated as follows:

- Circular concrete materials and associated material know-how were developed principally by ACCIONA.

- Floating breakwater design and engineering integration were developed principally by CLEMENT.

- Floating photovoltaic components and hinge designs were developed principally by Sunlit Sea.

- Aerodynamic assessment methods and mechanical testing activities were performed principally by SINTEF.

- Hydrodynamic modelling and wave basin assessment were performed principally by MARIN.

- Finite element modelling, structural integrity assessment, fatigue evaluation, corrosion monitoring, and structural health management methods were developed principally by CEIT.

> This allocation reduced ownership uncertainty while preserving the technical interaction required to develop and assess the integrated offshore FPV concept.

## 4.4 Management of Partner-Specific Results

### 4.4.1 Circular Concrete Materials and Associated Solutions

> ACCIONA was principally responsible for developing circular concrete materials for the floating breakwater. This work included concrete formulations, mix designs, structural shell materials, lightweight core materials, and related manufacturing and durability considerations.
>
> The detailed formulations, compositions, and material-development know-how constituted Results generated principally by ACCIONA and remained under the control of the generating partner, subject to the Consortium Agreement.
>
> Other partners supported characterisation and validation:

- SINTEF contributed testing and assessment of mechanical and structural properties.

- CEIT contributed sensor and monitoring expertise, including corrosion-monitoring methods.

- Test measurements and technical reports were used to evaluate whether the materials met the structural and durability requirements of the project.

> The project distinguished between ownership of a material formulation and ownership of the methods used to test, model, or monitor it. Testing, sensor measurements, numerical analysis, and technical feedback did not transfer ownership of the underlying concrete formulation.
>
> Where a partner generated an independent test method, sensor configuration, analytical process, numerical model, or software-related Result, that Result remained attributable to the generating partner.

### 4.4.2 Floating Breakwater Design

> CLEMENT was principally responsible for the engineering development of the floating breakwater concept. The design process used technical inputs generated elsewhere in the project, including:

- metocean conditions and environmental design cases;

- FPV system requirements supplied by Sunlit Sea;

- sustainable concrete properties developed by ACCIONA;

- structural testing results generated by SINTEF;

- hydrodynamic assessment and basin-test results generated by MARIN; and

- finite element assessments performed by CEIT.

> The use of these inputs did not automatically convert the breakwater design into a jointly owned Result. Each partner retained ownership of its independently generated material technology, numerical methodology, test method, model, dataset, software, or technical know-how.
>
> The breakwater geometry, engineering configuration, and design integration generated by CLEMENT remained attributable to CLEMENT, subject to any documented inseparable intellectual contributions and the ownership provisions of the Consortium Agreement.

### 4.4.3 Aerodynamic and Hydrodynamic Assessment

> The aerodynamic and hydrodynamic activities were technically related but distinguishable from an IPR perspective.
>
> SINTEF conducted aerodynamic assessment of the FPV and breakwater systems. The corresponding numerical workflows, loading assessments, methods, and technical know-how remained attributable to SINTEF.
>
> MARIN performed hydrodynamic modelling and wave basin testing of the FPV system, floating breakwater, and combined configurations. Partner-specific hydrodynamic models, experimental methodologies, validation procedures, and associated know-how remained attributable to MARIN, subject to applicable Background and ownership provisions.
>
> The resulting data and findings were shared within the consortium to support design and validation. Such sharing provided the access required to perform project tasks but did not transfer ownership of the underlying models, methods, tools, software, or know-how.
>
> Selected basin-test data, public reports, and scientific publications were released following consortium review. Public disclosure of selected Results did not imply disclosure of complete numerical models, source files, model configurations, or proprietary analytical workflows.

### 4.4.4 Finite Element Modelling and Structural Integrity Assessment

> CEIT performed finite element and structural integrity assessments of reinforced concrete elements, FPV components, and critical structural connections. These activities supported evaluation of load transfer, stress and strain behaviour, fatigue, reliability, and degradation mechanisms.
>
> Partner-specific finite element models, numerical workflows, structural integrity methodologies, monitoring approaches, and software-related outputs remained attributable to CEIT.
>
> Where the analyses used:

- material properties supplied by ACCIONA;

- geometry supplied by CLEMENT; or

- loading conditions supplied by SINTEF or MARIN;

> those inputs remained attributable to their respective owners.
>
> The use of another partner's material properties, geometry, or loading conditions in a numerical assessment did not transfer ownership of the underlying material, design, or loading methodology. Similarly, assessing another partner's component did not transfer ownership of that component to the modelling partner.
>
> The assessed technology and the assessment methodology were therefore treated as distinct Results.

## 4.5 Management of the FPV Hinge Development

> The hinge system connecting the FPV modules provides an important example of how the IPR framework operated in practice.
>
> The original Sunlit Sea hinge configuration was identified as requiring further development for the demanding offshore conditions considered within SUREWAVE. Mechanical testing and assessment by SINTEF identified limitations in the structural performance of the original configuration.
>
> Hydrodynamic investigations by MARIN indicated that wave-induced compression and extension could generate snapping loads in the hinge connections. These effects were assessed using numerical hydrodynamic models and wave basin experiments covering original and modified configurations.
>
> The iterative development process included:

- modified hinge concepts developed by Sunlit Sea;

- mechanical testing, material characterisation, and numerical assessment by SINTEF;

- finite element, fatigue, and structural assessment by CEIT; and

- hydrodynamic modelling and wave basin assessment by MARIN.

> The combined findings were used by Sunlit Sea to refine the hinge and connection designs.
>
> For IPR purposes, the project distinguished among:

- the pre-existing FPV and hinge technology introduced by Sunlit Sea;

- new hinge configurations developed during SUREWAVE;

- mechanical testing methods and results generated by SINTEF;

- numerical and structural assessment methods generated by CEIT; and

- hydrodynamic modelling and basin-testing methods generated by MARIN.

> Testing, modelling, validation, measurements, or technical feedback did not in themselves create joint ownership of the modified hinge designs. Joint ownership would arise only if two or more partners made inseparable intellectual contributions to a specific design or Result.
>
> The development history was documented through test reports, deliverables, numerical studies, design revisions, meeting records, datasets, and publications. Before any patent, registered-design, licensing, or commercial deployment decision is taken, the relevant partners should confirm:

- the chronology of the design changes;

- the inventor or designer contributions;

- the distinction between pre-existing Background and new Results;

- the contribution of each testing and modelling activity; and

- whether any inseparable contribution requires a joint-ownership arrangement.

## 4.6 Dissemination Review and Non-Objection Procedure

> A formal review procedure was applied before public disclosure of project information. The procedure covered:

- journal manuscripts;

- conference papers and abstracts;

- posters and presentations;

- public deliverables;

- public datasets;

- Zenodo records;

- technical brochures;

- project website material; and

- stakeholder outputs containing project-specific technical information.

> Before publication, the proposed material was circulated among consortium partners. Partners could assess whether the planned disclosure:

- contained confidential information;

- included another partner's Background or Results without permission;

- disclosed potentially protectable Results prematurely;

- contained commercially sensitive design details;

- could adversely affect future use or exploitation; or

- incorrectly described authorship, ownership, affiliation, or technical responsibility.

> Where comments were received, the originating authors revised the material, clarified ownership or attribution, removed sensitive information, or postponed submission where necessary.
>
> Where no justified objection was raised within the review period established by the Consortium Agreement, the dissemination activity proceeded.
>
> Evidence of the review process was retained through email correspondence, SharePoint records, internal review forms, partner responses, meeting documentation, revised drafts, and approved final versions.
>
> The project publication inventory provides additional evidence of how the procedure operated. Consortium partners reviewed the publication list, checked the accuracy and completeness of their outputs, and identified missing contributions for inclusion in the final inventory.

## 4.7 Protection of Results and Open Science

> The project followed a differentiated approach to public and restricted information.
>
> **Public outputs**
>
> Publicly disclosed outputs included:

- approved public deliverables;

- peer-reviewed publications;

- conference contributions;

- selected research datasets;

- approved technical reports;

- aggregated sustainability assessment results; and

- reviewed Zenodo deposits.

> **Restricted outputs**
>
> Information retained under partner or consortium control included:

- detailed material formulations;

- proprietary engineering calculations;

- commercially sensitive design details;

- source code and internal software implementations;

- complete numerical models and configuration files;

- protected or licensed training data;

- detailed structural models and analytical workflows; and

- information whose disclosure could prejudice patentability, registered-design protection, confidentiality, licensing opportunities, or future commercial use.

> Approved reports and datasets were deposited in the SUREWAVE Zenodo Community. Public release constituted a dissemination decision and did not transfer ownership of the disclosed information.
>
> Applicable copyright, database rights, attribution requirements, repository licences, confidentiality obligations, and partner-specific ownership continued to apply after public release.
>
> This approach enabled SUREWAVE to meet Horizon Europe Open Science requirements while protecting project Results with commercial, technical, or strategic value.

# Conclusions {#conclusions .SUREWAVE}

> The IPR management framework established in Deliverable D1.4 and the SUREWAVE Consortium Agreement remained effective throughout the project. It provided the contractual basis for managing ownership of Results, access rights to Background and Results, confidentiality, joint ownership, dissemination review, and protection of project-generated knowledge.
>
> The framework was successfully applied to the modular technical structure of SUREWAVE. Each partner was primarily responsible for Results generated within its area of expertise, while contributions from other partners generally consisted of requirements, testing, modelling, measurements, validation, or technical feedback. These contributions did not automatically create joint ownership unless they formed an inseparable intellectual part of a specific Result.
>
> A structured dissemination review and non-objection procedure was applied to scientific publications, conference contributions, public deliverables, datasets, Zenodo records, and relevant communication materials. This process allowed the consortium to identify and protect confidential information, potentially protectable Results, and commercially sensitive knowledge before public disclosure.
>
> The consortium maintained an appropriate balance between IPR protection and Horizon Europe Open Science requirements. Approved reports, publications, and selected datasets were made publicly available, while detailed material formulations, engineering designs, numerical models, source code, software implementations, and other commercially sensitive information remained under the control of the corresponding owners. Public disclosure did not constitute a transfer of ownership, and applicable copyright, database rights, licences, confidentiality obligations, and attribution requirements remained in force.
>
> No major ownership or access-right dispute requiring formal escalation was reported to project management. The Consortium Agreement, technical documentation, contribution records, and internal review procedures were sufficient to manage the principal interfaces between partner-specific Results.
>
> Detailed business models, market strategies, commercialisation pathways, and deployment plans are addressed separately under WP8. After project completion, each Result owner remains responsible for determining the appropriate protection and use of its intellectual assets. Where future demonstration or commercial deployment requires another partner's Background or Results, additional licensing, supply, service, collaboration, or joint-development agreements may be required. Patentability, registered-design protection, and Freedom to Operate should also be assessed by the relevant owners before commercial use, where appropriate.
>
> Overall, the SUREWAVE IPR framework provided an effective basis for protecting project Results, supporting technical collaboration and controlled dissemination, and preparing the project outcomes for future research, demonstration, licensing, and commercial application.
