EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

**D1.7, Updated Data Management Plan**, provides an overview of the data-management activities implemented throughout the SUREWAVE project and documents the data and other research outputs generated, collected, processed, shared, secured, and preserved up to the final stage of project implementation. It builds upon the initial Data Management Plan and reflects the datasets, software tools, numerical models, publications, deliverables, and technical documentation produced during the project.

SUREWAVE generated a broad portfolio of scientific and technical outputs associated with offshore floating photovoltaic systems, floating breakwaters, circular concrete materials, mooring and anchoring systems, hydrodynamic and aerodynamic modelling, structural integrity assessment, monitoring technologies, experimental validation, sustainability assessment, and stakeholder engagement. Publicly shareable outputs have been disseminated through trusted repositories and scientific publication channels, while confidential, proprietary, and commercially sensitive information has remained under controlled partner access in accordance with the Consortium Agreement and the IPR Management Plan.

In accordance with the Grant Agreement and Horizon Europe Open Science requirements, SUREWAVE implemented the FAIR principles of making research outputs Findable, Accessible, Interoperable, and Reusable. Approved public deliverables, scientific publications, and selected datasets have been deposited in the SUREWAVE Zenodo Community and assigned persistent identifiers, including Digital Object Identifiers, where applicable. Metadata, documentation, licences, and links between related datasets, deliverables, and publications have been provided to improve discoverability, interpretation, citation, and reuse.

This updated DMP describes the research data and other outputs generated during the project, their origin, purpose, formats, and accessibility conditions, together with the procedures applied for data collection, processing, quality assurance, documentation, storage, security, sharing, and preservation. It also explains the implementation of metadata and documentation practices, repository and publication arrangements, access rights, intellectual-property considerations, and the distinction between public and restricted outputs.

Restricted information includes proprietary engineering designs, detailed simulation models, software source code, confidential material formulations, licensed third-party information, and other commercially sensitive knowledge. Such outputs remain under the control of the responsible partners and are preserved according to their institutional policies, contractual obligations, and continuing scientific or commercial value.

Overall, the updated DMP demonstrates how SUREWAVE has implemented effective data governance, FAIR data management, Open Science practices, and long-term preservation measures. These arrangements maximise the scientific, technical, environmental, economic, and societal value of the project's results while ensuring appropriate protection of confidential information, intellectual property, and exploitable project knowledge.

# Introduction  {#introduction .SUREWAVE}

This document constitutes the updated Data Management Plan for the SUREWAVE project and reflects the data-management activities and research outputs generated up to Month 48. It builds upon the initial DMP and describes how project data and other research outputs have been collected, documented, stored, shared, protected, preserved, and prepared for reuse in accordance with the FAIR principles and Horizon Europe Open Science requirements.

SUREWAVE has generated a broad range of outputs relating to offshore floating photovoltaic systems, floating breakwaters, circular concrete materials, mooring and anchoring systems, structural integrity, monitoring, numerical modelling, experimental validation, and sustainability assessment. These outputs include experimental and monitoring data, engineering models, software tools, public deliverables, scientific publications, technical documentation, and selected open datasets.

This updated DMP documents the data types and formats generated or reused by the project, their origin and purpose, the procedures applied for quality assurance and metadata creation, the conditions governing access and reuse, and the arrangements for long-term preservation. It also distinguishes between public outputs suitable for open dissemination and restricted information requiring protection because of intellectual property, confidentiality, third-party licensing, personal-data, security, or commercial considerations.

Responsibility for data management has been shared across the consortium. WP leaders and data-generating partners have been responsible for identifying, documenting, classifying, securing, and preserving the outputs generated through their activities. SINTEF has coordinated project-level DMP implementation, the data inventory, FAIR compliance monitoring, repository publication, and reporting.

Before public release, deliverables, publications, datasets, and other dissemination materials underwent the internal review procedures defined in the Consortium Agreement and the IPR Management Plan. Approved public outputs have been disseminated through Zenodo, scientific publishers, institutional repositories, the project website, conferences, and other project communication channels. Restricted engineering files, detailed models, software components, material formulations, and commercially sensitive information remain under the control of the responsible partners.

Public outputs are accompanied, where applicable, by metadata, documentation, licences, persistent identifiers, and links to related datasets, deliverables, or publications. These measures support their continued discovery, accessibility, interpretation, and reuse beyond the project lifetime.

The DMP has been maintained as a living document and updated as new datasets, publications, software tools, repository records, and dissemination decisions emerged. This version presents the final major update and complements the project's IPR, dissemination, communication, and exploitation activities without duplicating the detailed procedures addressed in the corresponding project deliverables.

# Data Summary  {#data-summary .SUREWAVE}

## 2.1 Overview and Relationship to Project Objectives {#overview-and-relationship-to-project-objectives .surewave_sub}

The project addressed technical, environmental, economic, and social challenges associated with deploying floating photovoltaic systems in representative European offshore conditions.

The data generated and reused within SUREWAVE supported the following project activities:

- development and validation of floating breakwater concepts for reducing environmental loads acting on offshore FPV systems;

- development and characterisation of circular concrete materials for marine applications;

- aerodynamic, hydrodynamic, and structural modelling of the integrated system;

- design and optimisation of mooring, anchoring, and connection systems;

- laboratory testing and wave basin validation;

- long-term marine exposure, corrosion, chloride ingress, and biofouling monitoring;

- development of structural health monitoring and predictive-maintenance tools;

- development of machine-learning-supported material assessment methods;

- technological, environmental, economic, and social sustainability assessments; and

- validation and demonstration of components and system concepts under representative offshore conditions.

The data were generated to support component design, model development, validation, risk reduction, system integration, sustainability assessment, scientific dissemination, and future technical exploitation. Each dataset was associated with the originating WP, relevant task, responsible partner, deliverable, and access classification.

## 2.2 Reuse of Existing Data {#reuse-of-existing-data .surewave_sub}

SUREWAVE reused existing data where these data were suitable, sufficiently documented, and legally available for project purposes. Reused information included:

- **ERA5 hindcast data** and processed metocean information used to establish wind, wave, and environmental design conditions for representative locations in the Baltic Sea, Mediterranean, and North Sea;

- scientific literature and published engineering data used to establish material-property ranges, design assumptions, environmental conditions, and state-of-the-art benchmarks;

- partner-owned Background, including pre-existing FPV technology, component designs, modelling tools, numerical methods, material-development knowledge, and testing procedures;

- publicly available technical standards, design guidance, material standards, and marine engineering methods;

- licensed or publicly accessible environmental, economic, and social databases used in sustainability assessments; and

- experimental and technical data generated in earlier partner activities where the corresponding access rights permitted use within SUREWAVE.

Existing data were reused to avoid unnecessary duplication, establish representative environmental and design conditions, benchmark the SUREWAVE technologies, support model development, and complement project-generated experimental information.

Reuse was limited where data lacked sufficient metadata, were not representative of the required offshore conditions, were subject to incompatible licences, contained confidential third-party information, or could not support the required level of validation. In such cases, SUREWAVE generated new laboratory, numerical, basin-test, monitoring, or assessment data.

Where commercial or licensed databases were used, public dissemination was limited to aggregated results or indicators unless the underlying licence permitted redistribution.

## 2.3 Data Types and Formats {#data-types-and-formats .surewave_sub}

SUREWAVE generated, processed, or reused the following principal categories of data.

**Environmental and metocean data**

- wind and wave statistics;

- current conditions;

- marine-growth assumptions;

- environmental design parameters;

- directional and return-period statistics; and

- site-specific conditions for representative European offshore locations.

**Engineering and design data**

- FPV component specifications;

- floating breakwater designs;

- mooring, anchoring, and connection data;

- structural design parameters;

- material specifications;

- bills of materials;

- CAD information;

- finite element models; and

- aerodynamic and hydrodynamic simulation outputs.

**Experimental and monitoring data**

- material-characterisation results;

- ultimate-strength and connection testing;

- fatigue measurements;

- load-displacement data;

- wave basin time series;

- motions, forces, RAOs, and wave-attenuation data;

- sensor calibration results;

- corrosion-monitoring data;

- chloride-ingress profiles;

- biofouling observations; and

- long-term marine-degradation measurements.

**Sustainability and assessment data**

- technological risk matrices;

- environmental inventories and impact indicators;

- economic models, including CAPEX, OPEX, LCOE, NPV, and IRR calculations;

- social and supply-chain indicators; and

- integrated sustainability indicators and scenario comparisons.

**Software and digital assets**

- Surewave-ANN;

- Structural Health Management System tools;

- fatigue crack-propagation models;

- structural integrity models;

- aerodynamic and hydrodynamic modelling workflows; and

- scripts, parameter files, and supporting model configurations.

The main data formats included:

- **CSV and XLSX** for tabular data and processed engineering results;

- **HDF5** for large experimental and wave basin datasets;

- **JSON** for structured monitoring and metadata-related information;

- **PDF and DOCX** for reports, documentation, methodologies, and public deliverables;

- **PPTX** for internal technical and management reporting;

- **CAD and model-dependent formats** for component geometry and design development;

- **images and video formats** for test documentation, prototype activities, and observations;

- **Python and MATLAB-related files** for numerical processing, machine learning, and software tools; and

- **solver-specific formats** for CFD, finite element, mooring, and hydrodynamic simulations.

Open and widely used formats were selected where practicable. Proprietary or model-dependent formats were retained where required by specialised engineering software. Where feasible, processed results were also exported to CSV, XLSX, HDF5, PDF, or another broadly accessible format.

## 2.4 Data Volume {#data-volume .surewave_sub}

The largest data volumes were generated by CFD simulations, finite element simulations, wave basin experiments, monitoring campaigns, videos, and software-model archives. Smaller volumes were generated by reports, spreadsheets, processed indicators, and publication-supporting data.

At M48, the responsible partners retain detailed information on the size of their raw and processed datasets. The total project data volume has not been centrally aggregated because large raw simulation and experimental files are distributed across partner infrastructures. Public Zenodo records account for only the curated and approved portion of the project outputs. The principal volume drivers are CFD and finite element models, wave basin time series, monitoring data, images, and video recordings. Each generating partner remains responsible for maintaining the detailed storage inventory of its raw and processed data.

## 2.5 Data Origin and Provenance {#data-origin-and-provenance .surewave_sub}

The provenance of SUREWAVE data was documented through the originating WP, task, partner, deliverable, test campaign, modelling activity, or assessment workflow.

Project data originated from:

- numerical simulations performed by project partners;

- laboratory testing of materials, connections, structural components, and sensors;

- wave basin experiments;

- prototype manufacturing and deployment activities;

- long-term environmental and structural monitoring;

- existing metocean and environmental information;

- literature and standards;

- licensed or public sustainability databases;

- partner-owned Background; and

- stakeholder engagement and assessment activities.

Source information was maintained, where applicable, through:

- file names and version histories;

- responsible partner and author information;

- dates of generation and modification;

- test or model identifiers;

- model versions and parameter files;

- units, coordinate systems, and variable descriptions;

- processing and quality-control steps;

- associated deliverables and publications;

- access and dissemination classifications; and

- repository identifiers and DOIs for public records.

Raw data, processed data, and published outputs were distinguished where appropriate. Public repository records contain metadata describing the title, creators, project funding, version, publication date, resource type, keywords, licence, related deliverables, and associated publications.

## 2.6 Data Accessibility and Preservation {#data-accessibility-and-preservation .surewave_sub}

SUREWAVE data were classified as public or restricted according to the Grant Agreement, Consortium Agreement, intellectual property considerations, confidentiality obligations, third-party licences, and the approved dissemination status.

Public outputs include approved deliverables, publications, selected datasets, and dissemination materials. These outputs are being preserved through:

- the SUREWAVE Zenodo Community;

- publisher and institutional repositories;

- the project website; and

- partner archives.

Restricted outputs include proprietary engineering designs, detailed numerical models, confidential material formulations, source code, internal software implementations, licensed input data, and commercially sensitive know-how. These outputs remain in the controlled repositories of the responsible partners and, where appropriate, the project SharePoint environment.

A public deliverable describing a dataset does not necessarily mean that the underlying source data are publicly available. Data are described as an **open dataset** only where the corresponding files have been approved for public release and deposited in Zenodo or another trusted repository.

SUREWAVE data and outputs may be useful to:

- floating photovoltaic developers and component manufacturers;

- floating breakwater designers;

- offshore wind, wave-energy, and other marine renewable-energy developers;

- mooring, anchoring, and marine-structure engineers;

- concrete manufacturers and marine-material researchers;

- structural health monitoring and sensor developers;

- operation and maintenance providers;

- life-cycle assessment practitioners;

- economic and social sustainability researchers;

- public authorities and marine spatial planners;

- standardisation organisations;

- insurers, investors, and project developers;

- universities and research institutes; and

- European and national research projects working on offshore renewables.

The metocean dataset may support early-stage offshore design studies. The anchoring and connection dataset may support mooring-system comparisons and engineering education. Wave basin data may be reused for validating hydrodynamic models. Economic-model data may support cost and bankability studies. Public reports and assessment results may inform policy, sustainability evaluation, technology development, and the design of future offshore FPV demonstration projects.

The generated and reused data support the technical, scientific, environmental, economic, and social objectives of SUREWAVE. Publicly approved datasets and reports are preserved in trusted repositories and made findable through metadata and persistent identifiers. Restricted data continue to be controlled by the responsible partners in accordance with intellectual property, confidentiality, security, and third-party licensing requirements. # **FAIR Data in SUREWAVE**  {#fair-data-in-surewave .SUREWAVE}

SUREWAVE implemented the FAIR principles of making research outputs **Findable, Accessible, Interoperable, and Reusable** throughout the project. FAIR implementation applied to datasets, public deliverables, scientific publications, software-related outputs, modelling results, and supporting documentation.

The level of openness depended on the nature of each output. Publicly shareable Results were deposited in trusted repositories, while detailed engineering models, confidential material formulations, software source code, commercially sensitive designs, and third-party licensed information remained under controlled access. A public report describing a dataset was not automatically treated as evidence that the underlying source data were openly available.

## 3.1 Making Data Findable, Including Provisions for Metadata {#making-data-findable-including-provisions-for-metadata .surewave_sub}

### Persistent identifiers

Public datasets, reports, and other research outputs deposited in the SUREWAVE Zenodo Community were assigned persistent Digital Object Identifiers. These DOIs provide stable, resolvable references that support citation, discovery, and long-term identification.

Scientific publications were identified through publisher-assigned DOIs where available. Internal and restricted data that did not receive a public DOI were identified through structured file names, version numbers, WP and task references, responsible partner information, and storage-location records.

Where several versions of a public output existed, Zenodo versioning was used to distinguish individual versions while maintaining the relationship between them.

### Metadata

Public records deposited in Zenodo were accompanied by appropriate metadata to support discovery, citation, interpretation, and reuse. Depending on the type of output, the metadata included the title, creators and contributors, organisational affiliations, description or abstract, resource type, publication date, version, language, keywords, access status, licence, funding programme, project acronym, Grant Agreement number, and links to associated deliverables, publications, or datasets.

Where relevant, dataset-level documentation also described the origin and provenance of the data, the generating WP, task and partner, the data collection or simulation methodology, processing and quality-control procedures, variable definitions, units of measurement, coordinate systems, model or test identifiers, software dependencies, known limitations, conditions of use, and contact information for the responsible partner. This information was provided through repository metadata, README files, related deliverables, technical documentation, or associated scientific publications, depending on the nature and complexity of the dataset.

For internal data stored in the SUREWAVE SharePoint environment or partner-managed repositories, the minimum descriptive information included the file name, creation or modification date, version, file format, description, relevant WP, responsible partner, lead author or owner, and dissemination classification. Additional technical metadata were maintained by the responsible partner where required to support traceability, quality assurance, interpretation, and future reuse.

### Metadata standards

SUREWAVE used Zenodo's general metadata structure for public repository records, while publication metadata followed publisher and repository conventions, including DOI-compatible bibliographic information. As no single discipline-specific standard covered the project's diverse outputs, metadata were supplemented with README documentation, model- and test-specific parameters, standard engineering terminology, SI units, structured variable definitions, and partner quality and traceability records. Where a disciplinary standard was not practical, sufficient descriptive and technical information was provided to explain the data's origin, content, methodology, units, and limitations.

### Search keywords

Public records included keywords describing their specific technical content, such as SUREWAVE, floating photovoltaics, offshore solar energy, floating breakwaters, metocean data, wave basin testing, hydrodynamic modelling, mooring and anchoring, circular concrete, structural integrity, corrosion monitoring, fatigue testing, Life Cycle Assessment, and sustainability assessment. Records did not rely solely on the project acronym for discovery.

### Harvesting and indexing

Zenodo metadata can be indexed by search engines, OpenAIRE, scholarly discovery services, and metadata-harvesting systems. The inclusion of funding information, the Grant Agreement number, creators, keywords, resource type, and related identifiers support both project-level and subject-level discovery. Public outputs were also linked from the project website, publisher platforms, and institutional repositories where applicable.

### Qualified references

Metadata records included meaningful links between related outputs wherever possible. Datasets were linked to the deliverables they supported, publications were linked to relevant experimental data, and updated records were connected to earlier versions. For example, the metocean dataset was linked to D2.1, the anchoring and connection dataset to D3.3, the wave basin dataset to D6.2, and the economic model to D7.4.

### Open and Restricted Data

Not all SUREWAVE data were made openly available. Access decisions considered the agreed dissemination level, intellectual property rights, confidentiality, third-party licences, personal-data protection, security, and the legitimate commercial interests of the beneficiaries. Approved public outputs included public deliverables, peer-reviewed publications, selected metocean, anchoring, connection, wave basin, and economic-assessment data, aggregated sustainability results, and supporting documentation. Restricted outputs included detailed concrete formulations, proprietary breakwater geometry and optimisation results, source code, internal software implementations, complete numerical models, configuration files, protected training data, confidential business information, personal stakeholder data, and third-party data that could not legally be redistributed.

Legal and contractual restrictions arose from database licences, publishing agreements, confidentiality obligations, data-protection requirements, and the Consortium Agreement. Additional restrictions were applied where disclosure could prejudice patentability, licensing opportunities, proprietary technology, commercial use, or another beneficiary's legitimate interests.

### Access Protocols and Decisions

Open Zenodo records are available without payment through standard web protocols, and their DOIs resolve to public landing pages containing metadata and files. Restricted internal data are accessible only to authorised users through institutional authentication and role-based permissions. Requests for post-project access will be assessed by the responsible data owner and may require identity and affiliation verification, a confidentiality or data-use agreement, citation conditions, redistribution limits, a licence, or payment for specialist services. Access may be refused where disclosure would conflict with legal, contractual, security, intellectual-property, or commercial obligations.

SUREWAVE did not establish a project-wide data access committee because the restricted outputs are predominantly technical or commercially sensitive rather than clinical or highly sensitive personal data. Decisions remain with the corresponding owner, supported where necessary by project management, legal advisers, data-protection personnel, or the relevant consortium body.

### Open Metadata and Availability

Metadata for public Zenodo records are openly accessible and made available under the repository's applicable terms, including CC0 where required and supported. The metadata identify the output, access conditions, responsible creators, deposited files, and relationships to relevant deliverables, publications, or datasets. Where a dataset remains restricted, public metadata may still describe its existence, subject, ownership, access conditions, and contact route, provided that this does not reveal confidential information.

Public Zenodo outputs are intended to remain accessible for the lifetime of the repository. Restricted data are preserved according to the institutional retention policies of the corresponding owners and applicable legal, contractual, scientific, and commercial requirements. Where files cannot be retained indefinitely, descriptive metadata and project records are preserved as far as legally and practically possible.

Public datasets were accompanied, where relevant, by README files, data dictionaries, methodology descriptions, deliverables, or scientific publications. This documentation described the file structure, variables, units, test or model configuration, assumptions, processing and quality-control procedures, software requirements, dependencies, limitations, and recommended citation. Software may be archived in Zenodo or linked to a version-control platform where release is compatible with intellectual-property, confidentiality, cybersecurity, commercial, and third-party licensing requirements. Where software cannot be released, the required software and version are documented without disclosing restricted implementation details.

Where an embargo was necessary, it was limited to the period required for quality control, scientific publication, consortium review, intellectual-property assessment, or resolution of confidentiality and third-party licensing issues. Approved outputs were released as soon as practicable after completion of the relevant review or protection decision. No indefinite embargo was applied to outputs approved for public release.

## 3.3 Making Data Interoperable {#making-data-interoperable .surewave_sub}

SUREWAVE used widely accepted formats wherever practical, including CSV and XLSX for tabular data, HDF5 for large experimental and time-series data, JSON for structured information, PDF and DOCX for documentation, standard image and video formats for test records, and Python- or MATLAB-compatible files for processing and software tools. Specialised CAD, CFD, finite element, mooring, and hydrodynamic formats were retained where required by engineering software, with representative or processed outputs exported to more accessible formats whenever feasible.

Interoperability was supported through SI units, documented coordinate systems and reference directions, explicit variable descriptions, meaningful file and column names, controlled naming and versioning, model and test identifiers, software and solver versions, standard engineering terminology, and links to associated reports and publications. Raw and processed files were distinguished where appropriate. No project-specific ontology was required; specialised abbreviations, classifications, and variables were explained in the corresponding README file, data dictionary, report, or metadata record. Qualified references connected datasets to relevant deliverables, publications, software, later versions, and complementary project outputs.

## 3.4 Increasing Data Reuse {#increasing-data-reuse .surewave_sub}

Public datasets were documented sufficiently to support interpretation, validation, and reuse. Depending on the output, documentation covered methodology, test configuration, variable definitions, units, coordinate systems, model assumptions, processing, calibration, quality control, missing data, software requirements, limitations, responsible partners, related outputs, and citation instructions. For specialised datasets, the associated technical deliverable or publication formed part of the reuse documentation.

Restricted outputs may be reused subject to owner approval and applicable contractual, legal, technical, and commercial conditions.

Relevant records included the creator, organisation, date, version, source data, model or test identifier, equipment and calibration information, software version, inputs, processing steps, quality checks, access classification, related outputs, and repository DOI.

Quality assurance was performed by the responsible partner and adapted to the type of output. Measures included equipment calibration, documented test procedures, verification of data completeness and consistency, checks of units and file structures, repeat testing where necessary, validation of numerical models against experiments or benchmarks, comparison of simulated and basin-test results, internal deliverable review, scientific peer review, metadata verification, version control, and preservation of raw data separately from processed results where appropriate. ## 4. Other Research Outputs

In addition to research data, SUREWAVE generated digital and physical research outputs, including software tools, numerical models, engineering designs, testing and monitoring methodologies, material solutions, scientific publications, technical reports, and the floating breakwater prototype.

Digital outputs include the Surewave-ANN concrete-modelling platform, structural integrity and fatigue-assessment tools, the Structural Health Management System, aerodynamic and hydrodynamic modelling workflows, and supporting scripts and model configurations. These outputs are maintained by the partners that generated them, together with available documentation on versions, dependencies, input and output formats, assumptions, validation, and limitations. Public descriptions are provided through deliverables and publications, while source code, complete models, training data, and proprietary workflows remain restricted where release could affect intellectual property, third-party licences, cybersecurity, or future exploitation.

Engineering outputs include FPV components, modified hinge designs, the floating breakwater concept, mooring and anchoring configurations, transition elements, bills of materials, CAD information, and manufacturing or installation documentation. Public deliverables describe the approved system-level information, while detailed drawings, calculations, optimisation files, and commercially sensitive design information remain under the control of the corresponding owners.

Physical outputs include circular concrete formulations, laboratory specimens, sensor-equipped components, tested FPV connections, and the floating breakwater prototype. The responsible partners manage these outputs according to institutional procedures, storage feasibility, safety requirements, intellectual property considerations, and their potential value for future research or demonstration. Where physical samples cannot be retained, photographs, preparation records, identifiers, measurements, and test results are preserved to maintain traceability.

Other outputs, including experimental protocols, scientific publications, public deliverables, conference contributions, and technical documentation, were reviewed before disclosure in accordance with the Consortium Agreement. Approved outputs are made available through scientific publishers, institutional repositories, the project website, and the SUREWAVE Zenodo Community. Persistent identifiers and standard licences are applied where appropriate and where the consortium holds the necessary rights.

The partner generating an output remains responsible for its documentation, version management, access classification, preservation, and any decision concerning public release. Restricted software, models, detailed designs, material formulations, and technical know-how remain in controlled partner repositories. Public outputs are preserved through trusted repositories and linked, where relevant, to associated datasets, deliverables, and publications.

This approach extends FAIR-oriented management to non-data research outputs while recognising that some software, physical materials, engineering designs, and proprietary methods cannot be openly shared because of intellectual property, confidentiality, security, commercial interests, or third-party licensing restrictions.

## 5. Allocation of Resources

Data management activities were coordinated under WP1 and implemented throughout the project lifecycle. The main resources required were personnel effort for data organisation, quality control, documentation, metadata preparation, access classification, repository publication, FAIR compliance monitoring, and long-term preservation planning. These activities were covered through the project budget and the contributions of the participating partners.

Overall coordination of the Data Management Plan was undertaken by SINTEF, with support from all partners according to their responsibilities as data generators and owners. Each partner remained responsible for maintaining, documenting, securing, and preserving the data and other research outputs generated within its WPs. SINTEF coordinated the project-level data inventory, Zenodo Community, FAIR implementation, and DMP reporting.

The project used existing institutional infrastructure and trusted repositories wherever possible. Internal collaboration and document management were supported through the SUREWAVE SharePoint environment, while approved public outputs were deposited in Zenodo (<https://zenodo.org/communities/surewave/>). Consequently, no separate repository infrastructure was developed specifically for SUREWAVE, and no additional project-specific fee was required for depositing public records in Zenodo.

Resources for public outputs included preparation of files and supporting documentation, metadata creation, licence and access-status decisions, DOI registration through Zenodo, and links between datasets, deliverables, and publications. Restricted outputs remain under the responsibility of the corresponding data owners and are preserved according to their institutional policies, intellectual-property requirements, and continuing scientific or commercial value.

The storage architecture, repository arrangements, metadata procedures, FAIR implementation, and long-term preservation measures are described in Sections 2.6, 3.1, 3.2, and 6 and are not repeated here.

## 6. Data Security

### 6.1 Internal Data and Access Control

Consortium-level documentation and selected internal datasets were maintained in the SUREWAVE SharePoint environment, while large experimental files, simulation outputs, numerical models, source code, and commercially sensitive information were retained within the secure institutional infrastructures of the responsible partners.

Access to the SUREWAVE SharePoint environment was restricted to authorised users and managed through authenticated accounts and role-based permissions. SharePoint provided version control, controlled sharing, and document-history functions. Individual partners were responsible for securing local copies of raw data, working files, engineering models, and other restricted outputs in accordance with their institutional information-security and backup procedures.

Restricted information included detailed material formulations, proprietary engineering designs, CFD and finite element models, software source code, machine-learning training data, commercially sensitive calculations, and exploitable project Results. Access to such information was controlled according to the Consortium Agreement, the IPR Management Plan, applicable confidentiality obligations, and partner-specific security procedures.

### 6.2 Personal Data

Personal and identifiable information, including stakeholder contact details and information collected through engagement activities, was accessible only to authorised personnel. Public reporting used aggregated, anonymised, or otherwise approved information where appropriate. Personal data were managed in accordance with applicable GDPR requirements and the institutional procedures of the responsible partner.

### 6.3 Public Repository Security and Preservation

Approved public outputs were deposited in Zenodo (<https://zenodo.org/communities/surewave/> ) and made accessible through the SUREWAVE Zenodo Community. Zenodo provides persistent identifiers, repository-level replication, integrity measures, versioning support, and long-term preservation infrastructure. The corresponding repository arrangements are described in the FAIR Data section and are therefore not repeated here.

Public release through Zenodo was performed only after the relevant quality, confidentiality, dissemination, and intellectual-property reviews had been completed. Publication in Zenodo did not transfer ownership of the deposited output. Applicable copyrights, licences, database rights, attribution requirements, and third-party conditions remained in force.

### 6.4 Security Responsibilities

Each data-generating partner remained responsible for applying appropriate security measures to its data and other research outputs. These measures included access control, version management, backup, protection against unauthorised modification, and compliance with applicable institutional, contractual, and legal requirements.

Any suspected loss, unauthorised access, corruption, or disclosure of restricted project information was to be handled according to the incident-management procedures of the responsible organisation and, where relevant, reported to project management.

## 7. Ethical Aspects

No major research-ethics issues were identified in relation to the technical datasets generated by SUREWAVE. Most project data concerned engineering design, numerical simulations, laboratory testing, wave basin experiments, materials, monitoring, and sustainability assessment.

Where stakeholder or personal information was collected, the responsible partners were required to apply appropriate consent, confidentiality, data-minimisation, access-control, and GDPR procedures. Personal contact information was not treated as an open research dataset. Public outputs used aggregated or appropriately reviewed information to avoid disclosure of identifiable personal data.

Environmental, economic, and social assessment activities relied partly on published or licensed databases. The use and redistribution of such information remained subject to the corresponding database licences and terms of use.

## 8. Other Procedures

No additional project-specific national, sectoral, or departmental data-management procedure was established beyond the requirements described in this DMP. However, each beneficiary remained responsible for complying with applicable institutional policies, national legislation, GDPR requirements, the Grant Agreement, the Consortium Agreement, third-party licence conditions, and discipline-specific quality procedures.

Where a partner's institutional requirements were more stringent than the project-level arrangements, the institutional requirements applied to the relevant data and research outputs.

## 9. Conclusions

The updated Data Management Plan demonstrates that SUREWAVE has implemented systematic data governance throughout the project lifecycle. The procedures established in the initial DMP were progressively adapted to reflect the datasets, software tools, numerical models, publications, deliverables, prototypes, and other research outputs generated up to Month 48.

The consortium applied the FAIR principles by documenting project outputs, assigning persistent identifiers to public Zenodo records, providing descriptive metadata, linking related datasets and deliverables, and using accessible formats where technically feasible. Approved public deliverables, datasets, publications, and economic assessment models have been deposited in trusted repositories, particularly the SUREWAVE Zenodo Community. Restricted engineering models, source code, detailed material formulations, proprietary designs, and commercially sensitive know-how remain under the control of the responsible partners.

The project generated and reused a broad range of environmental, engineering, experimental, monitoring, sustainability, and economic data. These outputs supported the development and validation of the offshore floating photovoltaic system, floating breakwater, circular concrete materials, mooring and anchoring arrangements, connection systems, structural integrity methods, monitoring technologies, and integrated sustainability assessments. The detailed data inventory presented in Table 1 provides the corresponding formats, access conditions, related deliverables, and preservation arrangements.

Public access was balanced with intellectual-property rights, confidentiality, third-party licensing conditions, personal-data protection, security requirements, and the legitimate commercial interests of the beneficiaries. Public release was undertaken only after appropriate quality, dissemination, and IPR reviews. A public deliverable was not considered equivalent to an open dataset unless the underlying data files had been separately approved and deposited in a trusted repository.

Responsibility for data management was shared across the consortium. SINTEF coordinated the project-level DMP, data inventory, FAIR monitoring, and Zenodo activities, while each data-generating partner remained responsible for documenting, securing, preserving, and determining the access status of its own outputs. Public outputs will remain accessible through Zenodo, publisher platforms, institutional repositories, and the project website where maintained. Restricted outputs will continue to be preserved according to the institutional policies and legal, contractual, scientific, and commercial requirements of their owners.

Overall, SUREWAVE established an effective framework for making public research outputs discoverable, accessible, interpretable, and reusable while protecting confidential and exploitable Results. This framework supports the continued scientific and technical use of the project's outputs after project completion and provides a sound basis for future research, offshore floating photovoltaic development, modelling validation, sustainability assessment, demonstration, and industrial application.
