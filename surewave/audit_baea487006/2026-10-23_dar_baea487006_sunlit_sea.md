An audit engagement involves performing relevant procedures to obtain evidence about the amounts claimed in the financial statements. The procedures selected depend on the auditors\' judgement, including the assessment of the risks of material misstatement of the financial statements, whether due to fraud or error.

### 1.3 Observations and recommendations for systems improvement

[Observation 1]{.underline}: The auditors detected that the time records did not include: the project number and the reference to the project's tasks or work packages. Finally, the auditors detected that for the time records of the Chief Executive Officer are approved by himself even though there is a hierarchical superior who could effectively perform the supervision (see Qualitative Finding 1).

[Recommendation 1:]{.underline} We recommend that the beneficiary consider using the monthly declaration template for days worked on the project, as per Article 20 of the grant agreement and its annotations. This limits record-keeping burden and avoids the need for conversion of hours into day-equivalents. Alternatively, time records must include at least the details specified in this monthly declaration template.

[Observation 2]{.underline}: For the audited project, the beneficiary applied an incorrect methodology to calculate the personnel cost rates. The beneficiary calculated hourly rates by dividing each employee's estimated total remuneration by a standard annual productive time of 1 800 hours. (see Finding 2).

[Recommendation 2:]{.underline} In accordance with Article 6.2.A.1 of the grant agreement and its annotations, costs for employees (or equivalent) should be calculated as follows-- "*You have to calculate a daily rate per person for the reporting period. For this, (irrespective of the situation, i.e. full-time, part-time, partial hire, etc), you have to use the following formula: {actual personnel costs during the months within the reporting period} divided by {maximum declarable day-equivalents}. The actual personnel costs for the person are those eligible cost recorded in accordance with your usual cost accounting practice in your (statutory) accounts until the end of the reporting period for which you are calculating the daily rate"* and *"To calculate the daily rate, you first need to determine the maximum declarable day-equivalents. Since you may not declare more than 100% of your personnel cost, the number of declarable day-equivalents in each reporting period is capped. The maximum declarable day-equivalents for each reporting period are calculated as follows: {((215 / 12) multiplied by the number of months \[during which the person is employed\] within the reporting period) multiplied by the working time factor \[e.g. 1 for full-time, 0.5 for 50% part time etc.\]}. You will round up or down to the nearest half day-equivalent.*

[Observation 3]{.underline}: For two persons, the hours declared in the breakdown of costs differed from the actual hours recorded in the time records. (see Finding 3).

[Recommendation 3:]{.underline} According to Article 20.1(a) of the grant agreement: Records and other documentation to support the costs declared: "*for actual costs: adequate records and supporting documents to prove the costs declared (such as contracts, subcontracts, invoices and accounting records); in addition, the beneficiaries' usual accounting and internal control procedures must enable direct reconciliation between the amounts declared, the amounts recorded in their accounts and the amounts stated in the supporting documents".* We recommend that the beneficiary through its internal control procedures ensures that the time claimed reconciles to the time recorded to the supporting evidence in accordance with Article 20.1.(a), 6.2.A.1 and 6.2.A.2 of the grant agreement.

[Observation 4]{.underline}: The beneficiary did not round the days worked on the project to the nearest half-day (see Finding 4).

[Recommendation 4:]{.underline} In accordance with Article 6.2.A.1 of the grant agreement, personnel costs must be calculated using the number of day-equivalents worked on the project (rounded up or down to the nearest half-day).

[Observation 5]{.underline}: The beneficiary declared costs for an SME owner not receiving salary under the Natural persons working under a direct contract (A.2) cost category, although the eligibility conditions for this category were not fully met. Consequently, the portion of the claimed costs that did not qualify under the Natural persons working under a direct contract (A.2) cost category has been rejected and reclassified from personnel cost (A.2) to as SME (A.4) (see Findings 5 and 6).

[Recommendation 5:]{.underline} The beneficiary should establish and implement procedures to ensure that the costs declared fulfil the relevant eligibility criteria laid down in Articles 6.2.A.2 and 6.2.A.4 of the grant agreement and its annotations.

[Observation 6]{.underline}: The beneficiary did not calculate personnel costs of an SME owner not receiving a salary as unit costs and declared them under A.2 Natural persons working under a direct contract cost category instead under A.4 SME owners cost category (see Finding 6).

[Recommendation 6:]{.underline} As indicated in Article 6.2.A.4 of the grant agreement the beneficiary should calculate costs of SME owners not receiving a salary as unit costs in accordance with the method set out in Annex 2a to the grant agreement. They should be declared in the financial statements under A.4 cost category.

[Observation 7]{.underline}: The supplier for subcontracting (Kodeworks AS) was selected without competitive tendering. The supplier is an entity wholly owned by a single individual who is both the Chief Financial Officer and an SME owner of the beneficiary. It was verified that this person charged costs for hours worked on the project simultaneously under the personnel costs category and the subcontracting costs category. In addition, it could not be ensured that conflicts of interest have been avoided as the supplier is owned by one of the owners of the beneficiary and economic interests exist.

[Recommendation 7:]{.underline} According to the annotations to Article 6.1 (a)(vii) and Article 12 of the grant agreement: *"a cost item or element can NOT be declared more than once within the same or another budget category or activity (e.g. cost for internally invoiced goods and services cannot include cost elements already charged under equipment cost)."* and *"conflicts of interests must be avoided irrespective of whether they actually affect quality or cost, or whether the persons involved actually gain any benefit (or not)".* Therefore, the beneficiary should adapt his internal controls to ensure avoidance of conflict of interest and double fund risk.

During our audit, we noted the following controls:

- the segregation of duties is adequate with regard to the time recording system;

- the system prevents charging hours above the workable hours of the week*;*

- time records are filled in on a daily basis and all hours need to be recorded*;*

- time records are certified by the supervisor.

The beneficiary uses an electronic full-time recording system called Werkz for the registration of working hours. All employees are provided with a personal username and password to access the system.

Employees are required to record their working hours on a daily basis. Recorded hours must be allocated directly to the specific projects on which the employee has worked, ensuring accurate project‑level time tracking. Employees are not permitted to allocate the same working hour to more than one project. Any overtime must be agreed in advance with the employer and, if approved, recorded in a separate field in the system.

At the end of each month, employees submit their completed time records in Werkz. Once submitted, no changes can be made unless explicitly approved. Upon submission, the project manager receives an automatic notification and conducts a review of the reported working hours for the month. If errors, inconsistencies, or discrepancies are identified, the reporting period may be reopened, and the time records corrected.

Based on the audit work performed, we confirmed that the time recording system complies with the minimum requirements established under the annotations to Article 20 of the grant agreement:

- electronic signatures linking the electronic identity data with the electronic validation data require a password and a username;

- there is a documented and secure process for managing user rights;

- an auditable log of all electronic transactions.

Moreover, based on the work performed, we also noted that the time records of the sampled persons verified on-site within the Werkz time recording system specifically included the following information according to the annotations to Article 20.1 of the grant agreement:

- the title of the project, as specified in the grant agreement;

- the beneficiary's full name, as specified in the grant agreement;

- the full name of the person working for the project;

- the number of days per month worked for the project in the period covered by the time record.

[Qualitative Finding 1:]{.underline} The auditors noted that the number of the project as specified in the grant agreement was not included, however, the number can be tracked as the title of the project is included and referenced in the Annex 1 of the grant agreement. In addition, the employees' signatures are not visible in the time records. However, the beneficiary provided additional evidence regarding their time recording system, which requires unique credentials in order to log into the system, which demonstrates that each employee is responsible for recording their hours in the system.

Moreover, the auditors also noticed that the supervisor\'s full name, date and signature are not visible in the time records provided, however, as additional evidence, the Chief Executive Officer sent an extract from the time recording system of the log of approval, including the approval date, proving that the time records have been supervised by him, even though there is a hierarchical superior who could effectively perform the supervision.

Furthermore, the reference to the project's tasks or work packages was missing in the time records. As additional evidence, the beneficiary has submitted a summary of the tasks performed by the employees within each of the work packages.

Additionally, the auditors detected that for the time records of the Chief Executive Officer are supervised and signed by himself, However, given the absence of a hierarchical superior within the organisation, there is no higher-level authority available to review and approve the CEO's time records.

We have raised recommendation 1 in section 1.3 of this audit report.

Based on the work performed, we consider the time recording process reliable as per the annotations to Article 20 of the grant agreement.

Absences are stored within the time records inside the system, allowing recorded working time to be cross-checked against the time declared in the project. No discrepancies were noted.

***Day-equivalents calculation***

For those persons who work for the project, the beneficiary used their computer-based time recording system to record all the hours worked for the project.

During the audit, the auditors detected that the personnel costs declared in the financial statement were erroneous, as the beneficiary did not convert the hours worked in the project into day-equivalents. The auditors calculated the costs as follows: 

- Day-equivalents = {number of hours worked by the person on the project during the reporting period divided by (a day-equivalent of 8 hours)}. The resulting figure has not been then rounded up or down to the nearest half-day. Please refer to Finding 4 for more details.

In addition, the auditors detected that for two persons, the hours declared in the breakdown of costs differed from the actual hours recorded in the time records. Please refer to Finding 3 for more details.

***Daily rate calculation***

[A1. Employees]{.underline}

During the audit, the auditors detected that the methodology followed to calculate the personnel cost was incorrect for the personnel costs claimed in the financial statement as the beneficiary did not calculate daily rates. Instead, The beneficiary calculated hourly rates by dividing each employee's estimated total remuneration by a standard annual productive time of 1 800 hours (see Finding 2). The total remuneration included gross salary and social security contributions, all statutory contributions according to national law.

[A.4. SME owners not receiving a salary]{.underline}

For the audited project, for one of the SME co-owners, the beneficiary calculated the daily rate for the SME owner, Erik Larsen under personnel category A.2 Natural persons working under a direct contract. However, SME owners are not eligible to be declared under category A.2, which is limited to natural persons working under a direct contract with the beneficiary other than an employment contract. Consequently, a reclassification from A.2 Natural persons working under a direct contract to A.4 SME Owners has been proposed. Please refer to Findings 5 and 6 for further details.

For SME owners (A.4), the costs must be declared as unit cost, using the unit cost (daily rate) fixed by the authorising decision C(2020)7115 and set out in Annex 2a. The amount per unit (daily rate) is prefixed in the authorising decision, adjusted depending on the country where the beneficiary is established (Norway: NO: 128,7%) according to the following methodology (please refer to Finding 5 for more details):

Amount per unit \[daily rate\] =

### 3.2 Subcontracting

***Work performed***

The tests were performed on a full scope basis during our audit work according to the procedures set out in the audit programme.

***Subcontracting procedures***

The beneficiary has internal procedures for purchases and procurement related to product development, engineering, and research activities. The objective of this policy is to ensure that all purchases are carried out in compliance with the principles of sound financial management and best value for money.

All procurement activities are governed by the following principles:

- All purchases must be necessary, reasonable, and cost‑effective for the intended purpose.

- All purchases must be supported by adequate and verifiable documentation, such as quotes, supplier correspondence, evaluation notes, invoices, contracts, and proof of delivery or service performance.

- Conflicts of interest must be avoided and, where relevant, disclosed.

- Procurement decisions must balance price, quality, reliability, delivery time, and supplier reputation, rather than focusing solely on lowest cost.

The procurement process is initiated when an employee submits a purchase request. For each request, the Chief Engineer or designated technical staff conducts appropriate market research and identifies available procurement options. Market conditions, supplier reputation, delivery time, country of origin, and overall professionalism are taken into account.

The assessment of the different suppliers is reviewed by the Managing Director, who approves the purchase, requests additional information, or adjusts the proposed procurement approach. Payments are processed only after approval and verification that all required documentation is complete and accurate.

The policy does not establish formal monetary thresholds. Instead, the beneficiary strives to obtain several offers for high-value purchases whenever feasible. Procurement decisions are made based on overall value, including technical suitability for the purpose, quality, supplier reliability, and long‑term usefulness. Evidence of comparisons made and the justification for the selected option is retained as part of the procurement documentation.

The supplier selected for subcontracting (Kodeworks AS) was selected without competitive tendering. More specific this is the only cost item declared in the subcontracting cost category and it refers to services provided to the beneficiary. In addition, the supplier is an entity wholly owned by a single individual who is both the Chief Financial Officer and an SME owner of the beneficiary. It was verified that this person charged costs for hours worked on the project simultaneously under the personnel costs category and the subcontracting costs category. See Finding 5 below for more information.
