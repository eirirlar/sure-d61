- WP5 modelling and SHMS complete (D5.1 coupled aero/hydro; D5.3 structural integrity FE; D5.4 corrosion sensor development; D5.5 crack propagation algorithms; D5.6 SHMS demonstrator; D5.7 validation report). D5.2 missing locally.

- WP8 dissemination and exploitation partly delivered (D8.2 societal SWOT; D8.5 M18 master plan). D8.1 (M6 initial plan), D8.3 (technical brochure), D8.4 (exploitation pathways & business plan) missing — see T109 and T110.

Headline technical findings (synthesized from D7.6 as the integrated report):

- Mediterranean 10 MW: LCoE €0.062/kWh, IRR 10.7%, payback ~8 years — commercially viable.
- Baltic 10 MW: LCoE €0.096/kWh, IRR 2.2%, payback 14–16 years — marginal.
- North Sea 10 MW: LCoE €0.139/kWh, negative NPV/IRR — not viable at commercial discount rates.
- Climate benefit vs. coal >80% emission reduction across all sites.
- Innovative circular breakwater materials show no material sustainability advantage over conventional concrete designs.
- Critical open technical challenge: hinge durability under North-Sea loads. Generation-3 redesigned hinge with full-width support raised ultimate load capacity from 14 kN to 37 kN, adequate for Baltic (34 kN loads) and Mediterranean (33 kN), still marginal for North Sea (40 kN).
- Location selection dominates over technical design choices in the sustainability optimization.

### CFD note · Estimation of aerodynamic forces on PV modules using CFD simulations

- File: `2023-03-10_surewave_cfd_simulations_plate.md` · non-numbered · R / SEN · Version 1.0
- Lead: SINTEF (Balram Panjwani) · Contributors: none · Due — · Actual 10.03.2023
- Summary: OpenFOAM CFD study of an 8×8 PV-panel matrix with/without a breakwater 2 m upwind, at 0–20° angles of attack. Validated against published experimental data. Breakwater cuts drag and lift substantially at low AoA (at 5° AoA: drag 372→10, lift 2350→300 for a 16×16 matrix), but effectiveness drops beyond 10° AoA where the panels see increased wind loads. Confirms RANS suitability at low AoA; less accurate at high AoA.
- Cross-refs: supports WP3/WP5 design and load work
- Open issues: RANS accuracy limited at high AoA (>50°); no full-scale offshore validation

### D3.2 · Floating breakwater design (shape, size, composition)

- File: `2023-08-08_surewave_d3.2_floating_breakwater_design.md` · WP3 / T3.2 · OTHER / SEN · Version 2.0
- Lead: CLEMENT · Contributors: none noted · Due 31.07.2023 · Actual 08.08.2023
- Summary: Coupled aero-hydrodynamic study of floating-breakwater wave-attenuation performance across the three reference sites using FLOW-3D HYDRO. Comparison against Macagno / Wiegel / Cox / Fousert-van Tol theoretical approaches. Wave-attenuation peaks in the 1–3 second period range (>40% reduction). Selected design: 20 m long × 5 m wide × 2.5 m high with double keels; 123.4 tonnes at 2.03 m draft. Larger width and draft improve efficiency; keels raise longer-period performance; circular layout minimizes force concentrations.

### D3.3 · Anchoring and connections to PV system

- File: `2023-12-08_surewave_d3.3_final.md` · WP3 / T3.3 + T3.4 · R / PU · Version 3.0
- Lead: CLEMENT · Contributors: none noted · Due 30.11.2023 · Actual 08.12.2023
- Summary: Anchoring/connection strategy across the three sites (Baltic mild, Mediterranean mid, North Sea harsh). Circular layout selected over octagonal/triangular/ellipse/square for minimum force concentration and uniform load distribution. ### D5.1 · Coupled aero-hydrodynamic analyses and design optimization

- File: `2024-04-16_surewave_d5.1.md` · WP5 / T5.2 · R / SEN · Version 4.0

### D6.1 · Ultimate strength capacity of critical connections

- File: `2024-09-12_surewave_d6.1_ultimate_strength_capacity.md` · WP6 / T6.2 · R / PU · Version 2.0
- Lead: SINTEF · Contributors: CLEMENT · Due 31.07.2024 · Actual 12.09.2024
- Summary: Lab testing at SINTEF of three critical connection systems: PU hinges between modules; steel-wire cable systems between breakwaters; new breakwater-to-module connections. Tests: tensile, compression, shear, torsion with digital image correlation. PU hinges deformable with low ultimate strength (3.5 kN average) vs. WP5 expected loads 8–10 kN, and very low compressive strength allowing module–module collision. Steel-wire connections meet spec (402 kN, close to 450 kN rating). Breakwater-to-module connection meets 41 kN requirement with rope as weak point. Conclusion: PU hinges need redesign; steel wire and new tailor-designed connections robust.
- Cross-refs: WP5 operational loads; D3.3 design requirements; feeds T6.3 (D6.3 redesign validation)
- Open issues: PU hinge redesign needed before implementation; sharp bracket edges to be smoothed to reduce rope tearing

### D6.3 · Mechanical performance of the new connection system between the FPVs

- Lead: SINTEF · Contributors: SUNLIT SEA · Due 31.01.2025 · Actual 12.02.2025
- Summary: Lab validation of the Sunlit Sea redesigned PU connection with tensile / compression / shear / fatigue tests at SINTEF. ### D7.3 · Environmental assessment of offshore floating photovoltaic systems

- File: `2025-10-06_surewave_d7.3_environmental_report.md` · WP7 / T7.3 · R / PU · Version 4.0

- Summary: LCA + local environmental impact assessment across the three sites. ### D7.5 · Social risks of offshore floating photovoltaic systems

- File: `2025-10-06_surewave_d7.5_social_report.md` · WP7 / T7.5 · R / PU · Version 3.0
- Lead: IFEU · Contributors: (authored by IFEU) · Due 31.05.2025 · Actual 06.10.2025
- Summary: Social LCA evaluating risks across the offshore FPV supply chain and construction/operation phases. Offshore FPV social risks can be substantially lower than fossil reference if high-risk supply chains are avoided, material-intensive subsea cables are minimized, and high-irradiation locations selected. Hotspots: solar-cell production (concentrated in China; Xinjiang forced-labour concerns), steel for anchoring and breakwater reinforcement, aluminium and copper for cables/frames, marine fuel consumption. Construction and maintenance carries labour-rights and offshore-work-safety risks though small overall. Material intensity per kWh (driven by location: irradiation, cable length, metocean) is the main lever. Breakwater type and layout have negligible impact on social risks. Recommendations: EU low-risk sourcing, certified suppliers in high-risk nations, direct engagement, EU CSDDD implementation, diversify PV production capacity within EU.
- Cross-refs: D7.1, D7.3, D7.4, D7.6; EU Corporate Sustainability Due Diligence Directive
- Open issues: Chinese solar-cell supply-chain concentration hard to mitigate; embedded upstream sourcing from high-risk countries in lower-risk-country products requires better traceability; Xinjiang forced-labour allegations need independent verification

### D7.6 · Integrated sustainability assessment of offshore floating PV systems

- File: `2025-11-05_surewave_d7.6_integrated_sustainability_report.md` · WP7 / T7.6 · R / PU · Version 4.0
- Lead: IFEU · Contributors: SINTEF · Due 30.09.2025 · Actual 05.11.2025
- Summary: Integrated technological + environmental + economic + social assessment across 12 scenarios covering three sites and multiple system configurations. Offshore FPV is a sustainable additional renewable option to replace fossil electricity — impacts higher than onshore PV or wind but acceptable when location and deployment conditions are optimized. Mediterranean 10 MW is best across all dimensions: LCoE €0.062/kWh, IRR 10.7%, 8-year payback, best environmental and social outcomes. All scenarios show >80% emission reduction vs. coal, low land-use footprint, and low social risk if high-risk sourcing avoided. Critical technological challenge: hinge durability between PV modules — Gen-3 redesigned hinges with full-width support raised ultimate load capacity 14 kN → 37 kN, adequate for Baltic (34 kN) and Mediterranean (33 kN) but still marginal for North Sea (40 kN). Key drivers: high irradiation, minimal cable, shallow water, connection to existing offshore infrastructure. Location dominates over technical design; innovative circular breakwaters offer no material sustainability advantage over conventional designs.
- Cross-refs: Breyer et al. 2025 (D7.3); Karg & Keller 2025 (D7.5); Larsen 2025 (D7.4); Panjwani 2025 (technological); D7.1
