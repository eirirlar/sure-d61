# Market Definition Canvas — KER1 Integrated offshore floating photovoltaic system

Horizon Results Booster G2M-A workshop input · Sunlit Sea AS · 5 November 2026

Key Exploitable Result: Sunlit Sea's offshore floating photovoltaic platform (Generation 2), commercial name Sunlit Sea CONNECT. Canvas completed per the META Knowledge to Market Jobs-to-be-Done Market Definition template (seven boxes: product, job executor, abstracted job executor, function of the product, other products used, abstracted job statement, with the market definition in the centre).

---

## Box 1. Product / service / idea being innovated

The Sunlit Sea CONNECT offshore floating photovoltaic platform. Each float combines an aluminium bottom plate and a cast polyurethane frame that carries a standard commercial 710–740 Wp glass-glass photovoltaic panel. Panels are decoupled from float manufacture and dropped in at deployment. Floats connect along their full edge through Generation-3 polyurethane hinges developed and validated under the SUREWAVE Horizon Europe project (Grant Agreement 101083342). The platform is deployed with mooring, anchoring and grid-transition elements, either standalone or behind a floating breakwater. It is a product system, manufactured and sold as a whole.

## Box 2. Job executor — who is using the product to get a job done?

- Project development managers at utility-scale renewable energy developers and independent power producers who plan 1–10 MW offshore photovoltaic installations.
- Asset managers at operating offshore wind farms who are evaluating in-lease solar co-location to monetise shared infrastructure.
- Energy and sustainability directors at coastal industrial off-takers (utilities, port authorities, process industries) procuring renewable capacity via corporate power purchase agreement or on-balance-sheet ownership.
- Engineering-procurement-construction technical leads at marine contractors who will install and commission the platform on behalf of the above.
- Investment-committee members and lending-institution technical due diligence teams who approve the capital commitment.

These are the human decision-makers involved in the buying and deployment decision, across the development chain from site identification through to installed operation.

## Box 3. Abstracted job executor — one classifying term

Offshore renewable energy project developer. The term covers the categories in Box 2 across the primary (utility developer / IPP), secondary (offshore wind co-location) and tertiary (coastal industrial off-taker) segments, since in each case the person using the product is the person deciding to develop, build and operate an offshore generating asset.

## Centre statement — market definition

A market is defined as a group of *offshore renewable energy project developers* trying to get the job of *delivering utility-scale renewable electricity from offshore water surfaces where onshore land is scarce or wind alone does not meet portfolio objectives* done.

## Box 5. Function of the product — what job does it get done?

Primary job statement, in verb / object / contextual clarifier form:

- Generate electricity from solar irradiance on sheltered and exposed European coastal water surfaces, at utility-scale project sizes (1–10 MW first generation, scaling to 50+ MW by 2033).

Secondary functions that extend the primary:

- Shade and reduce evaporation from the water body beneath the array.
- Co-locate with existing offshore wind leases to share substation, grid connection, mooring corridors and operations base.
- Provide platform-integrated structural health monitoring data to the operator, enabling condition-based rather than calendar-based maintenance.

## Box 6. Other products used in conjunction, and their functions

- Offshore wind turbines (fixed-bottom and floating) — generate electricity from offshore wind. Complementary rather than substitute: shared infrastructure and non-correlated generation profile are the co-location drivers.
- Onshore utility photovoltaic installations — generate electricity from solar irradiance on land. Substitute when land is available; complement when land is exhausted.
- Calm-water inland floating photovoltaic installations (reservoirs, ponds, dams) — generate electricity from solar irradiance on inland fresh water. Substitute for inland sites; not applicable offshore.
- Grid connection infrastructure: offshore substations, submarine cables, onshore grid interconnection points — transmit offshore-generated electricity to shore and into the transmission network.
- Mooring systems and anchoring — hold the floating photovoltaic platform in position against wind, wave and current loading.
- Floating breakwater structures (including the SUREWAVE concrete breakwater, KER3 owned by Clement Germany) — attenuate wave energy at exposed sites to bring loads within the floating photovoltaic platform's structural envelope.
- Structural health monitoring platforms (including the SUREWAVE platform, KER6 owned by Ceit) — monitor the condition of the floating structure and inform maintenance planning.
- Power purchase agreements, corporate renewable energy guarantees, grid-balancing contracts — monetise the generated electricity and secure the project's revenue stream.
- Site development services: marine spatial planning, environmental impact assessment, cultural-heritage screening, permitting, grid-connection application — turn a candidate sea area into a buildable project.
- Project financing instruments: equity, project debt, blended finance with grant co-funding (EIC Transition, EUPI-PV Partnership, Enova support) — provide the capital stack for construction.

## Box 7. Abstracted job statement — in the job executor's own words

"I need to grow renewable generation capacity in a coastal market where onshore solar is blocked by land scarcity and permit friction, and where I have, or could acquire, offshore lease area or nearshore water space that no other technology in my portfolio can currently use productively. I need the capacity to clear my offtake-price benchmark, to pass my investment committee's due diligence on technology risk, to be financeable under current lender appetite, and to integrate operationally with the rest of my offshore asset base over a twenty-plus-year lifetime."

## Supporting context for the workshop

Mediterranean sites are the segment where this job currently closes commercially: levelised cost of energy €0.062/kWh at 10 MW scale, internal rate of return 10.7 %, 8-year payback (SUREWAVE Deliverable 7.4 economic assessment). Baltic sites are marginal in the current cost base. North Sea sites are not yet viable at the present Generation-3 hinge iteration. Nordic nearshore sites (Vollsfjorden, Gunneklevfjorden, Skien havn, Storavatnet) sit in the Sunlit Sea develop-operate-sell channel and provide the reference pipeline for the first commercial deployments, served alongside the Mediterranean utility-scale channel.

The job becomes easier to close as three conditions improve: dedicated DNV or IEC certification path for offshore floating photovoltaic; first operating commercial-scale pilot (2028–2029 window) that de-risks technology diligence for subsequent buyers; and a Generation-4 hinge iteration that opens the North Sea wave climate. These conditions are tracked in the exploitation roadmap and are the subject of the SuRE Horizon Europe follow-on project (Grant Agreement 101135567) and the EIC Transition and EUPI-PV Partnership proposals currently in preparation.
