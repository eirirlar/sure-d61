### 4.7 Financial plan

Sunlit Sea prepared the financial plan for KER1 as the annex to its Horizon Results Booster business plan, the annex it chose at the Booster business-planning workshop of 13 November 2026. The plan was completed on 22 November 2026. It projects Sunlit Sea's income statement, cash flow and balance sheet for 2027–2031, in euro at nominal prices, in four scenarios for public funding, with sensitivities on cost, price, volume and timing. This section gives the resulting figures for the company as a whole. The Booster mentor reviews the plan at the second business-planning workshop in December 2026; Sunlit Sea then updates it after the first equity round, for adoption by its board in Q2 2027 (milestone M2, Section 4.9). The underlying projects, customers and prices are commercially sensitive and are not given here.

Volumes are built bottom-up from Sunlit Sea's pipeline. The plan has three Norwegian reference sites (a harbour, an industrial basin and a lake), kit leads in Germany, Denmark and the Baltic states, and a first 10 MW-class kit supply contract of about 10 MWp, assumed signed early in 2030 and delivered in two halves in 2030 and 2031. Sunlit Sea builds and owns the first, smallest reference plant (2027) and delivers the two multi-megawatt reference plants (Section 4.4) as EPC contractor to the hosts' plant companies: the first in 2028, the second in 2030–2031. From 2030 it sells mainly kits with site engineering.

The ranges span the four funding scenarios. In the base case, about 28 MWp is delivered over 2027–2031: about 7 MWp in 2030 and about 16 MWp in 2031. The volume splits by status:

- 3.6 MWp (13 %) at sites with permits, or with confirmed grid capacity and host commitment;
- 4.1 MWp (15 %) at named sites or leads;
- 20 MWp (72 %) of target volume not yet identified, including the 10 MW-class contract.

Sunlit Sea's plan makes the second equity round conditional on a letter of intent for that contract.

The four scenarios differ only in public funding:

- Base: the extension of Sunlit Sea's scope in the SuRE project (about €0.4M of grant in 2027) is approved, and no new EU project is won. The CINEA decision on the extension is pending. The plan places the extension's demonstrator scope at Sunlit Sea's first reference plant, in operation from Q3 2027. Its eligible cost is staff time, equipment rental, travel and other costs, and the marine works and grid connection of that plant. SuRE and SUREWAVE fund different work: SUREWAVE funded the design, tests and assessment of the integrated system up to 2026; SuRE funds the development of prototypes 4 and 5 of the Generation 2 platform and, with the extension, its demonstration at the first reference plant (Section 10).
- EIC: the base case plus the EIC Transition project for the exposed-site version of KER1 (Section 10), with a Sunlit Sea share of about €0.75M over 2027–2030, and a first commercial plant from the kit at the demonstrator site after the project.
- EU upside: the EIC case plus the EUPI-PV nearshore demonstrator (Section 10), if Sunlit Sea applies and the proposal is funded: about €0.9M of grant-funded work over 2027–2030 and a kit delivery for the demonstrator in 2029.
- Grant downside: no SuRE extension, or none for the first reference plant, and no new EU project.

Grant income is not counted as revenue. The EIC and EU-upside cases also include the first commercial plant at the demonstrator site after the project.

- EBITDA break-even in 2030 holds in all four funding scenarios. The net result is negative through 2030 and positive from 2031. Without EPC deliveries, kit and engineering sales give a positive EBITDA from about 7 MWp of kits a year and cover all costs, including depreciation, from about 9 MWp a year.
- The plan raises €5.5M of equity in two rounds: €3.5M in the first quarter of 2027 and €2.0M in the second half of 2029. The base case uses the first round and about €0.1–0.3M of the second; the rest of the second round is a buffer for delays and guarantees and funds growth beyond the plan. Plant investments are financed at plant level, with project debt, owner equity and national investment support. The exceptions are the first reference plant, which Sunlit Sea owns, and a minority share of about €0.2M in the company that owns the first multi-megawatt plant.
- New EU project funding improves cash at the end of 2031 by up to about €0.7M against the base case, and by about €0.9M against the grant downside. The grants fund development work at close to cost. The EIC Transition project takes KER1 to exposed sites. The EUPI-PV project, if Sunlit Sea joins and it is funded, gives a nearshore reference plant in 2029.
- Beyond the plan period, the indicative outlook has kit sales of about 18 MWp in 2032, growing to 25–45 MWp a year in 2033–2035. For orientation, Wood Mackenzie forecasts a cumulative floating solar capacity of 2.2 GW in Germany, 1.2 GW in France and 1 GW in the Netherlands by 2033 (reported by pv magazine, November 2024). Spread by Sunlit Sea over 2024–2033, that is at most about 0.4–0.5 GW of new capacity a year in the three markets, less the capacity already installed. The 2031 volume of about 16 MWp would be about 3–4 % of it, although part of Sunlit Sea's volume is in markets outside these three. Most floating PV in Europe is on man-made inland water (Section 4.3), where CONNECT is not the target product, so the comparison is an upper bound. The nearshore and sheltered-coastal segment that the platform targets has no published size. Sunlit Sea plans a count of suitable sites in Norway, Sweden, Denmark, Germany and the Baltic states with the customer interviews in early 2027.

Breakwater classes are bought by the plant owner. Where Sunlit Sea delivers the full system as EPC contractor, it buys the breakwater and includes it in the EPC price (Section 5). The Norwegian sites that Sunlit Sea delivers as EPC contractor need no breakwater class on current site data, and kit buyers buy any breakwater themselves as plant owners, so the plan has no breakwater class in its revenue or costs. Two sensitivities test this. The first reference plant, which Sunlit Sea owns, uses small wavebreakers on its exposed side. They are not yet quoted and are tested at about €0.1M. That lowers cash by about €0.1M and the result over the period by about €0.05M, since the cost is depreciated over the plant's life. If the two larger Norwegian sites needed a light breakwater on one side and it were not priced into the EPC contracts, the result over the period would fall by about €0.9M. At sites that need a breakwater class, Sunlit Sea's gain is the kit and engineering for a site that would otherwise be closed to floating PV.

Conditions for the plan:

- Kit price: the plan's kit gross margin target is 40–50 %, and the plan meets it at today's kit price and production cost. Cost reductions from series moulds, a lighter bottom plate, casting near the markets where that lowers cost, and volume purchasing give room to lower the price where buyers need it, or to absorb a breakwater at more exposed sites.
- Investment support for the first plants: at first-of-a-kind cost, the first plants need investment support of about a quarter of their cost to match the host's alternative supply. Support of that order is sought from national schemes for first-of-a-kind energy technology. Without support, plants built from the kit are competitive where power costs more than at the first Norwegian sites. Examples are industry with higher power prices, sites limited by grid capacity, and southern Europe, where the yield is higher. In markets with poor solar irradiation, national support for floating solar is assumed.
- References and volume: the plan is most sensitive to the timing of the reference plants and to kit volume and margin in 2030–2031.
  - If the reference plants and kit sales come one year later, the net result over 2027–2031 falls by about €3.6M, 2031 EBITDA is about −€0.1M, and cash falls below zero (lowest year-end cash about −€0.3M). About €0.5M more equity keeps a cash buffer of €0.2M. The second round then moves to 2030.
  - If the 10 MW-class contract is not won, the net result over the period falls by about €2.4M and 2031 EBITDA is about €0.7M; cash stays positive.
  - Kit volume 30 % lower in 2030–2031 moves 2031 EBITDA by about €0.9M; a lower kit price or a higher kit cost, within the range tested, moves it by about €0.4M.
  - A combined commercial downside (one year later, kit volume 30 % lower and no further kit cost reduction) gives a net result over the period of about −€5.6M, against −€1.4M in the base case, and a 2031 EBITDA of about −€0.6M. It needs about €1.0M more equity, or the 2029–2031 hires and the second mould set are held back.

All of these depend on the first plants operating as designed, and the plan ties hiring and investment to four decision gates set by those plants:

1. First equity round closed (Q1 2027): order the first reference plant and start hiring.
2. Investment decision by the plant company at the first multi-megawatt site (Q4 2027): sign the EPC contract and commit the minority share; if the decision slips, the 2028 hires are delayed.
3. First reference plant through its first winter, with loads and yield within design values (Q1–Q2 2028): release float production for the multi-megawatt plant and order the industrial moulds.
4. The multi-megawatt plant in operation for a year, and a letter of intent for the 10 MW-class contract (2029): raise the second round, order the second mould set and hire the application engineers.
