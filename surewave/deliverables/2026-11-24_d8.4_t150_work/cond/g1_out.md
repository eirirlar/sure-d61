## Executive summary

This is the consortium's plan for exploiting the results of SUREWAVE, an offshore floating photovoltaic (FPV) system with a circular-concrete floating breakwater, after the extended project ends on 31 December 2026. The project aimed at technology readiness level (TRL) 4–5 for the system at its end (Grant Agreement, Annex 1). SINTEF's technological risk assessment (D7.2), summarised in the integrated sustainability assessment D7.6 (IFEU), rates the integrated concept at TRL 5 on MARIN's wave-basin tests and SINTEF's component tests. The proposal of September 2026 to EIC Transition, a European Innovation Council scheme, starts from TRL 4. This plan uses TRL 4–5 for the integrated system and Sunlit Sea's CONNECT platform. ACCIONA's reduced-scale breakwater prototype, monitored by Ceit, was exposed at sea in Gijón for about nine months (Section 6.2).

As lead of work package 8 (WP8) on exploitation, Sunlit Sea proposed seven Key Exploitable Results (KERs), one anchored in each beneficiary, from the 27 results that the partners reported (Section 3.1). KER1 Integrated floating PV system with breakwater is the proposal's global solution, the product P1 *Offshore FPV plant*, to be marketed by Sunlit Sea AS. It integrates the breakwater, mooring, anchoring and transition element designed by Clement Germany, which remain Clement Germany's (Section 7.1). Sunlit Sea led WP2, the FPV and global system design (D3.1, D3.4) in WP3, led by Clement Germany, and the economic assessment D7.4, and redesigned the hinge (Section 6.1). The partners that developed them exploit KER2 concrete formulations (ACCIONA), KER3 breakwater design (Clement Germany), KER4 modelling and testing methods (SINTEF), KER5 hydrodynamic models and basin testing (MARIN and SINTEF), KER6 structural health monitoring (Ceit) and KER7 sustainability assessment (IFEU).

KER1 has a full business plan, prepared by Sunlit Sea with the Horizon Results Booster, the European Commission's free support service for exploiting project results:

- First product: CONNECT, Sunlit Sea's Generation 2 floating platform, for sheltered coastal sites. It replaces the Generation 1 aluminium float, which the SUREWAVE array used; Sunlit Sea started the redesign from the SUREWAVE hinge tests and models and continues it in the parallel project SuRE (Section 4.1). CONNECT goes into Sunlit Sea's Norwegian reference plants from 2027 and is sold as a kit from 2028.
- Breakwater classes: where a site needs more protection, a breakwater on the exposed side only, in three classes sized by Sunlit Sea from the SUREWAVE breakwater, for Clement Germany to confirm. If the EIC Transition proposal is funded, a field demonstrator follows in 2028–2030. The proposal targets the first commercial site with a breakwater class after the EIC Transition project, which would end about 2030; Sunlit Sea's financial plan models it in 2031.
- Open sea: the ring-breakwater plant is the long-term configuration, with a pilot proposal about 2031 and commercial plants from about 2035.

D7.4 (version 2.0) gives the D7.4 reference plant, a 10 MWp (megawatt-peak) open-sea plant in the Mediterranean, a levelised cost of electricity of €0.076/kWh, an internal rate of return of 15.6 % and a net present value of €7.3M; in the Baltic Sea it is at break-even and in the North Sea not viable at current costs (Section 4.6). Sunlit Sea's financial plan shows that the first plants need investment support of about a quarter of their cost, and it shows break-even in earnings before interest, tax, depreciation and amortisation (EBITDA) in 2030 in all four funding scenarios, provided the Norwegian reference plants come on time (Section 4.7). The largest single risk is a one-year delay of those plants (Section 4.8). The route differs from the proposal's 25 MW offshore pilot in 2025–2027 and 1,240 MW by 2032 (Section 4.10).

KER1 is protected by trade secrets and copyright; patenting was assessed with a patent attorney and not pursued, because of extensive prior art. A decision on design registration follows by Q3 2027, after the confirmation of contributions to the hinge redesign that Sunlit Sea requests from the partners concerned in Q1 2027, as the WP8 lead proposes (Section 7.4).

SUREWAVE met or exceeded 11 of the 16 dissemination and communication targets of the Grant Agreement and D8.5; the exceptions are peer-reviewed publications, conference workshops, infographics, the final summary video and a press release at the start. Of 22 further reach targets, three are met and one for 2024 only; three are partly met, five not met, seven not measured, no new PhD is recorded, and two are open. Five of the seven peer-reviewed papers are deposited after publication; one meets the deposit-at-publication rule (Section 2.5). Five projects have used or use SUREWAVE results (Section 10).

## 1. Purpose and scope

The Grant Agreement describes D8.4, the output of Task 8.4 *Exploitation pathways and business plan*, as a plan including KER and intellectual property rights (IPR) analysis, value innovation and market analysis, value proposition testing, and business models, business plans and exploitation action plans. Objective O8 asks for a definitive business model and business plan for the global solution; this plan starts replication at sheltered and nearshore sites (Section 4.10). The proposal names P1 as the product to be marketed by Sunlit Sea, with the other partners compensated through licences or royalties, and R2–R10 exploited by the partners that developed them; R11, without an owner in the proposal, is placed with IFEU, which led D7.6 (Section 3.4).

Sections 3–6 give the KER map, the KER1 business plan, the fair-price logic for results feeding KER1 and each partner's pathway; Sections 7–15 cover IPR, agreements, projects, risks, obligations and conclusions.

## 2. Method and input

### 2.1 Update of the exploitation plan

The plan was issued as D8.1 (month 6), D8.5 (month 18, with preliminary KERs and key performance indicators, KPIs) and this final plan, which regroups the KERs into seven, uses D7.4 version 2.0, D7.6 and the technical brochure D8.3, and replaces D8.5's patent plan for a new hinge and float with trade secrets, copyright and a design-registration decision (Section 7.2).

### 2.2 Partner input

All seven beneficiaries submitted written exploitation summaries, reporting 27 results, between November 2025 and October 2026. Each partner's pathway follows what that partner has stated; the roles in KER1 (Section 5) and the protection positions (Section 7.2) are set out by the WP8 lead from the partners' inputs and the project's results.

### 2.3 KER1 business planning with the Horizon Results Booster

The coordinator applied for the Booster's Go-to-Market service on 30 September 2026. Since October 2026 Sunlit Sea has used it for KER1, mentored by META Group, and prepared the Booster's canvases, risk map, Business Opportunity Self-Assessment Tool (BOSAT), business plan and financial plan. At the review of 13 November the expert judged the files to be of very high quality and asked for no major changes; KER1 scored above 3 of 5 in all six BOSAT dimensions, lowest in intellectual property and finance. Because several partners are potential suppliers of Sunlit Sea, prices and named projects are given in aggregate only.

### 2.4 Assessment of the KERs

The proposal's five-step method was applied: identification of results and IPR management under the IPR management plans D1.4 and D1.8; evaluation of business potential, for KER1 through the Booster's risk map and BOSAT in place of a separate SWOT analysis (strengths, weaknesses, opportunities, threats); business models with the Booster's canvases; IPR analysis (Section 7); and business and exploitation plans (Sections 4 and 6). A KER gets a full business plan when it is a new product line for its owner, the core of the global solution, and the proposal asks for one; a KER that supplies or serves KER1 gets a pathway, an action plan and its role in KER1 (Section 3.3).

How the reach serves exploitation. The audiences match the KERs' markets: floating solar and offshore conferences reach the buyers and partners of KER1, KER3 and KER5; concrete, materials and monitoring conferences the users of KER2, KER4 and KER6; and life-cycle assessment forums the clients of KER7. The joint events with other EU floating PV projects and the CINEA clustering event link SUREWAVE with its CINEA portfolio.
