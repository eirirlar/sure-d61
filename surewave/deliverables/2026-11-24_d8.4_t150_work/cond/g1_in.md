## Executive summary

This is the consortium's plan for exploiting the results of SUREWAVE after the extended project ends on 31 December 2026. SUREWAVE developed an offshore floating photovoltaic (FPV) system that combines a floating breakwater in circular concrete with a redesigned FPV array, a modelling toolchain and a structural health management system. The project started at technology readiness level (TRL) 2–3 and aimed at TRL 4–5 for the system at its end (Grant Agreement, Annex 1). This plan rates the integrated system and Sunlit Sea's CONNECT platform at TRL 4–5. SINTEF's technological risk assessment (D7.2), summarised in the integrated sustainability assessment D7.6 (IFEU), rates the integrated concept at TRL 5 on MARIN's wave-basin tests and SINTEF's component tests. The proposal of September 2026 to EIC Transition, the European Innovation Council's scheme for taking research results towards the market, starts from TRL 4. Sunlit Sea rates CONNECT at TRL 4–5 in its Horizon Results Booster business plan (Section 4.1). ACCIONA's reduced-scale concrete breakwater prototype, monitored by Ceit, was exposed at sea for about nine months in the Port of Gijón, without the FPV array (Section 6.2).

Sunlit Sea leads work package 8 (WP8) on dissemination, exploitation and communication. As WP8 lead it proposed seven Key Exploitable Results (KERs), one anchored in each beneficiary, and mapped to them the 27 results that the partners reported. Of these, 23 form the seven KERs; four, on network, visibility and work in technical committees, are reported as impact (Section 3.1). KER1 Integrated floating PV system with breakwater is the global solution of the proposal, the product P1 *Offshore FPV plant*, to be marketed by Sunlit Sea AS. Sunlit Sea also led the work package on the global framework and specifications (WP2), in which it established the Stakeholder Advisory Board (D2.4). In the work package on the development of the floating PV solution (WP3), led by Clement Germany, it led the design of the floating PV components and of the global system (D3.1, D3.4). It led the economic assessment (D7.4) and redesigned the hinge (Section 6.1).

KER1 integrates the breakwater, its mooring and anchoring and the transition element designed by Clement Germany; these components remain Clement Germany's (Section 7.1).

The other six KERs are exploited by the partners that developed them: KER2 Circular concrete formulations (ACCIONA), KER3 Floating breakwater design (Clement Germany), KER4 Modelling and testing methods (SINTEF), KER5 Hydrodynamic models and basin testing (MARIN and SINTEF), KER6 Structural health monitoring and eddy-current sensing (Ceit), and KER7 Integrated sustainability assessment (IFEU). Section 3.4 traces each of the proposal's 11 expected results to what was delivered.

This plan gives each KER a level of detail set by the rule in Section 2.4:

- KER1 has a full business plan, prepared by Sunlit Sea with the Horizon Results Booster, the European Commission's free support service for the exploitation of project results.
  - First product: Sunlit Sea CONNECT, Sunlit Sea's Generation 2 floating platform and the core of KER1, for sheltered coastal sites. It replaces the Generation 1 aluminium float, which the SUREWAVE array used. Sunlit Sea started the Generation 2 redesign from the SUREWAVE hinge tests and models and continues it in the parallel project SuRE (Section 4.1). CONNECT goes first into Sunlit Sea's Norwegian reference plants from 2027 and is sold as a kit from 2028. At sheltered sites the plants need little or no breakwater, so the plant cost is mainly the FPV system. The first Norwegian reference plant, in a port in 2027, uses small wavebreakers (floating barriers bought from a wavebreaker supplier, against wind waves and vessel wakes) on its exposed side and gives the first field record of the platform behind protection.
  - Breakwater classes: where a site needs more protection, a breakwater is placed only on the exposed side, in one of three classes (light, medium, heavy). Sunlit Sea sized the classes from the SUREWAVE breakwater; Clement Germany is to confirm them. If the EIC Transition proposal is funded, a field demonstrator follows in 2028–2030. The EIC Transition proposal targets the first commercial site with a breakwater class after the EIC Transition project, which would end about 2030, and Sunlit Sea's financial plan models it in 2031, in its EIC Transition scenario. The site is expected at an inland lake or inner water exposed to wind waves, where other uses such as berths can carry part of the breakwater's cost. The plant owner buys the breakwater. Where Sunlit Sea delivers the full system as engineering-procurement-construction (EPC) contractor, it buys the breakwater and includes it in the EPC price.
  - Open sea: the full open-sea plant with a ring breakwater is the long-term configuration. Sunlit Sea plans an open-sea pilot proposal about 2031, after the exposed-site field record, and commercial plants from about 2035.
- The results that feed into KER1 (breakwater design, concrete, testing, modelling, monitoring and sustainability assessment) are described by their supply or service role, the type of agreement and the logic for a fair price.
- Every partner has a short exploitation pathway and a four-year action plan for 2027–2030. They follow the partners' written exploitation summaries: ACCIONA's of November 2025, Clement Germany's of September 2026 and the other five of September and October 2026.

The SUREWAVE economic assessment D7.4, led by Sunlit Sea (version 2.0), gives the D7.4 reference plant, a 10 MWp (megawatt-peak) open-sea plant in the Mediterranean with a conventional-concrete breakwater, a levelised cost of electricity (LCOE) of €0.076/kWh, an internal rate of return (IRR) of 15.6 % and a net present value (NPV) of €7.3M, with a payback of 10 years. In the Baltic Sea the plant is at break-even, and in the North Sea it is not viable at current costs (Section 4.6). The value-proposition tests (Section 4.3.1) show that buyers and lenders want reference plants and an independent design review before they buy. Sunlit Sea's financial plan shows that the first plants need investment support of about a quarter of their cost (Sections 4.3.1 and 4.7).

KER1's financial plan shows break-even in earnings before interest, tax, depreciation and amortisation (EBITDA) for Sunlit Sea in 2030 in all four funding scenarios (base, EIC Transition, EU upside and grant downside), provided the Norwegian reference plants come on time, and a positive net result from 2031. The base case assumes an extension of Sunlit Sea's work in SuRE, a separate Horizon Europe project that funds different work; the decision is pending at CINEA (Section 4.7). In the base case about 28 MWp is delivered over 2027–2031, of which about 7 MWp in 2030 and about 16 MWp in 2031. About 28 % of that volume is at sites with permits, named sites or leads; the rest is target volume. The plan raises €5.5M of equity in two rounds, in 2027 and 2029; the base case uses the first round and a small part of the second, and the rest is a buffer against delay and for growth beyond the plan.

The largest single risk is a one-year delay of the Norwegian reference plants. The risk map places four risks in its action zone: high cost outside the best sites, buyers waiting for a track record, dependency on key people, and the Norwegian reference plants a year late (Section 4.8). The route and targets differ from the proposal's 25 MW offshore pilot in 2025–2027 and its 1,240 MW installed by 2032. Section 4.10 sets out the changes and the reasons.

KER1 is protected by trade secrets and copyright. Patenting of the Generation 2 hinge and float was assessed with a patent attorney and not pursued, because of extensive prior art; a decision on design registration follows by the third quarter (Q3) of 2027, after the confirmation of contributions to the hinge redesign that Sunlit Sea requests from the partners concerned in Q1 2027, as the WP8 lead proposes (Sections 7.2 and 7.4). For four years after the SUREWAVE project, to 31 December 2030, each beneficiary uses its best efforts to exploit its results; for results not exploited by 31 December 2027, the beneficiaries use the Horizon Results Platform to find interested parties (Sections 6.8 and 13).

SUREWAVE met or exceeded 11 of the 16 dissemination and communication targets of the Grant Agreement and D8.5 in the first table of Section 2.5. The exceptions are peer-reviewed publications, conference workshops, infographics (the sixth is scheduled for 15 December 2026), the final summary video and a press release at the start of the project. Of the 22 further reach targets in the second table, three are met and one is met for 2024 only; three are partly met, five are not met, seven were not measured, no new PhD is recorded, and two are open or apply after the SUREWAVE project. One of the met targets is D8.5's target of at least two new commercial agreements or partnerships: it is met for partnerships, with SuRE and CIRCWIND, two Horizon Europe projects that adopt SUREWAVE results; commercial agreements are still to come.

Projects that started before SUREWAVE or run alongside it have used or use its results. MariSol (Research Council of Norway, 2020–2025, Sunlit Sea with the Institute for Energy Technology (IFE) and other partners) used the SUREWAVE nearshore models to calculate a wave-corrected performance ratio. In SuRE (Horizon Europe, September 2024 – August 2027), Sunlit Sea develops Generation 2 prototypes 4 and 5; SuRE grew out of contacts made through the SUREWAVE Stakeholder Advisory Board. SINTEF reports use of its methods in CIRCWIND (Horizon Europe), the Norwegian research centre SFI BLUES and the national project FlexiShape. A national project of SINTEF with the industrial partner GEOPON and Ceit's industrial transfers of its eddy-current technology are planned. The EIC Transition proposal for the version of KER1 for exposed sites, behind a directional breakwater, is under evaluation (Section 10).

## 1. Purpose and scope

The Grant Agreement describes D8.4 as a plan including: (1) Key Exploitable Result (KER) analysis and analysis of the intellectual property rights (IPR); (2) value innovation analysis and market analysis; (3) value proposition and value proposition testing; (4) development of business models, business plans and exploitation action plans. It is the output of Task 8.4, *Exploitation pathways and business plan*. Task 8.4 is to lay the basis for the exploitation agreements between technology developers and exploiters, and to set the ground for decisions on the business models for technology transfer and the rationale for establishing a fair price. Objective O8 of the proposal asks for a definitive business model and business plan for the global solution. The plan is to include the specific complexity of the offshore renewable energy market, IPR, the regulatory framework, market uptake barriers and the fostering of massive replication into the offshore energy sector. This plan starts replication at sheltered and nearshore coastal sites and moves to the open sea later; Section 4.10 gives the reasons.

The proposal names the global solution P1, *Offshore FPV plant*, as the product to be marketed by Sunlit Sea, with the other partners compensated through licences or royalties for the results that contribute to it. The Grant Agreement states that the individual results R2–R10 are exploited by the partners that developed them. R11, the integrated sustainability assessment framework, is listed with the other results but has no owner in the proposal's table of preliminary KERs; this plan places it with IFEU, which led the integrated assessment D7.6 (Section 3.4). Section 3 maps all results to the KERs.

This plan has three levels:

1. A map of all seven KERs: owner, readiness, exploitation route and relation to the global solution (Section 3).
2. A full business plan for KER1, including the financial plan (Section 4).
3. For the results that feed into KER1: the supply or service role, the type of agreement and the fair-price logic (Section 5). For every partner: an exploitation pathway and a four-year action plan (Section 6).

Section 2.5 reports the dissemination and communication results; Annex A gives the item-by-item record behind them. Sections 7–13 cover intellectual property; standardisation; exploitation agreements; earlier, parallel and follow-on projects; the use of Horizon Europe support; consortium-level risks; and the reporting and post-project obligations under the Grant Agreement, with the targets proposed for the annual exploitation reviews. Section 14 gives the timeline and Section 15 the conclusions. Annex B lists the SUREWAVE deliverables cited, with their lead beneficiaries and dissemination levels.

### 2.4 Assessment of the KERs

The proposal set a five-step exploitation method. This plan applied it as follows:

| Step in the proposal | How it was applied | Section |

Each KER is assessed against four criteria:

- the owner's stated commercial intent;
- market readiness;
- its relation to the global solution: core, supplying KER1, or standalone;
- the commitments in the proposal.

The criteria are applied as a rule, not as a score. A KER gets a full business plan when it is a new product line for its owner, is the core of the global solution, and the proposal asks for a business plan for it. A KER that supplies or serves KER1 within its owner's existing business gets an exploitation pathway, a four-year action plan and its role in KER1. Section 3.3 gives the assessment for each KER.

How the reach serves exploitation. The conference audiences match the markets of the KERs. The concrete, fracture, materials and monitoring communities include the potential users of KER2 Circular concrete formulations, KER4 Modelling and testing methods and KER6 Structural health monitoring and eddy-current sensing. The Discussion Forums on life-cycle assessment (LCA) in Wädenswil, ISNRE and EU PVSEC reach the potential clients of KER7 Integrated sustainability assessment. The joint events with BAMBOO and NaturSea-PV, and the CINEA clustering event, link SUREWAVE with the other EU-funded floating PV and solar projects of its CINEA portfolio.
