## 7. Intellectual property

### 7.1 Ownership and background

Results belong to the beneficiary that generated them, as set in the Grant Agreement and the Consortium Agreement. Joint ownership arises only where two or more beneficiaries make inseparable intellectual contributions to the same result; technical requirements, testing, modelling, validation, measurements and specialist feedback by other partners do not create it by themselves (D1.8, the IPR management plan). Each KER is owned as listed in Section 3; KER5 combines separate MARIN and SINTEF results, each owned by its developer. Background brought into the project remains with its owner, and the Grant Agreement requires the beneficiaries to identify it in a written agreement. The rules for background are set in the Consortium Agreement and the IPR management plan D1.4, and D1.8 records that background remained the property of the partner that owned or controlled it; among the background it names are the original floating PV technology and pre-existing hinge designs that Sunlit Sea brought in (D1.8).

For KER1, D1.8 records three positions. Sunlit Sea is the principal generating partner of the floating PV components and the modified hinge designs, with pre-existing background and new project results distinguished by design version. The floating breakwater design is Clement Germany's foreground, subject to confirmation of any documented inseparable contributions. The integrated offshore floating PV system configuration integrates separately owned components, methods, data and results, with no automatic transfer of component ownership. D1.8 adds that this ownership information should be confirmed by the result owners before any patent filing, registered-design application, licence, assignment, joint-development agreement or commercial deployment.

Applied to KER1, the WP8 lead proposes this split, to be confirmed with the documented contributors before the design-registration decision (Section 7.4). Sunlit Sea's SUREWAVE results are the design of the floating PV array and of the global system (D3.1, D3.4); the hinge redesign (Section 4.1); the nearshore wave and wind models, the wave-corrected performance ratio and the wave-loss module (Section 6.1); and the economic assessment and its cost model (D7.4). Its background is the Generation 1 float and hinge, used in the SUREWAVE reference design, and its earlier patent. The commercial carrier of these results is Sunlit Sea CONNECT, the Generation 2 platform with cast polyurethane floats. Sunlit Sea started its redesign in SUREWAVE, from the hinge tests and models of the SUREWAVE array, and continues it in the parallel project SuRE (Section 4.1). The breakwater, its mooring and anchoring and the transition element remain Clement Germany's (KER3); integrating them in KER1 transfers no ownership.

D1.8 (September 2026) records that no major ownership or access-right dispute requiring formal escalation was reported to project management.

## 15. Conclusions

SUREWAVE leaves seven Key Exploitable Results with named owners and routes. They rest on the project's technical record: the breakwater, mooring and anchoring design and the CFD study of Clement Germany; the circular concrete mixes of ACCIONA; the floating PV and global system design and the hinge redesign of Sunlit Sea; the component and connection tests at SINTEF; two 1:10-scale wave-basin campaigns at MARIN, of a 3 × 5 module array behind a breakwater section, and MARIN's DIFFRAC and mooring-optimisation work; the structural health management demonstrator of Ceit; the economic assessment of Sunlit Sea and the integrated sustainability assessment of IFEU; and the nine-month sea exposure of ACCIONA's reduced-scale concrete breakwater prototype in the Port of Gijón, without the PV array, monitored by Ceit (Section 6.2). The integrated system and the CONNECT platform are at TRL 4–5 (Section 4.1). The dissemination and communication results are in Section 2.5 and Annex A.

KER1, the integrated floating PV system with breakwater, is commercialised by Sunlit Sea, which led the global framework and specifications (WP2), the floating PV and global system design, the economic assessment D7.4 and WP8. KER1 integrates the breakwater, mooring and anchoring designed by Clement Germany (KER3). It starts with the floating platform at sheltered coastal sites, where the first Norwegian reference plants can be built at low capital cost, and moves to exposed sites with a directional breakwater and to the open sea as the field record and the cost base allow.

Protection on the exposed side starts with small wavebreakers at the first Norwegian reference plant in 2027. The breakwater classes follow at inland lakes and inner waters exposed to wind waves, where a light breakwater can share its cost with other uses. The EIC Transition proposal targets the first commercial site with a breakwater class after the EIC Transition project, which would end about 2030, and the financial plan models it in 2031. In the economic assessment (D7.4), the Mediterranean case of the 10 MWp D7.4 reference plant has the lowest cost of energy, €0.076/kWh, with an internal rate of return of 15.6 % (Section 4.6). The Mediterranean is the planned first open-sea market; the financing of a first plant there is still open.

Sunlit Sea's financial plan reaches EBITDA break-even in 2030 and a positive net result in 2031 if the Norwegian reference plants come on time, with €5.5M of equity raised in two rounds, in 2027 and 2029. A one-year delay of those plants turns 2031 EBITDA slightly negative; the first plants need investment support of about a quarter of their cost (Section 4.7).

The partners' results reach KER1 as engineering, supply, testing, modelling, monitoring and sustainability services, paid per contract under bilateral agreements still to be concluded, or under licence where a partner's design or tool is built or embedded by others (Section 5). Each partner also exploits its results outside floating PV: in concrete construction, marine infrastructure, aeronautics inspection, research and testing services, software licences, monitoring and sustainability studies. SuRE, MariSol, CIRCWIND, SFI BLUES and FlexiShape have used or are using SUREWAVE results; a national project with GEOPON and Ceit's industrial transfers are planned, and an EIC Transition proposal is under evaluation (Section 10). The WP8 lead proposes an annual exploitation review through 2030. Each beneficiary remains bound by the post-project obligations of the Grant Agreement (Section 13).
