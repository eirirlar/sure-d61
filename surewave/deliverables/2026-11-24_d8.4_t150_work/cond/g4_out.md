## 5. Results that feed into KER1: roles, agreements and fair price

The proposal foresaw that partners whose results contribute to the global solution, then a 50 MWp open-sea plant, would be compensated through licences or royalties. The first commercial product is the platform for sheltered sites, built on Sunlit Sea's own designs and nearshore models. From the first commercial site that needs one of the breakwater classes (Section 4.6.2), the breakwater is part of KER1. In the EIC Transition demonstrator, if the project is funded, the breakwater would be built within that project, under its grant and consortium agreements (Section 10).

SINTEF's connection tests and MARIN's basin tests informed the hinge redesign. D1.8 names Sunlit Sea as the principal generating partner of the modified hinge designs and asks the partners concerned to confirm the contributions before any patent, design registration, licence or commercial deployment; Sunlit Sea requests this confirmation and the WP8 lead proposes it for Q1 2027 (Section 7.4). Sunlit Sea's use of the partners' test results after the SUREWAVE project requires access rights under the Grant Agreement and the Consortium Agreement, which it requests as set out in Section 9.

The partner results reach KER1 in three ways:

- The breakwater (KER3, with KER2 materials) is a component of KER1 from the first commercial site that needs a breakwater class. Clement Germany designed the breakwater and its mooring and anchoring (D3.2, D3.3), and engineers, supplies and installs it. The plant owner buys the breakwater from Clement Germany; where Sunlit Sea delivers the full system as EPC contractor, Sunlit Sea buys it and includes it in the EPC price; and where Clement Germany leads a project, Sunlit Sea supplies the floating PV system as its subcontractor.
- Testing, modelling, monitoring and sustainability results reach KER1 as services, contracted per campaign or project.
- If Sunlit Sea or a third party were to build a breakwater from Clement Germany's design, or embed a partner's model or tool in Sunlit Sea's site-planning tool, a licence on fair and reasonable conditions, which may include royalties, would be agreed under the Consortium Agreement.

The WP8 lead proposes that each owner is paid where its result is used, for that use, and that the kit price carries no general partner charge. Each owner keeps the right to exploit its result outside floating PV. Services are priced per campaign at the owner's market rates; supplied components such as the breakwater per project on cost and market benchmarks; and a licence to build or embed a partner's design or tool as a share of the value it creates, as a royalty or a fixed fee. These bases apply the Grant Agreement's definition of fair and reasonable conditions: appropriate conditions, including possible financial terms or royalty-free conditions, that take into account the actual or potential value of the results or background and the scope, duration or other characteristics of the exploitation. Prices and terms would be set in bilateral agreements.

| KER | Role for KER1 | Agreement type | Payment basis |
|---|---|---|---|
| KER3 Floating breakwater design (Clement Germany) | Breakwater engineering, supply and installation, with its mooring and anchoring | Engineering and supply agreement, including the access rights needed, sought in 2027 | Per project, by one of the three supply routes above |
| KER2 Circular concrete formulations (ACCIONA) | Breakwater shell and core material | Own projects; where others cast, a licence of the mix know-how | Contract price; or licence fee or royalty plus consultancy |
| KER4 Modelling and testing methods (SINTEF) | Testing and qualification of new hinges | Contract research or test-service agreement | Per campaign |
| KER5 Hydrodynamic models and basin testing (MARIN, SINTEF) | Basin testing, mooring optimisation, array hydrodynamics | Service agreements; a DIFFRAC licence subscription if Sunlit Sea runs the software | Per campaign or subscription |
| KER6 Structural health monitoring and eddy-current sensing (Ceit) | Optional monitoring of concrete breakwaters in larger plants | Contract R&D for a customised SHMS | Per project |
| KER7 Integrated sustainability assessment (IFEU) | Life-cycle and sustainability evidence for buyers, lenders and permits | Commissioned studies | Per study |

Partner results enter KER1 at the breakwater, in qualification and in optional monitoring. Ownership of the breakwater, its mooring and anchoring is set out in Section 7.1.

## 6. Partner exploitation pathways and four-year action plans (2027–2030)

Each pathway sets out what the partner reported in its own exploitation summary: its results, their evidence and its exploitation routes. The value propositions of KERs 2–7 are those stated by their owners; they were not tested in the project, and each owner tests them in its own market. The WP8 lead set the period and indicator of each action, and rows that the partner's input does not contain are marked "added by the WP8 lead". The periods fall within the exploitation period, 1 January 2027 to 31 December 2030, over which the exploitation obligations of the Grant Agreement run (Section 13).

### 6.1 Sunlit Sea AS (KER1)

Sunlit Sea led WP2, the global framework and specifications, including the Stakeholder Advisory Board (D2.4). In WP3, led by Clement Germany, it led the design of the internal floating PV components (D3.1) and the coupled global system design (D3.4). It led the economic assessment D7.4 and published its cost model on Zenodo (doi 10.5281/zenodo.17534609). As WP8 lead it wrote D8.1 and this plan, and led D8.3 and D8.5 with SINTEF.

Sunlit Sea's SUREWAVE results in KER1 are the design of the floating PV array and of the global system (D3.1, D3.4), the hinge redesign, the nearshore wave and wind models with the wave-corrected performance ratio and the wave-loss module of its site-planning tool, and the D7.4 cost model. Their commercial carrier is Sunlit Sea CONNECT, the Generation 2 platform with cast polyurethane floats. The SUREWAVE array used Sunlit Sea's Generation 1 aluminium floats; Sunlit Sea started the Generation 2 redesign in SUREWAVE and continues it in the parallel project SuRE (Section 4.1). The business plan is Section 4 and the action plan Section 4.9.

### 6.2 ACCIONA (KER2)

Value proposition: circular concrete mixes for marine structures: a cellular concrete core in place of expanded polystyrene (CLC), a lighter structural concrete with recycled glass aggregate (LWAC), and a high-performance concrete that allows thinner shells (HPC). Market: ACCIONA's own construction projects and the clients of its consultancy.

ACCIONA led WP4 (D4.1–D4.3). CLC reached 365 kg/m³ with 25 kg of cement per cubic metre. D4.1 reports that the LWAC mix with CEM III cement (Sample 18) reached 68 MPa at a density near 2,000 kg/m³; ACCIONA's summary gives 1,950 kg/m³. HPC reached 128 MPa against a requirement of 120 MPa. D4.2 concludes that HPC is the most suitable shell material (with mechanical tests by SINTEF). At breakwater level, D7.3 (IFEU) finds no relevant greenhouse-gas saving against a conventional breakwater.

ACCIONA cast the reduced-scale prototype (D4.3) and exposed it for about nine months at the Port of Gijón (Task 6.5, the subject of D6.4). The joint preprint by ACCIONA, SINTEF and Ceit (Preprints 202611.1592) judges the short-term marine performance satisfactory and calls for longer open-sea exposure.

Route: use of the mixes in ACCIONA's construction projects, further research with partners, and consultancy.

### 6.4 SINTEF (KER4, KER5)

Value proposition: modelling, testing and qualification methods for floating structures, flexible connections and circular concrete. Market: floating PV manufacturers, developers of flexible marine structures, circular-concrete industry and research funders.

SINTEF coordinated SUREWAVE, with the IPR management plan D1.8, led the use-case definition (D2.1), the technical validation (WP6; D6.1, D6.3), the Surewave-ANN tool (D5.2), the sustainability framework task (Task 7.1, reported by IFEU in D7.1) and the technological risk assessment (D7.2). Its results include aerodynamic and hydrodynamic modelling (Energies 17(19) 4873, 2024), concrete testing with digital image correlation (DIC), and connection testing. The steel-wire-rope assemblies reached a mean of 402 kN against a rated 450 kN (D6.1). The breakwater–array connections reached a mean of 40.88 kN against 42 kN, which D6.1 finds aligned and the preprint gives as about 2.7 % below (Preprints 202611.1612); D6.1 places the protected rope–bracket interface from the third valid test, the preprint on the last two. The hinge tests are in Section 4.1.

### 6.5 MARIN (KER5)

Value proposition: hydrodynamic software, mooring optimisation and basin testing. Market: floating PV developers at every design stage.

MARIN extended DIFFRAC from about 100 to nearly 4,000 floating bodies. For a 2 MWp farm inside a ring of 32 breakwaters, the ring reduced averaged axial connector loads by up to 20 % and vertical loads by up to 50 % (Energies 19(7) 1609, 2026). MARIN validated DIFFRAC against the second SUREWAVE basin campaign and, in D5.1, against Sunlit Sea's Stadt Towing Tank tests, carried out outside SUREWAVE; hinge loads were not measured and could not be validated. In D5.1, genetic-algorithm optimisation reduced mooring-line mass by 34 % (Mediterranean) and 42 % (North Sea) for the 10 MWp ring and by 56 % and 59 % for the 2 MWp ring. Fairlead tensions rise in three of the six optimised designs, up to about 2.5 times the reference; the weighting between mass and tension was left open with Clement Germany. The basin campaigns tested a 1:10 3 × 5 array behind one breakwater section, the ring only simulated; the second used paired second-generation connectors (D6.2; Section 4.1).

Route: DIFFRAC licences and studies, mooring assessment and basin testing.

### 6.6 Ceit (KER6)

Value proposition: monitoring and structural health management tailored to the client's structures and, under investigation, earlier detection of corrosion in reinforced concrete. Market: end users and integrators in aeronautics and in civil and marine concrete monitoring.

Ceit led WP5 (D5.3–D5.7) and the sensing task of WP6. Its eddy-current sensor assesses a rebar from up to 5–6 cm outside the concrete and quantified material loss to about 0.17 mm in the laboratory (D5.4; Sensors 24(13) 4211, 2024). At Gijón, Ceit operated the corrosion and acceleration monitoring over the nine-month exposure; field detection of active corrosion was not demonstrated and a long-term corrosion trend has not yet been established (Preprints 202611.1592). The SHMS demonstrator (D5.6) combines Ceit's own stress fields with MARIN's loads and SINTEF's material predictions; validation with long-term field data is further work (D5.7).

Route: industrial transfer of the eddy-current technology (planned); contract R&D and consultancy; co-developed SHMS tools.

### 6.8 Horizon Results Platform and communication after the SUREWAVE project

If a beneficiary's results are not exploited by 31 December 2027, the Grant Agreement requires it to use the Horizon Results Platform to find interested parties, unless otherwise agreed with the granting authority. The WP8 lead proposes to apply this to each result, with each beneficiary reporting at the first annual review, by the end of Q3 2027, and unexploited results entered by their owners in Q1 2028; Sunlit Sea uploads the KER1 profile in Q1 2027. For communication, the WP8 lead proposes that surewave.eu stays online until at least 31 December 2030, kept by Sunlit Sea, that the LinkedIn page and the Zenodo community stay open, and that each beneficiary reports its communication at the annual review (Section 13).
