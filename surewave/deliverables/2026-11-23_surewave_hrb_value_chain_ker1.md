# Value chain analysis — KER1 Sunlit Sea CONNECT floating photovoltaic platform

Horizon Results Booster G2M input · Sunlit Sea AS · 23 November 2026 · confidential: contains Sunlit Sea's costs and prices, for the Booster mentors only

This analysis maps who does what in a CONNECT deployment, from site screening to operation and sale, what each step depends on, and where the complexity and the single points of failure sit. It follows the mentor's note on the key-person risk in the risk map: a value chain analysis helps manage the complexity of deployments. The results sharpen the key-person and partnership interventions in the risk map and the roles and actions in the exploitation roadmap.

The chain differs between the two phases of the business model in the financial plan:

- Phase 1 (2027–2029, deliveries to 2031): Sunlit Sea develops and delivers the first Norwegian plants as developer and EPC contractor to the plant companies, and owns the reference plant at Skien havn.
- Phase 2 (mainly from 2030; first kits from 2028): Sunlit Sea sells the kit with the solar panel included, plus site engineering, to developers and contractors who build the plants.

## 1. The chain, step by step

| Step | What happens | Phase 1: who | Phase 2: who | Depends on |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Site screening and design basis | Wave and wind conditions, need for wavebreakers or a breakwater, yield, layout | Sunlit Sea with its planning tool (SUREWAVE nearshore models); Prosolar for local yield simulation | Sunlit Sea as paid engineering for the developer | Site data; the planning tool, which only Sunlit Sea runs |
| 2. Permits and grid | Harbour and municipal permits, environmental acceptance of the mooring, grid capacity | Prosolar and Sunlit Sea with the host; municipality, harbour authority, environment agency, local grid company | Developer or local contractor | Permit timelines; host's water-area rights |
| 3. Offtake and financing | PPA with the host, Enova investment support, project debt, owner equity | Plant company (host's project company) with Sunlit Sea as developer; Skien havn is planned as Sunlit Sea's own plant (a joint venture with the local contractor, or the contractor investing first, are open options); Sunlit Sea takes a minority share at Gunneklevfjorden | Developer or plant owner | Investment support of about a quarter of the cost of a first-of-a-kind plant; a PPA price the host accepts |
| 4. Product design and qualification | Design freeze, hinge generations, DNV design-basis assessment, tests | Sunlit Sea (design); IFE (structural simulation); SINTEF and MARIN (tests); DNV (design basis) | Same; the developer relies on the DNV assessment | The CEO holds the design and casting knowledge |
| 5. Components | Polyurethane casting, bottom plates, panels, module integration | Casting vendor in China (Tongge) and in-house 3D-printed moulds for pilot volumes; Haitai Solar (frame design); metal suppliers; module market; Metsolar (module integration) | The lowest-cost qualified series source, in Europe where it is cost-competitive (not yet qualified); same panel and plate supply | Prototype casting in China today, with the cost basis quoted ex works China or Lithuania; no source qualified for series production; panels are about half of the kit cost and follow the module market; moulds owned by Sunlit Sea |
| 6. Kit packing and logistics | Floats, hinges, plates and panels packed per plant and shipped to site | Direct from vendors, coordinated by Sunlit Sea; packing equipment bought in 2027 | Packing in Lithuania from 2027–2028, Metsolar as candidate | A packing partner not yet contracted |
| 7. Assembly, launch and mooring | Assembly at a local quay, launch, mooring, electrical works, wavebreakers or breakwater | Local contractors (Prosolar for electrical works; a marine contractor for mooring) under Sunlit Sea's supervision; wavebreakers at Skien havn from a supplier still to be chosen | Developer's contractors, trained and supported by Sunlit Sea; breakwater classes from Clement Germany from about 2030 | Sunlit Sea's site supervision; a few qualified installation crews |
| 8. Operation and maintenance | Inspection, spare hinges, monitoring, yield reporting | Plant owner with Prosolar; Sunlit Sea support and spares; Ceit monitoring as an option for larger plants | Owner or its operator; Sunlit Sea support and spares | Field data flowing back to Sunlit Sea for the reference record |
| 9. Sale or exit | Skien havn kept as reference while its two-year permit runs; after that the permit is extended, or the plant is sold, moved or written down; Sunlit Sea's minority share at Gunneklevfjorden held or sold | Sunlit Sea | Not applicable | Operating record |

A phase 1 plant involves eight to ten parties: host and offtaker, plant company, Enova, bank, local contractor, marine contractor, casting vendor, module supplier, DNV and Sunlit Sea. As EPC contractor, Sunlit Sea carries the interfaces between them. In phase 2 the developer carries most interfaces, and Sunlit Sea's part narrows to steps 1, 4, 5 and 6 and to support in steps 7 and 8.

## 2. Where the complexity and the single points of failure sit

| Point | Why it matters | Effect if it fails | Intervention |
|---|---|---|---|
| Two key people at Sunlit Sea | The CEO holds the design and casting knowledge; the technical lead holds the planning tool, sales, projects and administration. In phase 1 both are on the critical path of steps 1, 3, 4, 5 and 7. In 2027, before the first hires, the team is 2.0 FTE plus 1.5 FTE funded by the SuRE extension for the Skien havn demonstrator | All milestones slip; a one-year delay lowers the net result over 2027–2031 by about €3.6M | 2027: the SuRE-extension staff take steps 5 and 7 at Skien havn; design, casting and planning-tool procedures documented by Q2 2027; deputies by Q2 2027: IFE for step 4 (structural design), Prosolar for the yield part of step 1. After round 1, hires in the order the chain needs them: a project engineer and a project manager in early 2028 (steps 2, 3, 7), a sales lead in mid-2028, a production and quality engineer in mid-2029 (steps 5, 6), application engineers in 2030–2031 (step 1 for kit buyers) |
| No casting source qualified for series production | Casting runs at a prototype supplier in China; trade, freight or quality problems stop deliveries | Gunneklevfjorden delayed; kit ramp-up at risk | In-house moulds for pilot volumes; a series source qualified before the first megawatt-scale order, in Europe where it is cost-competitive; moulds owned by Sunlit Sea, so production can move |
| Float production for Gunneklevfjorden | About 4,400 floats are cast between the winter inspection at Skien havn (Q1–Q2 2028) and commissioning (Q3 2028), with pilot moulds | Commissioning slips into 2029, with the EPC revenue | Casting capacity confirmed with the vendor before the EPC contract is signed (Q4 2027); commissioning date in the EPC contract set from that capacity |
| One local contractor for both Norwegian projects | Prosolar supports permits, electrical works and operation at Skien havn and Gunneklevfjorden, and holds the Skien havn permit | Permit or installation delays at both sites at once | Written agreement on roles and the permit at Skien havn in Q1 2027, which also settles the ownership option; a second electrical contractor invited in the Gunneklevfjorden subcontract tender (2027); a second contractor qualified for Storavatnet |
| Plant financing outside Sunlit Sea's control | Each phase 1 plant needs a PPA, Enova support and debt in the plant company | No investment decision at Gunneklevfjorden; no multi-megawatt reference | Support case prepared with Enova; investment decision and PPA as decision gate 2 in the financial plan; hiring tied to the gate |
| Wavebreakers and breakwaters | The Skien havn wavebreakers have no supplier or price yet; the breakwater classes depend on Clement Germany | Skien havn cost or timing; exposed sites delayed after 2030 | Wavebreaker quote before the Skien havn order; bilateral agreement with Clement Germany in 2027 |
| Field data back to Sunlit Sea | The reference record is the main sales argument; it depends on owners and operators reporting yield and inspections | Weaker case for kit sales and round 2 | Data access written into PPAs, EPC and kit contracts; measured yield published |
| Kit packing partner not yet contracted | Phase 2 depends on shipping a complete kit per plant | Kit sales from 2028 handled case by case at higher cost | Packing with Metsolar or another partner in Lithuania under a framework agreement in 2027–2028, ahead of the first kits |

## 3. Metsolar as a value-chain option

Metsolar in Lithuania makes PV modules, has engineering and production capability, and works with Sunlit Sea on panel integration in SuRE. Three set-ups are possible:

| Set-up | What Metsolar does | For | Against |
|---|---|---|---|
| Subcontractor (base case) | Module integration and kit packing; later casting or assembly under contract | No capital or governance needed; can start in 2027–2028; production stays flexible | Each step contracted separately; less commitment on capacity |
| Production joint venture | A jointly owned company that casts, assembles and packs CONNECT kits in Lithuania | European production and capacity commitment; production load moves off Sunlit Sea's key people | Needs capital, a shareholders' agreement and volume that does not exist before 2030; dilutes control over casting know-how |
| Production licence | Metsolar produces and sells kits in markets Sunlit Sea does not serve | Reach without own capital | Needs protectable IP and a licence model; premature before design registration and freedom-to-operate review |

Recommendation: a framework agreement with Metsolar as subcontractor for module integration and kit packing in 2027–2028, with an option to extend it to casting. A joint venture is assessed at the series-production decision in 2029, together with round 2 and the second mould set, when volume and the reference record exist. This follows the mentor's advice to work with partners first and formalise later with expert support.

## 4. Where the margin sits

| Step | Phase 1 margin for Sunlit Sea | Phase 2 margin for Sunlit Sea |
|---|---|---|
| Design, site engineering (steps 1, 4) | Development and engineering fees | Engineering at about 4 % of plant cost, about 70 % gross before staff |
| Components and kit (steps 5, 6) | Inside the EPC price | Kit gross margin about 44–49 % |
| Delivery (steps 2, 3, 7) | EPC margin about 12–14 %, about €0.09 per watt, with the full delivery risk | Carried by the developer |
| Operation (step 8) | Power sales at Skien havn | Spare parts and support |

Most of the margin sits in design, the moulds and the kit, which Sunlit Sea keeps in both phases. Delivery earns a thin margin for the most risk, so it is limited to the three Norwegian reference projects and passed to developers in phase 2.

## 5. Consequences for the risk map and the exploitation roadmap

Risk map:

- Key-person dependency: rated higher (probability 7), since both key people are on the critical path of most phase 1 steps; the intervention names the 2027 cover, the deputies, the hires per step and their timing, and the documented procedures.
- Industrialisation: the Metsolar framework agreement is added, with the joint venture assessed in 2029.
- New partnership risk: one local contractor on both Norwegian projects.
- Float production capacity for Gunneklevfjorden, under the industrialisation risk.

Exploitation roadmap:

- Roles name the step of the chain each partner covers.
- Actions add the written agreement with the local contractor and the permit at Skien havn (Q1 2027), the wavebreaker quote before the Skien havn order, the Metsolar framework agreement (2027–2028), the documented procedures (2027), and data access in the plant contracts.
