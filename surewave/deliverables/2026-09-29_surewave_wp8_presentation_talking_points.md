## 9. D8.2 — societal needs and engagement (delivered M36, 2025-09-30)

- IFEU-led with Sunlit Sea support. This is the Task 8.1 output — the SSH ("Social Sciences and Humanities") strand that Horizon Europe requires the project to carry.
- Purpose: understand what deploying offshore FPV means for people — not just measure environmental and economic impact.
- Method: SWOT-based social-stakeholder engagement (local marine industries, citizens, associations targeted at potential deployment locations), moderated ratings across strengths, opportunities, weaknesses, threats. Rated impacts by importance.
- Headline results:
    - Strengths / opportunities considered most important: renewable-energy contribution, EU energy sovereignty, coastal jobs, industrial spillover.
    - Weaknesses / threats flagged: visual impact, marine-space conflict with fishing and navigation, uncertainty in permitting, long-term maintenance workforce risk.
- What we did with it: informed the social sustainability section of D7.6 (Integrated Sustainability Assessment) and the "path to market" framing in the technical brochure.

## 14. Lessons learned

- KPIs need to be set against the deliverable timeline, not just against project duration — the "50% of publications by M18" bar was never realistic because the technical results weren't there yet.
- Video content earned engagement disproportionate to effort. Wideangle partnership was the highest-leverage decision we made in the second half.
- Stakeholder Advisory Board delivered more than expected — one SAB relationship spawned a follow-on EU project. Worth investing in SAB quality over SAB size.
- Coordination across seven partners for consistent messaging is a real workload. Style guide + monthly cadence made it manageable; a shared asset library would have made it easier.
- Confidential vs public dissemination boundary — for a project with commercial partners, this needs to be clarified per deliverable at start, not at end. We had to revisit some framings late.
