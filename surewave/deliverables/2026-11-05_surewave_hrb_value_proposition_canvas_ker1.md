# Value Proposition Canvas — KER1 Integrated offshore floating photovoltaic system

Horizon Results Booster G2M-A workshop input · Sunlit Sea AS · 5 November 2026

Key Exploitable Result: Sunlit Sea's offshore floating photovoltaic platform (Generation 2), commercial name Sunlit Sea CONNECT. Canvas completed per the G2M Value Proposition Canvas template — one customer segment per canvas, with customer profile (jobs / pains / gains) on one side and value map (products and services / pain relievers / gain creators) on the other.

## Customer Segment

Utility-scale renewable energy developers and independent power producers developing 1–10 MW offshore photovoltaic capacity in European coastal waters during the 2027–2030 window. This is the segment identified as highest commercial readiness in the companion Market Definition Canvas. Dedicated Value Proposition Canvases for the offshore-wind co-location segment and the coastal industrial off-taker segment are prepared separately; this canvas focuses on the primary segment per the template's one-segment-at-a-time rule.

## Customer profile

### Customer jobs

The set of activities this customer is trying to do, drawn from the abstracted job statement in the companion Market Definition Canvas.

Functional jobs:

- Grow a renewable generation portfolio in coastal markets where onshore solar is blocked by land scarcity and permit friction.
- Build out multi-technology offshore capacity (wind plus solar) to improve portfolio-level firm capacity and reduce intermittency risk.
- Deliver committed project schedules to investment committees, lenders and offtakers under investment-grade due diligence.
- Meet offtake-price benchmarks that clear corporate power purchase agreement or regulated-market contracts.
- Operate the asset over 20–25 years with predictable operational expenditure and an insurable risk profile.

Social jobs:

- Position the company as a technology leader in offshore renewables, visible on the European offshore floating photovoltaic market as it emerges.
- Signal portfolio diversification to institutional investors increasingly focused on offshore renewables beyond fixed-bottom wind.

Emotional jobs:

- Avoid the career risk of being first on an unproven technology without a validated design basis.
- Avoid the stakeholder risk of a visible offshore failure in year one of operation.

### Pains

What annoys the customer before, during and after getting the job done.

- Existing floating photovoltaic designs are adapted from reservoirs and freshwater ponds and fail under offshore wave loading; the customer sees no credible product at commercial scale offshore.
- No bankable design basis: certification bodies have no established standard for offshore floating photovoltaic; underwriters price technology risk conservatively or decline cover.
- Capital cost per installed watt is high relative to onshore solar; the economics only close in specific sea-basin and irradiation combinations that the customer's internal analysts rarely have the data to evaluate.
- Site development is long and expensive: marine spatial planning, environmental impact assessment, grid connection application and cultural-heritage screening each take months to years, and most developers do not have offshore-marine permitting teams in-house.
- Operations and maintenance uncertainty: offshore photovoltaic has no track record for condition-based maintenance; owners fear an operational expenditure profile that erodes the project economics.
- Supply chain immaturity: components (floats, hinges, mooring transition elements) are not available through the standard European solar supplier base; dependency on a single technology provider is a procurement red flag.
- Competitive signal confusion: a growing number of offshore floating photovoltaic concepts exist in Europe at different technology readiness levels, with no shared frame for comparing them. The customer cannot tell credible from marketing.

### Gains

The outcomes and benefits the customer expects to gain when the job is done well.

- A validated design basis the customer's technical due diligence team can accept, backed by independent third-party testing.
- A cost case that clears the customer's offtake-price benchmark — target levelised cost of energy below €70/MWh at 10 MW Mediterranean scale.
- Portfolio-diversification value: non-correlated solar generation that complements wind portfolios and improves firm-capacity revenue.
- Demonstrator-stage evidence from comparable sites that the customer can reference in investment-committee memos and lender briefings.
- A deployment path that uses existing offshore infrastructure (substations, grid connections, mooring corridors) where available, reducing the incremental infrastructure bill.
- A maintenance model aligned with the customer's existing offshore-asset operations rather than requiring a new offshore-photovoltaic-specific workforce.
- A supplier relationship that credibly scales to multiple projects per year rather than a one-off pilot provider.

## Value map — Sunlit Sea CONNECT offshore floating photovoltaic platform

### Products and services

The products and services offered, and the components of the solution needed to help this customer segment complete their jobs.

- Sunlit Sea CONNECT platform: each float combines an aluminium bottom plate and a cast polyurethane frame that carries a standard commercial 710–740 Wp glass-glass photovoltaic panel; panels are decoupled from float manufacture and dropped in at deployment. Floats connect along the full edge through Generation-3 polyurethane hinges. Mooring and transition elements complete the system. Deployed standalone or behind a floating breakwater.
- Site-specific engineering and system integration services drawing on the validated SUREWAVE modelling toolchain (coupled wave-structural-electrical model chain developed and validated under Grant Agreement 101083342).
- Original equipment manufacturer sales evolving toward a project-development-and-supply model: Sunlit Sea supplies the floating photovoltaic subsystem, procures concrete breakwater units from qualified suppliers where applicable, and integrates through subcontracted marine construction partners.
- Structural health monitoring platform integrated into the offering (SUREWAVE partner subsystem owned by Ceit), providing condition-based maintenance data to the owner.
- Reference deployments from Sunlit Sea's Generation 1 commercial fleet (Mannheim Cablepark 60 kWp in operation since 2023; Skiftestjørna 105 kWp in operation since autumn 2024) as evidence of commercial-scale delivery capability.

### Pain relievers

How the products and services alleviate specific customer pains.

- Validated design basis: SUREWAVE has validated the coupled model chain at Technology Readiness Level 4–5 against wave-tank and component-scale open-sea testing. Design basis aligned against DNV ST-C108, ST-E309 and RP-0584 pending a product-specific certification framework. Addresses the "no credible offshore product" pain head-on.
- Generation-3 polyurethane hinge raised the ultimate load capacity of the critical connection from 14 kN to 37 kN, validated through SINTEF mechanical and failure-analysis testing under SUREWAVE. The distributed full-edge hinge geometry removes the corner-load concentration that caused snapping-load failures on Generation 1 in laboratory and wave-tank testing. Direct consortium-internal technology-transfer sequence with SINTEF as external validator.
- Mediterranean 10 MW cost case closes: levelised cost of energy €0.062/kWh, internal rate of return 10.7 %, 8-year payback (SUREWAVE Deliverable 7.4). Clears mainstream offtake-price benchmarks; comparable to or better than published floating offshore wind levelised cost of energy numbers.
- Supply chain Europe-grounded on the heavy components: cast polyurethane, aluminium bottom plate and concrete breakwater are structurally European by value density. Standard glass-glass photovoltaic panels remain globally supplied, as is the industry norm.
- Integrated structural health monitoring platform turns operations and maintenance from a worry into a data product the owner uses for condition-based maintenance planning.
- Follow-on vehicles already underway signal scalable supply: Sunlit Sea develops Generation 2 prototypes 4 and 5 under the SuRE Horizon Europe project (Grant Agreement 101135567); SINTEF technology reuse in CIRWIND, SFI BLUES, Mikroflexishape and GEOPON extends the capability base. The customer is not buying a research artefact.
- Reference pipeline the customer can visit: Vollsfjorden (EV PowerCharge offtake path), Gunneklevfjorden (Enova forprosjekt-funded, Hærøya Industripark host), Skien havn (350 kWp, Aaltvedt Betong power purchase agreement path), Storavatnet (long-term Haugaland Næringspark site).

### Gain creators

How the products and services generate the desired customer gains.

- Cast polyurethane float-frame architecture gives a manufacturability path that scales cleanly from 3D-printed moulds at pilot scale to industrial casting at commercial volume, with the aluminium bottom plate produced on standard metalwork equipment. Avoids the bespoke composite marine fabrication of most offshore floating photovoltaic competitors and the plastic / high-density polyethylene pond-pattern geometry that inland floating photovoltaic incumbents adapt upward. Decoupling the standard commercial photovoltaic panel from the float lets Sunlit Sea ride the ongoing cost reduction of mainstream 710–740 Wp glass-glass panels rather than carrying a custom-panel cost base.
- Open-sea survivability demonstrated at component scale (SUREWAVE Deliverable 6.3). Combined with the SuRE follow-on prototype path, the platform is on track for Technology Readiness Level 6 pilot deployment ahead of competing offshore floating photovoltaic systems still at component-testing stage.
- Floating-wind co-location ready: the hinge-connected cast-polyurethane-and-aluminium geometry integrates into existing offshore wind leases with shared substation and shared mooring corridors.
- Portfolio-level firm-capacity gain: adding solar generation to an offshore wind portfolio raises portfolio capacity factor and improves grid-service revenue. Argued quantitatively for customers who already operate a wind portfolio.
- Serviceable hinge-replacement: the Generation-3 polyurethane hinge is a defined subassembly that can be inspected and replaced at the end of its useful life without recovering the full array. Operations model aligned with other offshore floating-asset lifecycles.
- Visible European strategic autonomy case: concrete-and-aluminium offshore solar is a layer of the renewable energy value chain that is structurally European. Positions the customer as participating in that build-out.
- Commissioning from standard quays: the hinge-connected floating array deploys without specialist offshore vessels, shortening the commissioning window and reducing marine-contractor exposure compared to concepts requiring specialised offshore installation craft.

## Fit

Sunlit Sea's offshore floating photovoltaic platform, with its Generation-3 polyurethane hinge and pressed-aluminium float architecture, resolves the customer-side pains that have blocked offshore floating photovoltaic adoption to date — no validated design basis, no bankable cost case, no credible scale-up path — through a combination of SUREWAVE-validated technology, Mediterranean-scale economics that clear the customer's offtake benchmarks, European supply chain anchoring on the heavy cast-polyurethane and aluminium components, and a pilot-to-commercial path already underway through the SuRE follow-on project. The fit is strongest for utility-scale renewable energy developers and independent power producers with 1–10 MW Mediterranean site opportunities in the 2027–2030 commercial window.
