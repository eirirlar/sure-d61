# Lean Canvas — KER1 Sunlit Sea CONNECT floating photovoltaic platform

Horizon Results Booster G2M input · Sunlit Sea AS · 14 November 2026 · Iteration #1

The canvas summarises the exploitation strategy at a glance. Each box is kept short enough to fit the G2M Lean Canvas template; the characterisation table and the exploitation roadmap carry the detail.

## KER name

KER1 — Sunlit Sea CONNECT floating photovoltaic platform for nearshore and sheltered coastal waters

## Problem

- Coastal industry and ports need local renewable power but have no free land
- Floating solar built for lakes fails in sea waves
- Lenders need a trusted design basis and predictable maintenance cost over 20+ years

### Alternative solutions

- Grid power or a power purchase agreement from elsewhere
- Rooftop or ground-mounted solar on scarce land
- Calm-water floating solar in the most sheltered spots
- Offshore wind: large scale, long lead time
- Offshore solar concepts still at pilot stage

## Solution

- Cast polyurethane floats on flat bottom plates, one standard panel each
- Flexible full-edge hinges: the array follows the waves
- Breakwater only on the exposed sides, sized per site with our planning tool
- Assembly and launch from ordinary quays

## Key metrics

- MWp in operation and under contract
- Measured yield against simulation (±10 %)
- Cost per watt of the floating system
- Hinge load capacity and fatigue life
- Sites in the pipeline, power purchase agreements signed

## Unique Value Proposition

Floating solar for coastal water that bends with the waves.

Our SUREWAVE-validated nearshore floating solar platform helps coastal industry and renewable developers who want to grow renewable power where onshore land is blocked, by matching the design to the real wave conditions of each site and by bringing the cost of electricity down with standard solar panels and simple manufacturing, unlike calm-water floating solar that fails in waves and offshore concepts that need costly full protection or specialist vessels.

## Unfair advantage

- Hinge tested and improved under SUREWAVE (ultimate load 14 kN to 37 kN)
- Generation 1 in commercial operation since 2023
- Site-adaptation know-how and planning tool
- Norwegian reference sites in development

## Channels

- Rixen Cableway, sales partner in Germany
- Industrial park owners and site hosts
- Enova and EU demonstration projects
- Clusters, Horizon Results Platform, industry events

## Customer segments

- Coastal industry and ports with local power demand and no land
- Developers and independent power producers building 0.3–10 MW plants on coastal water
- Nordics first, then the Baltic and the Mediterranean

- Hærøya Industripark, Gunneklevfjorden (3.2 MWp)
- Haugaland Næringspark, Storavatnet (3.2 MWp)
- Storugns, Gotland (~1 MWp)
- Rixen Cableway, Germany

## Cost structure

- Development and testing: prototypes, test campaigns, DNV design-basis assessment
- Production: moulds, casting, bottom plates, module integration with a European partner
- Distribution: transport, quay assembly, mooring and breakwater
- People: small core team plus partners; project development and permits
- Equity in own reference plants until they are sold

## Revenue streams

- Platform supply per watt (€0.30–0.40/W for the floating system)
- Site engineering fees (8–12 % of project capital cost)
- Power sales under power purchase agreements from own plants
- Sale of operating plants to long-term owners
