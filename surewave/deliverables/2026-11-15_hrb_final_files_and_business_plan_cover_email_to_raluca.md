Changes to the exploitation files after your feedback:

- Characterisation table: first evidence for the end-user problems, the social and environmental benefits, and the support we need to reach more customers.
- Exploitation roadmap: a timeline for each action and the technology readiness level each technical step leads to. CONNECT reaches TRL 6 for sheltered sites in 2028, after the first winter at Skien havn, and for exposed sites with a directional breakwater at the end of the EIC Transition project. Revenue is now split by role: on our own plants we act as an independent power producer and earn from selling power under power purchase agreements, while kit supply and engineering are sold to plants owned by others.
- Risk map: risks 3 and 4 moved to the IP/legal and technology categories, as you suggested. We added why we prefer European production, figures on breakwater cost from the SUREWAVE economic assessment, alternative materials, a brief funding strategy, and the value chain analysis under the key-person risk. The ratings are unchanged.

In the business plan, three points where your view would help before the December workshop:

- Market size: published forecasts cover floating solar in general (8.7 GW installed worldwide at the end of 2024, 77 GW forecast for 2033). There is no reliable figure for nearshore floating solar, so the obtainable market is sized bottom-up from our own pipeline. Is that acceptable for the Booster, or should we add a top-down estimate?
- Business model: the plan describes how the model changes with scale, from owning our first plants, to supplying kits, then EPC and later a larger portfolio. We would like to test that sequence with you in the financial plan.
- Customer evidence: it comes from our early adopters and the projects in development. A structured round of customer interviews is planned for early 2027.

Sections 1 and 6–9 and the financial plan will follow after the workshop, as agreed.
