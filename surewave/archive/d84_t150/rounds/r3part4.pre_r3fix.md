### 4.7 Financial plan

Breakwater classes are bought by the plant owner. Where Sunlit Sea delivers the full system as EPC contractor, it buys the breakwater and includes it in the EPC price (Section 5). The Norwegian sites that Sunlit Sea delivers as EPC contractor need no breakwater class on current site data, and kit buyers buy any breakwater themselves as plant owners, so the plan has no breakwater class in its revenue or costs. Two sensitivities test this. The first reference plant, which Sunlit Sea owns, uses small wavebreakers on its exposed side. They are not yet quoted and are tested at about €0.1M. That lowers cash by about €0.1M and the result over the period by about €0.05M, since the cost is depreciated over the plant's life. If the two larger Norwegian sites needed a light breakwater on one side and it were not priced into the EPC contracts, the result over the period would fall by about €0.9M. At sites that need a breakwater class, Sunlit Sea's gain is the kit and engineering for a site that would otherwise be closed to floating PV.

Conditions for the plan:

- Kit price: the plan's kit gross margin target is 40–50 %, and the plan meets it at today's kit price and production cost. Cost reductions from series moulds, a lighter bottom plate, casting near the markets where that lowers cost, and volume purchasing give room to lower the price where buyers need it, or to absorb a breakwater at more exposed sites.
- Investment support for the first plants: at first-of-a-kind cost, the first plants need investment support of about a quarter of their cost to match the host's alternative supply. Support of that order is sought from national schemes for first-of-a-kind energy technology. Without support, plants built from the kit are competitive where power costs more than at the first Norwegian sites. Examples are industry with higher power prices, sites limited by grid capacity, and southern Europe, where the yield is higher. In markets with poor solar irradiation, national support for floating solar is assumed.
- References and volume: the plan is most sensitive to the timing of the reference plants and to kit volume and margin in 2030–2031.
  - If the reference plants and kit sales come one year later, the net result over 2027–2031 falls by about €3.6M, 2031 EBITDA is about −€0.1M, and cash falls below zero (lowest year-end cash about −€0.3M). About €0.5M more equity keeps a cash buffer of €0.2M. The second round then moves to 2030.
  - If the 10 MW-class contract is not won, the net result over the period falls by about €2.4M and 2031 EBITDA is about €0.7M; cash stays positive.
  - Kit volume 30 % lower in 2030–2031 moves 2031 EBITDA by about €0.9M; a lower kit price or a higher kit cost, within the range tested, moves it by about €0.4M.
  - A combined commercial downside (one year later, kit volume 30 % lower and no further kit cost reduction) gives a net result over the period of about −€5.6M, against −€1.4M in the base case, and a 2031 EBITDA of about −€0.6M. It needs about €1.0M more equity, or the 2029–2031 hires and the second mould set are held back.

All of these depend on the first plants operating as designed, and the plan ties hiring and investment to four decision gates set by those plants:

1. First equity round closed (Q1 2027): order the first reference plant and start hiring.
2. Investment decision by the plant company at the first multi-megawatt site (Q4 2027): sign the EPC contract and commit the minority share; if the decision slips, the 2028 hires are delayed.
3. First reference plant through its first winter, with loads and yield within design values (Q1–Q2 2028): release float production for the multi-megawatt plant and order the industrial moulds.
4. The multi-megawatt plant in operation for a year, and a letter of intent for the 10 MW-class contract (2029): raise the second round, order the second mould set and hire the application engineers.

The feasibility of the intervention is rated on the same scale. ## 5. Results that feed into KER1: roles, agreements and fair price

The proposal foresaw that partners whose results contribute to the global solution, then a 50 MWp open-sea plant, would be compensated through licences or royalties. The first commercial product is the platform for sheltered sites. It is built on Sunlit Sea's own designs and on Sunlit Sea's nearshore wave and wind models, developed in SUREWAVE from ERA5 and GEBCO data. From the first site that needs one of the breakwater classes, the breakwater is part of KER1. That site is targeted about 2030 in the EIC Transition proposal and modelled in 2031 in the financial plan; it comes in 2029 if the EUPI-PV nearshore demonstration goes ahead (Section 10). The small wavebreakers at the first reference plant (a Norwegian port, 2027) are bought for that plant and are part of its cost, which Sunlit Sea carries as owner. SINTEF's connection tests and MARIN's basin tests informed the hinge redesign. The redesign is Sunlit Sea's result; testing, modelling and technical feedback did not in themselves create joint ownership of it (D1.8). Its use of the partners' test results is covered by access rights under the Grant Agreement and the Consortium Agreement. Sunlit Sea requests them in writing from SINTEF and MARIN in Q1 2027, before the design freeze; the Grant Agreement's deadline for such requests is 31 December 2027 (Section 9).

The partner results reach KER1 in three ways:

- The breakwater (KER3, with KER2 materials) is a component of KER1 from the first site that needs a breakwater class. This covers the light and medium classes for inland and nearshore sites as well as the exposed-site and open-sea configurations. Clement Germany designed the breakwater and its mooring and anchoring (D3.2, D3.3), and engineers, supplies and installs it. The supply route depends on the project. The plant owner buys the breakwater from Clement Germany; where Sunlit Sea delivers the full system as EPC contractor, Sunlit Sea buys it and includes it in the EPC price; and where Clement Germany leads a project, Sunlit Sea supplies the floating PV system as its subcontractor. If Sunlit Sea or a third party were to build a breakwater from Clement Germany's design, a licence on fair and reasonable conditions, which may include royalties, would be agreed under the Consortium Agreement.
- Testing, modelling, monitoring and sustainability results reach KER1 as services, contracted per campaign or project.
- If a partner's model or tool were embedded in Sunlit Sea's site-planning tool, a licence would be agreed in the same way.

The WP8 lead proposes that each owner is paid where its result is used, for that use, and that the kit price carries no general partner charge. Each owner keeps the right to exploit its result outside floating PV. The basis differs by type of use. Services such as testing, modelling and studies are priced per campaign at the owner's market rates. Supplied components such as the breakwater are priced per project on cost and market benchmarks. A licence to build or embed a partner's design or tool is priced as a share of the value it creates, as a royalty or a fixed fee. These bases apply the Grant Agreement's definition of fair and reasonable conditions: appropriate conditions, including possible financial terms or royalty-free conditions, that take into account the actual or potential value of the results or background and the scope, duration or other characteristics of the exploitation. The terms for the access rights to the test results used in the hinge redesign are set when Sunlit Sea requests them in Q1 2027. Prices and detailed terms would be set in the bilateral agreements.
