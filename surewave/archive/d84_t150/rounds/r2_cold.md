# T150 round 2: cold-reader review of D8.4 (d84_merged.md)

Persona: an evaluator at another agency who has never seen the project. For every claim and choice the reader asks "how do we know?" and "why this?". Line numbers refer to d84_merged.md (1,420 lines, 39,934 words by heading-to-heading count). Repo sources were opened only where a doubt could be settled quickly. Decisions D1–D34 (Round 1 revisions win) are taken as given. Errors inside them are reported.

Verdict: the length mostly comes from evidence the requirements ask for, and the evidence should stay. The structure does not serve the reader. Three things make the reader hunt. (1) Before the reader reaches the KERs (Section 3, line 445), a 6,700-word dissemination record sits inside "Method and input". (2) The same technical evidence is written out in full three to five times (3.1, 3.2, 3.4, 4.1, 6.x, 4.10). (3) Some facts the reader needs to follow the business plan are spread over several sections: which reference plant is which, the TRL position, when and from whom the breakwater is bought, and when access rights are requested. The text could be about 25 % shorter in the main body without losing a single item of evidence. Moving the record to annexes saves about 8,000 words, and deduplication about 3,000–4,000 more.

1. Major. Structure, Section 2.5 (lines 166–441). "#### 2.5.1 Scientific publications ... #### 2.5.24 How the reach serves exploitation". The subsections are an item-by-item record of 24 indicators, 17 % of the document. They come before the KERs, inside "Method and input". A reader who wants the exploitation plan has to page through 270 lines of event tables first, and the methodological paragraphs (counting, evidence) are mixed in with the record. Source: section word counts above. Only two cross-references from outside Section 2.5 point to a 2.5.x subsection (grep "Section 2\.5\.[0-9]" after line 445: 2 hits, lines 1062 and 1320). Moving the subsections therefore costs two pointer edits. Fix: keep Section 2.5 in place with its number and with the two tables (lines 170–218), the "Sunlit Sea's own share" paragraph and the content of 2.5.24, which ties the reach to the KERs and belongs in the main text. Move the counting and evidence paragraphs (lines 196–200) and subsections 2.5.1–2.5.23 unchanged to "Annex A Dissemination and communication record", numbered A.1–A.23 in the same order. ## B. Numbers, logic and claims the reader cannot reconcile

9. Major. 4.1 hinge record (l. 577–583), together with 4.4 Lean Canvas (l. 750) and 4.8 (l. 956). The figures are now each labelled, but they are not compared like with like, and the reader draws the wrong conclusion. The tested segment is one fifth of the edge: 2.04 kN with the rope and 8.36 kN with a steel rod. Scaled to the full 1.88 m edge, the way D6.3 itself scales it ("represents only 1/5th of the total real connection. A gain of almost 3 times", surewave/background/2025-02-12_surewave_d6.3.md:849), that gives about 10 kN with the rope and about 42 kN with a rod. The design break load of D7.6 is 37 kN, and the simulated extreme loads are 33–34 kN in the Baltic and the Mediterranean (D7.6:689, 761). A cold reader concludes that the rope version would break at about a third of the simulated Baltic load. Only the rod version reaches the design value, and with the rod D6.3 limits the force in the polyurethane part to 6.9 kN for fatigue life (D6.3:841). The text says neither which variant CONNECT uses nor how 37 kN relates to the tested segment. Fix: add one bullet after "Design values": "Compared on the full 1.88 m edge, the tested segment corresponds to about 10 kN with the rope and about 42 kN with a steel rod (five segments). The 37 kN design break load of D7.6 is in the range of the rod version; with a rod, D6.3's fatigue analysis requires the force in the polyurethane part to stay below 6.9 kN. CONNECT uses [rope / rod / another holding element], and its break load is confirmed in the prototype 4 tests (Section 4.5)." Owner to fill the bracket. If the 37 kN has another basis in D7.6, state that basis instead.

Minor. 4.1 (l. 577): "D7.6 numbers the short hinge at the float corners as the second generation and the full-edge hinge as the third; SINTEF's test reports call the hinge tested before the redesign the first generation." 11. Major. 4.1.1 (l. 614): "Above about 1.3 m the waves that pass the breakwater exceed that version's working limit. There, or where open-sea swell reaches the site, the full SUREWAVE open-sea design applies: the same unit in a ring, with mooring and anchoring sized for open-sea storms, for design waves of up to 6.5 m in the Mediterranean (D5.1)." The logic jump: the paragraph has just said that longer waves pass a floating breakwater largely unreduced, and that above 1.3 m the transmitted waves exceed what the exposed-site platform takes. A ring of the same unit in 6.5 m seas transmits far more than 0.8 m. The reader asks how the array survives there, and why a ring works where a directional unit does not. The missing link is that the open-sea design was checked by a different criterion: simulated hinge loads in the 50-year sea states, against the hinge's design break load (D7.6; D5.1). It was not checked against Sunlit Sea's 0.45/0.6/0.8 m working limits. Fix, after "applies": "The open-sea design is checked differently: D5.1 and D7.6 simulated the hinge loads of the array in each site's extreme sea states and compared them with the hinge's design break load (Section 4.1), instead of applying the working wave limits used here for the commercial platform." Even so, the reader will see that the same status is treated two ways. The honest alternative is to drop the reason and say "The WP8 lead counts newsletters as written and other items as published."
